If we consider the hermetic forms a $\left(\mathrm{x}_{5}, \mathrm{x}_{6}\right)$ and b ( $\mathrm{x}_{4}, \mathrm{a}$ ) and c ( $\mathrm{R}_{3}$, a) and d ( $\mathrm{R}_{4}$, a)—that is, the solution manifolds  of [1, Eq. 19]—it immediately becomes clear that, with regard to the algebraic  properties, due to $\operatorname{Re}(\mathrm{a}, \mathrm{b})=0$, but $\operatorname{Re}(\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{d}) \neq 0$, the Hermitian forms a and b—interpreted as imaginary condensations—are just as closely related to one another as the complex Hermitian forms c and d. One can thus regard the four Hermitian forms as elements of the structured set 
$\mathrm{H}=\{(\mathrm{a}, \mathrm{b}),(\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{d})\}$ with $\|\mathrm{H}\|=\{2 ; 2\}=\mathrm{K}_{4}$. In fact, the formation and annihilation of pairs $(\mathrm{c}, \mathrm{d})+(\overline{\mathrm{c}}, \overline{\mathrm{d}}) \rightleftarrows \mathrm{b}$ are empirically known, whereby the strikethroughs denote the respective antiterms (i.e., those with negative time helicity). Furthermore, within this subset $\mathrm{c} \rightleftarrows \mathrm{d}$, the following can be observed: in a $\mathrm{R}_{3}$ transfer, group-theoretic properties are transmitted by neutrino fields [2, Chap. VIII]. In the other subset (a, b), according to [1, Eq. 24], it is only known that the terms of self-condensations, mapped in $\mathrm{R}_{4}$, appear as graviton fields G according to $\mathrm{a}\left(\mathrm{x}_{5}, \mathrm{x}_{6}\right) \rightarrow \mathrm{G}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{4}\right)$. A transition $\mathrm{b} \rightarrow \mathrm{G}$ would therefore be conceivable, but this process would have to proceed via an a-term, which can be justified by the fact that every time condensation b $\left(\mathrm{x}_{4}, \mathrm{a}\right)$, which corresponds to a photon, is structurally accompanied by an a-term. In the case a → G, the second-order gravitational tensor field would generally arise, but it is not gauge-invariant. In the process $b\left(x_{4}, a\right) \rightarrow a\left(x_{5}, x_{6}\right) \rightarrow G^{\prime}\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{4}\right)$, G’ must therefore be a field of a different nature but related to G; for the b-field is a gauge-invariant vector field, so that G’ must also possess these properties, in contrast to G. Thus, if $\mathrm{w}_{22}$ from (16) is the gravitational coupling constant of the non-gauge-invariant tensorial G-field,  then the gauge-invariant vector field G' generated by $\mathrm{b} \rightarrow \mathrm{a} \rightarrow \mathrm{G}$ , the gauge-invariant vector field G'—which is related to G—retains only the coupling constant $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha} \in \mathrm{~K}_{\mathrm{T}}$; that is, G' is represented by a transformational coupling constant. It  should also be noted that $\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{22}=\mathrm{w}_{22}-\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}$ is the coupling constant of a field  that, despite its gravitational nature, contains ponderable particles, which  justifies the interpretation of $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}$ as the coupling constant of the field G'.
