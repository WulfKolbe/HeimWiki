In addition to the Euclidean norm $|\overline{\mathrm{x}}|_{\mathrm{e}}=\left(\sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}^{2}\right)^{1 / 2}$, a non-Euclidean norm $|\overline{\mathrm{x}}|_{\mathrm{ne}}=|\overline{\mathrm{x}}|=\sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{\mathrm{n}} \cdot\left|\overline{\mathrm{x}}_{\mathrm{i}}\right|$ should now also be be possible, which, according to [Teubner1986](../references/WDorg4_REF_Teubner1986.md), leads to the concept of a normed metric space in the functional-analytic sense, defined by the existence of a distance $\mathrm{d}(\overline{\mathrm{x}}, \overline{\mathrm{y}})$.  The well-known distance axioms apply to this distance [[Allgemeine1964](../references/WDorg4_REF_Allgemeine1964.md), [7 bis 9](../references/WDorg4_REF_7_bis_9.md)]. As noted, the $b_{1} \bar{g}_{i}, b_{2} \bar{g}_{j}, b_{3} \bar{g}_{k}$ belong to a $R_{6}^{*}$, but an extension is possible. 

It can be assumed that $\mathrm{d}_{1}^{\prime}, \mathrm{d}_{2}^{\prime}$ can also form $\mathrm{b}_{1}^{\prime} \overline{\mathrm{g}}_{1}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}^{\prime} \overline{\mathrm{g}}_{\mathrm{m}}$  which are regarded as vector components of a $\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*,}$ with $\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*} \cap \mathrm{R}_{6}^{*,}=\{\overline{0}\}$. The $\cap$means here that the spaces $\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*}$ and $\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*}$, characterized by their coordinates, have no common coordinates. This is because the cardinal number $\mathrm{K}_{12}=\{3 ; 1 ; 2 ; 2 ; 4\}$ is obtained when a $\mathrm{K}_{6}^{\prime}=\{2 ; 4\}$ is added to the $\mathrm{K}_{6}=\{3 ; 1 ; 2\}$. Since the $\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*}$ differs structurally from the $\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*,}$ , this can also be assumed for the formation of $\mathrm{b}_{1}^{\prime}$ and $\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime}$.  Because of $\left(\mathrm{b}_{1}^{\prime} \overline{\mathrm{g}}_{1}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}^{\prime} \overline{\mathrm{g}}_{\mathrm{m}}\right) \in \mathrm{R}_{6}^{*,}$ and due to the structure of this $\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*,}$,  $\mathrm{b}_{1}^{\prime} \overline{\mathrm{g}}_{1} \in \mathrm{R}_{2}^{* \prime}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}^{\prime} \overline{\mathrm{g}}_{\mathrm{m}} \in \mathrm{R}_{4}^{* \prime}$ would be given by $\mathrm{R}_{2}^{* \prime} \cup \mathrm{R}_{4}^{* \prime}=\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*,}$. 

Using $\left(\mathrm{b}_{1}^{1}, \mathrm{~b}_{1}^{2}\right),\left(\mathrm{b}_{2}^{1}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}^{2}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}^{3}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}^{4}\right)$, a union space with coordinates $\left(\mathrm{b}_{1}^{\prime}, \mathrm{b}_{1}^{2}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}^{\prime}, \mathrm{b}_{2}^{2}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}^{3}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}^{4}\right)$ is formed. Using a combined Euclidean and non-Euclidean norm, $\mathrm{b}_{1}^{\prime}$ and $\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime}$ can be generated as follows: $\mathrm{c}_{1}=\sqrt{\mathrm{d}_{1}^{\prime 2}+\mathrm{d}_{2}^{\prime 2}+\mathrm{d}_{3}^{\prime 2}}, \mathrm{c}_{1}=\mathrm{b}_{1}^{\prime}+\mathrm{b}_{1}^{\prime}$ and $\mathrm{c}_{2}=\sqrt{\mathrm{d}_{1}{ }^{2}+\mathrm{d}_{2}{ }^{2}}$, as well as $\mathrm{c}_{2}=\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime}+\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime}+\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime}+\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime}$. 

As has already been derived, $\mathrm{d}_{1}, \mathrm{~d}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{d}_{3}$ can be represented with very high precision by the elements of the set $\mathrm{U}=\left\{\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}, \mathrm{a}_{3}\right\}=\{1,1 / 2,1 / 3\}$. Furthermore, $\mathrm{b}_{1}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{b}_{3}$ are generated from $\mathrm{d}_{1}, \mathrm{~d}_{2}$ and  $\mathrm{d}_{3}$, which belong to a $\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*}$ with the cardinality complex of the structured coordinate set $\mathrm{K}_{6}=\{3 ; 1 ; 2\}$. If one compares the set  U with $\mathrm{K}_{6}$ and denotes by $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{i}}^{*} \subset \mathrm{R}_{6}^{*}$ the subspaces of $\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*}$, then the following pattern emerges:
