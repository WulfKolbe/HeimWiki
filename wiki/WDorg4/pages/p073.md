There is agreement regarding the dimensional numbers of the subspaces. However, “A” can also be interpreted as a higher-order cardinal number complex $\mathrm{K}_{28}=\{(1 ; 3 ; 2 ; 2 ; 4),(2 ; 2 ; 2 ; 4 ; 6)\}$. Applied to the “present” moment $\mathrm{t}=\mathrm{T}$ this would then mean that two coordinate spaces $R_{12}\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{12}\right)$ and $R_{16}\left(x_{13} \ldots x_{28}\right)$ exist that are physically very different from one another. A $R_{12}\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{12}\right)$ is already evident from [1, 49, and Chap. IV, 5]; a $\mathrm{R}_{16}\left(\mathrm{x}_{13} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{28}\right)$ could have been significant during the cosmogony of the observable universe about $1,5 \cdot 10^{10}$ years ago. However, the elements $\mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{i}+1}>\mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{i}}$, ordered ascendingly in A, corresponding to the calculus of cardinal numbers, be subjected to combinatorial operations, the simplest form of which is addition $\delta_{\mathrm{k}}=\mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{i}}+\mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{i}+1}$. This yields $\delta_{1}=4, \delta_{2}=12$ as well as  $\delta_{3}=24$ and $\delta_{4}=36$. Finally, A yields $\delta_{5}=\delta_{1}+\delta_{3}=28$, whereas $\delta_{6}=\delta_{5}+\delta_{4}=64$ is obtained. For entirely different reasons, a set of dimensional numbers $D^{\prime}=\{12,28,24\}$ has already been derived, whose elements are identical to $\delta_{2}, \delta_{5}, \delta_{3}$. Thus, $\delta_{1}=\operatorname{dim}\left(\mathrm{R}_{4}\right)=\operatorname{dim}\left(\mathrm{G}_{4}\right)$ holds, as do $\delta_{2}=\operatorname{dim}\left(\mathrm{R}_{12}\right)$, $\delta_{3}=\operatorname{dim}\left(\mathrm{R}_{24}\right)$, and $\delta_{5}=\operatorname{dim}\left(\mathrm{R}_{28}\right)$. This leaves $\delta_{4}=36$ and $\delta_{6}=64$. These are apparently the dimension numbers of representative spaces, since the components of energy-density tensors in $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ or $\mathrm{R}_{8}$ can, as has already been shown [1, Chap. IV, 4], be represented by basis vectors that then span a $\mathrm{R}_{36}$ or $\mathrm{R}_{64}$, provided that these tensors contain no zero components. However, this condition is always satisfied within the uncertainty interval of canonically conjugate quantities due to $\Delta \mathrm{E} \Delta \mathrm{t} \geq \hbar>0$ for $\Delta \mathrm{t}>0$. Thus, $\delta_{4}=\operatorname{dim}\left(\mathrm{R}_{36}\right)$ and $\delta_{6}=\operatorname{dim}\left(\mathrm{R}_{64}\right)$ also hold. These 6 dimensional numbers, which can be interpreted as preformations in the aperion $\mathrm{t}<0$, thus form the sets $\left\{\delta_{2}, \delta_{5}, \delta_{3}\right\}$  as well as $\left\{\delta_{4}\right\}$ and $\left\{\delta_{1}, \delta_{6}\right\}$, which can be combined as subsets to form a set of dimensional numbers $\underline{\mathrm{D}}=\left\{\left(\delta_{2}, \delta_{5}, \delta_{3}\right),\left(\delta_{4}\right),\left(\delta_{1}, \delta_{6}\right)\right\}$ whose cardinal number, in turn, is $\|\underline{\mathrm{D}}\|=\{3 ; 1 ; 2\}$, that is, $\mathrm{K}_{6}$. This union therefore also satisfies a $\mathrm{K}_{6}$ symmetry. Since  $\delta_{6}=\delta_{1} \cdot \delta_{1} \cdot \delta_{1}=\delta_{1}{ }^{3}$, the subset $\left\{\delta_{1}, \delta_{6}\right\}=\left\{\delta_{1}, \delta_{1} \cdot \delta_{1} \cdot \delta_{1}\right\}$ is determined with respect to its elements by a $\mathrm{K}_{4}=\{1 ; 3\}$-symmetry. The termination condition sought in A is here at the element $\mathrm{a}_{8}=19$, so that $\mathrm{j}=8$ holds.
