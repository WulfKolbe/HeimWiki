From the set of exponents $\overline{\mathrm{L}}$ in [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md), in addition to $\mathrm{R}_{12}$ a $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}^{*}$  with $1 \leq \mathrm{n}<\infty$, whose coordinates $\mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{n}}$ are dimensionless sets of numbers due to the dimensionality  $\left[\mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{n}}\right]=\sqrt{\mathrm{m} / \mathrm{m}}$ with m: meters. Throughout the following text, the superscript asterisk (*) does not denote the complex conjugate of a quantity, but rather serves as an index for dimensionless values that have been rendered dimensionless through the formation of ratios. For example, $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}^{*}$ means that the coordinates of this space are independent, dimensionless sets of pure numbers. In the case of a complex number c, the adjunction symbol $\mathrm{c}^{\mathrm{x}}$ is used for the complex conjugate, because the adjunction becomes the complex conjugate anyway in the absence of transposable indices. If the line elements of the subspaces of $\mathrm{R}_{12}$ are denoted by $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{G}} \in \mathrm{G}_{4}, \delta \mathrm{~s}_{\mathrm{I}} \in \mathrm{I}_{2}, \delta \mathrm{~s}_{\mathrm{S}} \in \mathrm{S}_{2}$ and $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathrm{T}_{1}$ and $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{R}} \in \mathrm{R}_{3}$ or $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{RZ}} \in \mathrm{R}_{4}$, respectively, then a $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{n}}^{*} \hat{=} \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}^{*}$ induces a mapping $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{G}} \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{V}}^{*} \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{~s}_{\mathrm{I}} \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{S}} \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{T}} \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{R}}$ according to [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md),  if $\mathrm{ds}_{\mathrm{V}}^{*} \in \mathrm{~V}_{\mathrm{n}}^{*}$ is a dimensionless “length element” of the mediating space $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{n}}^{*}$ due to $\left[\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{V}}^{*}\right]=\mathrm{m} / \mathrm{m}$. According to [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md), $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{V}}^{*}=\sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{4} \delta \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{i}}^{2}=\delta \mathrm{y}_{1}^{2}\left(\mathrm{x}_{9}\right)+\delta \mathrm{y}_{2}^{2}\left(\mathrm{x}_{10}\right)+\delta \mathrm{y}_{3}^{2}\left(\mathrm{x}_{11}\right)+\delta \mathrm{y}_{4}^{2}\left(\mathrm{x}_{12}\right)$ exists, whereby, if the $\delta \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{i}}$ are dimensionless “elementary lengths,”  $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{V}}^{*}=\sum_{\mathrm{j}=1}^{4}(-1)^{\mathrm{j}+1} \exp \left(-2 \pi\left(\mathrm{~m}_{\mathrm{j}} \pm 1 / 2\right)\right)$ can be written. The mapping of $y_{i}$ to $x_{k} \in G_{4}$ is unique here, namely $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{V}}^{*}=\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{V}}^{*}\left(\mathrm{y}_{1}\left(\mathrm{x}_{9}\right), \ldots, \mathrm{y}_{4}\left(\mathrm{x}_{12}\right)\right)$. In the mapping $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{V}}^{*} \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{~s}_{\mathrm{I}}$, there is a change in the coordinate space $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{n}}^{*} \rightarrow \mathrm{I}_{2}$, which, for dimensioning purposes, requires the transition $\left[\mathrm{ds}_{\mathrm{V}}^{*}\right] \rightarrow\left[\mathrm{ds}_{\mathrm{I}}\right]=\mathrm{m}$. 

Another mapping process would involve not leaving the intermediate space $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{n}}^{*}$ at all during the mapping. Then $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{G}} \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{G}}^{*}\left(\mathrm{y}_{1}\left(\mathrm{x}_{9} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{12}\right), \ldots, \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{n}}\left(\mathrm{x}_{9} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{12}\right)\right) \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{s}_{1}^{*}\left(\mathrm{y}_{1}\left(\mathrm{x}_{7}, \mathrm{x}_{8}\right), \ldots, \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{n}}\left(\mathrm{x}_{7}, \mathrm{x}_{8}\right)\right) \rightarrow$ would be $\rightarrow \delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{S}}^{*}\left(\mathrm{y}_{1}\left(\mathrm{x}_{5}, \mathrm{x}_{6}\right), \ldots, \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{n}}\left(\mathrm{x}_{5}, \mathrm{x}_{6}\right)\right) \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{RZ}}^{*}\left(\mathrm{y}_{1}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{4}\right), \ldots, \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{n}}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{4}\right)\right) \quad \mathrm{zu}$. If $\mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{k}}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{x}_{4}\right)$ is interpreted as a component of a probability amplitude in non-deterministic quantum theory, then in $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{RZ}}^{*}$ the dependence on $\left(\mathrm{y}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{k}-1}, \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{k}+1}, \ldots, \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{n}}\right)$ no longer appears when $\sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{\mathrm{n}}\left|\mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{i}}\left(\mathrm{x}_{5}, \mathrm{x}_{6}\right)\right|^{2}=\left|\mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{k}}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{x}_{4}\right)\right|^{2}$ is set.
