The number is proven. If, for example, we set n to 0, 1, or 2, then in all three cases the positive branch is $\mathrm{N}=2$ and the negative branch is $\mathrm{N}=0$. For all $\mathrm{n}>2$, however, the negative branch does not apply. For $\mathrm{R}_{3}$ with $\mathrm{n}=3$, N(n) does not hold, but for $\mathrm{n}=4$, the hyperspace is $\mathrm{N}=6$, that is,  $\mathrm{R}_{4} \subset \mathrm{R}_{6}$. If $\mathrm{n}=5$, or $\mathrm{n}>6$, then the dimension law no longer holds; however, for $\mathrm{n}=6$ the dimension number is $\mathrm{N}=12$, from which it follows that there must still be a hyperspace $\mathrm{R}_{12}$ that encompasses both spacetime and the energetically determined $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ as subspaces $\mathrm{R}_{4} \subset \mathrm{R}_{6} \subset \mathrm{R}_{12}$. 

The eigenvalue relations in $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ with $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}} \neq \mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}^{\mathrm{x}}$ can now be written in $\mathrm{R}_{6}$  in Hermitian form using $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}=\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ki}}^{*}$ [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md). However, the infinitesimal relations must be expressed in terms of a difference calculus because a surface element was derived as the geometric unit of measurement, for which the numerical value $\tau \approx 6,15 \cdot 10^{-70} \mathrm{~m}^{2}$ is given by [1, Eq. 15]. The formalism of this difference calculus was developed in [1, Chap. III] in its general form. 
The system of difference equations derived in this way in $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ as [1, Eq. 19] can be solved, revealing that there are 4 classes of solutions, which are “Hermitian forms” and  relate to the subspaces $\mathrm{R}_{3}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}, \mathrm{x}_{2}, \mathrm{x}_{3}\right)$, $\mathrm{T}_{1}\left(\mathrm{x}_{4}\right)$, and $\mathrm{S}_{2}\left(\mathrm{x}_{5}, \mathrm{x}_{6}\right)$. In what follows, the symbol $\cup$ will be used for the outer set product for the sake of brevity. Thus, it would be $\mathrm{F}=\mathrm{a} \cup \mathrm{b}$ with $\mathrm{a} \subset \mathrm{F}$, but also $\mathrm{b} \subset \mathrm{F}$  , which also applies to hermetic subspaces, so that the $\mathrm{R}_{6}=\mathrm{R}_{3} \cup \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{~S}_{2}$ undergoes a structuring of its coordinate set by these semantic units of the hermetic spaces. Here, the semantics of $\mathrm{x}_{5}$ and $\mathrm{x}_{6}$, as described in [2, Chap. V, 2], proves to be particularly suitable for interpreting the four solution manifolds; for $\mathrm{x}_{5}$ and $\mathrm{x}_{6}$ take on the  meaning of organizational coordinates. While the real, commutative coordinates $\mathrm{x}_{1}, \mathrm{x}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{x}_{3}$ span the physical manifold $\mathrm{R}_{3}$, which is compact with respect to the rotation group, , the imaginary-counting coordinates $\mathrm{x}_{4}$  through $\mathrm{x}_{6}$ are not commutative. 

The four possible forms of hermeticism, a through d, turn out to be non-Euclidean structures in their respective subspaces: a in $\mathrm{S}_{2}, \mathrm{~b}$, in $\mathrm{T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{~S}_{2}$, c in $\mathrm{R}_{3} \cup \mathrm{~S}_{2}$ and dim in $\mathrm{R}_{3} \cup \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{~S}_{2}$. The interpretation of these forms of hermetry was derived in $[1$, Chapter IV, 1–3 $]$. These forms of hermeticism are to be regarded as the primordial form of material occurrence par excellence.
