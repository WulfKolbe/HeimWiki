The primordial elements of set (6a) described in (1) allow for a representation of the dimensionless lengths $\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}$ according to (3) and (3a), as well as  (5) and (5a), which define both the origin of the world $\mathrm{t}=0$ and, at least in part, lengths of a type that occur later in time $(\mathrm{t}=\mathrm{T}>0)$. Furthermore, all of these $\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}$ are timeless elements of the mediator space $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}^{*}$. Since the probability amplitudes $\psi\left(\underline{x}_{1} \ldots \underline{x}_{4}\right)=\psi(\overrightarrow{\mathrm{r}}, \mathrm{t})$ are to be understood as mappings of functions of the $\mathrm{G}_{4}$ into the $\mathrm{R}_{4}^{\mathrm{a}}$ according to (9) and (9a), it can be established that these probability amplitudes, too, can be traced back to the $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}^{*}$. 

We can now investigate whether, by analogy with the $\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}$, there are also elementary probability amplitudes $\psi_{\mathrm{i}}=\beta_{\mathrm{i}}$ that are structured with $\mathrm{i}=1 \ldots 8$  in accordance with a $\mathrm{K}_{8}$ symmetry. It is to be expected that these  elementary probabilities do not, for the time being, depend on the $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ coordinates, i.e., they are of a spaceless and timeless nature. Dimensionless probability amplitudes $\beta_{1}$, which depend on the distance  between a charge $\mathrm{q}_{\mathrm{l}}$ and $\mathrm{l}=1 \ldots 4$,  already emerge from indeterministic quantum theory. Here, $\alpha_{1}=\beta_{1}{ }^{2}$ is the creation or annihilation probability of interaction constituents of type l, whereby the charge $\mathrm{q}_{\mathrm{l}}$ emits or absorbs interaction quanta of type 1. In current  quantum physics, these coupling constants $\beta_{1}$ cannot, on the whole—especially  in the low-energy range—be derived from a unified theory. 

To determine the coupling constants $\beta_{\mathrm{l}}$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{i}}$ a mathematical method must be used that is capable of tracing these constants back to the primordial elements and their symmetries at the time of cosmogonic origin, which corresponds to an analogy with the determination of $\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}$ according to  (3) and (3a) or (5) and (5a), respectively. Essentially, the $\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}$ could be approximated as a function $\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{j}}=\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{j}}(\mathrm{g})$ with $\mathrm{g}=1 / 12$ according to (1). However, the $\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{j}}(\mathrm{g})$ can only approximately represent the $\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}$ for $\mathrm{j}=1 \ldots 8$.
