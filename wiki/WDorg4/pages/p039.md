The following applies: $\mathrm{V}_{28}^{*}\left(\mathrm{y}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{y}_{28}\right)$, whereby the $\mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{k}}^{2}$—along with $1 \leq \mathrm{k} \leq 28$—may also represent primordial elements of the world. These coordinate squares of the $\mathrm{V}_{28}^{*}$ can be expressed in terms of the ratios of the sphere diameters of the $\mathrm{R}_{3}$ to the time $\mathrm{t}=0$. Furthermore, symmetry relations must be investigated that mutually support the existence of such primordial elements. The chain of mappings of “elementary lengths,” namely $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{G}} \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{G}}^{*} \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{~s}_{\mathrm{I}}^{*} \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{~s}_{\mathrm{S}}^{*} \rightarrow \delta \mathrm{~s}_{\mathrm{RZ}}^{*}$, may determine the probability amplitudes of present-day indeterministic quantum theory. Using the mathematics suitable for describing the $\mathrm{V}_{28}^{*}$ at the time $\mathrm{t}=0$, it must then be possible to express all coupling constants $\beta_{\mathrm{i}}$ of all interactions (including as yet unknown types) in a unified form. This appears possible because the mathematics valid at the time $\mathrm{t}=0$ is not only capable of establishing a set of elementary lengths $\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{i}}^{\prime}, \mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{j}}, \mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{j}}^{\prime}$ at the time $\mathrm{t}=0$ at the time of the origin of the world, but could also define probability amplitudes that are identical to the coupling constants.
