If $\mathrm{m}, \alpha$ and $\beta$ are cardinal numbers, then, as is well known, according to the cardinal number calculus, $\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{m}^{\alpha+\beta}$ or $\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{m}^{\alpha \beta}$ or, more generally, with the cardinal numbers $m_{i}$, provided that $a=\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_{i}$ represents the sum and $b=\prod_{i=1}^{n} m_{i}$ represents the product, $\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{m}^{\mathrm{a}}$ and $\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{m}^{\mathrm{b}}$, respectively. By analogy with the use of  primitive elements (as rational numbers) in place of cardinal numbers, it is now necessary, in extending the concept of numbers, to develop a basis that belongs to the algebraic field of real numbers. Here, one can start from the formation law of elementary lengths $\delta \mathrm{x}_{7,8}$ in $\mathrm{R}_{12}$, that is, from $\mathrm{e}^{-1}$, which suggests the generalizations $A=\exp \left(-\sum_{i=1}^{n} k_{i}\right)$ and $B=\exp \left(-\prod_{i=1}^{n} k_{i}\right)$. The functions $f_{3}$ and $f_{4}$ may now follow the exponential notation of A and B. If $\mathrm{f}_{3}$ and $\mathrm{f}_{4}$ applied to the second term, respectively, of the given sequences, this implies a successive expansion of the set brackets to arrive at a numerical value for A or B. For example, $\mathrm{f}_{3}$ or $\mathrm{f}_{4}$ , when applied to $\left(\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}\right),\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}\right)\right)$, result in the representations $\left(\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}\right),\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}\right)\right) \rightarrow \exp \left(-2 \mathrm{a}_{1}-\mathrm{a}_{2}\right) \quad$ and $\quad\left(\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}\right),\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}\right)\right) \rightarrow \exp \left(-\mathrm{a}_{1}^{2} \mathrm{a}_{2}\right) \quad$, respectively.  Since in the second term of the fifth set sequence, $\mathrm{a}_{1}$ appears a total of five times, but $\mathrm{a}_{2}$ appears only once, the following results for the two elements of the set $\mathrm{w}_{5}$ by analogy to $\mathrm{w}_{4}$, when r and s from (17) are now substituted:  $\mathrm{w}_{5_{\alpha}}^{\prime}=\exp \left(-5 \mathrm{r}-\mathrm{a}_{2}\right)$ and $\mathrm{w}_{5 \beta}^{\prime}=\exp \left(-\mathrm{a}_{2} \mathrm{~s}^{5}\right)$, provided that, by analogy with $\mathrm{w}_{4}$, the variables are again distributed among the function operators. 

Since there are no variables left other than r and s, for $\mathrm{a}_{2}$ the respective identity element of the corresponding conjunction operation can be set, resulting in $w_{5 \alpha}^{\prime}=\exp (-5 r)$ and $w_{5 \beta}^{\prime}=\exp \left(-s^{5}\right)$. This specific choice of $\mathrm{a}_{2}$ differs from $\mathrm{a}_{1}=\ldots=\mathrm{a}_{4}=\mathrm{n}$ or p in $\mathrm{w}_{4}$. If, in addition, $\mathrm{f}_{1}=()^{1 / 2}$ is applied to generate a subset of $\left(\mathrm{f}_{1}, \mathrm{f}_{2}\right)$ ($\mathrm{f}_{11}, \mathrm{f}_{12}, \mathrm{f}_{13}, \mathrm{f}_{21}$ ) with the $\mathrm{K}_{4}$ symmetry $\mathrm{K}_{4}=\{3 ; 1\}$, then it appears three times in $\mathrm{f}_{1}$, namely as $\sqrt{\mathrm{m}}, \sqrt{\mathrm{w}_{5 \alpha}^{\prime}}$ and $\sqrt{\mathrm{w}_{5 \beta}^{\prime}}$, resulting in $w_{5 \alpha}=\sqrt{w_{5 \alpha}^{\prime}}$ and $w_{5 \beta}=\sqrt{w_{5 \beta}^{\prime}}$. 

The numerical values of (16) show that the set $\mathrm{w}_{21} \ldots \mathrm{w}_{24}$ consists of the physically known coupling constants, each of which defines an interaction field corresponding to a specific Hermitian form in $\mathrm{R}_{6}$, to which, as shown in [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md), the material world must be  related; which in turn defines the $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ in terms of energy.
