As is well known, the intensity of an interaction field with finite range (let r be a $\mathrm{R}_{3}$-distance and $\mathrm{r}_{0}$ is such a constant) decays exponentially with the factor $\exp \left(-\mathrm{r} / \mathrm{r}_{0}\right)$. If such an exponential decay process is assumed for the change in an uncertainty field—which is generally always possible—then $r / r_{0}$ determined by the ratio of the energy limits of this field, i.e., the Wq energies in the interaction field. Since the energy thresholds according to (21) are given by $\mathrm{E}_{5}$ and $\mathrm{E}_{6}$ and always hold for an energy $\mathrm{E} \sim 1 / \mathrm{r}$ , the approximation $\hbar=\hbar$ 'exp $\left(-\mathrm{E}_{5} / \mathrm{E}_{6}\right)$ can be made for $\hbar$. Since, according to (21), the ratio $\mathrm{E}_{5} / \mathrm{E}_{6} \approx 63$ follows, $\hbar^{\prime} \approx 2,3 \cdot 10^{27} \hbar \gg \hbar$ would be the consequence for the cosmogonic phase according to $\mathrm{T}_{1}$. This phase of an inflationary cosmogony thus begins at time $\mathrm{T}_{1}$ with particle energies of $\mathrm{E}_{5}$ each, which decay to $\mathrm{E}_{6}$ during the cosmogenic time interval, marking the end of the matter cosmogony of an elementary universe. If there is a momentum difference $\Delta \mathrm{p}$ and the canonically conjugate distance $\Delta \mathrm{r}$ then, on the one hand, according to (8), at least $\Delta \mathrm{p} \Delta \mathrm{r}=\hbar$ holds, but on the other hand, $\Delta \mathrm{p}^{\prime} \Delta \mathrm{r}^{\prime}=\hbar$ holds, while for the momentum difference $\Delta \mathrm{p}=\Delta \mathrm{p}^{\prime}$ must be satisfied as a conservation law. This means that $\Delta \mathrm{p} \Delta \mathrm{r}^{\prime}=\hbar^{\prime}$ also holds, so that $\Delta \mathbf{r}^{\prime} / \Delta \mathbf{r}=\hbar / \hbar \approx 2,3 \cdot 10^{27}$ must hold. This means that a spherical volume  with radius $r$ expands during the inflationary phase of the elementary universe  according to $\mathrm{r}^{\prime} / \mathrm{r}=\exp \left(\mathrm{E}_{5} / \mathrm{E}_{6}\right)=2,3 \cdot 10^{27}$ to a radius of $\mathrm{r}^{\prime} \gg \mathrm{r}$.  Correspondingly, due to $\mathrm{r}^{\prime} / \mathrm{r}=\mathrm{c}^{\prime} / \mathrm{c}$, the same follows for the expansion velocity $\mathrm{c}^{\prime}=\mathrm{uc}$ when the abbreviation $\mathrm{u}=\exp \left(\mathrm{E}_{5} / \mathrm{E}_{6}\right) \approx 2,3 \cdot 10^{27}$ is introduced. Upon completion of this cosmogonic but inflationary time interval $\mathrm{T}_{1} \leq \mathrm{t} \leq \mathrm{T}_{2}<\mathrm{T}$, the expansion velocity of this energetic fireball drops to a value below c. Incidentally, r' = ur agrees quite well with the radius ratio given in [Wissenschaft1984b](../references/WDorg4_REF_Wissenschaft1984b.md).
