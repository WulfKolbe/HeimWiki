In general, $\mathrm{F} \neq \mathrm{F}^{\mathrm{x}}$ is complex in nature when the adjoint symbol is used to denote complex conjugation. The real function that can be generated from $\mathrm{F} \neq \mathrm{F}^{\mathrm{x}}$ is $\mathrm{B}=\mathrm{FF}^{\mathrm{x}}$. 

If $\phi\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{3}, \mathrm{t}\right)$ is a function in $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ that arises from the compactification of the  hyperspace coordinates $x_{k}$ with $k>4$, then the following holds for the functional connection in the hyperspace $\phi\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{3}, \mathrm{t}, \mathrm{x}_{5} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{12}\right)$ or, according to [Company1973](../references/WDorg4_REF_Company1973.md), , $\phi\left(\mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{t}, \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{k}}\right)=\varphi\left(\mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{t}\right) \cdot \exp \left(\mathrm{i} \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{k}} \mu \mathrm{n}\right), \mathrm{n} \in \mathrm{N}$ also holds if, in terms of dimensions, $\left[\mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{k}}\right]=\mathrm{m}$ and $[\mu]=1 / \mathrm{m}$. Let R denote the curvature caused by a material elementary particle in $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ (see [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) and [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md)), then $\mathrm{R}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{4}\right)$ would exist, and because $\mathrm{B}\left(\underline{\mathrm{x}}_{1} \ldots \underline{\mathrm{x}}_{4}\right)$ can be represented by $\mathrm{B}\left(\mathrm{x}_{9} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{12}\right)$, a measure of curvature 
$\underline{\mathrm{R}}\left(\underline{\mathrm{x}}_{1} \ldots \underline{\mathrm{x}}_{4}\right)=\mathrm{B}\left(\mathrm{x}_{9} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{12}\right) \mathrm{R}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{4}\right)$ or $\underline{\mathrm{R}}=\mathrm{B}\left(\underline{\mathrm{x}}_{1} \ldots \underline{\mathrm{x}}_{4}\right) \mathrm{R}$. Here, the space-time coordinates derived from the mapping process in $\mathrm{G}_{4}$ are emphasized to clarify a physical distinction with respect to the coordinates $\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{4}\right)$ from [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) and [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md). 

If $\alpha=$ is a constant proportionality factor and $\rho$ a spatial energy density, then formally, according to [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md), in a non-tensor notation,  $\mathrm{R}=\alpha \rho$. If E is the energy in the volume difference $\Delta \mathrm{V}$ of $\mathrm{R}_{3}$, then, as is well known, $\mathrm{E}=\int_{\Delta \mathrm{V}} \rho \mathrm{dV}$ or $\alpha \mathrm{E}=\int_{\Delta \mathrm{V}} \mathrm{RdV}$ holds. However, $\underline{\mathrm{R}}=\mathrm{BR}$ and $\underline{\mathrm{R}}=\alpha \underline{\rho}$ also hold, so $\underline{\mathrm{E}}=\int_{\Delta \mathrm{V}} \rho \mathrm{dV}=1 / \alpha \int_{\Delta \mathrm{V}} \underline{\mathrm{R}} \mathrm{dV}$ or 
$\alpha \underline{E}=\int_{\Delta V} B R d V=\int_{\Delta V} B \alpha \rho d V=\alpha \int_{\Delta V} \rho B d V \quad$ or $\int_{V} \underline{\rho} d V=\int_{\Delta V} \rho B d V$, i.e., $\underline{\rho}=B \rho$. 
If these are material elementary structures—that is, the quanta  of the possible forms of hermeticism, i.e., the solution manifolds of  [1, Eq. 19]— then the $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ structures of the relevant elementary matter field quanta remain unchanged and cannot dissipate, so that the possible spatial energy density fluctuations caused by $\underline{\rho}=\mathrm{B} \rho$ cause such an elementary structure to lose its localization. $B=F F^{x}$ can be integrated over a $R_{4}$-interval $\Omega$,  which yields $\int_{\Omega} \mathrm{FF}^{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{d} \Omega=$ const $<\infty$, because the sequentially updated  $\mathrm{R}_{3}$ volumes, when updated sequentially, behave with respect to $\mathrm{x}_{4}$ like $\mathrm{R}_{3}$-distance spaces. Thus, the spacetime integral according to $\int_{\Omega} \mathrm{FF}^{\mathrm{x}} \mathrm{d} \Omega=1$ can also be normalized if $\Omega$ represents the entire $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ domain.
