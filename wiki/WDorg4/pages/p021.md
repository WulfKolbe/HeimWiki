Particles caused by the transcoordinates—which only occur at energies ranging from $10^{15}$ to $10^{17} \mathrm{GeV}$—are supposed to interact within the known low-energy particle range. 

Instead of the supergravity theory, the ten-dimensional superstring theory was developed, in which the chirality problem no longer arises.  Six of the ten coordinates are again compactified here. In this theory as well—just as in supergravity theory—the problem arises of explaining and correctly predicting the low-energy particle masses. Overall, it is not clear how the low, empirically measured particle masses, in the form of uniform spectra, together with the quantum numbers characterizing the particles, are supposed to emerge from these theories.  Finally, there is no way to determine whether and how the coupling constants of the various interactions, together with their symmetry groups, can be derived from these theories.
