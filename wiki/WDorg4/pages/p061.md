In such intervals, H' then approximates three-dimensional functions using  Fourier integrals. 
III): $\mathrm{R}_{4(2 \mathrm{v}+1)}^{*}\left(\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}^{-\mathrm{M}^{*}} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{4}^{-\mathrm{M}^{*}}\right) \ldots\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}^{\mathrm{M}^{*}} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{4}^{\mathrm{M}^{*}}\right)\right) \rightarrow \mathrm{R}_{4}^{\prime}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{4}\right)$: Let us first examine only the one-dimensional mapping $\mathrm{R}_{2 \mathrm{v}+1}^{*}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}^{-\mathrm{M}^{*}} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{1}^{\mathrm{M}^{*}}\right) \rightarrow \mathrm{R}_{4}^{\prime}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}\right)$. In $\mathrm{R}_{2 \mathrm{v}+1}^{*}$, a one-dimensional function Z in the form of a Fourier series $\mathrm{Z}=\sum_{\mathrm{a}=-\mathrm{M}}^{\mathrm{M}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{a}} \cdot \exp \left(\mathrm{ix}_{1}^{\mathrm{a}^{*}}\right)$  which, upon renaming the indices $\mathrm{a} \rightarrow \mathrm{v} \mathrm{zu} \mathrm{Z}=\sum_{\mathrm{v}=-\mathrm{M}}^{\mathrm{M}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{v}} \exp \left(\mathrm{ix}_{1}^{\mathrm{v}^{*}}\right)$, becomes  . Using the abbreviations $\mathrm{x}_{1} \leq \mathrm{L}$ and $1 / \mathrm{x}_{10}^{\mathrm{v}}=\pi \mathrm{v} / \mathrm{L}=\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{v}}$ and  $\mathrm{C}\left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{v}}\right)=\sqrt{2 / \pi} \cdot \mathrm{L} \cdot \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{v}}$, Z becomes a finite Fourier series $\mathrm{Z} \rightarrow \mathrm{F}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}\right)$,  for which the expression $\mathrm{F}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}\right)=\sum_{\mathrm{v}=-\mathrm{M}}^{\mathrm{M}} \sqrt{\pi / 2} \frac{\mathrm{C}\left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{v}}\right)}{\mathrm{L}} \exp \left(\mathrm{iK}_{\mathrm{v}} \mathrm{x}_{1}\right)$ must be set. This series, in turn, yields a Fourier integral with $\mathrm{L} \rightarrow \infty$, namely $\mathrm{F}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}\right) \cdot \sqrt{2 \pi} \approx \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \mathrm{C}(\mathrm{K}) \exp \left(\mathrm{iKx}_{1}\right) \mathrm{dK}$ as an approximation. If the time coordinate $\mathrm{x}_{4}=\mathrm{ict}$ is included in Z, then 
$\underline{\mathrm{Z}}=\sum_{\mathrm{v}=-\mathrm{M}}^{\mathrm{M}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{v}} \exp \left(\mathrm{i}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}^{\mathrm{v}^{*}}+\mathrm{x}_{4}^{\mathrm{v}^{*}}\right)\right)$ results, with $\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{v}}=$ constant. In this function, the truncations $\mathrm{x}_{4}=$ ict as well as $\mathrm{ic} / \mathrm{x}_{40}^{\mathrm{v}}=-\omega_{\mathrm{v}}\left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{v}}\right)$ or, specifically, $2 \mathrm{~m} \omega_{\mathrm{v}}=\hbar \mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{v}}^{2}$ can be used. This then yields $\underline{\mathrm{Z}} \mathrm{zu}$ 
$\mathrm{F}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}, \mathrm{t}\right)=\sum_{\mathrm{v}=-\mathrm{M}}^{\mathrm{M}} \frac{1}{\mathrm{~L}} \sqrt{\pi / 2} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{v}}\left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{v}}\right) \exp \left(\mathrm{i}\left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{v}} \mathrm{x}_{1}-\omega_{\mathrm{v}}\left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{v}}\right) \mathrm{t}\right)\right)$, where the sum  can be approximated using $\mathrm{L} \rightarrow \infty$ as a Fourier integral 
$\sqrt{2 \pi} \cdot \mathrm{~F}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}, \mathrm{t}\right) \approx \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \mathrm{C}(\mathrm{K}) \exp \left(\mathrm{i}\left(\mathrm{Kx}_{1}-\omega(\mathrm{K}) \mathrm{t}\right)\right) \mathrm{dK}$. See [[GmbH1975](../references/WDorg4_REF_GmbH1975.md),] for more information. 
IV): The mapping process $\beta \mathrm{G}_{4} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}_{4(2 \mathrm{v}+1)}^{*}$ can now proceed in such a way that the  reference lengths $\mathrm{x}_{90}^{\mathrm{v}} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{120}^{\mathrm{v}}$ as well as $\mathrm{x}_{10}^{\mathrm{v}}$ and $\mathrm{x}_{40}^{\mathrm{v}}$ are not subject to any restrictions for the time being, and thus all functions exist. Only through access to  time—that is, the mapping into the $\mathrm{R}_{4}^{\prime}$—is the material structure (defined by $\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{v}}\left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{v}}\right), \omega_{\mathrm{v}}\left(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{v}}\right)$ or C(K), $\omega(\mathrm{K})$) established and selected as a function. In the general case, $\mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{m}} \neq 0$ with $\mathrm{m}=1 \ldots 4$ and the constraint $\omega(\overrightarrow{\mathrm{K}})$, a general function is thus mapped to $\mathrm{R}_{4}^{\prime}$. The Fourier integral of this mapping is then 
$2 \pi \sqrt{2 \pi} \mathrm{~F}(\overrightarrow{\mathrm{r}}, \mathrm{t}) \approx \iiint_{-\infty}^{\infty} \mathrm{C}(\overrightarrow{\mathrm{K}}) \exp (\mathrm{i}(\overrightarrow{\mathrm{K}} \overrightarrow{\mathrm{r}}-\omega(\mathrm{K}) \mathrm{t})) \mathrm{d} \overrightarrow{\mathrm{K}}$. If, on the other hand, 
$\omega$ does not depend on $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{K}}$, then we obtain 
$4 \pi^{2} \mathrm{~F}(\overrightarrow{\mathrm{r}}, \mathrm{t})=\iiint_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int \mathrm{c}(\overrightarrow{\mathrm{k}}, \omega) \exp (\mathrm{i}(\overrightarrow{\mathrm{k}} \overrightarrow{\mathrm{r}}-\omega \mathrm{t})) \mathrm{d} \overrightarrow{\mathrm{k}} \mathrm{d} \omega$ according to [Transforms1973](../references/WDorg4_REF_Transforms1973.md).
