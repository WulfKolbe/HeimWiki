According to the equations (9) through (10a) derived from hyper-space dynamics $\mathrm{G}_{4} \rightarrow \mathrm{I}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}_{4}$ in deterministic quantum theory (9) through (10a), any interaction between elementary matter field quanta (Mq) results in an exchange of interaction quanta, which are also to be regarded as Mq. The emission and absorption of such interaction quanta thus always presuppose charge structures for all interacting Mq, on whose structure the interaction quanta (Wq) depend.  The interaction force is determined by the frequency of the exchange of emitted and absorbed Wq, whereby the probability amplitude for this is the respective coupling constant, such that its square—and thus the probability of the exchange process—appears in (16a)  as the interaction constant. Furthermore, such a coupling constant is directly proportional to the relevant charge as the source or sink of the  Wq charge, whereas the interaction field is the sum  of all Wq. According to (16), the coupling constants are not parameter functions, but there is the possibility [Teilchen1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Teilchen1984.md) and [Teubner1983](../references/WDorg4_REF_Teubner1983.md), that the generation of Wq from the vacuum can shield or amplify the sources of the corresponding Wq. In such a case, the interaction depends functionally on the distance of the Wq from the charge center and thus, by the uncertainty principle, on an energy, so that the coupling constant in question is referred to as a sliding coupling constant. Contrary to [Teilchen1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Teilchen1984.md) and [Teubner1983](../references/WDorg4_REF_Teubner1983.md), in what follows we will not use $\alpha_{\mathrm{i}}$, but rather $\beta_{\mathrm{i}}=\sqrt{\alpha_{\mathrm{i}}}=\psi_{\mathrm{i}}$ will be understood as the coupling constant or, respectively, as the probability amplitude of the Wq exchange, in accordance with (16a). The real  probability $\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{i}}$ of the creation or annihilation of Wq would  then be $\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{i}}=\beta_{\mathrm{i}}^{2}$. Whenever a coupling depends on an energy  or on an Mq mass that causes the emission or absorption of  Wq, the coupling constant must be related to a mass,  for which it is appropriate to choose the elementary mass whose particles are ‘relevant’ in terms of mass in the universe. Since these are the masses of protons $\left(\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{p}}\right)$ and neutrons $\left(\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{n}}\right)$, but $\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{n}} \neq \mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{p}}$
