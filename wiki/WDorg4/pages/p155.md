The possibility of an extension—realized in this paper—of the semiclassical structural theory derived in [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md) already follows from [1, Eq. 3d]. This relationship is ultimately a criterion for determining whether a reference space $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{p}}$, used for empirical reasons, is complete with respect to its dimensions or whether $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{p}} \subset \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}$, together with $\mathrm{n}>\mathrm{p}$, is a subspace of an  n-dimensional $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}$. With regard to physical spacetime $\mathrm{R}_{4}$, this resulted in $\mathrm{R}_{4} \subset \mathrm{R}_{6}$, which led to the semi-classical structure theory in [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) and [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md).  According to the solution manifold of the world selector [1, Eq. 19], i.e., the four forms of hermeticism (gravitational field, electromagnetic field, uncharged, and charged mass fields), the energetically defined world coordinates $\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{6}$ of the $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ form a structured set $\left\{\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{3}\right),\left(\mathrm{x}_{4}\right),\left(\mathrm{x}_{5}, \mathrm{x}_{6}\right)\right\}$ with  $\mathrm{K}_{6}=\{3 ; 1 ; 2\}$, through which the subspace structure of the material world  $\mathrm{R}_{6}=\mathrm{R}_{3}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{3}\right) \cup \mathrm{T}_{1}\left(\mathrm{x}_{4}\right) \cup \mathrm{S}_{2}\left(\mathrm{x}_{5}, \mathrm{x}_{6}\right)$ is conditioned by the organizational coordinates $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$ in $S_{2}$ beyond $R_{4}$. Applying  [1, Eq. 3d] to $\mathrm{p}=5$ or $\mathrm{p}>6$ yields no additional hyperspaces. Only for $\mathrm{p}=6$ does a twelve-dimensional hyperspace of the world result. Although $\mathrm{R}_{6} \subset \mathrm{R}_{12}$ holds, in the region of coordinates $\mathrm{x}_{7} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{12}$ the concept of energy or matter is no longer defined, whereas the concept of volume is. Consequently, the material world $\mathrm{R}_{6} \subset \mathrm{R}_{12}$ appears, so to speak, to be “placed” within the hyperspace, so that the region $\mathrm{x}_{7} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{12}$ can be understood as the non-material side of the world, which is, however, logically quantifiable physics. The set of $\mathrm{R}_{12}$ coordinates is also structured in the form $\left\{\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{3}\right),\left(\mathrm{x}_{4}\right),\left(\mathrm{x}_{5}, \mathrm{x}_{6}\right),\left(\mathrm{x}_{7}, \mathrm{x}_{8}\right),\left(\mathrm{x}_{9} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{12}\right)\right\}$ and forms the cardinal number complex $\mathrm{K}_{12}=\{3 ; 1 ; 2 ; 2 ; 4\}$, which, together with $\mathrm{I}_{2}\left(\mathrm{x}_{7}, \mathrm{x}_{8}\right)$ and $\mathrm{G}_{4}\left(\mathrm{x}_{9} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{12}\right)$, leads to the subspace structure $\mathrm{R}_{12}=\mathrm{R}_{3} \cup \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{~S}_{2} \cup \mathrm{I}_{2} \cup \mathrm{G}_{4}$. It has been shown that within the subspace $\mathrm{R}_{8}=\mathrm{R}_{3} \cup \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{~S}_{2} \cup \mathrm{I}_{2}$, structures in the sense of $\mathrm{I}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{R}_{3}=\mathrm{R}_{4}^{\mathrm{a}}$ can be directly mapped onto one another. Furthermore, $\mathrm{x}_{7}$ and $\mathrm{x}_{8}$ could be interpreted as informational coordinates and derived for all $\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{8}$ elementary lengths.
