If physics is to be understood from its very origins, it becomes necessary to find a basis that can no longer be logically reduced any further, from which the entire, exceedingly broad spectrum of phenomenological manifestations of the material world can be understood. Only then will it be possible not only to understand the physics of energetic fields, as manifested in the four empirical classes of interaction fields, but also to explain non-energetic fields that, for example, (and empirically confirmed) appear in quantum physics as “probability fields.” 

The method of general geometrization proved to be particularly suitable; for then space and the object within that space no longer appear to be fundamentally incongruent, but rather the object in space can be explained within the framework of geometrization as a non-Euclidean structure of space. This path  was first taken by A. EINSTEIN, whose SRT, on the one hand, showed that the real dimensions of physical space $\mathrm{R}_{3}$ can be supplemented by a  fourth dimension—namely, imaginary light-time—to form four-dimensional  spacetime $\mathrm{R}_{4}$, while the extension of this SRT to GRT ultimately led to the geometrization of the phenomenon of gravitation, such that a gravitational field is to be understood as a spacetime structure of Riemannian geometry. However, the material source of the field does not appear in a geometrized form, but rather in the relationships as a phenomenological quantity. These ideas were further developed by Th. KALUZA, O. KLEIN, P. JORDAN, as well as R. PENROSE and others, and, in a radical form, by B. HEIM [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) and [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md), even extending into the microdomain of the quantum principle. It became apparent that the $R_{4}$ must be extended—for reasons of algebraic symmetry—by two additional imaginary coordinates to form a six-dimensional space $\mathrm{R}_{6}$, such that $\mathrm{R}_{4} \subset \mathrm{R}_{6}$ is a subspace of this $\mathrm{R}_{6}$,  which must evidently be understood as the reference space of the material world,  because the coordinates of the $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ are energetically defined  due to these symmetry principles.
