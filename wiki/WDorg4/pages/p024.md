Since the indices i, k, and m run through the digits of the $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ dimensions, this means that there are $4^{3}=64$ tensor structural equations 
$\mathrm{C}_{(\mathrm{p})} \varphi_{\mathrm{km}}^{(\mathrm{p})}=\lambda_{(\mathrm{p})}(\mathrm{k}, \mathrm{m}) \varphi_{\mathrm{km}}^{(\mathrm{p})}$. However, as a result of the aforementioned algebraic symmetries, a total of 28 of these relations become $\mathrm{C}_{(\mathrm{p})} \varphi_{\mathrm{km}}^{(\mathrm{p})}=0$,  which also means $\lambda_{(\mathrm{p})}(\mathrm{k}, \mathrm{m}) \varphi_{\mathrm{km}}^{(\mathrm{p})}=0$. Since $\varphi_{\mathrm{km}}^{\mathrm{p}} \neq 0$ always holds when a  non-Euclidean $\mathrm{R}_{4}$structure is present, these 28 equations are satisfied solely by $\lambda_{(\mathrm{p})}(\mathrm{k}, \mathrm{m})=0$, that is, by empty eigenvalue spectra of metric structural levels of $\mathrm{R}_{4}$, as explained in [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md). Since the 
$\lambda_{(\mathrm{p})}(\mathrm{k}, \mathrm{m}) \varphi_{\mathrm{km}}^{(\mathrm{p})} \neq 0$ obviously represent metric structural levels that are equivalent to phenomenological energy densities, these 
$64-28=36$ components of non-zero eigenvalue spectra must be the  components of a tensor whose square matrix can only be of the type  $\sqrt{36}=6$ , which, however, presupposes an energetically determined reference space  $\mathrm{R}_{6} \supset \mathrm{R}_{4}$ that contains spacetime as a subspace. 

In the macroscopic regime, the energy density tensor of the $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ then becomes a matrix scheme which, as a spacetime section, approximately contains the phenomenological energy density tensor of GRT, but is subject to a double boundary condition. 

In addition to the algebraic symmetries used (which are independent of the respective  symmetry of $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}$), there is another symmetry [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md), which appears when one derives the existence of the $\lambda_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathrm{k}, \mathrm{m})$ as eigenvalues.  Based on this symmetry, it follows that, of the 36 components of the $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ tensor, another 12 components vanish. As shown in [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md), these can only be the 12 spacelike components of the double R-transformation. If these components are identified with 0, the determinant of the tensor does not vanish as a result. 

Since, in the tensor scheme supplemented in this way, only the two timelike series containing the index 4 run through the entire matrix scheme, the formation law of $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ can be derived from $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ be derived in the sense of a dimensional law. If a $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}$ exists and metric structures  of the form [1, Eq. 3] are present in it, then a hyperspace  $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{N}} \supset \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}$ always exists, provided that the dimensional law [1, Eq. 3d], i.e., 
$\mathrm{N}=1 \pm \sqrt{1+\mathrm{n}(\mathrm{n}-1)(\mathrm{n}-2)}$, is satisfied such that $\mathrm{N}(\mathrm{n})$ turns out to be a positive integer
