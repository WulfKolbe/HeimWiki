In hyperspace $\mathrm{R}_{12}=\mathrm{R}_{3} \cup \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{~S}_{2} \cup \mathrm{I}_{2} \cup \mathrm{G}_{4}$, the mapping $\mathrm{G}_{4}\left(\mathrm{x}_{9}, \ldots, \mathrm{x}_{12}\right) \rightarrow \mathrm{I}_{2}\left(\mathrm{x}_{7}, \mathrm{x}_{8}\right)$ according to [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) relates the ambiguous elementary lengths $\delta \mathrm{s}_{7,8}=\mathrm{i} \delta \mathrm{l}_{0} \sum_{\mathrm{j}=1}^{4}(-1)^{\mathrm{j}+1} \exp \left(-2 \pi\left(\mathrm{~m}_{\mathrm{j}} \pm 1 / 2\right)\right)$ to $-\infty<\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{j}}<\infty$. Although the  extension $\mathrm{G}_{4} \rightarrow \mathrm{I}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~S}_{2}$ is not problematic, a  difficulty arises with $\mathrm{S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}_{4}$, because the prerequisite for mapping a  $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{p}}$ structure into (or onto) a $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{q}}$—that is, $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{p}} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{q}}$—is possible only under the condition $p \geq q$. This condition is not met in the case of $R_{2} \rightarrow R_{4}$, but the  subspace structure $\mathrm{R}_{4}=\mathrm{R}_{3} \cup \mathrm{~T}_{1}$ holds. The mapping $\mathrm{S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}_{3}$ also does not satisfy the requirement, whereas $\mathrm{S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~T}_{1}$ does, especially since, unlike the real $\mathrm{R}_{3}$ coordinates, the coordinate $\mathrm{x}_{4}$ of the time structure counts in binary fashion in the same way as $\mathrm{x}_{5}$ and $\mathrm{x}_{6}$ of $\mathrm{S}_{2}$. Furthermore, in $\mathrm{R}_{4}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{4}\right)$, every $\mathrm{R}_{3-}$ lies on a spacetime line, so that $\mathrm{S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~T}_{1}$ occurs primarily—that is, the $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ mapping in the form $\mathrm{S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{R}_{3}$ occurs via the time structure. In what follows,  $\mathrm{S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{R}_{3}$ always stands for the short form $\mathrm{S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}_{4}$, which is possible despite  $\mathrm{p}<\mathrm{q}$; since this mapping always takes place via the time structure.  For the elementary lengths $\delta \mathrm{x}_{7,8}$ of $\mathrm{I}_{2}\left(\mathrm{x}_{7}, \mathrm{x}_{8}\right)$, in the case $\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{j}}=$ const is always $\delta \mathrm{s}_{7}=\delta \mathrm{s}_{8}=0$; that is, according to the mapping chain $\mathrm{I}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{R}_{3}=\mathrm{R}_{4}$, there is no mapped elementary length in $\mathrm{R}_{4}$. However, such an element does exist if the condition $\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{j}}$ = const is not satisfied for all j. The question now arises as to whether, beyond the aforementioned mapping process $\beta \quad \mathrm{G}_{4} \rightarrow \mathrm{I}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{R}_{3}$, other functional relationships can also be transposed into spacetime. 

According to current empirically based quantum theory, the micro-range of material elementary structures is essentially determined by quantum duality; that is, for each of these microprocesses, complementary descriptive possibilities exist in the particle and wave pictures. This means that prior to a measurement process, only a probability wave in $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ specifies the possible position of a particle in space and time, whereas after the measurement, the spacetime position of the particle is fixed.
