In order to bring these effects onto a common basis, one can take the geometric path toward unification that A. EINSTEIN first demonstrated in *ART*. He succeeded in attributing gravity to a deformation of space and time; however, the attempt to unify gravity with electromagnetism using non-Hermitian metrics failed. Th. KALUZA and O. KLEIN then continued the  geometrization of the interaction fields. Th. KALUZA replaced four-dimensional space with a five-dimensional one and was thus able to explain the gravitational and electromagnetic fields in the familiar spacetime, while O. KLEIN also incorporated the quantum mechanical aspect. The solutions to a five-parameter Schrödinger equation yielded waves that moved in gravitational and electromagnetic fields, respectively, within four-dimensional spacetime and could be interpreted as particles. 

Unifying all four interactions theoretically seemed possible only recently. 

Using supersymmetry, which allows fermions and bosons (particles with half-integer and integer spin) to be represented in a unified manner, general relativity has now been formulated in the language of quantum theory. 

This theory has become known as supergravity theory. At least 11 spacetime coordinates are necessary to fully describe all 4 fundamental interactions (FI), with 4 coordinates being spread out and 7 rolled up (compactified). This would explain why the latter have not yet been observed in nature.  The diameter of the hypersphere associated with a four-dimensional spacetime point will be only a few Planck lengths (approx. $10^{-33} \mathrm{~m}$),  which, in quantum mechanical terms, would correspond to roughly $10^{18}$ proton masses. This  particle energy will not be attainable even with accelerators of the distant future. For a number of dimensions greater than 11, the  theory appears to fail. 

On the other hand, the theory of supergravity still appears problematic;  for according to this theory, neutrinos with the same frequency would have to appear in both  left- and right-handed chirality.
