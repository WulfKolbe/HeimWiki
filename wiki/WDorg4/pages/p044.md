This is the structure of set B. Using the subsets 
$\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}\right)=\mathrm{A}_{1},\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}\right)=\mathrm{A}_{2}$ through $\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{n}}\right)=\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{n}}$, another chain can be formed: 
3) $\left(\mathrm{A}_{1}\right) \subset\left(\mathrm{A}_{1}, \mathrm{~A}_{2}\right) \subset \ldots \subset\left(\mathrm{A}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{~A}_{\mathrm{n}}\right)$. 

The terms $\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{k}}=\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{k}}\right)$ can be substituted for $1 \leq \mathrm{k} \leq \mathrm{n}$, resulting in $\left(\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}\right)\right) \subset\left(\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}\right),\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}\right)\right) \subset \ldots \subset\left(\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}\right),\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}\right), \ldots,\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2} \ldots \mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{n}}\right)\right)$.  Here, the term $\left(\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}\right),\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}\right)\right)$ reveals an order with respect to $\mathrm{a}_{1}$ and $\mathrm{a}_{2}$. Reference can be made to the meaning of an ordered pair 
$\left(\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}\right),\left(\mathrm{a}_{2}\right)\right)=\left[\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}\right]$ [Allgemeine1964](../references/WDorg4_REF_Allgemeine1964.md). As is well known, the components of a vector $\left(a_{1}, \ldots, a_{n}\right) \in R_{n}$ also form an ordered n-tuple, that is, the elements in $\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{n}}\right)$ are not interchangeable. The concept of the Cartesian product—that is, a product set—also stems from an ordered pair. The chain from 3) can be used with 
$\left(\mathrm{A}_{1}\right)=\mathrm{B}_{1},\left(\mathrm{~A}_{1}, \mathrm{~A}_{2}\right)=\mathrm{B}_{2}, \ldots,\left(\mathrm{~A}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{~A}_{\mathrm{n}}\right)=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{n}}$ to form a further  chain 
4) $\left(\mathrm{B}_{1}\right) \subset\left(\mathrm{B}_{1}, \mathrm{~B}_{2}\right) \subset \ldots \subset\left(\mathrm{B}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{~B}_{\mathrm{n}}\right)$. 

By repeating this process, new set chains can be formed at increasingly higher levels, to which combination operations derived from the  elementary operations $\mathrm{o}_{1}$ and $\mathrm{o}_{2}$, such as $\left(\mathrm{o}_{1}\right),\left(\mathrm{o}_{1} \mathrm{o}_{2}\right),\left(\mathrm{o}_{1} \mathrm{o}_{2} \mathrm{o}_{1}\right) \ldots$, can be applied. The combination operations in the form  $\left(\mathrm{o}_{2} \mathrm{o}_{1}\right) \mathrm{B}$ can be applied to the set B, which yielded the arithmetic and geometric series for $\mathrm{o}_{2}=+$ and $\mathrm{o}_{1}=+$, and $\mathrm{o}_{2}=+$ and $\mathrm{o}_{1}=\cdot$, yielding the arithmetic and geometric series, respectively. This is a special case of the general formation of series or products. Explicitly, therefore, $\left(\mathrm{o}_{2} \mathrm{o}_{1}\right) \mathrm{B}=\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}\right) \mathrm{o}_{2}\left(\mathrm{a}_{1} \mathrm{o}_{1} \mathrm{a}_{2}\right) \mathrm{o}_{2}\left(\mathrm{a}_{1} \mathrm{o}_{1} \mathrm{a}_{2} \mathrm{o}_{1} \mathrm{a}_{3}\right) \mathrm{o}_{2}(\ldots) \ldots$. 

Since only $\mathrm{o}_{\mathrm{j}}=+$ or $\mathrm{o}_{\mathrm{j}}=\cdot$ is used, the series $\mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{n}}$ and $\mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{n}}^{\prime}$ can be formed from (++) B or $(+\cdot)$ B, but there are also the possibilities $(\cdot+)$ B, which leads to the product representation $(\cdot+) \mathrm{B}=\mathrm{a}_{1}\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}+\mathrm{a}_{2}\right) \ldots\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}+\ldots+\mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{n}}\right)$ or $(\cdot) B=a_{1}\left(a_{1} a_{2}\right)\left(a_{1} a_{2} a_{3}\right) \ldots\left(a_{1} \ldots a_{n}\right)$. 

If this algorithm is applied to the dimensionless measures $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{i}}$ in the world’s initial leap $\mathrm{t}=0$, then $\mathrm{d}_{1}=\mathrm{a}_{1}$ follows, as well as 
$\mathrm{d}_{2}=\mathrm{a}_{2}\left((++)\left(\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}\right),\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}\right)\right)\right)$ and $\mathrm{d}_{3}=(++)\left(\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}\right),\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}\right),\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}, \mathrm{a}_{3}\right)\right)$. 
If F is a superset $\mathrm{F}=\left\{\mathrm{M}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{m}}\right\}$ composed of subsets $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{k}}$ of $1 \leq \mathrm{k} \leq \mathrm{m}$, then the representatives of $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{k}}$ are those whose cardinal numbers are $\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{k}}$, which form the cardinal number complex $\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{i}}=\|\mathrm{F}\|=\left\{\mathrm{m}_{1} ; \mathrm{m}_{2} ; \ldots ; \mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{m}}\right\}$
