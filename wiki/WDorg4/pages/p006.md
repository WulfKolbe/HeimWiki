4. Geometrization of elementary particles; physical interpretation of geometric terms. In the micrometer range, the energy-momentum tensor can be set proportional to a geometric quantity analogous to the Christoffel symbols. The analogies to Einstein’s field equations then form a principle of equivalence, but not proportionality, and are transformed into purely geometric eigenvalue equations in the micro-range. 
5. The only underedived empirical natural constants used throughout the entire theory are $\gamma, \hbar, \varepsilon_{0}$ and $\mu_{0}$. 
6. Structure of an elementary particle consisting of highly structured, hierarchically ordered geometric quantities that cyclically change their structure in the sense of an internal dynamic. 
7. Derivation of the laws of symmetry strictly valid for elementary particles and determination of their rest masses. 
8. The existence of a “world equation,” one chain of approximations of which yields Einstein’s field equations of general relativity, while another chain of approximations yields the Dirac equations of quantum electrodynamics. 

The further exposition of the coordinate space presented here concerns a mathematical relationship that demonstrates whether a  reference space, spanned by p dimensions and in which an extended mathematical system of equations serves as a unification of geometrized physical structures, belongs as a subspace to a higher-dimensional  hyperspace (n-dimensional with $\mathrm{n}>\mathrm{p}$). The principle of relativity shows  that neither three-dimensional physical space nor physical time exists  on its own, but rather their connection to physical spacetime (in 4 dimensions). For this spacetime, therefore, $\mathrm{p}=4$ must be added to the dimensional law, which yields $\mathrm{n}=6$. It follows from this that this spacetime is not complete. Beyond spacetime, there must therefore be two more dimensions, so that the material—that is, energetic—world of physics must be related to a six-dimensional space. For $p=5$ and all $p>6$, there are no more hyperspaces. Only for $\mathrm{p}=6$ does a hyperspace still follow from the world, which is spanned by $\mathrm{n}=12$ dimensions.
