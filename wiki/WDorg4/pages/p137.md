Gravimetrically, this field could be investigated empirically, provided that the sensitivity of the available gravimeters can be sufficiently increased. 

Since $\mathrm{m}_{4 \alpha}=1,0867 \cdot 10^{-45} \mathrm{~kg}$ holds, the mass energy is given by  $\mathrm{m}_{4 \alpha} \mathrm{c}^{2}=9,76677 \cdot 10^{-29}$ Ws. This would mean that for the field mass of this $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}$ field, $\mathrm{M}_{4 \alpha}=\mathrm{m}_{4 \alpha} \exp (28 \pi)=1,7324 \cdot 10^{-7} \mathrm{~kg}$—that is, for the field energy—at least $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{M}}=\mathrm{M}_{4 \alpha} \mathrm{c}^{2}=1,557 \cdot 10^{10} \mathrm{Ws}$ applies. From this, it becomes immediately clear that a direct experimental investigation of  photon transmutation into $\underline{\underline{\mathrm{w}}}_{5}$ quanta is bound to extremely high field energy, which lies far beyond current technical capabilities.
