The possibility of an extension with regard to the fundamentals already follows from the dimension law N(n) discussed earlier, with $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}} \subset \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{N}}$  from [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md), which resulted from the validity of symmetries. 

According to this N (n) dimensional law, the spacetime of $\mathrm{n}=4$ was assigned the integer N (4) $=6$—that is, a $\mathrm{R}_{6} \supset \mathrm{R}_{4}$ responsible for the energetic structures of the world. Another space follows for $\mathrm{n}=6$, namely $\mathrm{N}(6)=12$,  that is, a $\mathrm{R}_{12} \supset \mathrm{R}_{6}$, whereas for $\mathrm{n}=3$ or $\mathrm{n}=5$ and all $\mathrm{n}>6$ the N-values are not integers and therefore do not appear as dimensional numbers of spaces  $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{N}}$. Thus, the $\mathrm{R}_{12}$ is to be understood as the hyperspace of an energetic  world $\mathrm{R}_{6}$, which is a subspace $\mathrm{R}_{6} \subset \mathrm{R}_{12}$ of this hyperspace.  Because of $\mathrm{R}_{4} \subset \mathrm{R}_{6} \subset \mathrm{R}_{12}$, the coordinates of the $\mathrm{R}_{4}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{x}_{4}\right)$ are thus supplemented by $\mathrm{x}_{5}$  and $x_{6}$ to form the $R_{6}\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{6}\right)$, while the coordinates $x_{7}$ through $x_{12}$  span a space $\mathrm{V}_{6}\left(\mathrm{x}_{7}, \ldots, \mathrm{x}_{12}\right)$, so that $\mathrm{R}_{12}=\mathrm{R}_{6} \cup \mathrm{~V}_{6}$ holds, as was shown in  [1, Chap. IV, 5]. It was also proven there that, even in hyperspace, all $\mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}$ for $\mathrm{i}>3$ are imaginary. The question of the elementary lengths $\delta \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{j}}$ in $\mathrm{R}_{12}$ is obviously essential. For the $\mathrm{R}_{6}$, $\delta \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{j}}=\delta \mathrm{s}_{0}=\sqrt{\tau}$ was obtained for $\mathrm{j} \leq 3$, but $\delta \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{j}}=\mathrm{i} \delta \mathrm{s}_{0}$ for $\mathrm{j}>3$. As described in [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md), the length elements $\delta \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}$ for $\mathrm{i} \geq 7$ from an elementary length $\delta \mathrm{l}_{0}$ originating from the temporal world origin $\mathrm{t}=0$, in accordance with [2, Eq. 37, Eq. 47, Eq. 48], supplemented by [1, Chap. IV, 6], and a power measure $L$ according to [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md), whereby the reciprocals $1 / \alpha_{\mathrm{i}}$ are, via $\alpha_{i} \in \mathrm{~L}$, the dimensional numbers of the subspaces of this $\mathrm{R}_{12}$. In an analogy to special and general relativity, as shown in  [1, Chap. IV, 5], the Heisenberg uncertainty principle and other premises of abstract  and concrete quantum theory can then be derived using the hyperspace coordinates $\mathrm{x}_{7}$ and $\mathrm{x}_{8}$, which enables a geometric interpretation of indeterministic quantum theory. It turns out that the differences $\Delta \mathrm{x}_{7}$ and $\Delta \mathrm{x}_{8}$ (located beyond the $\mathrm{R}_{6}$) are decisive for the quantum-theoretical uncertainty of canonically conjugate quantities.
