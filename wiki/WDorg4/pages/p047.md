The element $\mathrm{a}_{12}=1 / 12$ should be understood as a fundamental element that can be formed from the elements of the set U in the form $\mathbf{a}_{12}=\mathbf{a}_{3}^{1} \cdot \mathbf{a}_{2}^{2} \cdot \mathbf{a}_{1}^{3}$. In what follows, we will attempt to express the values $\mathrm{b}_{1}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}, \mathrm{~b}_{3}, \mathrm{~b}_{1}^{\prime}$ and $\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime}$ obtained so far using this elementary number, for which the  reduction $\mathrm{a}_{12}=\mathrm{g}$—that is, $12 \mathrm{~g}=1$—is to be set. 

Here it becomes apparent that the following relations hold, namely $\mathrm{b}_{1} \approx 9 \mathrm{~g}, \mathrm{~b}_{2} \approx 7 \mathrm{~g}, \mathrm{~b}_{3} \approx 16 \mathrm{~g}$ as well as $\mathrm{b}_{1}^{\prime} \approx 6 \mathrm{~g}$ and $\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime} \approx 3 \mathrm{~g}$. If the symbol $\hat{+}$ is used analogously to (++) for the set concatenation operation—so that, for example, it becomes $\mathrm{g} \hat{+} \mathrm{g} \hat{+} \mathrm{g}=(++) \mathrm{T}(\mathrm{g}, \mathrm{g}, \mathrm{g})=\mathrm{g}+(\mathrm{g}+\mathrm{g})+(\mathrm{g}+\mathrm{g}+\mathrm{g})=6 \mathrm{~g}=0,5$—then the representation 
$\mathrm{f}_{1}(\mathrm{~g})=\mathrm{g}, \mathrm{f}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})=\mathrm{g} \hat{+} \mathrm{g}, \ldots, \mathrm{f}_{8}(\mathrm{~g})=\mathrm{g} \hat{+} \mathrm{g} \hat{+} \mathrm{g} \hat{+}(-2 \mathrm{~g}) \hat{+} \mathrm{g} \hat{+} \mathrm{g} \hat{+} \mathrm{g} \hat{+}(-2 \mathrm{~g})$ can also be used. A cardinal number complex $\mathrm{K}_{4}=\{3 ; 1\} \subset \mathrm{K}_{6}$ becomes apparent because in $\mathrm{f}_{8}$ three g’s connected by $\hat{+}$ are also connected to a value (-2g). In addition to the 5 values $\mathrm{b}_{1}$ through $\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime}$, there must, for reasons of symmetry, be one more value $\mathrm{b}_{3}^{\prime} \approx \mathrm{g}$ for which the most primitive function $\mathrm{b}_{3}^{\prime} \approx \mathrm{f}_{1}(\mathrm{~g})$ can also be defined. Therefore, the following holds: 
$\mathrm{b}_{1} \approx 12 \mathrm{~g}, \mathrm{~b}_{2} \approx 7 \mathrm{~g}, \mathrm{~b}_{3} \approx 16 \mathrm{~g}, \mathrm{~b}_{1}^{\prime} \approx 6 \mathrm{~g}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}^{\prime} \approx 3 \mathrm{~g}, \mathrm{~b}_{3}^{\prime} \approx \mathrm{g}, 12 \mathrm{~g}=1$

For reasons already explained, the following holds: 
$\left(\mathrm{b}_{1} \overline{\mathrm{~g}}_{\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{b}_{2} \overline{\mathrm{~g}}_{\mathrm{j}}, \mathrm{b}_{3} \overline{\mathrm{~g}}_{\mathrm{k}}, \mathrm{b}_{1}^{\prime} \overline{\mathrm{g}}_{\mathrm{l}}, \mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime} \overline{\mathrm{g}}_{\mathrm{m}}\right) \in \mathrm{R}_{12}^{*}=\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*} \cup \mathrm{R}_{6}^{*,}$. As has already been shown, a representation space $\mathrm{D}_{24} \subset \mathrm{D}_{64}$ is possible, so that with respect to $\mathrm{b}_{3}^{\prime}$ an  element $\mathrm{b}_{3}^{\prime} \overline{\mathrm{g}}_{\mathrm{r}} \in \mathrm{R}_{12}^{*,}$ is conceivable, which then, due to $\mathrm{R}_{12}^{*} \cup \mathrm{R}_{12}^{*,}=\mathrm{R}_{24}^{*} \mathrm{zu}$, belongs to a $\mathrm{R}_{24}^{*}$. Since $\mathrm{b}_{3}^{\prime} \overline{\mathrm{g}}_{\mathrm{r}}$ does not belong to $\mathrm{R}_{12}^{*}$, but rather to the $\mathrm{R}_{12}^{*,}$,  a different education law than the one used so far can be adopted for $\mathrm{b}_{3}$. By selecting $\mathrm{c}_{3}=\mathrm{d}_{1}^{\prime 2}-\mathrm{d}_{2}^{\prime 2}$ and $\mathrm{c}_{3}=2 \mathrm{~b}_{3}^{\prime}$, $\mathrm{b}_{3}^{\prime} \approx \mathrm{g}$ is obtained. Now, $\mathrm{f}_{1}(\mathrm{~g})=\mathrm{g}$ is a numerical value that constructs the remaining $\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{i}}(\mathrm{g})$ using $\mathrm{i} \neq 1$ and is therefore of fundamental importance. Accordingly, $\mathrm{b}_{3}^{\prime} \approx \mathrm{g}$ could be converted to $\mathrm{b}_{3}^{\prime}=\mathrm{g}$ , which, however, would then necessitate corresponding changes to the other values from $\mathrm{b}_{1}$ to $\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime}$. 

While the operation $\hat{+}$ corresponds to the (++) operation, ${ }^{\wedge}$ could be used in a similar manner according to the $(+)$ operation. This then allows for further symmetrization with respect to $\mathrm{b}_{1}, \mathrm{~b}_{2}, \mathrm{~b}_{3}$ and $\mathrm{d}_{1}, \mathrm{~d}_{2}, \mathrm{~d}_{3}$, respectively. If we set $2 \mathrm{~b}_{2}($ as the old value, $) \approx 2 \mathrm{~b}_{2}($ as the new value, $)$ and $2 \mathrm{~b}_{2}(\mathrm{neu})=\mathrm{a}_{2}\left[\left(\mathrm{a}_{2} \hat{} \cdot \mathrm{a}_{2} \hat{} \cdot \mathrm{a}_{2}\right) \cdot\left(\mathrm{a}_{1} \hat{+} \mathrm{a}_{3}\right)\right]+\mathrm{a}$ with $\mathrm{a}=4 / 27$, then $\mathrm{b}_{2}$ (new) is used as the last valid reference value, whose deviation from $\mathrm{b}_{2}$ (old)  is only $\delta \approx 6 \cdot 10^{-6}$.
