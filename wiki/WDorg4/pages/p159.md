According to [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md), all elements of the spectra of complex hermetics c and d appear in the reference space $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ of the material world as dynamic flux aggregates of the elementary condenser flows (flucktones) described in [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md), such that their duration of existence is always an integer multiple of the flux period. There is certainly, for every integral-type flow aggregate, a relatively large number of isomeric aggregates whose existence times could differ slightly beyond the quantum-theoretical uncertainties. In general, these differences are likely to lie beyond the limits of measurability; however, it is conceivable that, in an extreme case—with the same mass and the same set of quantum numbers—this time difference could become measurable.  This raises the further question of whether the two components of the K °, namely $\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{o}}$ and $\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{o}}$, whose temporal stability intervals differ drastically while their other properties are identical. 
A further consequence follows from hyperspace dynamics in the sense of (9) and (9a), as well as from the interpretation of the interaction constants (24) as probabilities. In general, the principle of interactions is obviously of fundamental importance. If, in $\mathrm{R}_{3} \subset \mathrm{R}_{4}$, a finite number of n material entities exist within a finite volume of this space, then each of these entities can be assigned an organizational level in $\mathrm{S}_{2}\left(\mathrm{x}_{5}, \mathrm{x}_{6}\right) \subset \mathrm{R}_{6}$ which determines the degree of freedom of this element, such that these n elements form a statistical  collective. If, according to (9) and (9a), interactions (24) occur as  probabilities, then in this interaction system the n elements, as components of this system, relinquish their respective individuality, and a new structure emerges with an organizational  level in $\mathrm{S}_{2}$, which lies above the level of each of its components. In general, new properties also emerge, so that, with respect to the $\mathrm{S}_{2}$ level, the whole may possess different properties than the mere sum of the n components would suggest. 
Since all material phenomena are governed by such interactive processes in the $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ of the material world, the nature of material phenomena is fundamentally dynamic, such that a change $\mathrm{R}_{6} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}_{12} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}_{6}^{\prime} \neq \mathrm{R}_{6}$, as described by (9) and (9a), always governs these phenomena. This raises the fundamental question of an approach to a holistic description of all structures of the $\mathrm{R}_{12}$.
