The Wq particles of the weak interaction must be vector bosons, of which $\mathrm{W}^{ \pm}$ and $\mathrm{Z}^{0}$ have indeed been empirically [Teilchen1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Teilchen1984.md), in which the mass of the Wq is thought to be mediated by so-called “Higgs particles.” This Higgs model evidently corresponds to the coupling constants of the sets $\mathrm{w}_{1}$ and $\mathrm{w}_{4}$ according to (16), such that this property, that the Wq, which is imponderable (for the time being, with the exception of the photon), becomes massive (i.e., ponderable) [Teilchen1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Teilchen1984.md), can be represented in a unified form precisely by these coupling constants. Consequently, $\mathrm{w}_{1}$ and $\mathrm{w}_{4}$ correspond to  correspond to the totality of all hypothetical Higgs fields, whereby in the simplest case an additive or subtractive combination with the elements $\mathrm{w}_{2}$ is possible. 

In accordance with the algebraic structure, $\mathrm{w}_{1}$ and $\mathrm{w}_{4}$ belong together just as the elements $\left(\mathrm{w}_{21}, \mathrm{w}_{24}\right)$ and $\left(\mathrm{w}_{22}, \mathrm{w}_{23}\right)$ do in $\mathrm{w}_{2}$. Since the numerical values of these elements of $\mathrm{w}_{2}$ must not be significantly altered in order for them to  accurately reflect the physical properties of the interaction fields they describe, only the representations $\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{21}=\left|\mathrm{w}_{21}-\mathrm{w}_{11}\right|$ as well as $\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{24}=\left|\mathrm{w}_{24}-\mathrm{w}_{12}\right|$ and  $\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{23}=\left|\mathrm{w}_{23}+\mathrm{w}_{4 \beta}\right|$ or $\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{22}=\left|\mathrm{w}_{22}-\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}\right|$ may be considered. At any given time, only two  elements within a set bracket may be combined with two corresponding elements of another set bracket. Furthermore, in $\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{21}$ through $\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{24}$, the join set \{-, -, -, +\} is used, which satisfies a symmetry with respect to $\mathrm{K}_{4}=\{3 ; 1\}$. +\} is used, which satisfies a symmetry with respect to $\mathrm{K}_{4}=\{3 ; 1\}$. 

If $\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{24}$ is related to the previously mentioned nucleon mass $\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{N}}$ as rest energy $\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{N}} \mathrm{c}^{2}$ , then the following holds for the corresponding coupling constant  $\beta_{4}=\underline{w}_{24} /\left(m_{N} c^{2}\right)$ and, consequently, for the interaction constant $\alpha_{4}=\beta_{4}^{2}$, which yields a numerical value of $\alpha_{4}=1,2321 \cdot 10^{-5}(\mathrm{GeV})^{-2}$. When compared with the empirical Fermi constant of $1,16637 \cdot 10^{-5}(\mathrm{GeV})^{-2}$, this results in a deviation of $\delta=5,6 \cdot 10^{-2}$. The inclusion of the coupling constant $\mathrm{w}_{12}$ in $\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{24}$ points to ponderable Wq particles, which are indeed empirically observable in this interaction. Since $\mathrm{w}_{12}$ corresponds to a field that causes the weak Ww particles to be ponderable, $\mathrm{w}_{12}$ be associated with a Higgs field [Wissenschaft1987](../references/WDorg4_REF_Wissenschaft1987.md).
