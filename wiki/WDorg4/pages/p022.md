The theory of B. HEIM  can also be classified within the context of the geometrization of elementary physical structures. HEIM also starts from the field equations of general relativity, but generalizes and geometrizes them in an even more radical way than KALUZA. 

According to the field equations of general relativity, the Ricci tensor (trace of the curvature tensor) $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{ik}}$ is related to the energy density tensor $\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{ik}}$ as given by $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{ik}}=\alpha\left(\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{ik}}-\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}} \mathrm{T} / 2\right)$. Here, $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}$ is the fundamental metric tensor of a spacetime in a $\mathrm{R}_{4}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}, \ldots \mathrm{x}_{4}\right)$ with $\mathrm{x}_{4}=\mathrm{ict}$ and $\mathrm{i}, \mathrm{k}=1 \ldots 4$. (In the case of a dependence on $1 \leq \mathrm{i} \leq \mathrm{n}$ elements $\alpha_{\mathrm{i}}$, we set ( $\alpha_{1}, \ldots, \alpha_{n}$ ) for brevity, because $\mathrm{i}+1$ always follows i.) For the factor $\alpha$, it is well known that $\alpha \mathrm{c}^{4}=8 \pi \gamma$ holds, where c is the speed of light and $\gamma$ is the gravitational constant (according to NEWTON). In the case of Einstein’s metric, the Riemannian symmetry $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}=\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ki}}$ holds for the fundamental tensor. 

According to [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md), these relations are generalized. First, this approach considers not only the gravitational field, but the totality of all possible interactions. The fields are understood as a single unit together with their sources. This means that a transition from the symmetry $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}=\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ki}}$ to a general non-Hermitianity $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}} \neq \mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ki}}^{*}$ , whereby the non-Hermitian spacetime relations must be extended into the micro-region according to a correspondence principle [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md). If the  transition of the Christoffel symbols into the micro-region is symbolized by $\Gamma_{\mathrm{km}}^{\mathrm{i}} \rightarrow \varphi_{\mathrm{km}}^{\mathrm{i}}$, then it becomes apparent at first that the $\varphi_{\mathrm{km}}^{\mathrm{i}}$ —in contrast to the macroscopic quantities $\Gamma_{\mathrm{km}}^{\mathrm{i}}$—become components of real tensor fields, provided that the Poincaré group holds in the microdomain. On the other hand, it should be noted that  the components $\varphi_{\mathrm{km}}^{\mathrm{i}}$ must be understood as unified state functions of all interaction fields (in accordance with the conceptual approach), but geodesic coordinates can always be found only for a single interaction field, so that while $\varphi_{\mathrm{km}}^{\mathrm{i}}=0$ implies the nonexistence of any field structures in the relevant $\mathrm{R}_{4}$region, but not a  general geodesy; for the concept of geodesy can refer only to a single  field structure at a time.
