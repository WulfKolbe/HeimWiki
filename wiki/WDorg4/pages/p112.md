Using the unit of measurement “Fermi,” namely $1 \mathrm{fm}=10^{-15} \mathrm{~m}$, the following holds: $\delta \mathrm{s}_{2}=0,2522 \mathrm{fm}$ and $\delta \mathrm{s}_{4}=0,042032 \mathrm{fm}$, where these two lengths are associated with the strong and electromagnetic forces within the proton’s structure. Empirical data from electron scattering on protons show that, on average, the quasi-corpuscular subconstituents of the proton have radii of less than $0,05 \mathrm{fm}$. In  proton-proton scattering, however, the radii of the subconstituents are found to be approximately 0.3 fm, according to [Wissenschaft1987b](../references/WDorg4_REF_Wissenschaft1987b.md). Nevertheless, the agreement between $\delta \mathrm{s}_{2}$ and $\delta \mathrm{s}_{4}$ with empirical data is striking. These theoretical derivations, which are consistent with empirical data, run parallel to and complement the structural investigations in [2, Chap. VIII], which, however, showed that the quasi-corpuscular subconstituents of the ponderable Hermite forms c and d are to be interpreted as spatial components of the structural flux aggregate, which the respective corpuscle represents in $\mathrm{R}_{6}$. If this interpretation of the substructure of, for example, a proton ( $\mathrm{k}+1=3$ subconstituent), then in the case of proton → proton collisions, the scattering processes described in [2, Chap. VIII] become just as transparent as the occurrence of so-called “jets” in inelastic and deeply inelastic collisions. Furthermore, it is entirely conceivable that there is another process that can occur whenever a proton collides with a stable, sufficiently light particle, but in which only one subconstituent is relevant. 

According to [2, Chapter VIII], the electron satisfies this condition. In electron → proton collisions, a situation could arise in which the electron, appearing as a subconstituent, strikes one of the proton’s three subconstituents head-on.  In such a case, the electron’s kinetic energy could excite the condenser flux of the subconstituent in question and, for a very  brief period of time (shorter than the aggregate’s flux period) to seemingly vanish, only to then appear in physical space as the proton’s excitation energy. It would therefore be conceivable that collisions $\mathrm{e}^{-} \rightarrow \mathrm{p}$ could be empirically observed in which the kinetic energy of the $\mathrm{e}^{-}$ is immediately and completely transferred.
