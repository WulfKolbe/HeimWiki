Even at low energy values E, the relationship $3 \mathrm{w}_{21}(\mathrm{E})-\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{3}=\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}$ is certainly possible. The coupling constant $3 \mathrm{w}_{21}$ (E)—since $\mathrm{w}_{21}$ refers to electromagnetic coupling—can be generated according to the principle of stimulated photon emission, because if an electromagnetic field in a given $\mathrm{R}_{3}$ volume, then the probability of a photon being emitted is determined by $\mathrm{w}_{21} \sqrt{\mathrm{~N}+1}$. For $3 \mathrm{w}_{21}$, therefore, $\sqrt{\mathrm{N}+1}=3$ must hold, i.e., $\mathrm{N}=8$, which must be maintained with very tight tolerance. Likewise, it appears essential to avoid the avalanche effect caused by stimulated emission. To solve the equation $3 \mathrm{w}_{21}-\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{3}=\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}$, it can first be noted  that when considering the numerical values $\mathrm{w}_{4_{\alpha}} \ll \mathrm{w}_{21}$ and  also $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha} \ll \underline{\mathrm{w}}_{3}$, i.e., $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha} \approx 0$ and therefore $3 \mathrm{w}_{21}-\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{3} \approx 0$, appear. To determine the momentum or energy dependence of $\mathrm{w}_{21}$ the radiation correction to the Coulomb potential $\phi(\mathrm{r})$ of a point electric charge, as given in [Relativistische1980](../references/WDorg4_REF_Relativistische1980.md), can be used. Explicitly, this implies 
$\phi(\mathrm{r})=\frac{\mathrm{e}_{1}}{\mathrm{r}}\left\{1+\frac{2 \alpha_{10}}{3 \pi} \int_{1}^{\infty} \exp (-2 \mathrm{mrx})\left(1+\frac{1}{2 \mathrm{x}^{2}}\right) \frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{x}^{2}-1}}{\mathrm{x}^{2}} \mathrm{dx}\right\}$, that is, 
$\phi(\mathrm{r})=\frac{\mathrm{e}_{1}}{\mathrm{r}} \quad \mathrm{A}(\mathrm{r})$, where $\mathrm{e}_{1}$ is the elementary charge, $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{10}=\mathrm{e}_{1}^{2}$ is the electromagnetic interaction constant at an energy of $\mathrm{E} \approx 0$ and  $\alpha_{1}=e_{1}^{2} A^{2}$ represents $E \neq 0$, but is to be written as the abbreviation $c \alpha_{1}=e_{1}^{2} A^{2}$.  In summary, this yields 
$3 \sqrt{\alpha_{10}}\left(1+\frac{2 \alpha_{10}}{3 \pi} \int_{1}^{\infty} \exp (-2 \operatorname{mrx})\left(1+\frac{1}{2 \mathrm{x}^{2}}\right) \frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{x}^{2}-1}}{\mathrm{x}^{2}} \mathrm{dx}\right)-\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{3} \approx 0$. Since in this  relation the mass m to be determined lies in the range of the electron mass, the integral can only be evaluated numerically. In this way, if $\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{e}}$ represents the electron mass, one obtains the value $\mathrm{m} \approx 2,35 \mathrm{~m}_{\mathrm{e}}$,  which, however, agrees well with $\mathrm{m}=\mathrm{E}_{1} / \mathrm{c}^{2}$. Consequently, with the transition probability $(\mathrm{N}+1) \alpha_{1}\left(\mathrm{E}_{1}\right)$, it would be possible to generate $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}$ quanta. Assuming that induced emission is also possible for these terms, the relationship $3 w_{21}\left(E_{1}^{\prime}\right)-\underline{w}_{3}=w_{4 \alpha} \cdot \sqrt{M}$ would hold with $M \gg 1$. The number M of these vector terms of the field $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}$ depends, on the one hand, on the photon transmutation rate $\rightarrow \mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}-$ quanta, denoted by R, and, on the other hand, on the number p of photons available for the transmutation, such that $\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{R}, \mathrm{p})$ holds, whereby an avalanche-like $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}$process could become possible.
