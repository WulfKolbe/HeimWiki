If M has grown sufficiently, then the condition $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha} \sqrt{\mathrm{M}}-\underline{\underline{\mathrm{w}}}_{3}=\underline{\underline{\mathrm{w}}}_{5}$ is satisfied, which leads to the fact that, with the numbers $\mathrm{M}^{\prime}>\mathrm{M}$ and  L>> 1, an induced emission occurs according to $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha} \cdot \sqrt{\mathrm{M}^{\prime}}-\underline{\underline{\mathrm{w}}}_{3}=\underline{\underline{\mathrm{w}}}_{5} \cdot \sqrt{\mathrm{~L}}$. 

The two relations $3 \mathrm{w}_{21}(\mathrm{E})-\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{3}=\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha} \cdot \sqrt{\mathrm{M}}$ and  $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha} \cdot \sqrt{\mathrm{M}^{\prime}}-\underline{\underline{\mathrm{w}}}_{3}=\underline{\underline{\mathrm{w}}}_{5} \cdot \sqrt{\mathrm{~L}}$ can be further analyzed by taking into account  that the length elements $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{j}}$ from (19a) and (19) can be traced back to the set $\mathrm{U}_{0}$ of cosmic primordial elements and the subset $\mathrm{D}_{3}=\{12,28,24\}$ of the set of dimensional numbers D. Due to $\delta \mathrm{s}_{4}=\delta \mathrm{l}_{0}^{\prime} \exp (-12 \pi)$ and $\delta \mathrm{s}_{5}=\delta \mathrm{l}_{0}^{\prime} \exp (-24 \pi)$, $\delta \mathrm{s}_{4}=\delta \mathrm{s}_{5} \exp (-12 \pi)$ can also be written. 

Based on the standardization $\|\Delta \overrightarrow{\mathrm{x}}\|=\sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{\mathrm{n}}\left|\Delta \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}\right|$ possible at the origin of the world $\mathrm{t}=0$, $\Delta \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}} \in \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}$ would hold, so that $\mathrm{n}=\operatorname{dim}\left(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}\right)$ can be interpreted as the dimensional number of a $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}$. If all $\Delta \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}$ are of equal size and $\Delta \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}=\Delta \mathrm{x}_{0}$ holds, then $\|\Delta \overrightarrow{\mathrm{x}}\|=\mathrm{n} \Delta \mathrm{x}_{0}$. Thus, $\delta \mathrm{s}_{4}=\mathrm{A} \delta \mathrm{s}_{5}$ can also be interpreted as a $\mathrm{A}=\exp (+12 \pi)$ as a dimensional number, and in an extended set of dimensional numbers, $\mathrm{B}=\exp (28 \pi)$ would then be the largest possible element. On the other hand, the factor 3 from $3 \mathrm{w}_{21}(\mathrm{E})-\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{3}$, derived from $\sqrt{\mathrm{N}+1}$, results in $\mathrm{N}=8$ photons, whereby N can also be interpreted as $\mathrm{N}=\operatorname{dim}\left(\mathrm{R}_{8}\right)$. However, if the factor from $\mathrm{w}_{21}$ is interpreted in this way, then the factor $\sqrt{\mathrm{M}}$ ' of the coupling $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}$ be interpreted in the same way according to $\mathrm{M}^{\prime} \approx \mathrm{B}=\exp (28 \pi)$, which yields $\sqrt{\mathrm{M}}^{\prime} \approx \exp (14 \pi)$. For $\sqrt{\mathrm{M}}$, on the other hand, $\sqrt{\exp (12 \pi)}=\exp (6 \pi)$ can be set. Therefore, $\sqrt{\mathrm{M}}, \mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}-\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{3}=\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{5} \sqrt{\mathrm{~L}}$ remains under discussion, in which the factor $\sqrt{\mathrm{L}}$ must be determined. 

Since, as will be shown, both $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha^{-}}$ and $\mathrm{w}_{5}$terms have energy masses, $\sqrt{\mathrm{L}}$ should be determined based on the energy balance of the left and right sides. Since $\quad \underline{\mathrm{m}}_{5} \approx \mathrm{~m}_{4 \alpha} \exp (-24 \pi)$, $\sqrt{\mathrm{L}}$ would be approximately given by $\sqrt{\exp (28 \pi) \exp (24 \pi)}=\exp (26 \pi)$. This yields the equations $3 \mathrm{w}_{21}(\mathrm{E})-\underline{\mathrm{w}}_{3} \approx(\exp (6 \pi)) \cdot \mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}$ and $\sqrt{\mathrm{M}^{\prime}}, \mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}-\underline{\underline{w}}_{3} \approx(\exp (26 \pi)) \underline{\underline{w}}_{5}$. It is also worth noting here that an energy balance of the first equation with respect to the $\mathrm{w}_{4 \alpha}$ masses is also approximately correct here.
