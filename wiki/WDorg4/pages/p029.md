The theory presented in  is, by its very nature, a semi-classical  structural theory in $\mathrm{R}_{6}$, in which the concept of the action quantum (in the empirical sense) was taken into account. It was possible to describe, with a high degree of accuracy, the invariant properties of individual elementary corpuscles, such as the required quantum number sets or their invariant energy masses. Empirically, these mass terms do not appear to be subject to the uncertainty principle in terms of measurement—that is, they appear to be determined—whereas the uncertainty principle determines the bandwidth. In phase space, this energy mass stands, so to speak, like a “thorn” in the center of a “base” of the bandwidth determined by the uncertainty principle. These determined terms of the mass spectrum initially appear problematic; however, it becomes clear from [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) that, due to the nonlinear nature of the relationship describing the stationary masses [1, Eq. 19], from [1, Chap. IV], the state functions cannot be interpreted in terms of quantum theory as probability functions. Although linear state operators exist for the components of the field function in the abstract function space, these are linked in a nonlinear form in the higher-order context according to [1, Eq. 19]  to be linked in a nonlinear form, which determines the deterministic nature of stationary mass centers, which may therefore need to be understood as self-couplings. For this reason, while it is possible to specify mass spectra in a numerically calculable form, it is not possible to describe the probabilistic behavior of large collectives of otherwise indistinguishable corpuscles or the complex probability functions resulting from the uncertainty principle. This stems from the fact that  was, for good reason, not formulated in the quantum-theoretical sense, because the initial hope was to obtain the invariants of the elementary corpuscles in this way. It follows from this that the task now must be to fundamentally extend the structural considerations of $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ in a fundamental way, which will certainly not lead to success if certain, more empirical premises of quantum theory are simply incorporated into the analysis.
