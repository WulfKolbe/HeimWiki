Dimensions $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{i}}=\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{i}} / \mathrm{D}_{1}, \mathrm{~d}_{\mathrm{i}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{i}}^{\prime} / \mathrm{D}_{1}, \mathrm{~d}_{\mathrm{j}}=\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{j}} / \mathrm{D}_{1}$ and $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{j}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{j}}^{\prime} / \mathrm{D}_{1}$ result. Since these ratios remain unchanged when all diameters are given a negative sign, $\sqrt{\mathbf{D}^{2}}= \pm \mathbf{D}$ , with the lengths $D_{i}$ through $D_{j}^{\prime}$ to be set for D. Because of $x_{i}=e D_{i}{ }^{2}(E \sqrt{\pi})^{-1}, x_{1}=e D_{1}{ }^{2}(E \sqrt{\pi})^{-1}$ and thus $d_{i}=\frac{D_{i}}{D_{1}}=\sqrt{\frac{x_{i}}{x_{1}}}$, the $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{i}}$ through $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{j}}^{\prime}$ are independent of the unit area E. 

These dimensionless “lengths” $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{i}}$ through $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{j}}^{\prime}$ can now (due to their  dimensionlessness) be examined for mutual dependencies, i.e., symmetries; especially since it repeatedly turns out that nature generally manages with only a few parameters. For now, we will attempt to find a mutual dependence among the real $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{i}}$ values. Since the $d_{i}$ are not natural numbers, the $a_{1}, \ldots, a_{n}$ as variables for the $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{i}}=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{i}}\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{n}}\right)$ must be taken from at least the set of rational numbers, provided that the $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{i}}$ are constructed by simple addition and/or multiplication of these elements. The simplest rational numbers that go beyond the set of natural numbers $N$ are $1 / \mathrm{n}$ with $1 \leq \mathrm{n}<\infty$ and $\mathrm{n} \in \mathrm{N}$. Since repeated addition and (or) multiplication yield only $\mathrm{a}_{1}=1 / 1=1$ natural numbers, $\mathrm{n}=2$ and $\mathrm{n}=3$  are used alongside $\mathrm{n}=1$, so that an attempt can be made to express the $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{i}}$ using the elements $\mathrm{a}_{1}=1, \mathrm{a}_{2}=1 / 2$ and $\mathrm{a}_{3}=1 / 3$, which form a subset of the set of rational numbers Q. It turns out that $\mathrm{d}_{1} \approx \mathrm{a}_{1}$, but  $\mathrm{d}_{2} \approx \mathrm{a}_{2}\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}+\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}+\mathrm{a}_{3}\right)\right)$ and $\mathrm{d}_{3} \approx \mathrm{a}_{1}\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}+\mathrm{a}_{3}\right)+\left(\mathrm{a}_{1}+2 \mathrm{a}_{3}\right)$ hold, as was already shown in  [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md). The maximum error deviation is approximately $\delta=1,7 \cdot 10^{-2}$. The presentation of $\mathrm{d}_{1}$ through $\mathrm{d}_{3}$ thus contains a generalized formation law for arithmetic sequences, because
