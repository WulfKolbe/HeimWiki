The vector components defined by $\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{j}}(\mathrm{j}=1 \ldots 3)$ and $\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}(\mathrm{k}=1 \ldots 7)$ seem somewhat arbitrary in their definition, but this arbitrariness is compensated for by the fact that $\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{j}}$ and $\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}$ exhibit symmetries with respect to each other, which become apparent through their numerical calculation. 

Since, as a good approximation, the $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{j}}$ values are obtained according to $\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{j}} \approx \mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{j}} / \mathrm{D}_{1}$ by reference  to the diameter of the cosmogonic fundamental sphere,  an approximation $\mathrm{b}_{1} \approx 1$ results for $\mathrm{j}=1$, a value that can also be used as a  reference quantity. Through additive combination, symmetries of $b_{j}$ and $b_{k}^{\prime}$ become apparent when the combined elements arise from the same or at least similar formation laws.
