We will now examine in more detail the significance of the constants $\mathrm{w}_{\mathrm{i}}$ in (16). 

If we are looking for geometrically grounded natural constants that satisfy both their property of being natural constants and their representation of probabilities, then these constants should be valid not only in a $\mathrm{R}_{4}$, but also in a $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{n}}$—which is not defined in detail for the time being and contains the $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ as a subspace—and they should also incorporate the concept of probability. Now, [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) already yields a 64-component energy density tensor whose energy density components, as mentioned earlier, are proportional to lengths that are independent of one another according to the uncertainty principle; these lengths can be interpreted as independent components of a vector in a 64-dimensional space. Due to algebraic properties, the aforementioned 64 energy density components $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ik}}$ decompose into $12+24=36$ and 28 energy density components, respectively, for which $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ik}} \neq 0(\mathrm{i}, \mathrm{k}=1,2, \ldots, 8)$ holds, at least for a short time. The aforementioned $\mathrm{R}_{64}$ thus decomposes into a $\mathrm{R}_{64}=\left(\mathrm{R}_{12} \cup \mathrm{R}_{24}\right) \cup \mathrm{R}_{28}$. The set of dimension numbers $\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{z}}$ for the subspaces of this $\mathrm{R}_{64}$ is thus $\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{z}}=\{12,24,28,36,64,4\}$.  If one is searching for dimensional numbers valid in the $\mathrm{R}_{64}$, then $\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{z}}$ can be used. Thus, the set of variables for the aforementioned natural constants is $\mathrm{D}_{7}$, whose structured representation is the set $\underline{\mathrm{D}}=\{(36),(12,28,24),(4,64)\}$ according to (11).
