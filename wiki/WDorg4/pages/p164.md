As previously explained, interaction fields can be explained purely geometrically. Their different properties are caused by curved coordinates in at least one specific subspace of the $\mathrm{R}_{12}$. 

The curvature of a coordinate may change, but the dimensional numbers of the subspaces existing at that point in time remain invariant quantities. As can be shown, dimensional numbers are the variables of natural constants relative to a temporal “zero point” of a physical event that occurred at time a) $\mathrm{T}=0$, b) $\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{T}_{1}$, and c) $\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{w}}$. 

Here, $\mathrm{T}=0$ refers to the beginning of the world approximately $10^{115} \mathrm{~s}$ ago, and $\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{T}_{1}$ the kosmogony of an elementary universe approximately $4 \cdot 10^{17} \mathrm{~s}$ ago, and $\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{w}}$ denotes the  time of the emergence or disappearance of an interaction quantum. 

The dimensional numbers considered here include not only those of the subspaces of $\mathrm{R}_{12}$, but other dimensional numbers of spaces extending beyond this $\mathrm{R}_{12}$ may also be relevant. These can be determined, among other things, from the algebraic structure of a 64-component energy density tensor [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md). A set algorithm valid as of $\mathrm{T}=0$ must determine the functions on which the dimensional numbers depend. The discovery of this set algorithm, together with the natural constants $\mathrm{T}=0$ existing at the time $\mathrm{T}=0$, was described in detail in Chapter II, Section 2. 

However, the derived set algorithm is also capable of specifying the sets of dimensional numbers lying in an apeiron “before” the time T = 0. Reference is made here to Chapter III, 1. 

At time $\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{T}_{1}$, the change in a single length element from $\mathrm{b}_{1} \delta \mathrm{~s}_{0} \approx \delta \mathrm{~s}_{0}$ to $\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime} \delta \mathrm{s}_{0}$ , whereby $\delta \mathrm{s}_{0}$ corresponds approximately to the Planck elementary length, and furthermore $\mathrm{b}_{1} \approx 1, \mathrm{~b}_{2}^{\prime}<1$ holds. $\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime} \delta \mathrm{s}_{0}$ resulted from the emergence of a mass on the order of the Planck mass and the length element $\mathrm{b}_{1} \delta \mathrm{~s}_{0}$ was reduced to $\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime} \delta \mathrm{s}_{0}$ as a result of the curvature of the spacetime coordinates. The “length” $-\alpha \delta \mathrm{s}_{0}$ of $\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime} \delta \mathrm{s}_{0} \approx(1-\alpha) \delta \mathrm{s}_{0}$ subsequently decayed and split into a multitude of $\mathrm{b}_{1} \delta \mathrm{~s}_{0}$elements, leading to the generation of mass from a strongly  curved region of spacetime.
