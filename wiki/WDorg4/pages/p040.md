If, as in [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md), the diameter of the universe existing at universal time $t \geq 0$ is denoted by D and the elementary area (metron) according to [1, Eq. 15] as $\tau$, then $\mathbf{D}$ and $\tau$ are interdependent, which, according to $\mathbf{D}(\tau)$, is explicitly described by  the relations [2, Eq. 37] and [2, Eq. 37a]. The  relation [2, Eq. 37] is $\mathrm{f} \sqrt{3 / 2}\left(\frac{\mathrm{D}}{4 \sqrt{\tau}} \cdot \mathrm{f}^{3} \cdot \sqrt{3 / 2}-1\right)^{2}=\mathrm{D} / \sqrt{\tau}$,  while for the contraction f, the relation [2, Eq. 37a], i.e., $\left(\frac{\mathrm{eD} \sqrt{\tau}}{\pi \mathrm{E}}-1\right) \mathrm{f}^{2}=\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{eD} \sqrt{\tau}}{\pi \mathrm{E}}}$, must be set, where $\mathrm{E}=1 \mathrm{~m}^{2}$ symbolizes the unit area. In this context, $D(\tau)$ is related to $D$  and $\tau$ in a form that is still unknown from the respective world age $\mathrm{t} \geq 0 \mathrm{ab}$.  It can be stated with certainty that D (t) is currently expanding,  which means that, according to this relationship [2, Eq. 37], the elementary area  $\tau(\mathrm{t})$ decreases with the age of the universe, so that in some way the elementary natural constants may depend on this age of the universe, as was explicitly developed in [2, Chap. V]. If we substitute into $[1, \mathrm{Gl}$. 15] the empirical natural constants $\gamma$, h, and c valid for the present age of the universe,  then numerically we obtain $\tau \approx 6,15 \cdot 10^{-70} \mathrm{~m}^{2}$ or, for the elementary length, $\delta \mathrm{s}_{0}=\sqrt{\tau} \approx 2,48 \cdot 10^{-35} \mathrm{~m}$, which yields $\mathrm{D} \approx 6,026 \cdot 10^{125} \mathrm{~m}$ for the current diameter of the universe. This value exceeds by an extraordinary margin the optical radius of the known universe, for which $\mathrm{R} \approx 1,63 \cdot 10^{26} \mathrm{~m}$ follows from [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md), yet D also significantly exceeds the horizon distance of $10^{50} \mathrm{~m}$ in an inflationary universe according to [Wissenschaft1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Wissenschaft1984.md). Since the observable universe exhibits no preferred direction on large scales, a spherical shape could be assumed, as a very good approximation, even for the largest possible universe of the respective diameter D. Since $\tau$ is the ultimate geometric unit of the world, for the spherical surface of the universe, i.e., $\pi \mathrm{D}^{2}$,  , an integer multiple n of the elementary area $\tau$ can be assumed, so that $\pi \mathrm{D}^{2}=\mathrm{n} \tau$ must hold. In the cosmological relation [2, Eq. 37] with [2, Eq. 37a] from [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md), we can now substitute $\tau=\pi \mathrm{D}^{2} / \mathrm{n}$ which yields the relation D(n) [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md). At world time $\mathrm{t}=0$, the surface of the universe must therefore be a monometric sphere of area—

Die  Beziehung [2, Gl. 37] lautet $\mathrm{f} \sqrt{3 / 2}\left(\frac{\mathrm{D}}{4 \sqrt{\tau}} \cdot \mathrm{f}^{3} \cdot \sqrt{3 / 2}-1\right)^{2}=\mathrm{D} / \sqrt{\tau}$,  während für die Kürzung f die Beziehung [2, Gl. 37a], also  $\left(\frac{\mathrm{eD} \sqrt{\tau}}{\pi \mathrm{E}}-1\right) \mathrm{f}^{2}=\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{eD} \sqrt{\tau}}{\pi \mathrm{E}}}$ zu setzen ist, worin $\mathrm{E}=1 \mathrm{~m}^{2}$ die  Einheitsfläche symbolisiert. In diesem Zusammenhang $D(\tau)$ hängen $D$  und $\tau$ in einer noch unbekannten Form vom jeweiligen Weltalter $\mathrm{t} \geq 0 \mathrm{ab}$.  Mit Sicherheit kann festgestellt werden, daß D (t) gegenwärtig expandiert,  was bedeutet, daß nach dieser Beziehung [2, Gl. 37] die Elementarfläche  $\tau(\mathrm{t})$ mit dem Weltalter abnimmt, so daß in irgendeiner Weise die elementa ren Naturkonstanten von diesem Weltalter abhängig sein können, was in  [2, Kap. Dieser Wert übersteigt außerordentlich  den optischen Radius des bekannten Universums, für den $\mathrm{R} \approx 1,63 \cdot 10^{26} \mathrm{~m}$  aus [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) folgt, doch übersteigt D auch den Horizontabstand von $10^{50} \mathrm{~m}$  in einem inflationären Universum nach [Wissenschaft1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Wissenschaft1984.md) erheblich. Da das beobachtbare  Universum großräumig keine Vorzugsrichtung aufweist, wäre in sehr guter  Näherung eine kugelförmige Gestalt auch für das größtmögliche Universum  vom jeweiligen Durchmesser D anzunehmen.
