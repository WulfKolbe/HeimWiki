The approach outlined in [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) and [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md) toward a synmetronics of the $\mathrm{R}_{6}$world  appears to be more reasonable, since the transcoordinates here  differ completely in semantics from those of $\mathrm{R}_{4}$. They are not compactified, and their structures only appear geometrically in the $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ as spacetime mappings. The existence of the  structured subspace $\mathrm{R}_{8}=\mathrm{R}_{3} \cup \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{~S}_{2} \cup \mathrm{I}_{2} \subset \mathrm{R}_{12}$ was already demonstrated in [1, Eq. 3d] and [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md), and  this subspace will serve as the basis for the following discussion. 

The property of an interaction field whose coupling constant is the specific element of one of the sets (24) must, upon geometrization of the interactions, obviously be a geometric property of the structured $\mathrm{R}_{8}$. First, let us consider in $\mathrm{R}_{4}=\mathrm{R}_{3} \cup \mathrm{~T}_{1}$ the ART resulting from  [1, Eq. 1 and Eq. 1a] and approximated by $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}=\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ki}}$, in which $\Gamma_{\mathrm{km}}^{\mathrm{i}}$ provides a gravitational interaction field. However, the three-index symbols appear—only with respect to regular affinities—as mixed-variant 3rd-order tensor components that describe a force effect of the  tensorial gravitational potential $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}$. The $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}$ arise from general coordinate transformations of the $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ coordinates, which must satisfy the global Poincaré group. In this way, every point in one coordinate system is isomorphically mapped to a point in another coordinate system within this group. The Cartesian system, as a geodesic system of Euclidean or pseudo-Euclidean geometry, also belongs to this transformation group, so that a Euclidean tangent space can be found for every point in a non-Euclidean space. 

Such metric considerations can also be extended to the existing $\mathrm{R}_{8} \supset \mathrm{R}_{4}$, because spacetime is a structural subspace of this $\mathrm{R}_{8}=\mathrm{R}_{4} \cup \mathrm{~S}_{2} \cup \mathrm{I}_{2}$. In this extension, the indices of the coordinates range from 1 to 8. Eight-dimensional coordinate transformations of the general form $\mathrm{z}_{\mathrm{k}}=\mathrm{z}_{\mathrm{k}}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{8}\right)$ with $\mathrm{k}=1 \ldots 8$ can therefore be specified, where $\mathrm{z}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{z}_{8}$ and $\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{8}$ are non-Euclidean coordinates. At every point in a $\mathrm{R}_{8}$ spanned by the coordinates $\mathrm{z}_{\mathrm{k}}$ , a tangent space with the Euclidean coordinates $\xi_{p}=\xi_{p}\left(z_{1} \ldots z_{8}\right)$ can now be constructed, so that the dependence $\xi_{p}=\xi_{p}\left(z_{1}\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{8}\right) \ldots z_{8}\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{8}\right)\right)=\xi_{p}\left(z_{j}\left(x_{i}\right)\right)_{i, j=1}^{8}=$  $=\eta_{p}\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{8}\right)=\eta_{p}\left(x_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{8}$ holds.
