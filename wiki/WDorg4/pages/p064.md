According to these investigations, therefore, prior to a measurement process, it would not be R, but rather $\mathrm{F}=\psi$ in the form $\mathrm{R} \stackrel{\wedge}{=} \psi \cdot \psi^{\mathrm{x}}$, and due to $\mathrm{R} \sim \rho$ as energy density $\rho \hat{=} \eta \cdot \eta^{\mathrm{x}}$. For $\eta$, there must then also be a mapped value $\underline{\eta}=\eta \psi$, analogous to $\underline{R}$ as a consequence of (9). This means that only  during the brief time interval of a measurement process do real $\mathrm{R}_{4}$—or  $\mathrm{R}_{6}$structures R exist with real energy densities, in accordance with [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) and [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md).  Outside the time interval of this measurement, there would then be, so to speak, only  an “image” or an “idea” of an elementary structure characterized by R with a complex $\eta$ function. However, this situation coincides completely with the picture of the two worlds according to W. HEISENBERG, whose interpretation can be derived from relations (9) through (10a) [Goldmann1990](../references/WDorg4_REF_Goldmann1990.md). 

Due to the nature of the mapping process (9), $\psi=\psi\left(\underline{\mathrm{x}}_{1} \ldots \underline{\mathrm{x}}_{4}\right)$ and $\mathrm{R}=\mathrm{R}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{4}\right)$ hold, so that the two physically distinct spacetimes described by (9a) do in fact exist. This difference is also expressed in the different elementary lengths $\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{o}}=\sqrt{\tau}$ and $\delta \mathrm{l}_{\mathrm{v}}$. With regard to these elementary lengths, $\underline{\mathrm{R}}=\mathrm{BR}$ would then have to be set in the form $\underline{\mathrm{R}}\left(\delta \mathrm{l}_{\mathrm{v}}, \delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{o}}\right)=\mathrm{B}\left(\delta \mathrm{l}_{\mathrm{v}}\right) \mathrm{R}\left(\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{o}}\right)$, i.e., in (9a), the spacetimes $\mathrm{R}_{4}^{\mathrm{p}}\left(\delta \mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{o}}\right)$ and $\mathrm{R}_{4}^{\mathrm{w}}\left(\delta 1_{\mathrm{v}}\right)$ intertwine to form the empirically accessible spacetime $\mathrm{R}_{4}$, in which the operation of the quantum principle at the microlevel  becomes understandable. The relation $\underline{\mathrm{R}}=\mathrm{BR}$ cannot be directly compared to the general uncertainty relation (8) because it depends on two physically distinct spacetimes or elementary lengths, and thus on canonically conjugate quantities; this also makes the empirically well-founded concept of quantum duality and its complementary images transparent.
