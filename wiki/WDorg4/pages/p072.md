In the Apeiron, metaphorically characterized by t<0, however, it must be assumed that the preformational structures of this Apeiron can be traced back to the symmetric set $\mathrm{A}=\{1,3,5,7,11,13, \ldots\}$. This set A can also be written in the form $\underline{\mathrm{A}}=\{1,<>, 3,5,7 \ldots\}$, where <> symbolizes an empty element. A can now be structured based on $\mathrm{K}_{12}=\{6 ; 6\}$ , whereby this cardinal number is numerically complex and results from $\mathrm{K}_{12}=\{3 ; 1 ; 2 ; 2 ; 4\}$ by combining the first three and the last two cardinal numbers to form $3+1+2=6,4+2=6$.  This combination is possible because the distinction between the coordinates $\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{6}\right)$ and $\left(\mathrm{x}_{7} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{12}\right)$ lies in the fact that the former coordinates have elemental lengths dependent on the world age 
$\delta \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}=\delta \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}(\mathrm{t}), \mathrm{i}=1 \ldots 6$, whereas the latter coordinates are independent of time. This complex of cardinal numbers $\mathrm{K}_{12}$ must be regarded as belonging to $\mathrm{K}_{12}$—as one of the oldest algebraic laws of the world—and is therefore also valid “before” $\mathrm{t}=0$. This yields 
$\mathbf{B}=\{(1,<>, 3,5,7,11),(11,13,17,19,23,29)\}$. The second subset of B can now, using the simple combination rule $\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{i}}+\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{i}}=\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{i}+1}$, contain numbers $\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{i}}$ that can be interpreted as dimension numbers: 
$11+\mathrm{m}_{6}=13, \mathrm{~m}_{6}=2 ; 13+\mathrm{m}_{7}=17, \mathrm{~m}_{7}=4$; etc. The second set of dimension numbers is therefore $\mathrm{D}_{2}=\{2,4,2,4,6\}$. Indexing identical numbers is  necessary here due to the concept of sets. Since in the first subset the numbers 1 and 3 are separated by an empty element, the dimension numbers 1 and 3 are obtained for now. The remaining numbers in the first subset of B, on the other hand, yield the dimension numbers 2, 2, 4, resulting in the first set of dimension numbers $D_{1}=\{1,3, \stackrel{1}{2}, \stackrel{2}{2}, 4\}$. Overall, a higher-level set of dimension numbers A’ would thus exist in the metaphor $\mathrm{t}<0$, namely 
$\mathrm{A}^{\prime}=\{(1,3, \stackrel{1}{2}, \stackrel{2}{2}, 4),(\stackrel{1}{2}, \stackrel{1}{4}, \stackrel{2}{2}, \stackrel{2}{4}, 6)\}$, which is already considered ordered since there is a direct correspondence to the ordered set B.  The sum of the dimension numbers here is 30, whereas at the time $\mathrm{t}=0$  a $\mathrm{R}_{28}^{*}=\mathrm{R}_{12}^{*} \cup \mathrm{R}_{16}^{*}=\mathrm{R}_{16}^{*} \cup \mathrm{R}_{12}^{*}$ existed with a set of dimensional numbers \{12, 16\} or \{16, 12\}. Due to $1+3+2+2+4+2+4=18$ and  $2+4+6=12$, A’ might have switched from $\mathrm{t}<0$ to $\mathrm{t}=0 \mathrm{zu}$ have switched to “A” with $1+3+2+2+4+2+(4-2)=16$, thereby creating a set $\mathrm{A}^{\prime \prime}=\left\{(1,3, \stackrel{1}{2}, \stackrel{2}{2}, 4),\left(\stackrel{1}{2}^{2}, \stackrel{2}{2}, \stackrel{3}{2}, 4,6\right)\right\}$.
