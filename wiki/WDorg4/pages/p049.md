In addition to these diameters, there existed at the origin of time $\mathrm{t}=0$ of the world, there also existed the non-spatial structure—abstractly formulable through the cardinal number complex $\mathrm{K}_{6}$—of a $\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*}=\mathrm{R}_{3}^{*} \cup \mathrm{~T}_{1}^{*} \cup \mathrm{~S}_{2}^{*}$ and a 
$\mathrm{R}_{14}=\mathrm{R}_{6} \cup \mathrm{R}_{6}^{\prime} \cup \mathrm{R}_{2}^{\prime \prime}$ with $\mathrm{R}_{6}^{* \prime}=\mathrm{I}_{2}^{*} \cup \mathrm{G}_{4}^{*}$, which yields the cardinal number complex  $\mathrm{K}_{14}=\{(3 ; 1 ; 2)$; (2; 4); (2)\}. 

As has already been shown, a norm can be defined for a vector with coordinates $\left(\mathrm{x}_{1}, \ldots, \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{n}}\right)$ in both the Euclidean and non-Euclidean cases. 

The $\underline{d}_{\mathrm{i}}=\beta \mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{i}}$ can be defined in the same way as already shown, using $\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{1}=\left|\mathrm{b}_{1}\right|$ as well as $\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{2}=\left|\mathrm{b}_{2}\right|+\left|\mathrm{b}_{2}\right|$ and $\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{3}=\left|\mathrm{b}_{3}\right|+\left|\mathrm{b}_{3}\right|+\left|\mathrm{b}_{3}\right|$ or $\mathrm{b}_{\mathrm{i}}=\mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{i}} \underline{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathrm{i}}$. In contrast, the $\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathrm{i}}^{\prime}$ can belong only to one $\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*,}=\mathrm{I}_{2}^{*} \cup \mathrm{G}_{4}^{*}$. First, the Euclidean norms $\mathrm{c}_{1}^{2}=\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{1}{ }^{2}+\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{2}{ }^{2}+\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{3}{ }^{2}$ and $\mathrm{c}_{2}^{2}=\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{1}{ }^{2}+\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{2}{ }^{2}$ are defined. The non-Euclidean norm, on the other hand, yields $c_{1}=2\left|b_{1}^{\prime}\right|$ and $c_{2}=4\left|b_{2}^{\prime}\right|$, that is, $b_{1}^{\prime}=c_{1} / 2$ and  $\mathrm{b}_{2}^{\prime}=\mathrm{c}_{2}$ /4. Finally, $\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{1}^{\prime}$ and $\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{2}^{\prime}$ can be assigned to $\mathrm{R}_{2}^{* \prime}$ as a subspace of $\mathrm{R}_{14}^{*}=\mathrm{R}_{6}^{*} \cup \mathrm{R}_{6}^{* \prime} \cup \mathrm{R}_{2}^{* \text { " }}$, with a mixture of Euclidean and non-Euclidean norms. Thus, $\mathrm{c}_{3}=\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{1}{ }^{\prime 2}-\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{2}^{\prime 2}$ holds and, at the same time,  $\mathrm{c}_{3}=2\left|\mathrm{~b}_{3}^{\prime}\right|$ holds, so $\mathrm{b}_{3}^{\prime}=\mathrm{c}_{3} / 2=\left(\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{1}^{\prime 2}-\underline{\mathrm{d}}_{2}^{\prime 2}\right) / 2=0,0833328 \approx 1 / 12=\mathrm{g}$. Thus, the approximation (2) with the prime elements of the set U can be written in the form $\mathrm{v}=\mathrm{a}_{2}\left(\mathrm{a}_{2} \hat{} / \mathrm{a}_{2} / \mathrm{a}_{2}\right)\left(\mathrm{a}_{1} / \mathrm{a}_{3}\right)+\mathrm{a}, \quad \mathrm{a}=\mathrm{a}_{3}^{3} / \mathrm{a}_{2}^{2}, \quad \beta=\mathrm{v} / \mathrm{d}_{2}$,
