Hyperspace $\mathrm{R}_{12}=\mathrm{R}_{6} \cup \mathrm{~V}_{6}$ is also coupled with $\mathrm{V}_{6}=\mathrm{I}_{2} \cup \mathrm{G}_{4}$, so that it must be concluded that there must also be hermetic structures—and thus fields—in this $\mathrm{V}_{6}$, which are likewise characterized by space- and time-less coupling constants. Here, however, the concept of coupling must be used in an expanded sense. In $\mathrm{R}_{6}=\mathrm{R}_{3} \cup \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{~S}_{2}$, each of the four elementary forms of hermeticism, a through d, is accompanied in $\mathrm{R}_{4}=\mathrm{R}_{3} \cup \mathrm{~T}_{1}$ by the coordinates $\left(\mathrm{x}_{5}, \mathrm{x}_{6}\right)$, that is, by the $\mathrm{S}_{2}\left(\mathrm{x}_{5}, \mathrm{x}_{6}\right)$, whereby $\mathrm{x}_{5}$ and $\mathrm{x}_{6}$ are interpreted as organizational coordinates. Thus, the mapping $\mathrm{S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}_{4}$ alters structures in spacetime, specifically as a result of a modulation of the time structure $\mathrm{S}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{~T}_{1}$. The same line of reasoning now applies, however, to the $\mathrm{V}_{6}$ of hyperspace $\mathrm{R}_{12}=\mathrm{R}_{6} \cup \mathrm{~V}_{6}$, because  the mapping $\mathrm{G}_{4}\left(\mathrm{x}_{9} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{12}\right) \rightarrow \mathrm{I}_{2}\left(\mathrm{x}_{7}, \mathrm{x}_{8}\right) \rightarrow \mathrm{S}_{2}$ exists here, which ultimately alters the structures of $\mathrm{R}_{4}$. A variation in $\mathrm{V}_{6}$ structures results in a variation in $\mathrm{R}_{4}$ structures and, consequently, a change in the hermetic form; from this, it follows that the coupling constants associated with $\mathrm{V}_{6} \rightarrow \mathrm{~S}_{2}$ can only ever appear in conjunction with the elements of  $\mathrm{w}_{2}$.
