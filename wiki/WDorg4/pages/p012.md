In $\mathrm{R}_{6}$, the field equations ( $\mathrm{R}_{4} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}_{6}$ ) were fully geometrized and solved [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md). The possible variety of solutions necessitates a unique generalization, which ultimately leads, in [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md), through a reformulation of the cosmological problem, to the derivation of fundamental quantum number theorems, each of which provides a mass spectrum of possible ponderable mass terms (rest masses) as resonances of a ground state, whereby both the ground state and the upper resonance limit of the respective spectrum are determined by the corresponding fundamental quantum number theorem. These quantum number laws are thereby reduced to a fundamental symmetry of very limited scope. Although the agreement between the theoretical statements and the empirical data from high-energy physics is astonishing, all derivations in [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) and [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md) are of a semi-classical nature; however, the approach to further investigations can already be seen in [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) as the aforementioned dimensional law. Not only was $\mathrm{R}_{4} \subset \mathrm{R}_{6}$ demonstrated there, but also the existence of a  hyperspace $\mathrm{R}_{12} \supset \mathrm{R}_{6}$; however, there are no other hyperspaces that could be derived from the  dimension law. An analysis of the $\mathrm{R}_{12}$ coordinates according to [1, Chap. IV, 4 and 5] showed that the concept of energy is defined only in the tensor $\mathbf{R}_{6} \subset \mathbf{R}_{12}$ of the material world, but not in the hyperspace dimensions $\mathrm{x}_{7} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{12}$, so that it seems reasonable to assume that non-material hyperspace processes can influence the events of the material world $\mathrm{R}_{6}$. This paper will explore such questions.  It should also be noted here that whenever numerical quantities of a phenomenological nature are under discussion, the International System of Units (kg, m, s), supplemented by the electromagnetic units (V, A) and the absolute temperature scale (Kelvin), is used. Thus, the fundamental empirical natural constants are the action quantum $\mathrm{h}=2 \pi \hbar$, the gravitational constant (Newton) $\gamma$ and the speed of light in a vacuum c, as well as the influence constant $\varepsilon_{0}$ and the induction constant $\mu_{0}=4 \pi \cdot 10^{-7}$ (as the definition of the unit of measurement of the ampere, A) with the dimensions $[\mathrm{h}]=\mathrm{VAs}^{2}=\mathrm{Ws}^{2}$ as well as $[\gamma]=\mathrm{m}^{3} \mathrm{~kg}^{-1} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$ or $\left[\varepsilon_{0}\right]=\mathrm{AsV}^{-1} \mathrm{~m}^{-1}$  and $\left[\mu_{0}\right]=\mathrm{VsA}^{-1} \mathrm{~m}^{-1}$ or $[\mathrm{c}]=\mathrm{m} / \mathrm{s}$, respectively. 

A brief summary of the content of this paper, organized according to the chapters covered, is provided to serve as an overview and facilitate a smooth introduction.
