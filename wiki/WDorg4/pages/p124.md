The tangent space spanned at the point $\mathrm{P}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{8}\right)$ with Euclidean coordinates $\eta \delta_{p}$ has the property that, in general, it does not coincide with  the other tangent space (with Euclidean coordinates $\xi_{\mathrm{p}}$). Only in the special case can $\xi_{p}$ and $\delta_{p}$ be made to coincide. Using the summation convention, the covariant notation for the metric $d s^{2}=d \eta_{p} d \eta^{p}=g_{i k} d x^{i} d x^{k}$ with $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}=\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}\left(\mathrm{x}_{1} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{8}\right)$, $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}=\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ki}}$, and $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}=\sum_{\mathrm{p}=1}^{8} \frac{\partial \eta_{\mathrm{p}}}{\partial \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}} \frac{\partial \eta_{\mathrm{p}}}{\partial \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{k}}}$. 

If the symmetry $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}=\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ki}}$ is assumed for the fundamental tensor,  then the three-index symbols $\Gamma_{\mathrm{km}}^{\mathrm{i}}=\Gamma_{\mathrm{mk}}^{\mathrm{i}}$ also become symmetric in the covariant indices. This means, among other things, that the $\Gamma_{\mathrm{km}}^{\mathrm{i}}$ can be explicitly represented by the gik, but also that $\mathrm{ds}^{2}=\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}} \mathrm{dx}^{\mathrm{i}} \mathrm{dx}^{\mathrm{k}}=$ is constant. 

Because of $\partial \eta_{p} / \partial x_{i}=\left(\partial \xi_{p} / \partial z_{j}\right)\left(\partial z_{j} / \partial x_{i}\right)$ or with $a_{p i}^{(\omega)}=\frac{\partial \xi_{p}}{\partial z_{(\omega)}} \frac{\partial z_{(\omega)}}{\partial x_{i}}$ as well as $\frac{\partial \eta_{p}}{\partial \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}}=\sum_{\omega=1}^{3} \mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{pi}}^{(\omega)}+\mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{pi}}^{(4)}+\sum_{\omega=5}^{6} \mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{pi}}^{(\omega)}+\sum_{\omega=7}^{8} \mathrm{a}_{\mathrm{pi}}^{(\omega)}=\kappa_{\mathrm{pi}}^{(3)}+\kappa_{\mathrm{pi}}^{(2)}+\kappa_{\mathrm{pi}}^{(1)}+\kappa_{\mathrm{pi}}^{(0)}$, if the parenthesization of $\mathrm{j}=\omega$ means that the summation convention is lifted, the function $\xi_{\mathrm{p}}$ be assigned to the “Hermetric forms” through the indexing $\mu=0,1,2,3$. In this way, the function $\kappa_{\mathrm{pi}}^{(\mu)}$ can be introduced, which, by analogy with synmetronics according to [2, Chap. VI], yields an infinitesimal analogue to the synmetronic partial structures, namely $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}^{(\mu \nu)}=\kappa_{\mathrm{pi}}^{(\mu)} \cdot \kappa_{\mathrm{pk}}^{(\nu)}$, when, for the sake of brevity, the matrix trace of the tensor product $\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ik}}^{(\mu \nu)}=\sum_{\mathrm{p}} \kappa_{\mathrm{ip}}^{(\mu)} \cdot \kappa_{\mathrm{pk}}^{(\nu)}=\kappa_{\mathrm{pi}}^{(\mu)} \kappa_{\mathrm{pk}}^{(\nu)}$ is set to $\tau=0$ in the case $\tau=0$. These $\kappa_{\mathrm{pi}}^{(\mu)}$ are now, like the synmetronic lattice core selectors mentioned in [2, Chap. VI], suitable as structural units for describing the hermetic (non-Euclidean) subspace structure of $\mathrm{R}_{8}=\mathrm{R}_{3} \cup \mathrm{~T}_{1} \cup \mathrm{~S}_{2} \cup \mathrm{I}_{2}$ and, consequently, the hermetic forms a through d, among other things, provided that the summation limit $h$ of the $a_{p i}^{(\omega)}$ is defined in the form $\sum_{\omega=\mu}^{h} a_{p i}^{(\omega)}$ by the respective hermetic subspace, which is characterized by $\mu$. These groups of summands thus yield, for $\mathrm{h}(\mu)=3$, the subspace $\mathrm{R}_{3} \hat{=} \mu=3$, for $\mathrm{h}(\mu)=4$ the subspace $\mathrm{T}_{1} \hat{=} \mu=2$, and for $\mathrm{h}(\mu)=6$ the subspace $\mathrm{S}_{2} \hat{=} \mu=1$.
