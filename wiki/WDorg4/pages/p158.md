In the Merian calculation, the energetic interactions provide, with great accuracy, the constants of the strong and weak forces, as well as those of the electromagnetic and gravitational fields, so that hyperspace dynamics also results in a unified description of all interactions. Thus, the structural law of matter appears to have come into being at t = 0, only to take effect in the material world much later (following a material cosmogony $\mathrm{t}=\mathrm{T}_{1}$). 

Against this background, several questions arise regarding the spectra of complex Hermitian forms c and d described in [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md). From the total set of these elementary particles, it is apparently only possible to empirically detect the elements of a relatively small subset that exceed certain formation probabilities with respect to the high-energy physics boundary conditions of the experiment. One would therefore need to investigate a functional relationship between such formation probabilities and the theoretical particle properties [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md) and these experimental boundary conditions. On the other hand, a question also arises regarding the lifetimes of these elementary corpusculars. On the one hand, as shown in [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md), the internal structures of these elementary corpusculars and their resonances can be considered in the form of 4 configuration zones and their protosimplex occupations in the physical $\mathrm{R}_{3}$, whereas, on the other hand, their lifetimes (or full bandwidths) are empirically known. Temporal stability occurs only when the protosimplex configurations of the configuration zones consist solely of the invariant framework structure, which depends solely on the configuration number k—a condition that holds for $\mathrm{k}=1$ the electron and for $\mathrm{k}=2$ the proton. The temporal existence intervals of all other elementary particles appear to become shorter the more the protosimplex occupancies of their configuration zones deviate from these skeletal structures. Since, according to [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md), the formation process of these complex forms of hermetry constantly repeats itself as energy increases (excitation from the outer zone to the central zone), the further question arises as to whether there are metastable occupancies; for then (analogous to the periodic table of elements) these metastable states would repeat periodically in a resonance spectrum. Empirically, this would manifest as resonances with extremely long lifetimes (i.e., narrow bandwidths). This raises the further question of the extent to which the empirically discovered so-called “charm particles” can be understood in this context.
