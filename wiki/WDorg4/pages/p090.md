If, for example, two elements are combined in $\left\{\mathrm{w}_{1}, \mathrm{w}_{2}\right\}$, then this subset would correspond to $\left\{\mathrm{E}_{1}, \mathrm{E}_{2}\right\}$, whereby a combination of $\mathrm{w}_{1}$ and $\mathrm{w}_{2}$ also results in a combination of the corresponding properties $\mathrm{E}_{1}$ and $\mathrm{E}_{2}$. In this way, however, a new property resulting from the combination of $\mathrm{E}_{1}$ with $\mathrm{E}_{2}$ that is entirely new but secondary as a result of the combination of $\mathrm{w}_{1}$ with $\mathrm{w}_{2}$. 

The $\mathrm{w}_{\mathrm{ij}}$are of a spaceless and timeless nature,  because they trace back to the pre-existence of algebraic primordial structures that  cannot change over time $\mathrm{t} \geq 0$. Furthermore, the combination operations of the primordial elements are limited to the commutative operations of addition and multiplication, whereby this multiplication is ultimately also reducible to repeated additions. Finally, it can be noted that the sets are always structured and exhibit symmetries such as $\mathrm{K}_{12}$, but also $\mathrm{K}_{8}$ as well as $\mathrm{K}_{6}$ and $\mathrm{K}_{4}$. The cardinal number complexes form a chain 
$\mathrm{K}_{4}=\{3 ; 1\} \subset \mathrm{K}_{6} \subset \mathrm{~K}_{8} \subset \mathrm{~K}_{12}=\{3 ; 1 ; 2 ; 2 ; 4\}$. If one considers the numerical values resulting from (16), then $\mathrm{w}_{21}$ through $\mathrm{w}_{24}$ to be closely related to the empirical coupling constants of the four empirically known classes of  interactions, a fact also supported by the  algebraic origin of these $\mathrm{w}_{2 \mathrm{j}}$values (they all belong to the set $\mathrm{w}_{2}$). The properties of the $\mathrm{w}_{\mathrm{ij}}$ values will combine to form entirely new secondary properties when linked together. It can therefore be conjectured that the $\mathrm{w}_{2 \mathrm{j}}$ in (16) are algebraically related at least to the physical coupling constants of the present world. 

The simplest property of a term from (16), relative to the entire set of these terms, would be its existence or nonexistence in the set-theoretical sense. If we are dealing with the sets $\mathrm{w}_{1}$ and $\mathrm{w}_{2}$, then the new property would give rise to a distinguished subset $\left\{\overline{\mathrm{w}}_{1}, \overline{\mathrm{w}}_{2}\right\} \subset\left\{\mathrm{w}_{1}, \mathrm{w}_{2}\right\}$ within the set $\left\{\mathrm{w}_{1}, \mathrm{w}_{2}\right\}$.
