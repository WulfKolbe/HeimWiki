If we consider group B containing $\mathrm{S}_{2}\left(\mathrm{z}_{5}, \mathrm{z}_{6}\right)$, that is,  B $\equiv(\langle 11\rangle,\langle 12\rangle,\langle 13\rangle,\langle 21\rangle,\langle 31\rangle)$, then, by virtue of (25), it immediately becomes clear that, just as for A in $\mathrm{R}_{4}$, there is also a splitting for B, namely $\mathrm{B}_{1}$ for $\langle 11\rangle$, as well as two terms each, $\mathrm{B}_{2}$ or $\mathrm{B}_{3}$, which are determined by < 12 > + < 21 > or < 13 > + < 31 >, so that $\mathrm{B}=\mathrm{B}_{1}+\mathrm{B}_{2}+\mathrm{B}_{3}$ is composed of 3 partial fields. It was already evident from [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) that $\mathrm{S}_{2}\left(\mathrm{z}_{5}, \mathrm{z}_{6}\right)$, when projected into spacetime $\mathrm{R}_{4}$,  as a graviton field whose quanta are second-order tensor terms,  so that $\mathrm{B}_{1}$ should be interpreted as such a field. In contrast to the  spinor terms (half-integer spin), which do not appear here, the spin of the tensor terms under  discussion is an integer, and this integer is identical to  the tensor rank of the invariant quantity by which such a  boson field is characterized. In the present case, this is the second-order metric fundamental tensor, which in the non-Hermitian case is additively extended by an anti-Hermitian tensor, but which can always be interpreted as a rotation of a vector field. Accordingly, however, the field quanta of $\mathrm{B}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{B}_{3}$ must also be tensor terms. Since <12> corresponds to $\mathrm{A}_{1}$ in $\mathrm{R}_{4}$, but the $\mathrm{A}_{1}$quanta are vector terms, $\mathbf{B}_{2}$ should also be interpreted as such a field of vector terms, leaving only the possibility of scalar terms for $B_{3}$. If the respective spin of these tensor terms is added as a second index, then the following holds for the group of gravitational fields $\mathrm{B}=\mathrm{B}_{12}+\mathrm{B}_{21}+\mathrm{B}_{30}$.
