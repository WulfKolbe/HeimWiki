Heim's theory, [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md) and [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md), on the other hand, exhibits this property.  It is also based on the idea of a geometrization of physics. Unlike the aforementioned unification theories, four-dimensional spacetime is extended to a six-dimensional space by adding a two-dimensional space. There is no “rolling up” of the transcoordinates; rather, they are mapped onto the spacetime accessible to us. The properties of the two transcoordinates are no longer of a spatial or temporal nature, but rather have an organizational character. 

The modified Einstein field equations turn out to be eigenvalue equations with the Christoffel symbols as variables. The metric used is extended such that several fundamental tensors interact in the sense of a polymetry. Such a richer metric makes it possible to obtain, from the solutions of the eigenvalue equations, a particle spectrum that provides not only the masses of the known elementary particles but also their associated quantum numbers. 

Heim’s theory is a semi-classical theory that is particularly well-suited for describing particle properties (in the corpuscular picture). Quantities associated with the wave picture, such as the coupling constants of the four fundamental interactions—which represent the probability amplitudes for the creation or annihilation of interacting particles—cannot be uniformly derived from it. 

One of the objectives of the present paper was to derive both the coupling constants and their geometrically representable interaction fields from a self-consistent mathematical formalism. To this end, it was necessary to extend the geometrization program of Heim’s theory or to find a completely different mathematical approach. 

It became apparent that an extension from a six-dimensional coordinate space to one with eight or twelve dimensions was necessary in order to obtain all known and as yet unknown interaction fields.  This fact already followed from the approach to the aforementioned unified particle theory in [1, Eq. 3d] and [Resch1989](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1989.md).
