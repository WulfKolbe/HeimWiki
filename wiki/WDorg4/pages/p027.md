The relationship between $\gamma_{\mathrm{ik}}^{(\mu \nu)}$; n, $\kappa_{\mathrm{im}}^{(\mu)}$; n, and $\kappa_{\mathrm{mk}}^{(\nu)}$; n on the Euclidean and non-Euclidean coordinates of $\mathrm{R}_{6}$ can take various forms and thus defines a specific metric. In order to better understand the  meaning and properties of the lattice kernel selector, which plays an  essential role in [Resch1984](../references/WDorg4_REF_Resch1984.md), it is explained using the  following metric. 

It is assumed that non-Euclidean coordinates $\mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}(\mathrm{k}=1,2 \ldots 6)$ depend on non-Euclidean coordinates $\mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{n}}^{\prime}(\mathrm{n}=1,2 \ldots 6)$: $y_{k}^{\prime}=y_{k}^{\prime}\left(x_{1}^{\prime}, x_{2}^{\prime} \ldots x_{6}^{\prime}\right)=y_{k}^{\prime}\left(x_{n}^{\prime}\right)_{n=1}^{6}$. At every point in $R_{6}\left(y_{1}^{\prime}, y_{2}^{\prime} \ldots y_{6}^{\prime}\right)$  a Euclidean tangent space with coordinates $\mathrm{x}_{1}, \mathrm{x}_{2} \ldots \mathrm{x}_{6}$ and thus $x_{m}=x_{m}\left(y_{1}^{\prime}\left(x_{1}^{\prime} \ldots x_{6}^{\prime}\right), y_{2}^{\prime}\left(x_{1}^{\prime} \ldots x_{6}^{\prime}\right) \ldots\right)=x_{m}\left(y_{k}^{\prime}\left(x_{n}^{\prime}\right)\right)_{k, n=1}^{6}$ can be constructed. The formation of the total differential yields, with $\mathrm{dx}_{\mathrm{m}}=\frac{\partial \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{m}}}{\partial \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}} \frac{\partial \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}}{\partial \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\prime}} \mathrm{dx}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\prime}$, the metric $\mathrm{ds}^{2}=\mathrm{dx}_{\mathrm{m}} \mathrm{dx}_{\mathrm{m}}=\left(\frac{\partial \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{m}}}{\partial \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}} \frac{\partial \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{k}}^{\prime}}{\partial \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\prime}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{m}}}{\partial \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{l}}^{\prime}} \frac{\partial \mathrm{y}_{\mathrm{l}}^{\prime}}{\partial \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime}}\right) \cdot \mathrm{dx}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\prime} \mathrm{dx}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{rs}} \mathrm{dx}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\prime} \mathrm{dx}_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime}$, where indices appearing more than once are summed.
