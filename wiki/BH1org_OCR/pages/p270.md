If $M_{1}(T)=M$ were assumed to be a point mass, then here, for $y_{k}$ the optical radius, according to (46), so that in this volume $3 V_{k}=4 \pi y_{k}^{3}$ can be written for a fictitious mass density $4 \pi y_{k}^{3} s=3 M$. According to (45a), this implies the redshift constant  $H_{k}^{2}=\pi e \gamma s$ and the optical radius 
$y_{k}=c / H_{k}=2 c y_{k} \sqrt{y_{k}} / \sqrt{3 e \gamma M} \quad$ or $\quad 4 c^{2} y_{k}=3 e \gamma M$. It is highly  probable that the radius $R$ of the volume of matter is given by the cosmological equation $R=2 y_{k}$, if a uniform distribution of matter is no longer assumed, i.e., $2 c^{2} R=3 e \gamma M$, which holds for the average  actual mass density $4 \pi R^{3} \sigma=3 M$ and for the redshift constant $H=\sqrt{\pi e \gamma \sigma}$, or for the optical radius $Y=c / H$. The numerical values resulting from $M \approx 3,8074 \cdot 10^{52} k g$ are $R=1,15249 \cdot 10^{26} m \approx 13,409 \cdot 10^{9}$, as well as 
$\sigma \approx 5,93783 \cdot 10^{-27} \mathrm{kgm}^{-3}$, as well as 
$H \approx 1,839367 \cdot 10^{-18} s^{-1} \hat{=} 56,691 k m s^{-1} M p c^{-1}$ and 
$Y \approx 1,629868 \cdot 10^{26} m \approx 17,24 \cdot 10^{9}$. These values are highly consistent with  the astrophysical data, so that the Hubble radius  should be interpreted as the radius of the region of matter cosmogony 
$R_{H}=R$.
