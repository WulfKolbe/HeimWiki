However, as the number of empirically discovered elementary particles increased, the number of fundamental units in this model also had to be constantly expanded. In this way, the complexity of the model continued to increase until, ultimately, the total number of empirically discovered elementary particles was matched by a nearly equal number of conceptualized fundamental units such as “quarks” or “gluons,” or “Higgs,” to explain the structure of elementary particles. In our view, this methodology would be entirely justified if the hypothetical fundamental units could be understood in a coherent manner and the fundamental symmetries of a relativistic basic dynamics of elementary particles were clearly recognizable. Obviously, this does not seem even remotely possible at present. On the other hand, it appears fundamentally impossible to distinguish elementary systems from complex systems without empirical evidence. If one were to assign an unlimited number of virtual degrees of freedom to the elementary particles, then quarks would become physically non-real quasiparticles that would have only a phenomenological significance.
