For the second metronical domain of validity $\psi_{k l}=\left(E-e^{-\lambda_{k l} \mu}\right)^{-1}$, the point of infinity occurs only for $\mu ; n=0$, but this case must be ruled out from the outset because $\mu ; n=0$ implies that no metron exists at all, which contradicts the concept of a metron. Furthermore, $\mu ; n=0$ would apply only in the first domain of validity, but not in the second. There are no singular points of infinity at the extrema either; for, due to $\mu=\alpha+i \beta$, the spatial decay selector $e^{-\lambda_{k l} \alpha} \neq E$ always precedes the complex exponential law,  which makes a point of infinity impossible even in the even-numbered real spectrum. In fact, for $\tau>0$, metronic eigenvalues extend throughout the entire $R_{3}$, although their amplitudes decay rapidly in space, but are also preserved for $\alpha ; n \geqq \beta ; n$ as a harmonic component due to $e^{-i \lambda_{k l} \beta}=\cos \left(\lambda_{k l} \beta\right)-i \sin \left(\lambda_{k l} \beta\right)$. For $\tau \rightarrow 0$, on the other hand, a series of singularities occurs.  The first of these occurs at $\varsigma^{2}=r^{2}=0$, but this must be excluded in principle due to $\varepsilon>0$. The remaining singularities are a consequence of $\tau \rightarrow 0$ and can be factored out because they occur only in the region of the real even eigenvalues $\cos \left(\lambda_{k l} y\right)=+1$ for $\varsigma^{2}>r^{2}$, so that in this region only the imaginary eigenvalues $\sin \left(\lambda_{k l} y\right)=1$ and the odd real eigenvalues count.  In contrast, a singular surface arising solely from $\tau \rightarrow 0$ lies at $\varsigma^{2}=r^{2} \neq 0$, which must in any case be excluded as a consequence of the third domain of validity. This singular surface divides the entire  domain of definition of the condensation into two parts, namely  $0 \leqq r^{2}<\zeta^{2}$ and $r^{2}>\zeta^{2}$, where, in $0 \leqq r^{2}<\zeta^{2}$, due to $y^{2}>0$, a complex oscillation law always applies, whose discrete eigenvalue distribution is separated by $r^{2}=\varsigma^{2}$ from a decay region $y^{2}>0$.
