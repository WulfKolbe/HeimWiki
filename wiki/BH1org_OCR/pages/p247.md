In this final case to be discussed—that of total $R_{6}$ hermitianity $d$ of complex spacetime condensates—the following again holds: $\mu=\alpha+i \beta$, though in contrast to the form $c$ with $i \beta=\sum_{k=4}^{6} \alpha_{k}()_{k}$, so that the formalism largely resembles that of $c$. However, in the  third domain of validity, $\varsigma^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}+\eta^{2}+c^{2} t^{2}$ grows independently of $\varepsilon$ or $\eta$ constantly with $t$, that is, the $d$ structure gives rise to a structure that satisfies an oscillation law and expands in time at the speed of light within $R_{3}$. Now, the complex forms  $c$ or $d$ can be interpreted as couplings of the imaginary forms $a$ or $b$ to a  metric $R_{3}$ structure. According to (24), $b$ must always be interpreted as a photon due to the zero-line character of its worldlines, which means that $d$ must be interpreted as a spatial condensation that is coupled in some way to a photonic (i.e., electromagnetic) field, specifically as a consequence of the time dimension involved in the condensation process. 

Since the photon fields of the $b$ hermeticism according to (24) are of an electromagnetic nature and, in the $d$ hermeticism, a $b$ structure appears to be coupled to a $c$ term, the $d$ terms are ponderable $M q$, from which electromagnetic static fields emanate, so that here ponderable terms become source fields of $b$ structures. In the case of a $d$ hermetry, the ponderable $R_{3}$-structure is in a structural state that is identical to the phenomenological electric charge field, which appears as a source and sink field $Q_{ \pm} \neq 0$ in antisymmetry $Q_{+}=-Q_{-}$ and is phenomenologically and empirically described by its electromagnetic field via ( $d_{1}$ ). Accordingly, $d$ hermetics describe ponderable electrically charged $M q$.
