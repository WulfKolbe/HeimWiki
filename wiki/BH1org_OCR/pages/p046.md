The field of the non-Hermitian fundamental tensor 
$g_{i k}\left(x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}, x_{4}\right)$ describes an invariant metric state of the $R_{4}$ such that the $\Gamma_{k m}^{i}$ represent the deviations of this metric structural state from the pseudo-Euclidean $C$ system of the $x^{k}$coordinates, although these $\Gamma_{k m}^{i}$ behave only with respect to regular affinities with unitary transformation matrices such as mixed-variant tensor components of the third order. Relation (1) holds in the continuum of the macroscopic region, but discontinuity (2)  directly points to the existence of a continuous correspondence into  the microscopic region. The macromare field continuum of the fundamental tensor $g_{i k}$ can always be corrected in $R_{4}$ such that the $\Gamma_{k m}^{i}$, as macromare quantities, extend into the micro-range and can be transformed there into functions $\varphi_{k m}^{i}\left(x_{l} \ldots x_{4}\right) \neq \varphi_{m k}^{i}$ * that also behave, with respect to regular affinities, like mixed-variant tensor components of the third order. While the $\Gamma_{k m}^{i}$ need not be convergent, for the $\varphi_{k m}^{i}$ convergence can nevertheless be achieved, so that for a spacetime domain integral of the  form $J_{k m}^{i}=\int_{\Omega} \varphi_{k m}^{i} \varphi_{m k}^{i}{ }^{*} d \Omega<\infty \quad$ holds, which enables the normalization $J_{k m}^{i}=1$. However, this convergence is generally lost during the  transition $\varphi_{k m}^{i} \rightarrow \Gamma_{k m}^{i}$ according to the correspondence principle in the macroscopic continuum.
