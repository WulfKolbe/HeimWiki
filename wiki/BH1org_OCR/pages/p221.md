If the Hermitian forms $a$ through $d$ are to be examined in terms of their physical interpretation, then it suffices to consider them in the second validity range, which is still submicrometric; for the microphysical processes of an atomistic experiment are always present in the third, micrometric validity range. However, the approximation in the case of high metron numbers results in (20) $\Lambda_{k m} \ll 1$ and $\Lambda_{k m} \approx 0$, respectively. 

First, we shall examine the two imaginary forms of condensation of the hermetries $a$ and $b$. Here, $\mu=-\mu^{*}$ holds in any case, which expresses the imaginary character. Under the approximation assumption of high metron numbers, the first step is to determine the $C_{k m}=$ const from (20), which must be done individually for each Hermetry form. If the imaginary self-condensations $a$—which are to be examined first—are present, then the only possibilities for the indexing are $k=5$ and $k=6$, while the aggregate $\mu$ of the lattice selectors becomes $\mu=i \sqrt{\tau}\left(()_{5}+()_{6}\right)$. Furthermore, the condenser aggregate $\psi_{k m}$ is hermitian and convergent in the sense of an abstract function space or a metronomic function space. If this convergence property is used, then in the case $a$, it immediately follows that the constants are $C_{k m}=-1$. In the third domain of validity, $\mu ; n \rightarrow \varsigma$ is equal to $-\varsigma^{2}=x_{5}^{2}+x_{6}^{2}$, that is, if a forms condensation stages,  then $\lambda \cdot \varsigma$ must form point spectra, and this imposes a lower bound $\underline{\varepsilon}>0$ on $\varsigma$; since for $\underline{\varepsilon}=0$, a line spectrum would exist as a  continuum, which contradicts the nature of the condensation steps. Therefore, in the transition region of the antihermetic $R_{4}$, in the case of $a$, there must be a discontinuity $\varsigma \geqq \underline{\varepsilon}=$ const $>0$, possibly in the sense of a natural constant.
