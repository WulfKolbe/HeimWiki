However, it is obvious that $R_{\infty}^{*}$ is achieved by both $|n| \rightarrow \infty$ and $| \pm i n| \rightarrow \infty$  , which results in $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{12}=i n, \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{13}=-i n$, i.e., $\left|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mu}\right| \rightarrow \infty$ for $11 \leqq \mu \leqq 13$. In this second case, the volume element is $\Delta \underline{V}_{n}^{*} \sim(b)^{ \pm i n / 2}$. The relationship for this volume element can also be derived in an appropriate form from the element $\Delta V_{m}^{*}$ of a $m$-dimensional Hermitian space, if, for the dimensionless components $y_{i}^{2}$, $\left\langle y_{1}^{2}, \ldots, y_{\underline{n}}^{2}\right\rangle$ , for simplicity, $y_{i}^{2}=y^{2}$ is set to $\underline{n}$ for all components, where $\underline{n}$ are integers, and in addition to $c_{1,2}$ a further factor $c_{3}$ is defined. For the volume element, the following then applies: $\Delta V_{m}^{*} \sim\left(c_{3}\right)^{\underline{i n}} \cdot b_{j}^{|\underline{n}|}=\left(c_{3}\right)^{\underline{i n}} \cdot\left(y^{2}\right) \underline{\mid \underline{n}} \mid=\left[\left(c_{3}\right)^{i} \cdot y^{2}\right]^{\underline{n}}$, whereby a certain degree of arbitrariness regarding the power initially appears. To avoid  this arbitrariness, it is proposed to use the $1 \leqq s \leqq 4$ solutions of $x^{2}= \pm a$ with $a>0$, i.e., $x_{1,2}= \pm \sqrt{a}, x_{3,4}= \pm i \sqrt{a}$ as a starting point, whose reciprocals $1 / x_{s}$ represent a part of $\alpha_{i}$ for $a \rightarrow o$. In the previous section, $\left|\alpha_{\mu}\right| \rightarrow \infty, \alpha_{n} \rightarrow \infty$ was specified for $n \rightarrow \infty$. For  reasons of symmetry, the index interval of $\alpha_{i}$ must be limited to $1 \leqq i \leqq 12$ , so that of the possible $\alpha_{i}$, only $\alpha_{11}$ and $\alpha_{12}$  are effective.
