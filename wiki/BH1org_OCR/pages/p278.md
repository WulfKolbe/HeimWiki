If $K$ is an algebraic field and $f(w)$ is a polynomial (with no multiple roots), then $E$ is the splitting field of $f(w)$, since the following holds for the polynomial $f(w)=\left(w-w_{1}\right)\left(w-w_{2}\right) \cdot \ldots \cdot\left(w-w_{n}\right)$. In this polynomial of degree $n$, the elements $w_{k}$ with  $1 \leqq k \leqq n$ are the generators of $E$. Let $G$ be the group of automorphisms of $E$ over $K$. Then every $\sigma \in G$ can be written in terms of the $w_{k}$, since each of these elements permutes the $w_{k}$, so that $G$ can be regarded as a permutation group. Since only the indices of the $w_{k}$ in $f$ are permuted by $G$ and the degree $n$ is given, $G$ is isogenic with the Galois group $S_{n}$ of the polynomial $f$. This group is therefore the permutation group of $n$ elements, so that $\left|S_{n}\right|=n$ is the number of elements of $S_{n}$. In the case of the cosmological relations (37) and (37a), transformed to $w^{7}-w-q=0$, together with (15) and (15a), the Galois group $S_{7}$—consisting of $\left|S_{7}\right|=7!$ elements—applies in each case. 

At time $t=0$, $w=\eta$ is given by (47) and $q=a$, that is, $f(\eta)=\eta^{7}-\eta-a=0$. Due to $n=1$, this relation has, in addition to the three real solutions, four complex solutions that are conjugate to one another, and this is because, in the representation $f(\eta)=\prod_{k=1}^{7}\left(\eta-w_{k}\right)=\eta^{7}-\eta-a=0 \quad$ the condition $\sum_{k=1}^{7} w_{k}=1$ is satisfied due to the appearance of $-\eta$ in $f(\eta)$. If the  $w_{k}$ for $k \leqq 3$ refer to the three real zeros, then  $\left(w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}\right)^{*}=w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}$ due to the fact that $w_{k}=w_{k}^{*}$ is real, which  necessarily also holds for the complex zeros $k>3$ despite $w_{k} \neq w_{k}^{*}$  implies the condition $\left(\sum_{k=4}^{7} w_{k}\right)^{*}=\sum_{k=4}^{7} w_{k}$; for the  sum of $w_{k}$ is real. However, the sum is real for $k>3$ only if, for complex zeros $w_{k} \neq w_{k}^{*}$, only if $w_{6}=w_{4}^{*}$ and $w_{7}=w_{5}^{*}$ hold. If, for the reduction, $w_{k}=\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{k}^{*}$ is set to $k \leqq 3$ and $\beta_{i} \neq \beta_{i}^{*}$ for $i=1$ or $i=2$, then $w_{4}=\beta_{1}, w_{5}=\beta_{2}$ would apply, but $w_{6}=\beta_{1}^{*}$ and $w_{7}=\beta_{2}^{*}$.
