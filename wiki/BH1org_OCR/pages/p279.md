If there is a group $A$ that is isomorphic to a group of square matrices, then, as is well known, this group of square matrices is called a representation of $A$, whose order is the dimension of this representation; since the matrices transform a vector space that serves as the representation space. A permutation group $S_{n}$ permutes the $k \leqq n$ basis vectors $b_{k}$ of a representation space. Thus, each permutation of these elements $k \leqq n$ is assigned a linear transformation that permutes the $b_{k}$  as basis vectors of the $n$-dimensional representative vector space. If, for example, the group element had the form $\downarrow \begin{aligned} & 123 \ldots n \\ & 312 \ldots\end{aligned}$,  then this would mean that $b_{1}=(1,0,0 \ldots)$ is transformed into $b_{3}=(0,0,1 \ldots), b_{2} \rightarrow b_{1}$, and so on. The $b_{k}$, arranged in the form $b_{1}, b_{3}, \ldots$, form the columns of a square matrix of type $n$, which is identical to the matrix rank. The $n!$ group elements are thus assigned to the $n!$ matrices of rank $n$. In this way, a (natural) representation of dimension $n$ is obtained.
