The simple metron differential $\boldsymbol{\partial} \varphi=f(n)$ can be interpreted in a straightforward manner due to its formation $\partial \varphi=\varphi(n)-\varphi(n-1)$. Apparently, $f$ specifies the function numbers of $\varphi$ that lie between two metronic function values when the argument changes by the value 1. The steeper the increase in $\varphi$, the larger $f$ must be, and vice versa, so that $\partial \varphi>0$ indicates that $\varphi$ increases as $n$ , while $\varphi$ decreases as $n$ increases, provided that $\partial \varphi<0$ holds.  In the case of $\partial \varphi=0$, on the other hand, there must be an extreme value that is a maximum when $\partial^{2} \varphi<0$, but becomes a minimum for $\partial^{2} \varphi>0$. This is because, by definition, the second metron differential must describe the change in the direction of slope of a metronic function, since the first metron differential represents this slope. Thus, if $\partial^{2} \varphi=0$ holds, this means that at this point the direction of the slope of $\varphi$ changes in the sense of an inflection point. This fact is summarized in the system
