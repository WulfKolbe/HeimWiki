Could $Z_{+}=z_{, \alpha, \varepsilon}$ be postulated as the second partial derivative,  where the intervals $0 \leqq \alpha \leqq 2 \pi$ apply to the azimuth and $0 \leqq \varepsilon \leqq \pi / 2$  to the elevation angle. Thus, $9 \hbar z_{, \alpha, \varepsilon}=2 R_{-} Q_{ \pm}^{2}$ must be integrated in the form  $9 \hbar z=2 R_{-} \int_{0}^{2 \pi} \int_{0}^{\pi / 2} Q_{ \pm}^{2} d \varepsilon d \alpha$. Here, $Q_{ \pm}=$ can always be made constant with respect to $\alpha$ and $\varepsilon$ , so that for the integration, $9 \hbar z=2 \pi^{2} Q_{ \pm}^{2} R_{-}$ results as the determining equation for $z\left(Q_{ \pm}\right)$. 

It is $z\left(Q_{ \pm}\right) \sim Q_{ \pm}^{2}$, but on the other hand, $w^{2}=\left(\underline{M}_{c}^{4}-\underline{M}_{d}^{4}\right) / \underline{M}_{d}^{4}=w^{2}$ must also be $w\left(Q_{ \pm}\right)$, so that a relationship $w(z)$ exists. To possibly determine this relationship, the following approach is suggested: Using the unit length $s_{0}=1[m]$, the dimensionless quantities $w$ and $z$  can be dimensioned as distances $G=w s_{0}$ and $H=z s_{0}$,  so that, using unit vectors $\vec{H}_{0}^{2}=\vec{G}_{0}^{2}=1$, orientations relative to $\vec{H}$ and  $\vec{G}$ become possible. From $\vec{H}$ and $\vec{G}$, the $(x, y)$-plane, the orientations $\vec{e}_{x}^{2}=\vec{e}_{y}^{2}=1$ and $\vec{x}=\vec{e}_{x} x$, or $\vec{y}=\vec{e}_{y} y$ , using $\vec{e}_{x} \perp \vec{e}_{y}$, it is possible to construct a triangle $A B C$ whose vertex $A \hat{=} 0 \mathrm{im}$ is the origin. The following then holds for the sides: $\overline{A B}=\vec{x} \quad$, as well as $\overline{B C}=\vec{G} \quad$ and $\quad \overline{A C}=\vec{H} \quad$ with $\quad \vec{G} \| \vec{y} \perp \vec{x}$. If  `\varphi=\Varangle(\vec{x}, \vec{H})`, i.e., `\Varangle(\vec{G}, \vec{H})=\pi / 2-\varphi`, then $(\vec{G} / \vec{H})^{2}=w^{2} / z^{2}$ and $(\vec{G} / \vec{H})^{2}=\sin ^{2} \varphi$ would be, that is, $w^{2}=z^{2} \sin ^{2} \varphi$.  On the other hand, if $H$ is taken as the radius of a circle inscribed in a regular polygon with $N \geqq 3$ sides, then in radians, the central angle of a sector is $2 \pi / N$ and the length of a side is $2 H \sin \alpha$, if $\alpha=\pi / N$ is half the central angle. Let us now hypothetically postulate that such an angle $\alpha$ behaves with respect to $\varphi$ such that $\alpha \varphi=1 / z$; for, by virtue of $z \sim Q_{ \pm}^{2}$, $\varphi$ must must be equal to $z$ and $\alpha$. Substituting $\alpha=\pi / N$ then yields the hypothesis $\varphi \pi z=N \geqq 3$, which, when substituted, yields the relationship $w(z)=z \sin \left(\frac{N}{\pi z}\right)$.
