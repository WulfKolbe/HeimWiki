From equation (25), namely $r=\frac{2 n_{r}+1}{2 n_{\varsigma}+1} \varsigma_{(a \beta)}$, after squaring $\varsigma^{2}{ }_{(a \beta)}=\varepsilon^{2}+\eta^{2}$, we obtain $r^{2}-\left(\frac{2 n_{r}+1}{2 n_{\varsigma}+1}\right)^{2} \varepsilon^{2}=$  $=\left(\frac{2 n_{r}+1}{2 n_{\varsigma}+1}\right)^{2} \eta^{2}$. The two quantum numbers describe the behavior of the metronic eigenvalues in the range $0 \leqq r^{2}<\varsigma^{2}{ }_{(a \beta)}$, where $n_{r}<n_{\varsigma}$ must always remain constant. For $r=\varepsilon$—that is, on the singular surface in the case $\eta \neq 0$—$\eta / r= \pm 2\left(2 n_{r}+1\right)-1 \sqrt{\left(n_{\varsigma}+n_{r}+1\right) \cdot\left(n_{\varsigma}-n_{r}\right)}$ is then formed. If $j>0$ is any number that determines the magnitude $\eta>0$ in the system of quantum numbers, then $n_{r}=n_{\varsigma}-j$ be set, because $n_{\varsigma}>n_{r}$, as the principal quantum number, determines the actual limit of the spatial condensation with respect to the metronic eigenvalue range. For this reason, $n_{\varsigma}=n$ and $n_{r}=n-j$ can be set, which results in $\eta / r= \pm 2(2 n+1-2 j)^{-1} \sqrt{j(2 n+1-j}= \pm 2 f$  with $f(n, j)=(2 n+1-2 j)^{-1} \sqrt{j(2 n+1-j)}$. Undoubtedly, $f<1$ holds for all $j<n$, but $f_{\max }=f(1,1)$ for $n=j=1$, so that $\eta / r= \pm 2 f$ must always remain $|\eta / r|<2 \sqrt{2}$. What is essential is the interpretation of the quantities acting in $R_{3}$, which are, in themselves, imaginary. Both $\eta$ and $r$ in $\eta / r= \pm 2 f$ are, due to the complementarity $r=\varepsilon$, certainly characteristic $R_{3}$ distances of the field  of the neutrocorpuscule in question, which are independent of the relative state of motion in $R_{3}$. In addition to the Compton wavelength—which stems from quantum duality—in the sense of a metaphorical radius $r_{0}=\lambda_{C} / 2$ of the relevant matter field quantum $M q$  with $m c \lambda_{C}=h$ (not to be understood as a circular wave), there are, according to (12) and (14) through (14c), the distances characterizing the $G$ field: the lower reality bound $R_{-}$, the $G$ field wavelength $\Lambda=2 \varsigma$, and the upper reality bound $R_{+}$. Here, $\varsigma$ no longer  denotes the term in $y$ for $\tau \rightarrow 0$, but is used in the sense of (12). For  these four characteristic distances, the abbreviations  $(I)=R_{-}$, as well as $(I I)=\lambda_{C} / 2$, $(I I I)=\varsigma=\Lambda / 2$, and  $(I V)=R_{+}$.
