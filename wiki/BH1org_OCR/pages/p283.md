If, in the case of $D_{j}$, the diameters are related to one another, then in the simplest case the ratios $g_{j}=D_{j} / D_{1}$ are dimensionless numbers. If, as in IV., 4., the numerical values $D_{1}=D_{f}, D_{2}=D_{m}$ and $D_{3}=D_{p}$ are assigned to the $D_{j}$ , then the numerical values $g_{1}=1$, $g_{2}=1,1699745$, and $g_{3}=4,06763212$ result for these ratios. In addition to these three real solutions, $\eta^{7}-\eta-a=0$ must also have $4 \leqq k \leqq 7$ complex solutions, so that, in the most primitive case, the imaginary number $+i=\frac{1}{-i}$ must be included as an element of the set. The resulting set is then 
$U^{\prime}=\left\{H_{1}, H_{2}, H_{3}, i\right\}$, so that $U^{\prime \prime}=\left\{\left\{H_{1}, H_{2}, H_{3}\right\},\{i,-i\},\{0\}\right\}$ can be chosen to obtain $K=\{3 ; 2 ; 1\}$. Due to the symmetry imposed by $U^{\prime \prime}$, the reciprocal values of the elements of $U$ or $U^{\prime}$ are exponents of the elementary lengths $\delta l=\delta s_{0}=\sqrt{\tau}$ or $\delta l=i \delta s_{0}$. The volumes formed in this way reveal the dimensions of the Hermitian spaces. With

Thus, $\Delta x_{1} \Delta x_{2} \Delta x_{3} \sim(\delta l)^{3}$ holds, as do $\Delta x_{4} \sim \delta l$ and $\Delta x_{5} \Delta x_{6} \sim(\delta l)^{2}$.  Accordingly, for $U^{\prime \prime}$, one would then assume a proportionality to $\Delta x_{7}$ and $\Delta x_{8}$ a proportionality to $(\alpha \delta l)^{ \pm i}$, where $\alpha$ is a dimensionless factor yet to be determined.
