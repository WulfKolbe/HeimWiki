In his later years, Einstein attempted to unify electromagnetism with gravity using a mathematical theory, but was unsuccessful. 

Heim’s theory, now available in two volumes, is also based on the General Theory of Relativity (GTR), but it breaks new ground and therefore differs significantly from previous theories. 

The basic idea of Heim’s theory is the representation of physical fundamental units (fundamental particles) through geometric quantities. Its essential features are: 
1. The existence of a six-dimensional space $\left(R_{6}\right)$, which is a subspace of a twelve-dimensional space ($R_{12}$). The physically accessible four-dimensional spacetime $\left(R_{4}\right)$ is embedded in $R_{6}$. The  transcoordinates $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$ are of an imaginary nature, and their  direction is reversible. 
2. Quantization of multidimensional space due to an indistinguishable geometric unit of area $\tau$, which corresponds approximately to the square of the Planck length. 
3. Novel cosmology and the resulting Hermitian manifold geometry. The Hermitian fundamental tensor lying in $R_{6}$ is compositely composed of the non-Hermitian fundamental tensors describing the manifold geometry. 
4. Geometrization of elementary particles; physical interpretation of geometric terms. In the micrometer range, the energy-momentum tensor can be set proportional to a geometric quantity analogous to Christoffel symbols. Purely geometric eigenvalue equations are thus formed. 
5. The only non-derivable empirical natural constants used throughout the entire theory are $\gamma, \hbar, \varepsilon_{0}$ and $\mu_{0}$. 
6. Description of an elementary particle using geometric quantities that cyclically change their structure in the sense of an internal dynamic.
7. Derivation of the symmetry sets strictly valid for elementary particles and determination of their rest masses.
