According to W. DRÖSCHER, the considerations proposed in IV., 4. can be extended in yet another direction, which will be discussed below. The remarks made regarding Vol. II therefore also apply to this final section. 

The construction of $R_{6}$ according to (4) ultimately goes back to the algebraic structure of the $R_{4}$relations (3) and their symmetries (3a) and (3b), and is the result of an energetic analysis (3c). On the other hand, this led to the general dimensional law  (3d), according to which, in addition to $R_{4} \rightarrow R_{6}$, $R_{6} \rightarrow R_{12}$, i.e., $R_{4} \subset R_{6} \subset R_{12}$, is also possible. Since there can only be three real coordinates,  $x_{7}, \ldots, x_{12}$ and $x_{4}, \ldots, x_{6}$ are imaginary. Due to (3c), energetic considerations can only be carried out in $R_{6} \subset R_{12}$; energies in the range of $x_{7}, \ldots, x_{12}$ no longer seem to be possible. In contrast, volumes can exist in $R_{12}$. If the elementary lengths of the $R_{12}$ are to be determined, it first becomes clear that the action of the lattice selectors of the $R_{6}$ is limited to only one metron $C_{k} ; 1=\alpha_{k}$ such that the elementary lengths $\delta s_{0}=\sqrt{\tau}$ for $k \leqq 3$ and $i \delta s_{0}$ for $k>3$,  which are defined as $\delta s_{0}$ in the $R_{3}$ and $i \delta s_{0}$ in a $V_{3}\left(x_{4}, x_{5}, x_{6}\right)$, respectively $3!=6$  without changing their identity, since $\left|i \delta s_{0}\right|=\delta s_{0}$. The symmetry group of these permutations is $S_{3}$ in both $R_{3}$ and $V_{3}$, that is, in $R_{6}$ it is $S_{3} \times S_{3}$, because these permutations can be performed independently of one another.  From the relations used in IV., 4., the $t$ elementary lengths $\delta s_{k}, \delta s^{\prime}{ }_{k}$ can be determined, which describe the coordinates $x_{i}=x_{i}\left(\delta s_{k}, \delta s_{k}^{\prime}\right)$. If, at the same time, symmetry groups $S_{u} \ldots S_{w}$ can be determined from these relationships, then these symmetries certainly also describe those of coordinate permutations, because in nature, again for reasons of symmetry, symmetries of the same type are always used.
