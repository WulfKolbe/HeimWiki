In (1), $T_{i k}$, as a purely phenomenological tensor, can still be supplemented by $g_{i k}$ because these quantities are not only the components of the metric fundamental tensor but, due to (1b), can also be interpreted as components of a general tensorial interaction potential. For their matrix trace, $g_{. k}^{k .}=4 \quad \mathrm{im} \quad R_{4} \quad$ holds, and for the trace of the phenomenological tensor, $g^{i k} T_{i k}=T_{k}^{k}=T$ holds. Thus, the matrix trace of (1) is  $R \sim-T$, so that (1) can also be written in the form $R_{i k} \sim T_{i k}-\frac{1}{2} g_{i k} T=W_{i k}$, where the $W_{i k}$ are likewise components of an energy density tensor (in extended form). These energies refer to $R_{3}$ volumes and are themselves the temporal changes of actions $\omega_{i k}$. On the other hand, it is well known that the metric determinant—that is, the tensor scheme $g=\left|g_{i k}\right|_{4}<0$ written as a determinant— since $d s^{2}$ is an indefinite homogeneous quadratic differential form, so that for the functional determinant, $w=\sqrt{-g}$ must be set.
