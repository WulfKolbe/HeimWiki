On the other hand, by assumption, $y=f(x)$ is supposed to be a continuous function, and even $\tau>0$ cannot alter this continuity.  According to Equation (15a), the boundedness of the elements $\tau$ depends solely on the metric properties of $R_{p}$, which, however, in the present case are determined solely by the path $f(x)$ in $R_{2}$. The  domain of $f$ is the Euclidean $R_{2}$, i.e., the geodetic coordinates are Cartesian, and the region under discussion is bounded by $a \leqq x \leqq b$, as well as by 
$y_{a}=y(a), y_{b}=y(b)$ and the curve $y=f(x)$. If the boundary  of a metron is determined by the metric properties of the integral surface, then all metrons must be equal with respect to their metric boundaries if $F$ is to form a metric unit and $n$ is to be an integer. This means that, in the present case, the area $\tau>0$ is always defined by two ordinates $y_{\nu}$ and $y_{\nu-1}$  and the graph of the function $f(x)$ between $v-1$ and $v$,  so that for 
$\Delta F_{v}=\tilde{y}_{v} \Delta x_{v}=\int_{x_{v-1}}^{x_{v}} f d x$, the concept of the infinitesimal integral becomes applicable. Thus, $F=\lim _{\Delta F \rightarrow \tau} \sum_{v=1}^{n} \tilde{y}_{v} \Delta x_{v}=n \tau$ becomes $n \tau=\sum_{v=1}^{n} \int_{x_{v-1}}^{x_{v}} f d x$.  According to the integral concept, $\sum_{v=1}^{n} \int_{x_{v-1}}^{x_{v}}=\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{n}}$ always holds, thereby satisfying the  metric requirement that all $\tau$ from $F$ are continuously connected to one another, due to the continuity of $f$. Thus, 
$\sum_{v=1}^{n} \int_{x_{v-1}}^{x_{v}} f d x=\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{n}} f d x=F_{n}-F_{0}$ holds with $F_{n}=F\left(x_{n}\right)$ and  $F_{0}=F\left(x_{0}\right)=C$. Substituting this into $\lim _{\Delta F_{v} \rightarrow \tau} \sum_{v=1}^{n} \tilde{y}_{v} \Delta x_{v}=n \tau$ yields  $F\left(x_{n}\right)=n \tau+C$, where $C$ depends only on $x_{0}$. Because of this relationship, and since $F=\int f d x$ is derived from the function $f$, $x_{n}$ can be expressed as a number-theoretic function from $F\left(x_{n}\right)=n \tau+C$ to $x_{n}=x(n)$
