$S_{3}\left(x_{6}\right)$, where the imaginary coordinate of $R_{6}$ is specified after the symbol of the respective real space, which identifies the mapped complex vector space. These three independent real spaces thus span a $R_{9} \hat{=} R_{9}\left(T_{3}, G_{3}, S_{3}\right)$ in such a way that the physical $R_{3}$ must also be understood as a manifestation space of possible $R_{9}$ structures. However, since there can be neither stable gravitational orbits in the macroscopic realm nor stable atomic electron shells in the microscopic realm for $p>3$ real-world dimensions (which is determined by the unique $R_{6}$ signature), the $R_{9}$ must be interpreted either as an auxiliary construction analogous to $R_{+4}$ or, possibly, as a  frame of reference prior to the cosmogony of matter. If  this latter interpretation is accepted, the question arises as to whether  the dimensional number $n=9$ of the reference space $R_{n}$ can be successively extended to  any $n>9$, or whether this process can be clearly limited. 
According to the dimensional law (3d), there is no $R_{n}$ for which a $R_{9}$ exists, and likewise, for $n=9$, there is no value $N$ that satisfies this law. On the other hand, in $R_{12}$, the coordinates of the subspace $R_{6}$ can be described, due to the isomorphism of the $\operatorname{SU}(2)$ with respect to the $O(3)$ in the real form of a $R_{9}$. The remaining imaginary coordinates $x_{7}=-x_{7}^{*}$ through $x_{12}=-x_{12}^{*}$ in turn span, with the six real dimensions of the $R_{9}$, spanning, in turn, six additional complex vector planes for which the same isomorphism must hold, so that the $R_{12}$ corresponds, as an equivalent, to a $R_{21}$. Even in the case of the $R_{21}$, neither $n=21$ nor $N=21$ are compatible with (3d), so it can be concluded that both the $R_{9}$ and the $R_{21}$ are to be regarded merely as real equivalents $R_{6} \widehat{=} R_{9}$ and  $R_{12} \hat{=} R_{21}$, which can be used as auxiliary constructions of a real nature, because the $S U(2)$ of a complex two-dimensional vector space is isomorphic to the $O(3)$ of a real  three-dimensional space. It is highly probable that these equivalents, $R_{9}$ or $R_{21}$, have neither cosmological nor cosmogonic significance.
