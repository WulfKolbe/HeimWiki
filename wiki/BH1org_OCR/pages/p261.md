If these diameters are denoted by $d$, then the diameters in the relevant unit of measurement are $d_{f}=0,17617186, d_{m}=0,612679705$ and $d_{p}=0,716602318$, which would require supplementing (48) and (48a). 

To solve the equation $w^{7}-w=q$ for $n>1$ an analysis of the plane curve $y(x)=x^{7}-x-q$ can be performed by analogy to (47), such that $w^{7}-w=q$ the zero equation $y(w)=0$ is $x=w$. It turns out that for $n=1$ there are indeed three real zeros, but for all $n>1$ there is only one such zero. With regard to the update toward $n>1$, this uniqueness means that for all $n>1$ there is only one Metron, but a triple update occurs, the steps of which may be separated by a chronon in each case. 

To analyze this update, (37) and (37a) with $\pi D^{2}=n \tau$ are transformed into the form $w^{7}-w= \pm q$ , in which—analogous to (47)—only the positive branch is used, since only this branch yields a real positive solution for $w=1$. The relation $w^{7}-w-q=0$ can also be interpreted as the zero equation of the plane curve $y(x)=x^{7}-x-q$ for $x=w$, i.e., $y(w)=0$.  These zeros can be determined using Newton’s method, according to which $\tilde{x}=x-\left(x^{7}-x-q\right)\left(7 x^{6}-1\right)^{-1}$ must be set equal to $q=a^{3} \sqrt{n}$. Using the substitution $E u \sqrt{\pi n}=e D^{2}$  we obtain $4 w^{4}=\sqrt[3]{6 n / \pi} \sqrt{u}(u-1)^{-1}$, or with $4 w^{4} A=\sqrt[3]{6 n / \pi}$, the simplified form $(u-1)^{2}=u A^{2}$ follows. If $S=1+A^{2} / 2$ is introduced as a further abbreviation, then the two solutions yield the quadratic relationship $u_{1,2}=S\left(1 \pm \sqrt{1-1 / S^{2}}\right)$. If $e C^{2}=E \sqrt{\pi n}$ is introduced, then $u=D / C$ follows, that is, $u_{1,2}=D_{ \pm} / C$  or $D_{ \pm}=S\left(1 \pm \sqrt{1-1 / S^{2}}\right) C$, so that $D_{+}=D$ and $D_{-}=d$ hold.  If $\alpha>0$ is a natural number and $\vartheta$ denotes the chrono according to (35a), then a historical development of $D(n)$ and $d(n)$  in time intervals of $\alpha v$ certainly satisfy the two relations  $D_{ \pm}(n)=S\left(1 \pm \sqrt{1-1 / S^{2}}\right) C$.
