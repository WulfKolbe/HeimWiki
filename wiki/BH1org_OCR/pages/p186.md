This describes the properties of a metronic elementary cell, allowing us to make a statement about the possible spin orientations of the metrons bounding the $2 L_{2}=30$ in such a cell. Since, due to $\operatorname{ROT}_{6} \bar{\phi}=-\left(\operatorname{ROT}_{6} \bar{\phi}\right)^{\times}$, ${ }^{2} \bar{P}=-{ }^{2} \bar{P}^{x}$ is always true as well and, consequently, the metron tensor is anti-Hermitian, but the $2 L_{2}=30$ nonzero components describe the spin orientations of those metrons which bound the metronic elementary cell $\Delta$ of the $R_{6}$, all metronic spins within such a closed system must, in principle, compensate each other in pairs. By nature, there are many possibilities for this, but due to the assumed  emptiness of the reference world, the absence of metric structures must be required, which in turn necessitates complete isotropy of both the empty tensor and the superimposed spin orientation of this metronic  world tensor. In addition to this isotropy requirement, however, a structural potency must also be assumed in the empty world; for in fact, the world is not empty, and the existing structures must have emerged from an empty world due to an existing structural potency. The isotropy requirement of the hyperstructure is already satisfied by the Euclidean infinitesimal metric in the case of the absence of structure.
