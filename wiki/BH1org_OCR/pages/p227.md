Since $\underline{\varphi}=d \psi / d \xi$, i.e., $d \psi / d \xi=2 \lambda f(1+f)^{-1}$ holds, it is also possible to obtain $\psi$ by integration. $g_{55} g_{66}=B\left(1+A e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2} \xi}\right)^{-4}$, where $\xi$ is the imaginary argument.  The hermetic structure described in this way can also be interpreted in this manner. One can assume that this structure, which is  described by $\psi=\ln \left(g_{55} g_{66}\right)$ , exists as a field function over the antihermetic argument domain $R_{4}$ and describes a field in a Euclidean or pseudo-Euclidean space. In this case, $\psi(\xi)=\psi\left(x_{5}, x_{6}\right)$ would depend on the transcoordinates, and because of this interpretation, $g_{i k} \rightarrow \delta_{i k}$ must be set such that the metric $d s^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{6} d x_{k}^{2}=d r^{2}+d \xi^{2}$ is pseudo-Euclidean if $d r^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{4} d x_{k}^{2}$ is the metric of the pseudo-Euclidean approximation $R_{4}$. In general, $\psi=\psi\left(x_{5}, x_{6}\right)$ holds and thus defines  states beyond $R_{4}$, yet these latent processes directly intervene in the $R_{4}$ when $\psi$ describes processes that are metrically such that $\xi$ is directly determined by $r$.
