With $\beta \approx 2 \cdot 10^{-4}$, a merger with $\delta s_{0}^{\prime} \approx \delta s_{0}$ could become possible if $\delta s_{0}^{\prime} \leqq \delta s_{0}$, that is, if $\left(M_{0} / M\right)^{4}>0$ is assumed, and thus $M_{0}>0$ is also assumed. It is $\delta s_{0}^{\prime}(t)$ a function of the world age, for which $\delta s_{0}^{\prime}\left(t_{i}\right)>\delta s_{0}^{\prime}\left(t_{k}\right)$ holds for $t_{i}<t_{k}$. On the other hand, $\delta s_{0}^{\prime} \leqq \delta s_{0}$ is the prerequisite for $M_{0}>0$, so that the time of the cosmogony of matter is determined by $\delta s_{0}^{\prime}=\delta s_{0}$. If $\delta s_{1}$ is a spherical diameter that cannot be undershot, then $\delta s_{1} \pi / 2$ the maximum distance on this surface; that is, if one approaches in a straight line,  the approach is transformed into a curved path (ultimately  a semicircle), so that it is not $\delta s_{1}$, but rather $\delta s_{1} \pi / 2$ is a measure of the distance traveled. In the Euclidean sense, $\delta s_{0}$ cannot be undercut. It is either $\beta / \delta s^{\prime}=\beta / \delta s_{0} \pm \beta / \delta l_{v}=\beta\left(\delta l_{v} \pm \delta s_{0}\right) /\left(\delta s_{0} \delta l_{v}\right)$ or $\delta s^{\prime}=\delta s_{0} \delta l_{v}\left(\delta l_{v} \pm \delta s_{0}\right)^{-1}=\delta s_{0}\left(1 \pm \delta s_{0} / \delta l_{v}\right)^{-1} \approx$  $\approx \delta s_{0}\left(1 \mp \delta s_{0} / \delta l_{v}\right)=\delta s_{0} \mp Q$ with $Q=\left(\delta s_{0}\right)^{2} / \delta l_{v}$, such that 
$Q \delta l_{v}=\delta s_{0}^{2}$ is a length contribution element which, due to $\delta l_{v}$, provides the contribution from quantum theory.
