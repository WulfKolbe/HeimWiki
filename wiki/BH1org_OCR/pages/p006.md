8. The existence of a “world equation,” one chain of approximations of which yields Einstein’s field equations of general relativity, while another chain of approximations yields Dirac’s equations of relativistic quantum electrodynamics. 

Thus, Heim’s theory takes into account the trend—which has gained particular momentum recently—of attributing more physical properties to space itself. Whether physics can be understood solely in terms of the geometry of a multidimensional space remains to be seen. In any case, the wealth of theoretical data from Heim’s field theory—which is consistent with experimental observation—points to such a successful concept that this theory cannot be ignored and that an intensive study of it is necessary to come closer to—or to achieve—the long-sought goal of a unified physical worldview. 

To facilitate the study of these expositions, Resch Verlag has published the “Introduction to Burkhard Heim, Elementary Structures of Matter with a Glossary and Formula Index” (1985), compiled by Burkhard Heim and Walter Dröscher. This introduction also contains all the formulas from Volume 2, which was published in 1984. 

As the editor of this volume, which is now available in a completely revised edition, I owe special thanks to Burkhard Heim for his trust and to Walter Dröscher for his collaboration in the preparation of the manuscript, especially regarding the proof of existence of $R_{12}$, which is deduced by induction from the $R_{6}$, as well as for Chapters IV, 4 and IV, 5, as well as for proofreading  the corrections. I would like to thank Mag. Priska Kapferer for typesetting  the complex equation. 

May this publication lead to a more comprehensive understanding  of the world and humanity.
