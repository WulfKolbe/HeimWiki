The $x_{+4}=\omega t$ and $x_{-4}=$, which differ algebraically, imply the $R_{ \pm 4}$ as well as $\hat{A}_{ \pm}$ imply that, due to invariance under $\hat{A}_{-}$ for  ponderable structures in $R_{3}$, the speed limit $0 \leqq v<c$ applies, whereas in $R_{+4}$, due to $\hat{A}_{+}$any value $0 \leqq v<\infty$ is permitted if $\beta>0$ holds in (*a). 

The multiplication of $\hat{A}_{ \pm}$ yields $\left(\hat{A}_{-} \times \hat{A}_{+}\right)_{-}=\widehat{0}$, that is, due to this commutativity, the $\hat{A}_{ \pm}$ of the $R_{ \pm 4}$ are regular affinities, so that in $R_{4}$ an invariance under $\hat{B}=\hat{A}_{+} \hat{A}_{-}$ must be required. 

Because of the factor $\hat{A}_{-}$, $\hat{B}$ is also defined over the complex algebraic number field, and due to the orthogonality of the commuting factors, the orthogonality $\hat{B} \hat{B}_{T}=\hat{E}$ becomes immediately evident, whereby in the special case $\hat{B}_{\mathrm{T}}=\hat{B}^{*}$ holds. Now the elements of $\hat{A}_{+}$ appear in $\hat{B}$ as factors of the elements of $\hat{A}_{-}$ in such a way that, through $\hat{B}$, the speed limit $0 \leqq v<c$ also applies to ponderable structures in $R_{4}$. If, for empirical reasons,  $\beta<0$ were to turn out to be true in $(* \mathbf{a})$, then this would result in $x_{+4}=i \omega t$ as well as  $t g \psi_{+}=i \beta_{+}$, and $\hat{A}_{+}$ would have the same algebraic character as $\hat{A}_{-}$, so that $R_{+4}$ would also be a Minkowski space.  For $\hat{B}$, this meant that, although it has the boundary $0 \leqq v<c$, but  the factor $\beta$. becomes relevant in the case of $\left(\omega<c\right.$ as $1+\beta^{2}>2$. Since all  high-energy physics experiments at particle accelerators clearly point to  $0 \leqq v<c$, in the case of $\beta<0$ be required based on these experiments $\omega \geqq c$. For the construction of the $R_{ \pm 4}$, as well as the synthesis of $\widehat{B}$ and $R_{4}$, is, due to $0 \leqq v<c$, the ambiguity  $\beta>0$ or $\beta<0$ of the unknown constant in (*a) is completely irrelevant.  For this reason, the $R_{4}$ we are looking for can only be a Minkowski space  that, like the $R_{-4}$, is defined by the real, interchangeable $R_{3}$ coordinates—which are commutative with each other—and the imaginary time coordinate, which is not commutative with them
