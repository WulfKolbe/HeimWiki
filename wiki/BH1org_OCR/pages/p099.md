$G_{g}>0$ in $r>\varsigma$ does not appear to be ponderomotor in nature, but is merely of a latent nature. According to this concept, therefore, in $r>\varsigma$ a mass at rest relative to the source of $G_{g}$ or moving in the direction of $G_{g}>0$  would not experience any ponderomotor influence, yet this mass would be decelerated in its motion if it approached this field source from $r>\varsigma$ against the direction of $G_{g}>0$. Let there be two spiral nebula systems $S_{1}$  and $S_{2}$ with $\varsigma_{1}$ and $\varsigma_{2}$, whose total distance is $s \gg \varrho_{1}$ and  $s \gg \varrho_{2}$, or $s \gg \varsigma_{1}+\varsigma_{2}$, respectively, so that the distance between their centers of mass can be set to $s+\varrho_{1}+\varrho_{2} \approx s$. 

If a photon $E=m c^{2}$ with the imponderable field mass $m$ is emitted in $S_{2}$  , then this photonic inertial effect $m$ would be constantly influenced by $G_{1}>0$ on its way to $S_{1}$ in $r>\varsigma_{2}$ be constantly influenced by $G_{1}>0$, but not  by $G_{2}>0$ of the emitting system. The same applies to a photon traveling in the opposite direction, which is emitted by $S_{1}$. In this way, $E=m c^{2}=h v=c h / \lambda$ undergoes an energy reduction by $\delta E$ to $E-\delta E$, which, due to $E=c h / \lambda$, corresponds to an increase in the wavelength $\operatorname{um} \delta \lambda$ to $\lambda+\delta \lambda$, i.e., a redshift.  In fact, however, it is not only $G_{1}>0$ or, conversely, $G_{2}>0$ that is effective on its own, but rather the total mass in front of the traveling photon, which is relevant as $G_{g}>0$ in $R_{3}$.
