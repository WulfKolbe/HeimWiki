The factor $\sqrt{\tau^{\prime}}=\delta$ from the solution $x_{2}$ can be interpreted as a measure of minimal condensation $\widehat{[]}_{\text {min }} \neq \widehat{0}$, which corresponds to a minimal but non-negligible mass. According to the definition $T_{1}<T$, the following holds in the universe $\operatorname{sum} \delta s=\delta s_{1}+\delta s_{2}=2 \delta s_{0}=2 \sqrt{\tau}$, i.e., with $\underline{\delta s}=\delta s-\delta=2 \sqrt{\tau}-\delta=2 \sqrt{\tau}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2 \sqrt{\tau}}\right)$. On the other hand, $\delta s_{1}=\delta s_{0}\left(1-\alpha m_{\min }^{4}\right)$ holds, while $2 \delta s_{1} \rightarrow 2 \delta s_{0}$ holds for $m_{\min } \rightarrow 0$. We can now compare $2 \delta s_{1}$ with $\underline{\delta s}$, which yields $2 \alpha m_{\text {min }}^{4}=\sqrt{\tau^{\prime} / \tau}$ and, together with $1 / \alpha=2 \mu^{4}$, yields the determining equation $m_{\text {min }}^{4}=\mu^{4} \sqrt{\tau^{\prime} / \tau}$ for $m_{\text {min }}$. Substituting $\sqrt{\tau^{\prime}}$ into the solution $x_{2}$ and $\tau=\tau_{T}$ from (15)  yields the numerical value $9,0986728 \cdot 10^{-31}[k g]$. This value  practically coincides with the lower bound of the spectrum of electrically neutral particles given in (32), especially since in $m_{\text {min }}$ the electrically neutral maximum monomass $\mu^{4} \sqrt{2}=m(1,0)$ according to (27a) determines the numerical value on its own, apart from the dimensionless factor $\sqrt{\tau^{\prime} / \tau}$. According to quantum electrodynamics, an electric charge field corresponds to the mass fraction $\Delta=\frac{\alpha^{\prime} m}{2 \pi}+m \varepsilon \quad \mathrm{zu}$, where $\alpha^{\prime}$ is the fine-structure constant of light. Accordingly, empirically, $m_{e}=m_{\text {min }}+\Delta=m_{\text {min }}\left(1+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2 \pi}+\varepsilon\right)$ should be set equal to $\varepsilon<\alpha^{\prime}$ for the mass of electrons. With $\varepsilon=0$, $m_{e}=9,1092401 \cdot 10^{-31}[k g]$ follows numerically, while the measured value $9,1093897 \cdot 10^{-31}[k g]$, which is quite comparable to this, is . The error deviation between these two values for the electron mass is only 0.016 \%o. 

Given this high degree of accuracy, it must be concluded that $\sqrt{\tau^{\prime}}$ and thus also $D^{\prime}$ are part of the solution $x_{2}$ alongside $D$ and $\sqrt{\tau}$ exist entirely within $R_{3}$, such that $B D^{\prime}$ structures the subuniverses, but $\sqrt{\tau^{\prime}}$ describes the minimal condensation of complex hermetics in the sense of (32).
