For such a simple tensorium (whose structure is described by $\varphi$), there is a simple sequence of integer metron numbers $n$, which  specify the number of metrons contained up to the respective  position in the tensorium. The structure $\varphi(n)$ of the tensorium is called simple because only a sequence of metron numbers forms the argument. However, this structure is not one-dimensional because the $\tau$ in the $R_{p}$ are defined with $1 \leqq p \leqq m$ , and  one-dimensionality characterizes only the single special case $p=1$.  The function $\varphi(n)$ therefore describes the structure of a simple,  but $p$-dimensional metronic tensorium, whose argument consists of  an integer sequence of metron numbers. Since $n$ is an integer, the argument of $\varphi$ can only vary by $\pm 1$, and this  suggests a metronic revision of the differential quotient as the  second infinitesimal limit relation. 

Because of $x_{n}=x(n)$, $\varphi(n)=p\left(x_{n}\right)$ can also be set if $n$  no longer denotes the boundary of the domain of definition, but rather any  current metron number. The discrete interval $x_{0} \leqq x_{n} \leqq x_{N}$  becomes the continuum $a \leqq x \leqq b$ for $N \rightarrow \infty$ according to $\lim _{N \rightarrow \infty} x_{n}=x$,  because this is identical to $\tau=0$, and $\lim _{N \rightarrow \infty} p\left(x_{n}\right)=p(x)$ becomes a continuous function in this  approximation.
