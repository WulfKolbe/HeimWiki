According to the  investigations in Section II regarding (11), for the energy density $q$,  in the gravitational field interpreted in terms of energy, $8 \pi q=-\gamma M^{2} / r^{4}$ can be set. In the spherically symmetric case $d V=4 \pi x^{2} d x$, the gravitational energy in the $R_{3}$region $r \leqq x<\infty$, the integral 
$E_{g}=\int_{r}^{\infty} q d V=-\gamma M^{2} / 2 \int_{r}^{\infty} d x / x^{2}=-\gamma M^{2} /(2 r)$. If we set $2 E_{g}=E_{G}$ for brevity, then $-r E_{G}=\gamma M^{2}$, which is equivalent to energy-matter $E=M c^{2}$, a ratio of energies 
$A /(2 r)=\gamma M /\left(c^{2} r\right)=E_{G} /\left(M c^{2}\right)=E_{G} / E$ and therefore 
$\Delta r^{\prime} \approx \Delta r\left(1-E_{G} / E\right)$ and $\Delta t^{\prime} \approx \Delta t\left(1+E_{G} / E\right)$. According to the  special theory of relativity, the following applies to $\Delta r^{\prime}$ and $\Delta t^{\prime}$ with $c \beta=v$  (relative velocity in $R_{3}$) $\Delta r^{\prime} \approx \Delta r\left(1-\beta^{2} / 2\right)$ and $\Delta t^{\prime} \approx \Delta t\left(1+\beta^{2} / 2\right)$, where $M_{0}$ can also be set as the rest mass for $\beta \ll 1$, as well as $\beta^{2} / 2=M_{0} v^{2} /\left(2 M_{0} c^{2}\right)$. The differences $\Delta r^{\prime}$  and $\Delta t$ ' are thus determined in both general and special relativity by the ratio of an energy to a  threshold energy $E_{0}$ in the form $E / E_{0}$, which also applies to the  micrometer range, such that here $E_{0}$ depends neither on a particle mass nor on the relative velocity. In this  case, $\Delta E$ would be an energy difference associated with the energy $E=M_{0} c^{2}\left(1+\beta^{2} / 2\right)=M(v) c^{2}$. If $\beta \ll 1$, then—since $M_{0} c^{2}$ is a constant energy contribution— $\Delta E=\Delta\left(M_{0} c^{2}+M_{0} v^{2} / 2\right)=\Delta\left(v^{2}\right) M_{0} / 2$ can be assumed. 

The difference $\Delta s_{4}$ changes minimally by $\pm i \delta l_{v}$ due to $\Delta s_{4}= \pm \Delta s_{8}$, i.e., $\Delta s_{4}$ varies in the sense of a probability density function that attains the value 0 at $\Delta s_{4} \rightarrow \infty$. For this  reason, we obtain $\Delta s_{4}^{\prime}=\Delta s_{4}\left(1 \pm i \delta l_{v} / \Delta s_{4}\right)$.
