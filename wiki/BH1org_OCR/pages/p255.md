Ponderability or imponderability evidently stems from the complex or imaginary nature of the relevant form of hermetry, whereby the cause of ponderability is to be found in the existence of metric condensation levels of the $R_{3}$. The structural property of the $d$ terms, which manifests as an electric charge field, appears, according to (27), to be a consequence of the inclusion of time condensations in the complex condensation process. 

Although the four possible forms of hermeticity, $a$ through $d$, differ  very significantly in their structural properties, yet all $M q$ are characterized by the existence of inertial mass $m$ , which also appears as an imponderable field mass in $a$ and $b$. This  means, as already mentioned, that the energy-matter equivalent $E=m c^{2}$ applies uniformly to all forms of Hermeticism, so that, in addition to the  $c$ and $d$ spectra (27a) and (27), the representation $a$ and $b$, the representation $m=E / c^{2}$ with $E(a, b) \neq 0$ holds. Now, according to (23) and (24), but also according to (27) and (27a), there is a clear connection between these forms of hermitianism. Relation (27a) of the $c$ terms is a spectrum of inertial masses whose terms are so closely spaced that this spectrum must be referred to as a pseudocontinuum. On the other hand, based on empirical evidence, the ponderable $c$ terms also form a discrete point spectrum with respect to their inertial masses, which in no way resembles a pseudocontinuum.  However, since $E=m c^{2}$ is universally valid, it must be assumed  that the pseudocontinuum (27a) contains all possible ponderable and imponderable field masses, so that the pseudocontinuum implies all field masses of imaginary condensations that are at all possible according to (19); however, this pseudocontinuum must be superimposed by a discrete spectrum of ponderable complex terms. Accordingly, it can be established that relation (27) is of a completely universal nature, for in this pseudocontinuum, in addition to the discrete spectrum of ponderable $c$ terms, there is also that of the discrete $d$ forms.
