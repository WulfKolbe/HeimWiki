Around the time $T_{1}$, an environment $\Delta t$ a region $T_{1} \pm \Delta t$ can be defined, such that $T_{1}-\Delta t$ lies within $0 \leqq t<T_{1}$, but $T_{1}+\Delta t$ lies in  $T_{1}<t^{\prime} \leqq T$. The approach of $t \rightarrow T_{1}$ to $T_{1}-\Delta t$ is conditioned  by the metronomic line element $\delta s=\delta s_{0}=\sqrt{\tau}$ because of $\dot{\tau} \approx 0$,  it is also $d(\delta s) / d t=0$. If, on the other hand, this approximation $t=t^{\prime} \rightarrow T_{1}$ is performed from $T_{1}+\Delta t$, then $\delta s=\delta s_{1}=\sqrt{\tau}\left(1-R_{-} / \varsigma\right)$ would apply here,  and thus $t=t^{\prime} \rightarrow T_{1}$ would no longer be time-invariant, i.e.,  $d(\delta s) / d t \neq 0$, because, as a consequence of material genesis, $M\left(t^{\prime}\right) \neq 0$ holds.  This contradiction arising with respect to $T_{1}$ is resolved  when another metronomic length element $\delta s_{2}=\sqrt{\tau}\left(1+R_{-} / \varsigma\right)$  is introduced, because then $\delta s=\delta s_{1}+\delta s_{2}=2 \delta s_{0}$, that is,  $d(\delta s) / d t=0$. 

For brevity, let us introduce the constants $4 \omega c h^{2} \alpha=e \gamma^{2}$ and $A=M^{4}$, since then $\delta s_{1,2}=\sqrt{\tau}(1 \pm \alpha A)$. 

According to (27) and (27a), it seems extremely likely that, as a consequence of the breaking of the global symmetry group at  $\tau_{1}=\tau\left(T_{1}\right)$ terms $m(1,0)=m_{M}$ arise, so that in any case $A\left(T_{1}\right)=m_{M}^{4}$ holds, which after a time $\delta t^{\prime}$ (probably very short) into a cascade of $v>1$ elementary structures of matter  of intermediate mass $m_{0}$ according to $m_{M} \rightarrow v m_{0}$. Since the $m_{0} \ll m_{M}$ are, the following holds for $\varsigma$ according to (12a), the relation $Y_{1}=1$ holds practically exactly. At time $t^{\prime \prime}=T_{1}+\delta t^{\prime}$, $A\left(t^{\prime \prime}\right)=v m_{0}^{4}$ therefore holds, so that for $t^{\prime} \gg t^{\prime \prime}$ the total of $L \gg v$ elementary masses $m_{0}$ must be combined to form the mass $M_{1}=L m_{0}$  , so that $A\left(t^{\prime}\right)=M_{1} m_{0}^{3}=L m_{0}^{4}$ and $\delta s_{1,2}=\delta s_{0}\left(1 \pm \alpha A\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right)$ hold. Here, the metronic length elements $\delta s_{1}$ and $\delta s_{2}$ are mean values that apply throughout the entire universe  and are independent of the $R_{3}$ position. These elements also appear as functions of time in the interval $T_{1} \leqq t^{\prime} \leqq T$, which is attributable solely to $A$.
