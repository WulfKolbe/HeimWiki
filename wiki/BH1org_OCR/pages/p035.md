Thus, in general, one cannot calculate with $m \geqq 1$ non-gauge-invariant interaction fields, yet $m<n$ remains valid due to the verifiable gauge-invariant interaction fields. The  total set of $n$ fields of the $M q$ could thus consist of $1 \leqq m<n$ non-gauge-invariant ( + ) and $n-m>0$ gauge-invariant ( - ) interaction fields. For each field, a characteristic partial event structure—determined by the field symmetries—can be conceived, which is described by a geodesic, spacetime coordinate system $\vec{\xi}_{p}^{(j)}=\vec{\varepsilon}_{p}^{(j)} \xi_{p}^{(j)}$ , whereby the orientations $\vec{\varepsilon}_{p}^{(j)}$ do not necessarily form a normalized orthogonal system. The indices are given in $1 \leqq j \leqq n$, whereas the coordinate numbering follows $1 \leqq p \leqq 4$. These four $n$ geodetic coordinates can now be  referred to the Cartesian system $x_{k}$ using $1 \leqq k \leqq 4$ of the empty $R_{4}$, so that the transformations $\vec{\xi}_{p}^{(i)}\left(x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}, x_{4}\right)$ exist. 

Due to the empirical principle (a), only those transformations may be permitted that are one-to-one and, due to the correspondence principle following from principle (c), are continuous in the macroscopic range. Furthermore, due to the physical impossibility of divergent self-energy potentials, these isomorphisms must also be free of points at infinity. Overall, therefore, the $4 n$ transformations $\vec{\xi}_{p}^{(j)}\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{4}\right)$ must be those of the global Poincaré group. 

With regard to the structural $R_{4}$ properties of the $M q$, the $n$ fields be divided into the subgroup of $n-m>0$ gauge-invariant and $m \geqq 1$ non-gauge-invariant interactions.
