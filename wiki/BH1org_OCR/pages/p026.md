Since the experiment seemed worthwhile, we decided to investigate temporal changes in the field in terms of gravitational dynamics, implicitly taking $M(r)$ into account as a starting point. 

For this purpose, a Cartesian, right-handed coordinate system is defined in physical space $R_{3}$ with the real coordinates $x_{1}$,  $x_{2}$, and $x_{3}$, which may be functions of time $t$. For brevity, functional spatial dependencies (provided there are no ambiguities) are written in the form $f\left(x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}\right)=f(x)$. Furthermore, these coordinates are oriented with respect to the normalized orthogonal system $\vec{e}_{i} \vec{e}_{k}=\delta_{i k}$ according to $\vec{x}_{k}=\vec{e}_{k} x_{k}$. In the general case, a mass $M_{(0)}=$ const, composed of heterogeneous discontinuities, is referenced to this coordinate system such that the center of mass of this mass lies at the origin. The discontinuities of $M_{(0)}$ are assumed to move in the general case, whereby $\vec{v}=\sum_{k=1}^{3} d \vec{x}_{k} / d t$ is set as the respective velocity for these temporal changes in position. Since $M_{(0)}=$ is to be constant,  there must always be a closed surface $X_{0}$ that encloses the volume  $V_{0}=V\left(x_{0}\right)$ of $M_{(0)}$ in such a way that no discontinuous element of this mass $X_{0}$ and thus $V_{0}$ leaves $X_{0}$ despite $\vec{v} \neq \overrightarrow{0}$. This mass $M_{(0)}$ in $V_{(0)}$ is in any case the source of a gravitational field that appears both in $V_{(0)}$ and in volumes $V\left(x>x_{0}\right)>V_{0}$ , whereby $V(x)$ contains the source volume $V_{0}$. If, in accordance with the preceding, the gravitational field is conceived as an energetic state of space, then this state corresponds to a field mass $\mu(x, t)$, which consists of an internal component $\mu_{i}\left(x \leqq x_{0}\right)$ in $V_{0}$  and an external component $\mu_{e}\left(x>x_{0}\right)$ in $V(x)$ according to $\mu(x, t)=\mu_{e}(x, t)+\mu_{i}(x, t)$. In addition to these three field masses and the source mass $M_{(0)}=$ const $\mathrm{mu} \beta$ there are also the masses $M_{0}=\mu_{i}+M_{(0)}$ and, in general, $M(x, t)=\mu_{e}+M_{0}=\mu(x, t)+M_{(0)}$ in $V(x)$. If, for brevity, $V(x)=V$ by analogy with  $V_{0}$, then these six distinct  masses initially yield, as global densities of the field mass, $\sigma_{g \mu}=\mu / V$,  as well as $\sigma_{i}=\mu_{i} / V_{0}$ and $\sigma_{e}=\mu_{e} /\left(V-V_{0}\right)$, while for the field source densities $\sigma_{g}=M / V, \sigma_{g 0}=M_{0} / V, \sigma_{0}=M_{(0)} / V$,
