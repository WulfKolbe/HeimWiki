The derivation of a geometric unit of measurement must, taking (c) into account, be based on a general background phenomenon of material structures, for which ( $\mathbf{d}_{2}$ )  serves as an empirical basis. Since, for the $g_{i k}$ of the $R_{6}$-structure (8) and (8a) hold as a geodesic relation $\ddot{x}^{i}+\left\{\begin{array}{cc}i \\ k & m\end{array}\right\} \dot{x}^{k} \dot{x}^{m}=0$ with the parameter of phenomenological time counting, the $g_{i k}$ must be interpreted as general  tensorial interaction potentials, whereby  gravity must also be regarded as one of these interaction fields. These tensor potentials are related via (8) in $R_{6}$ by nonlinear second-order, second-degree relations,  because $g_{i k}=g_{k i}^{*}$, that is, $2 \Gamma_{i k m}=\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{im}, \mathrm{k}}+\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{ki}, \mathrm{m}}-\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{km}, \mathrm{i}}$. 

Since, according to (9), the gravitational field structure is to be understood heuristically as a partial structure of $R_{6}$ that, together with other partial structures, forms the $g_{i k}$ from (8), but this composition law is unknown due to the  heuristic nature of (9), the only remaining possibility is to describe the gravitational field from $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{2}}\right)$ according to (*) through (*b) as a scalar field, whereby this description—due to (8)—must certainly consist of an unknown nonlinear differential equation. 

The approach to (*) originated from $M(x)=M\left(x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}\right)$ and $\varphi(x) \rightarrow \varphi_{n}$  and led back to $r \varphi_{n}=\gamma M_{(0)}=$ const, whereby $M(x)$ is a consequence of the  energy-matter equivalence and the equivalence principle of inertia  and gravity. In the case of an undisturbed gravitational field, the relationships of spherical symmetry hold according to $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{2}}\right)$, which, according to $\varphi \rightarrow \varphi_{n}$, must also be applied to $M(x)$ with $\varphi(x)$. In this case, the $R_{3}$-coordinates appear only in the quadratic form  $x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}=r^{2}$, so that the distance $r$ from the center of gravity is the only variable.
