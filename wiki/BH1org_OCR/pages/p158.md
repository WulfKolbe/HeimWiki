A completely analogous analysis can be applied to the partial structures. In this context, it is first appropriate to define the concepts of a so-called sieve operator and the sieve chain, the necessity of which arises from the polymetry of partial structures from $M=\omega>1$ lattice nuclei ${ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(\mu)}$ with $1 \leqq \mu \leqq \omega$. Such a sieve operator $S(\mu)$ can always be used to eliminate the non-Euclidean nature of one of the lattice nuclei $S(\mu) ;{ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(\mu)} ; n={ }^{2} \bar{E}$.  Thus, if $F\left({ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(\mu)}\right)_{1}^{\omega}$ is some functional selector, then such a sieve operator eliminates a lattice kernel according to 
$S(j) ; F\left({ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(1)} \ldots{ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(j)} \ldots{ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(\omega)}\right)=F\left({ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(1)} \ldots{ }^{2} \bar{E}() /() \ldots{ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(\omega)}\right)$.  Now, $1 \leqq j \leqq s$ metric sieve operators can act successively in such a way that a $s$-term sieve chain is formed, which is to be symbolized by  $S(j)_{1}^{p}=S(1) ; \ldots S(j) ; \ldots S(P)$.
