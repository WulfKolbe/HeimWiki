Burkhard Heim, a graduate in physics, was born in Potsdam in 1925. In 1944, an explosion occurred while he was working in a laboratory at the Reich Institute for Chemical Technology in Berlin; as a result, he lost both hands, became almost completely blind, and suffered severe hearing loss. Beginning in 1945, he studied chemistry; in 1949, he began studying theoretical physics in Göttingen, completing his studies in 1954 with a Hauptdiplom. Since 1949, he has been conducting independent research on a general field theory in which all physical fields and their sources are uniformly described as dynamic properties of purely geometric structures. This theory was developed over the past three decades under—as one can easily imagine—the most difficult external conditions and has been partially published in several stages since 1975. Interest in Heim’s theory is growing steadily and is now being particularly stimulated by the publication of “Elementary Structures of Matter,” Volumes 1 and 2, along with “Introduction to Burkhard Heim, Elementary Structures of Matter, with a Glossary and Index of Formulas” by Burkhard Heim and Walter Dröscher.
