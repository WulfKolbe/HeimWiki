The assumption of the geodesic constraint on $\tau$ is supported by the  requirement of metric equivalence for all $\tau$. In general, let $R_{m}$ be an arbitrary-dimensional space (spanned by real and  imaginary coordinates), a subspace $R_{p}$ with $p \leqq m$  be given, whose volumes $V_{p}$, according to $V_{p}=N \tau$, consist of $N$ (integer) $p$-dimensional, metrically equivalent $\tau$-cells. For the smallest possible coordinate differences in $R_{p}$, $\delta x^{i}=\alpha^{i p} \sqrt{\tau}$ then holds, provided that the $\alpha^{i}=$ const specifies the real or imaginary  character of the $x^{i}$. Furthermore, if $w_{p}$ is the functional determinant defined by the metric determinant of the fundamental tensor, then the following holds for arbitrary $R_{p^{-}}$-volumes $V_{p}=\int_{x^{1}} \ldots \int_{x^{p}} w_{p} \prod_{i=1}^{p} d x^{i}$, which, when compared with $V_{p}=N \tau$, yields $\tau=\frac{1}{N} \int_{x^{1}} \ldots \int_{x^{p}} w_{p} \prod_{i=1}^{p} d x^{i}=w_{p} \prod_{i=1}^{p} \delta x^{i}$. If the $x^{i}$ bound the $\tau$, that is, $\delta x^{i}=\alpha^{i} p \sqrt{\tau}$ holds, then  using `$\prod_{i=1}^{p} \alpha^{i}=\left|\alpha^{i} \delta_{i k}\right|_{p}=\underline{\alpha}=$` const `$\neq 0$` also yields `$\prod_{i=1}^{p} \delta x^{i}=\tau \underline{\alpha}$`,  which, when substituted, yields $w_{p} \underline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=1$ or $w_{p}=$ const. However, this is only possible if the components of the fundamental tensor in $R_{p}$ are, with respect to the $x^{i}$ and $\Gamma_{k m}^{i}=0$, which, according to the geodesic equation, implies that $x^{i}$, due to $\ddot{x}^{i}=0$, as geodesic coordinates. For this reason, the $\tau$ are always geodetically bounded and therefore metrically equivalent, which also holds for $p=2$ in (15) and (15a).
