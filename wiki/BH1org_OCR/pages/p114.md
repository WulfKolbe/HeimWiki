In the context of metronomic differential operations, it is useful to develop some transformations for $k=1$. In addition to $\varphi(n)=\varphi$, let $u(n)=u$ and $v(n)=v$ metronome functions, and for brevity,  $\phi(n-1)=\phi^{\prime}$ is used, so that $\partial \varphi=\varphi-\varphi^{\prime}$ holds. First, let $\varphi=\sum_{j} u_{j}(n)$ be true. Then, clearly, $\partial \varphi=\partial \sum_{j} u_{j}=\sum_{j} u_{j}-\sum_{j} u_{j}^{\prime}=\sum_{j}\left(u_{j}-u_{j}^{\prime}\right)=\sum_{j} \check{\partial} u_{j}, \mathrm{~d} . \mathrm{h}$ holds, and $\partial \Sigma u_{j}(n)=\sum_{j} \partial u_{j}$ always holds. If, on the other hand, $\varphi=C=\operatorname{const}(n)$ holds, then $\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}$ must hold, which implies $\partial \varphi=\partial C=0$. Accordingly, the constant is always $\partial C=0$. For $\varphi=C u(n)$, this therefore implies $\partial \varphi=C u-$  $-C u^{\prime}=C ð u$, that is, the rule $\partial(C u)=C \partial u$, according to which constant factors $C$ reappear as factors. Finally, $\varphi=u v$ can be expressed as a product.
