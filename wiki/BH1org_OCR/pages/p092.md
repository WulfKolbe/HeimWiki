Evidently, the following holds for the volume of the effective region of an attractive gravitational field: $\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{3} \pi / 6$, but also $\frac{4}{3} \pi \varsigma^{3}$, which, by comparison, $\lambda^{\prime}=2 \varsigma$ suggests.  If (as is very likely) gravitons exist as a quantum gravitational field, then these gravitons must be bosons because they can be represented by tensorial state functions, and $\lambda^{\prime}$  can only depend on the average mass $m_{0}$ of atomic units, of which $L<\infty$ make up the macroscopic field source designated by $M_{0}$ according to $M_{0}=L m_{0}$. The macromolecular source $M_{0}$  and the profile $M(r)$ thus determine only the intensity of the field,  while $\lambda^{\prime}=2 \varsigma<\infty$ depends solely on the average mass $m_{0}$ of the atomic  units of the microstructure and defines a gravitational range of action with a spatial radius of $\varrho$. The following applies to the macroscopic gravitational spatial structure $r \varphi=\gamma M(r)$ with $M\left(r_{0}\right)=M_{0}$ and  $r_{0} \leqq r \leqq \varsigma<\infty$, such that the microstructure $m_{0}$ of $M_{0}$ for $M_{0}$ defines a  spatial surface $F=4 \pi \varsigma^{2}$ on which $\varphi(\varsigma)=0$ occurs. Due to the  extraordinary weakness of the gravitational field $\left|\mu_{g}\right| \ll m_{0}$ , $m_{0} \approx m(r)$ and $M_{0} \approx M(r)$ hold as a very good approximation, while $m(\varsigma) \neq 0 \quad$ is completely evident. Now, $\varphi$ is the square of a real velocity in $R_{3}$, so that for all $r$, $\varphi \geqq 0$ must always remain constant. 

Although this is satisfied by $\varphi_{n}$, it is not satisfied by $\varphi_{1}$ in the range $r<\varsigma$.
