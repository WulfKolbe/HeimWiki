For $m(r)$, the rest energy is $E_{r}=m(r) c^{2}$, so that, with $m_{0}=m\left(r_{0}\right)$ as the geometric boundary $r_{0} \leqq r$ of the element $m_{0}$ for the static gravitational field energy $E_{s}=E_{r}-m_{0} c^{2}=$ $=\left(m(r)-m_{0}\right) c^{2}$. 

Relations (*) and (*a) suggest that a static gravitational field should be understood as the steady state of the dynamic equilibrium of very weak field fluctuations. Let $E_{g}$ be the integral energy of these fluctuations, whose spatial density is $\eta_{g}=d E_{g} / d V$. If (*) is multiplied scalarly by $\vec{G}$ and (*a) is multiplied scalarly by $\vec{\mu}$  and the two products are subtracted from one another, then $\vec{G} \operatorname{rot} \vec{\mu}-\vec{\mu} \operatorname{rot} \vec{G}=\alpha \vec{G} \vec{G}-\beta \vec{\mu} \dot{\vec{\mu}}+\sigma \vec{G} \bar{v}-\left(\sigma-\sigma_{(0)}\right) \vec{\mu} \vec{f}$ results, where, by an operator theorem, $\vec{G} \operatorname{rot} \vec{\mu}-\vec{\mu} \operatorname{rot} \vec{G}=\operatorname{div}(\vec{G} \times \vec{\mu})$ holds. For the power density of the field fluctuations, it obviously holds that $\dot{\eta}_{g}=\operatorname{div}(\vec{G} \times \vec{\mu})+\left(\sigma-\sigma_{(0)}\right) \vec{\mu} \vec{f}-\sigma \vec{G} \vec{v}, \quad$ or $\quad \dot{\eta}_{g}=\alpha \vec{G} \vec{G}-\beta \hat{\mu} \dot{\vec{\mu}}$.  After integration over space and time, this yields $2 E_{g}=\alpha \int \vec{G}^{2} d V-\beta \int \vec{\mu}^{2} d V$, and for this integral, $E_{g}=-E_{s}$ must hold, that is, $m(r) c^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2} \int \vec{G}^{2} d V-\frac{\beta}{2} \int \vec{\mu}^{2} d V=m_{0} c^{2} \quad$ must be set, because $E_{g}$ must be identical to the static negative potential field energy. The relation $m c^{2}+E_{g}=m_{0} c^{2}$ is the macroscopic energy principle in a gravitational field, taking into account the unity of the field and the field source.
