Every metronic function $\varphi\left(n_{i}\right)_{1}^{L}$ can, due to the integerness  of $n_{i}$, also be interpreted as a $L$-fold sequence of numbers, for which all criteria of multiple sequences apply. Such a mapping law $\varphi$, when viewed as a sequence of numbers, generally selects—as the $n_{i}$ runs through the  positive integer real numbers—some sequence of numbers from the respective algebraic field, and the sequence identified by $\varphi$, in turn, must change in structure  when the assignment law $\varphi()$ is changed. However, entities that modify a  mapping law are defined as operators, from which it follows that there must also be metronic operators. If, for example, $\varphi$ is homogeneous of degree $h$ such that the relation  $\sum_{i=1}^{L} n_{i} \check{\partial}_{i} \varphi=(-1)^{h+1} \sum_{v=0}^{h-1}\binom{h}{v} \varphi$ holds, then $\sum_{i=1}^{L} n_{i} \check{\partial}_{i}=C$ can be regarded as a metronic operator. With the contraction $(-1)^{h+1} \sum_{v=0}^{h-1}\binom{h}{v}=\lambda$, we then obtain $C ; \varphi=\lambda \varphi$, which shows that the notation of metronic equations using metronic operators can be significantly simplified if “,,;” indicates that $C$ acts as a selector. 

A metronic operator thus acts on any sequence of numbers  in such a way that, from a corresponding algebraic field, a uniquely defined different sequence of numbers is selected; therefore, it seems appropriate to refer to metronic operators as selectors in order to achieve a precise distinction from the infinitesimal concept of an operator. Since all analytical operations can be represented by individual selectors, every metronic function $\varphi$ can be represented by a sequence of selectors that acts on the simple sequences $n_{i}>0$ of the positive integer real metronic digits.
