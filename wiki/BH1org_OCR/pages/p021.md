When changing inertial reference frames, a transformation group is a Lorentz group with a quadratic transformation matrix $\hat{A}_{-}$ of type 4 and orthogonal property $\hat{A}_{-} \hat{A}_{-T}=\hat{E}$ (identity matrix), where the term “orthogonal” refers to the underlying complex algebraic number field, such that in the special case it becomes $\hat{A}_{-T}=\hat{A}_{-}^{*} \neq \hat{A}_{-}$. Using $\hat{A}_{-}$, the real coordinates $x_{1}$,  $x_{2}$ and $x_{3}$ of the physical $R_{3}$ and the time $t$ are transformed, so that the concepts of space and time are relativized. In this way, the introduction of a fourth coordinate as imaginary light-time $x_{4}=$ ict  and the construction of a spacetime as Minkowski space $R_{-4}$ over  the field of complex algebraic numbers. The state changes in the material world then appear in the $R_{-4}$ as event structures that are invariant under $\hat{A}_{-}$. However, $\left(\mathrm{d}_{2}\right)$ is excluded here. One consequence of this Lorentz-invariant representation of the laws of nature is, among other things, the energy-matter equivalence $E=m c^{2}$, according to which every energy $E$ is equivalent to a mass $m$, if mass is generally understood as the inertial resistance to changes in motion in $R_{3}$. If $m_{0}$ is a rest mass and $v=\beta c$ is a relative velocity, then the following also holds for $m$ (as a translational mass) the well-known Lorentz-invariant expression $m \sqrt{1-\beta^{2}}=m_{0}$, and the same applies to the momentum $p=m v$. From this follows the relativistic energy principle $E_{Q}=p c$. On the other hand, it follows from (c) that action quantization directly results in a quantization of the energy of a wave field of frequency $v$ in a vacuum according to $E_{n}=n h v$ with an integer $n \geqq 1$. Substituting $v \lambda=c$ and using the  equation $E_{n}=E_{Q}$ then yields the relation $p \lambda=h$ as the de Broglie equation, which expresses a quantum duality such that each material quantum can appear in both a particle and a wave picture. For this reason, the energy quanta $h v$ of the electromagnetic field are referred to as photons. 

Here, a clarification of the terminology seems appropriate. In the literature, these photons and hypothetical gravitons are often described as massless. In fact, however, according to the energy-matter equivalence, these quanta must be assigned field masses—
