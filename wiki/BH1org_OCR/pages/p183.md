With the metronic direction coefficients $v^{\underline{k} \underline{k}}$, the following holds for a geodetically bounded metronic $R_{6}$-cell $\Delta \Omega=\alpha \tau^{3}$ with $\alpha=\left(g \prod_{k=1}^{6} \nu^{\underline{k} \underline{k}}\right)^{1 / 2}$ with $g=\left|g_{i k}\right|_{6}$. If the empty $R_{6}$ is Euclidean, then $\alpha=1$ and thus $\Delta \Omega=\tau^{3} \equiv \Delta$—that is, these cells are cubic in nature and can only be bounded by equidistant lines, which thus prove to be the geodesic lattice of the empty world. <sup>1</sup> On the other hand, in such an empty world there are no distinguishable event structures and thus no cosmic motion, so that for the direction cosines of the worldline angles $\alpha_{k}$ with the Cartesian reference coordinates $x_{k}$ the representations  $\cos \alpha_{k}=0$ hold for $k \neq 4$ and $\cos \alpha_{4}= \pm 1$. This means that all worldlines run parallel to the Cartesian coordinates and become identical to the straight geodesic lines. Due to $\Delta=\tau^{3}$, the geodesic lattice thus also becomes identical, and an identity between the lattice and hyperselectors is satisfied. As the domains of definition for these reference coordinates, $-\infty<x_{k}<+\infty$ can be set in the empty world, whereby there are no boundaries whatsoever for a finite domain of definition. The $L_{2}=15$ simple metronic  tensors are also unbounded planes in this empty world, so  that fine-structure selectors of their subgrids do not need to be determined. Furthermore, $g_{i k}=\gamma_{i k} ; n=\delta_{i k}$ and $(\boldsymbol{\partial} s)^{2}=\tau$ hold, i.e., $v^{\underline{i k}} \delta_{i k}=1$.  Furthermore, $x_{k}=C_{k} ; n$ holds with $C_{k}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}()_{k}$ and $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}=\sqrt{\tau}$ for $k \leqq 3$ and $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}=i \sqrt{\tau}$ for $k>3$ by virtue of (4).
