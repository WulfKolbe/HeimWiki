With $\vec{G}=\vec{A}+\operatorname{grad} \varphi$, $\vec{G}^{2}=\vec{A}^{2}+2 \vec{A} \operatorname{grad} \varphi+(\operatorname{grad} \varphi)^{2}$, so that, in contrast to the earlier notation, the unknown term

In the energy principle $E_{r}+E_{g}+E_{q}=E$ formulated in this way, in addition to $E_{r}=M c^{2}$ the corrected curve $\varphi(r)$ must be used in $E_{g}=\frac{\alpha}{2} \int(\operatorname{grad} \varphi)^{2} d V$.  Due to $\mu_{g}<0$, $\frac{d M}{d r}<0$ applies, and for the density $\eta_{q}=d E_{q} / d V>0$  of the energy component $E_{q}$ caused by $\mu_{q}=-\mu_{g}$, it could be assumed that $\eta_{q}$ the change in $M$ with $r$, relative  to the boundary surface $F$, is proportional, so that 
$F \eta_{q} \sim-\frac{d M}{d r}$ according to $\lim _{F \rightarrow \infty} \eta_{q}=0$, but also because of 
$F E_{q} \sim-\int \frac{d M}{d r} d V$ of the boundary $\lim _{F \rightarrow \infty} E_{q}=0$, i.e., $\lim _{\varsigma \rightarrow \infty} E=E^{\prime}$ holds. 
According to the energy-matter equivalence in $R_{4}$ and in the auxiliary construction $R_{-4}$ , the proportionality factor of $\eta_{q}$ can only be $c^{2}$, so that  $E_{q}=-\frac{c^{2}}{F} \int \frac{d M}{d r} d V$ must be substituted into $E$, which
