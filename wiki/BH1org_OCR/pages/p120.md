A metronic function $\varphi\left(n_{i}\right)_{1}^{L}$ can be interpreted not only as a number-theoretic function of integer indices, but also as a multiple sequence for which convergence analyses can be performed. $\varphi$ converges as $n_{i}$ increases according to the general convergence criterion whenever $\left|\varphi\left(n_{i}\right)_{1}^{L}-\varphi\left(n_{i}^{\prime}\right)_{1}^{L}\right|<\varepsilon$ falls below an arbitrarily small bound $\varepsilon>0$, provided that there exists some very large number $N=N(\varepsilon)>0$, and $n_{i}>N$ and $n_{i}^{\prime}>N$ can be specified. If this convergence criterion is satisfied, there must be a bound $g$ for $\varphi$ itself, such that $|\varphi-g|<\varepsilon$ becomes $n_{i}>N$, and this bound $g$ would then serve as the limit of the $L$-fold sequence $\varphi$, i.e., for $\left|\varphi\left(n_{i}\right)_{1}^{L}-g\right|<\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon>0$ and $n_{i}>N(\varepsilon)>0$, $\lim _{\left(n_{i}\right)_{1}^{L} \rightarrow \infty} \varphi=g$ holds, i.e., $\varphi \rightarrow g$ converges if all $1 \leqq i \leqq L$  sequences $n_{i} \rightarrow \infty$ grow beyond all bounds. $\varphi$ converges for  $g<\infty$, but diverges for $g \rightarrow \infty$.
