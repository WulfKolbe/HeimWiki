There is a functional selector $\Gamma$, which is defined in $\Omega$ such that a relationship $\Gamma ; \varphi=\lambda \varphi$—that is, a selector equation $\Gamma ; \psi=\lambda \psi$ arises due to  $\varphi=\psi ; n$ , then this selector equation is an analogue to the  eigenvalue problem of infinitesimal operators; that is, the selector equation $\Gamma ; \psi=\lambda \psi$ can be described as a metronic eigenvalue problem. The existence of such a metronic eigenvalue problem can be derived from the existence of metronic matrices; for a matrix $\hat{C}$ is defined even when its elements are metronic functions. However, if metronic matrices exist, then there must also be unitary metronic transformation matrices $\hat{S} \hat{S}^{\times}=\hat{E}$, with the help of which $\hat{C}$ can always be transformed, subject to Hermitianity $\hat{C}=\hat{C}^{\times}$ and the quadratic type $N$, according to $\hat{S} \hat{C} \hat{S}^{-1}=\hat{W}$ into a diagonal matrix $\hat{W}$,  provided that $N$ specifies the rank as the number of dimensions of the hyperstructure and $\hat{C}$  is at least semidefinite according to $\operatorname{sp} \hat{C} \geqq 0$. The diagonal elements $W_{i i}$ are obtained from the determinant relation of the characteristic equation $|\hat{C}-q \hat{E}|_{N}=0$, which in turn can be expanded into the invariant secular polynomial, yielding an equation of degree $N$ for $q$. Its $N$ solutions $q_{i}$ are then the diagonal elements that form $\hat{W}$. This means that $\beta|\hat{C}-q \hat{E}|_{N}=0$ is identical to $\hat{S} \hat{C}-\hat{W} \hat{S}=\hat{0}$ or $W_{i i}-q_{i}=0$. If we set $W_{i i}=\Gamma ; \varphi_{i}$ and $q_{i}=\lambda \varphi_{i}$, then, omitting the subscripts, we generally obtain $\Gamma ; \varphi-\lambda \varphi=0$, or, with $\varphi=\psi ; n$, the selector equation $\Gamma ; \psi=\lambda \psi$ of the metronic eigenvalue problem, whose existence is thus justified by the existence of Hermitian metronic matrices of quadratic type. The necessary condition was $\hat{C}=\hat{C}^{\times}$, and thus also $\hat{W}=\hat{W}^{\times}$, and this, in turn, implies the real nature of the eigenvalues $\lambda=\lambda^{*}$, whereby the selector $\bar{\Gamma}=\Gamma^{*}$ is characterized as a Hermitian selector.  However, if this selector is Hermitian, then the general condition for Hermitianity $\boldsymbol{S}\left(\varphi^{*} \Gamma ; \varphi-\varphi(\Gamma ; \varphi)^{*}\right){ }^{\varnothing} \Omega=0$ must also be satisfied. Substituting $\Gamma ; \varphi=\lambda \varphi$ then yields, by virtue of $\lambda=$ const, the relation $\left(\lambda-\lambda^{*}\right) \underset{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{S}} \varphi \varphi^{*} \delta \Omega=0$, which, by virtue of $\lambda=\lambda^{*}$, is always satisfied—
