It can be concluded that every $M q$, regardless of its nature, appears to be the source of an elementary gravitational field. It necessarily follows that general gravitation is the uniform background phenomenon against which the microscopic processes of the $M q$ , so that this general gravitation simultaneously represents the property common to all $M q$. It is therefore essential to first explore this background phenomenon of gravitation. 

However, empirically, extremely little is known about the phenomenon of gravity; in the macroscopic realm, for example, only Newton’s law of gravitation $\left(\mathrm{d}_{2}\right)$, which, in the undisturbed static case, describes a spherically symmetric, asymptotically varying Coulomb field, whereas in the micromacroscopic range, nothing is empirically known about the behavior of the gravitational field as described in $R_{3}$. However, a few additional statements can be made indirectly. If $M_{0}$ is the micromare field-generating mass and $r$ is a distance from the center of mass  of this mass, then it is known that outside a sphere with a radius of $r_{0} \leqq r$, filled with a micromare volume defined by $M_{0}$, the gravitational field function $\varphi_{n}$ is empirically $r \varphi_{n}=\gamma M_{0}$ according to $\left(\mathrm{d}_{2}\right)$ with the field vector $\vec{G}_{n}=\operatorname{grad} \varphi_{n}$ as the gravitational acceleration. It should be noted here that, in addition to  $\varphi_{n}$ from $\left(\mathrm{d}_{2}\right)$ there are other empirically testable predictions of the General Theory of Relativity, such as the diffraction of light by sufficiently strong gravitational fields, the redshift of light emitted by sufficiently strong gravitational field sources, different electromagnetic signal propagation times under different gravitational fields, or the axial precession of closed eccentric gravitational orbits. These observations, which go beyond $\left(\mathrm{d}_{2}\right)$, should not be taken into account for the time being, because, on the one hand, the primary concern at this stage is to develop the framework for a phenomenological theory of gravitational dynamics, while, on the other hand, the fundamental equations of general relativity (and thus also their solutions) will later emerge as approximations of a higher-order framework.
