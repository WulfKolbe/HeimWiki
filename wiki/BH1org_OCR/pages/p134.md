By analogy with the infinitesimal operator equations, there must also be selector equations, as shown above; however, their analysis must also draw in part on (M7). 

Selector equations always exist when functional selectors are related to one another by any relations. The most general form of a total differential selector equation is determined by a functional selector that relates metric differentials up to order $1 \leqq \nu \leqq N$ with $1 \leqq \kappa \leqq T$ selectors $p_{\kappa}$, such that powers of degrees 
$0 \leqq \mu \leqq M \neq N \neq T$ appear. Such general total differential selector relations of order $N$ and degree $M$ are generally of inhomogeneous form; yet homogenization methods can be applied in metronic integration. General  integration rules cannot be derived, so each differential selector equation must be treated individually, but there  is always the possibility of transformation and substitution. If, on the other hand, tensorial selector equations of arbitrary tensor order are present, or if the equations are partial selector equations  over a multidimensional argument, then a complete system of partial selector equations must always be specified for the solution. 

In general, a selector equation always describes a system of conditions satisfied by the set of metronic solution functions, such that the selector equation selects, from the infinite manifold of conceivable metronic functions, those that satisfy this system of selector conditions. Conversely, corresponding selector relations can be deduced inductively from given conditions.
