The elements $\delta s_{0}, \delta s_{1}$ and $\delta s_{2}$ are included in the $R_{3}$volume with a diameter of $D\left(t^{\prime}\right) \approx 10^{125}[\mathrm{~m}]$ and can be interpreted as Compton wavelengths in the submicrometer range, which, in accordance with quantum duality, are inversely proportional to the energy-masses, so that $M_{p}^{\prime} \delta s_{0}=M_{1}^{\prime} \delta s_{1}=M_{2}^{\prime} \delta s_{2}=h / c$ can be set. This is possible because the $\delta s_{1,2} \neq \delta s_{0}$ is a phenomenological measure of $\widehat{[]} \neq \widehat{0}$, i.e., $\bar{\lambda} \neq \overline{0}$ according to (19). It could therefore be conjectured that the $\delta s_{1,2}$, as wavelengths, are in some way related to the speculatively conceived virtual $X$ bosons, which appear according to $T_{1}$. With $u=\alpha A\left(t^{\prime}\right)$, $\delta s_{1,2}=\delta s_{0}(1 \pm u) \quad$ and $\quad M_{1}^{\prime} \quad$ combines with $\quad M_{2}^{\prime} \quad$ to form the total mass  $M_{g}^{\prime}=M_{1}^{\prime}+M_{2}^{\prime}=\left(1 / \delta s_{1}+1 / \delta s_{2}\right) h / c=2 M_{p}^{\prime}\left(1-u^{2}\right)^{-1} .{ }^{4}$ For $L m_{0}=0$, this becomes $u=0$, so that $\underline{M}^{\prime}=M_{g}^{\prime}(u=0)=2 M_{p}^{\prime}$ is the equivalent of a background energy that is largely independent of the age of the universe in $T_{1} \leqq t^{\prime} \leqq T$. Superimposed on the background energy is an active energetic equivalent of a potential nature,  $M_{v}^{\prime}$, thereby fulfilling the energy principle (a) in the form $M_{v}^{\prime}+\underline{M}^{\prime}=M_{g}^{\prime}$. It immediately follows from this that  $M_{v}^{\prime}=M_{g}^{\prime}-\underline{M}^{\prime}=2 M_{p}^{\prime} u^{2}\left(1-u^{2}\right)^{-1}$. For $M_{g}^{\prime}=M_{1}^{\prime}+M_{2}^{\prime} \quad$, W.  DRÖSCHER cites the model of a spring stretched between two mounts. When the spring is displaced, one part is always compressed and the other part is stretched. If the displacement occurs at the center of the spring, then the two spring energies correspond to $M_{1}^{\prime}$ and $M_{2}^{\prime}$, whereas if these energies are equal, only the background $\underline{M}^{\prime}$ remains as an analogue. Differentiating $M_{v}^{\prime}$ with respect to $u$ yields $d M_{v}^{\prime}=4 M_{p}^{\prime} u\left(1-u^{2}\right)^{-2} d u$  or $\left(d M_{v}^{\prime}\right) / M_{v}^{\prime}=2\left(1-u^{2}\right)^{-1} d u / u$. This relationship shows that, for sufficiently small values of $1-u^{2}$, significant amounts of energy can be converted when there is a change in $u$. Since $M_{v}$ corresponds to a potential energy, there are only two possibilities for the energy released as a result of the change in $u$ —which ultimately  stems from $m_{0}=m_{0}\left(t^{\prime}\right)$—there are only two possibilities:
