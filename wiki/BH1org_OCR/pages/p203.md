Inertial mass and the concept of the electric charge field are the  fundamental concepts underlying all material structures manifest in the physical $R_{3}$, which apparently exist solely due to $\overline{\operatorname{div}}_{6}{ }^{2} \bar{M}=\overline{0}$ solely to the change in certain field components in $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$, that is, $s_{(3)}$ and $s_{(4)}$. In all forms of hermeticism of possible world structures, therefore,  $s_{(3)}$ and (or) $s_{(4)}$ must in any case be hermetic, while  $s_{(1)}$ and (or) $s_{(2)}$ may be hermetic. If $A$ denotes the three  possible hermetries $s_{(1)}, s_{(2)}$ and $\left(s_{(1)}, s_{(2)}\right)$ of the $R_{4}$ subspace,  then there are apparently only three groups, each consisting of four hermetic forms, namely $H_{1}\left(s_{(3)}, A\right)$ as well as $H_{2}\left(s_{(4)}, A\right)$ and  $H_{3}\left(\left(s_{(3)}, s_{(4)}\right), A\right)$, which, however, reduces the number of possible hermetic forms to $Z_{2} \rightarrow Z_{3}=12$. 

Since hermetic forms of the type $A$ of $R_{4}$ can never exist on their own, but rather a hermeticity in $s_{(3)}$ and (or) $s_{(4)}$ occur simultaneously, it can be concluded that during a cosmogonic time interval, a hermetic state in the transcoordinates (relative to the $R_{4}$ ) and, secondarily, the forms $A$ are induced in $R_{4}$ and $R_{3}$, respectively. If we now assume in trace (19b) of (19) merely a hermitian property in $s_{(3)}$ or $s_{(4)}$, then, using the theorems from Chapter III, it follows that in this case all $\lambda_{p}(k, m)=0$, and thus there are no condensation stages in $x_{5}$  or $x_{6}$. However, if such condensation stages are absent in the  transcoordinates, then $s_{(1)}$ and $s_{(2)}$ of $R_{4}$  must also remain antihermetic. Thus, the classes $H_{1}$ and $H_{2}$ are omitted,  which once again reduces the possible forms of hermeticity to $Z_{3} \rightarrow Z_{4}=4$ of the  class $H_{3}$. If $H(x, y)$ symbolizes a hermeticity in $x$ and  $y$, then these four forms of hermeticism of physical solution manifolds of (19) are divided into the classes of imaginary condensations $a \hat{=} H\left(s_{(3)}, s_{(4)}\right)$ and $b \hat{=} H\left(s_{(2)}, s_{(3)}, s_{(4)}\right)$, as well as complex condensations $c \hat{=} H\left(s_{(1)}, s_{(3)}, s_{(4)}\right)$ and $d \hat{=} H\left(s_{(1)}, s_{(2)}, s_{(3)}, s_{(4)}\right)$; for in $c$ and $d$, the real unit $s_{(1)}$ also becomes hermetic.  Whereas in $a$ the $R_{4}$, in $b$ the $R_{3}$, and in $c$ only $x_{4}$ are antihermetic, in $d$ there is total $R_{6}$ hermetry. The hermeticity form $a$ characterizes self-condensations in the transcoordinates
