The constant behavior of these two angles from (5b) means that the corresponding worldlines are identical to their tangents. Thus, if $\beta=v / u=$ const is replaced by $\vec{Y}=$ const with $\vec{v}=$ const  in $R_{3}$ and $u=$ const, then there are there are no curved worldlines at all, and the homogeneous quadratic differential form of the metric $d s^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{6} d x_{k}^{2}$ characterizes the system $C$ (4) as geodetic due to its invariance with respect to $\hat{C}$ the system $C$ from (4) is characterized as geodesic.  In this case, there can be no distinguishable event structures in $R_{6}$, so that we are dealing with the case of an empty world. 

In general, $\vec{Y}\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{6}\right)$ is related to $\vec{v}\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{6}\right)$, as well as $\vec{u}\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{6}\right)$ and $\beta\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{6}\right)$ are functions of the $C$ coordinates, so that the two angles complementary to each other according to (5b) also become such functions, which then also applies to the tangent directions (5). However, this is only possible if the worldlines are curved, i.e., if separable event structures exist in the world. Nevertheless, there is the possibility of straight worldlines $\beta=$ const, namely when $v\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{6}\right)$ and $u\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{6}\right)$ are such that, due to the behavior of $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$,  such that the coordinate dependencies in the quotient $v / u$ cancel each other out, so that $\operatorname{da} \beta \beta=$ becomes constant. However, this is a special case that has no relevance whatsoever to the following investigations. If curved worldlines indicate event structures, then $R_{6}$ is no longer empty, but rather exhibits, as a world structure, metric deformations with respect to the $x_{k}$ of the $C$ system (4), which are of a non-Euclidean nature.
