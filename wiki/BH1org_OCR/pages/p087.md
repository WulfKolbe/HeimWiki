$\operatorname{div} \vec{G}=\sigma / \alpha$ from (*) yields $64 \pi \gamma \alpha=3$ as the proportionality factor in this relationship. 

Using $\quad \vec{G}=\vec{A}+\operatorname{grad} \varphi \quad$, $\quad \alpha \operatorname{div} \vec{G}=\sigma \quad \operatorname{der}$ becomes expression  $\operatorname{divgrad} \varphi=\sigma / \alpha-\operatorname{div} \vec{A}=\frac{4}{3} \pi \gamma\left(9 V \frac{d^{2} M}{d V^{2}}+6 \frac{d M}{d V}\right)$,  in which $M(r)=\mu(r)+M_{(0)}$. If, for the volume $V_{0}$ the field source $3 V_{0}=4 \pi r_{0}^{3}$ is assumed, and if only the outer region $V>V_{0}$, i.e., $r>r_{0}$, is under consideration, then in this outer region $d M_{(0)} / d V=0$, while $d \mu / d V \neq 0$ remains.  Although it is certain that $|\mu| \ll M_{(0)}$, the differential change in the field mass $d \mu / d V$ be of an order of magnitude that cannot be neglected, especially since the behavior of $\mu(r)$ is completely unknown. With $d M_{(0)} / d V=0$ for $r>r_{0}$ , $\operatorname{divgrad} \varphi$ therefore becomes $\mathrm{zu} \operatorname{divgrad} \varphi=\frac{4}{3} \pi \gamma\left(9 V \frac{d^{2} \mu}{d V^{2}}+6 \frac{d \mu}{d V}\right) \sim \sigma_{\mu}$, i.e., the differential field mass density. 

According to the gravitational-dynamic approach (*) through (*b), there are  three constants, namely $\alpha, \beta$ and $\omega$, of which $\alpha$ was determined by $64 \pi \gamma \alpha=3$ under the conditions of (*). Since, according to (*b), the relationship $\omega^{2} \boldsymbol{\alpha} \beta=1$ holds, only $\omega$ or $\beta$. 

It is unknown whether the gravitational field satisfies principle (c), but $\varphi(r)$ behaves additively with respect to the field source $M$. On the other hand, according to (c), due to the action quantum, there is neither a continuum of actions nor an energy continuum, although all energies satisfy the energy-matter equivalence. Consequently, there can be no continuum of ponderable masses either, which is entirely consistent with the empirical evidence for the atomistic nature of matter. The ponderable field source $M>0$ of the gravitational field is thus composed of $1 \leqq L<\infty$ atomic  elements $m(r)$ in accordance with $M=L m$, so that, regardless  of the open question of whether the gravitational field is quantized according to (c)  or not, there must in any case be elementary gravitational fields of the atomic  elements $m$ that can be described by $r \varphi(r)=\gamma m(r)$
