The basis of any theoretical mathematical analysis is always a system of experiences gathered by some observing human being. However, a person can only experience those aspects of the world that can be processed as an experience—that is, consciously lived—yet only what actually happens can be experienced. Events are consequences of event structures, such that the elements of the world that can be experienced by humans are events that can always be quantified (relative to some systems of measurement and reference points) by four numerical values when they concern events of the quantitativelymaterial part of the totality of the world. Regardless of how the elementary corpuscles might be speculatively interpreted, or which models may seem more or less appropriate, in our view, it can be stated quite generally that these elementary corpuscles are in any case event structures that stand out clearly from the background of indistinguishable events  in an empty world and are at the same time centers of interactions of whatever kind. For this reason, it seems justified to us to  attempt, in a radical manner, a multidimensional geometrization of the empirical-phenomenological facts and, from this perspective, to find indirectly a  mathematical scheme, which serves as an analogue to the empirical findings and permits a description of fundamental symmetries as well as higher-order symmetries, whose invariance requirements might possibly enable a relativistic basic dynamics.
