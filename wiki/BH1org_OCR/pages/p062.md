This resulted in the regular and orthogonal matrix $\hat{B}$ in $R_{4}$ of the Lorentz group of equivalent inertial frames, which leaves the sum of the  coordinate squares in $R_{4}$ invariant. Now, by means of a suitable double transformation of $\hat{B}$, an orthogonal quadratic matrix $\hat{C}$ of the quadratic matrix type 6 can always be constructed in $R_{6}$, such that the spacetime matrix segment consisting of $\hat{C}$ is identical to $\hat{B}$  and the orthogonality is satisfied by $\hat{C} \hat{C}_{\mathrm{T}}=\hat{E}$. The regularity of this six-row affinity matrix is determined by the behavior of the matrix determinant $\left|C_{i k}\right|_{6} \neq 0$, which results in $\operatorname{def} \hat{C}=0$ and therefore results in $\operatorname{rg} \hat{C}=6$—that is, the rank is identical to that of matrices of type and dimension—, which expresses the regularity of $\hat{C}$. For the matrix determinant, $\left|C_{i k}\right|_{6}=1$ can always be used. 

The distance $\vec{s}$ of a world point from the coordinate origin, namely $\vec{s}=\sum_{k=1}^{6} \vec{e}_{k} x_{k}$ changes when the system $C$ is transformed into an equi-axial system $C^{\prime}$ via $\hat{C}$ the transformation $\overrightarrow{s^{\prime}}=\hat{C} \vec{s}$ or in the direction $\vec{s}=\hat{C}^{-1} \vec{s}^{\prime}=\hat{C}_{T} \vec{s}^{\prime}$. Because of $\hat{C} \hat{C}_{T}=\hat{E}$, the indefinite quadratic form $s^{2}$ remains, but also $\vec{s} d \vec{s}$ and $d s^{2}$ are invariant under the regular affinities $\hat{C}$. These invariants are indefinite homogeneous quadratic forms whose inertia index 3 remains fundamentally invariant due to the $R_{6}$ signature ( + + + - - - ).  The invariant differential form $d s^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{6} d x_{k}^{2}$ is the metric of a world free of event structures in a pseudo-Euclidean $R_{6}$,  where the pseudo-Euclidean nature is characterized by the geodesics of the Cartesian $C$ system. 

This invariance of such homogeneous quadratic forms with respect to $\hat{C}=$ const—as a consequence of orthogonality—is the invariance with respect to the general group of parallel translations and coordinate rotations, so that this alone suffices to show that the $x_{k}$ prove to be 6 sets of values independent of one another, which is why these $x_{k}$ from (4) and (4a) can thus be identified as dimensions of a $R_{6}$.
