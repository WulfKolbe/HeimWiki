a) All 30 boundary metrons of $\Delta$ are oriented in such a way that all spin vectors point outward. Thus, $\Delta$ acquires an exogenous spin and is designated $\Delta_{+}$. 
b) All spin vectors point toward the interior of $\Delta$, resulting in an endogenous spin and $\Delta_{-}$. The worlds $R_{6( \pm)}$ that can be constructed from $\Delta_{ \pm}$,  namely the exogenous world and the endogenous world, are indeed completely isotropic,  but they would contain no structural potency; for if a  structural potency were present, then even the exchange  of two metronic elementary cells would have to result in a spin structure. It follows that the cases $a$ and $b$ do indeed represent metronic possibilities, but these are not realized in the empty world. 
c) It is possible to combine the two spin-oriented worlds $R_{6( \pm)}$ while satisfying the isotropy requirement in such a way that a metronic spin combination results. A cell $\Delta_{ \pm}$ is surrounded by $2 L_{2}=30$ cells $\Delta_{\mp}$, and each of these 30 cells  can be surrounded by $30-1=29$ cells $\Delta_{ \pm}$, for a total of $30 \cdot 29$ cells $\Delta_{ \pm}$, and so on. In this way, a spin-combined world $R_{6(0)}$ is created, in which each cell $\Delta_{+}$ is surrounded by 30 cells $\Delta_{-}$ and each cell $\Delta_{-}$ is in turn bounded by 30 cells $\Delta_{+}$. This  latter case satisfies the isotropy requirement, yet at the same time exhibits a structural power. For if two adjacent elementary cells are swapped, a spin structure has already emerged. 

In summary, with regard to the empty metronic world tensor, it can be stated that the hyperstructure is Euclidean and the fine-structure lattice is rectilinear and equidistant. In addition to this metronic isotropy, there is also a spin isotropy with respect to the superimposed spin orientation, so that an exogenous and an endogenous world $R_{6( \pm)}$ combine with the real structurally potent world $R_{6(0)}$. In this way, the spin-oriented hyperstructure of the real but empty metronic world tensorium is formed through the spin combination of the metronic elementary cells
