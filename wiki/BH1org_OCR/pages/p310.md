To illustrate the accuracy of these uniform mathematical relationships, the numerically calculated properties of some stable and metastable particle states are presented below in tabular form. The first column of this table contains the corresponding particle symbol, while the second column summarizes the six theoretical quantum numbers in the form $(k P Q \kappa) q_{x}(C)$, which characterize the particle in question. $k$ is the configuration number, which turns out to be the empirical baryon number increased by 1.  Furthermore, $P$ is the double isospin, and $Q$ is the double spin quantum number (thus, $Q$ is even for bosons and odd for fermions), while $\kappa$ is a quantum number introduced for theoretical reasons, which, as the so-called doublet number, characterizes the multiplicity of the isospin doublets for a value $k$. The electric charge quantum number (with charge sign) of the component $x$  of an isospin multiplet was symbolized by $q_{x}$, and the structure distributor was symbolized as a quantum number by $C$. The theoretical value of the structural distributor describes the distribution of $c$ and $d$ hermitian matrices in multiplets and, incidentally, turns out to be identical to the strangeness quantum number introduced for empirical reasons. Since this  quantum number can only take the values 0, 1, 2, and 3, the numbers—which in principle appear positive—are not separated by commas, but $q_{x}$ and $C$. In the third column of the table, the theoretical structural occupation numbers of the four internal structural configuration zones of an elementary particle are given in the form of quadruplets of numbers $n, m, p, \sigma$ , while the fourth column contains the respective theoretical mass $M$ of the particles in $M e V$.
