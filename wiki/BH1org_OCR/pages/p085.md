Gravitational field described by the scalar state function $r \varphi(r)=\gamma M(r)$  is described as a central field with the unknown position function $M(r)$ in $R_{3}$. Although the approximation $\vec{G} \rightarrow \vec{G}_{n}=\operatorname{grad} \varphi_{n}$ holds for the field vector, on the other hand, according to (*a), $\operatorname{rot} \vec{G} \neq \overrightarrow{0}$ always holds; for even if $\vec{\mu}=\overrightarrow{0}$ were true, due to $\mu(r) \neq 0$ in $M(r)=\mu(r)+M_{(0)}$ both $\sigma-\sigma_{(0)} \neq 0$ and $\vec{f} \neq \overrightarrow{0} . \mathrm{Da}$ are exactly $=\overrightarrow{0}$ , but $\vec{G} \rightarrow \operatorname{grad} \varphi_{n}$ and yet $\operatorname{rot} \vec{G} \neq \overrightarrow{0}$ remains, so $M(r)$ results in (*a) that $\vec{G}$ is additively composed of a gradient and a vorticity field of the form $\vec{G}=\vec{A}+\operatorname{grad} \varphi$ with $\operatorname{rot} \vec{A} \neq \overrightarrow{0}$, so that in the Newtonian approximation $\vec{G} \rightarrow \vec{G}_{n}$ must always be set to  $\vec{A} \rightarrow \overrightarrow{0}$ or $\operatorname{rot} \vec{A} \rightarrow \overrightarrow{0}$. This implies that  $\operatorname{rot} \vec{A} \sim \beta \vec{\mu}+\left(\sigma-\sigma_{(0)}\right) \vec{f} / \alpha \neq \overrightarrow{0}$ in (*a). For this reason, the static, undisturbed spherically symmetric gravitational field must be regarded as the steady state of a dynamic equilibrium of field fluctuations, the magnitudes of which, however, are likely to lie below the current limit of measurability, since it is certain that $\beta \vec{\mu}$ and $\sigma-\sigma_{(0)}$ will be very small in magnitude; since the unknown  function $\mu(r)$, as the field mass, remains very small in the observable  range in any case. This could be due to oscillations of real and virtual  structural levels, which were mentioned in the text according to (8b). 
If $b=$ is a constant, one-dimensional, freely available proportionality factor that is scaled by $\gamma$ is dimensioned with respect to $b \gamma$, then it can be assumed that, with high probability, the difference  between the source of the field $\vec{A}$ and $b \gamma \sigma_{(0)}$ is proportional to $b \gamma$ of the global field mass density $\sigma_{g \mu}=\mu(r) / V$. It would therefore be conceivable to adopt an approach that leads to $\operatorname{div} \vec{A}=b \gamma\left(\sigma_{g \mu}+\sigma_{(0)}\right)=b \gamma\left(\mu(r)+M_{(0)}\right) / V=b \gamma M(r) / V$. $\operatorname{Im}$ In the spherically symmetric case, $3 V=4 \pi r^{3}$ and $d V=4 \pi r^{2} d r$, so that the value $b=4 \pi / 3$ suggests itself for the constant; for then $\operatorname{div} \vec{A}=\gamma M / r^{3}$, which, with the substitution $r \varphi=\gamma M(r)$, allows for the  simple expression $\operatorname{div} \vec{A}=\varphi / r^{2}$.
