In what follows, the $R_{6}$ will be referenced to the system described in (4) and (4a)  described system $C$ of Cartesian right-handed world coordinates $x_{k}$. The points of the $R_{6}$, i.e., the world points, are then each identified by 6 coordinate values and, evidently due to the time counting $x_{4}$, must also be interpreted as events. However, this interpretation implies that a distinction must be made between  physically manifestable—i.e., manifest—events  $x_{5}=x_{6}=0$ and latent events $x_{5} \neq 0$ and (or) $x_{6} \neq 0$ must be distinguished. Here, the manifest events are the points of the spacetime subspace $R_{4}$ of the world, while the latent or virtual events are the world points outside this subspace. Linear manifolds of world points  are to be designated as worldlines. These worldlines do not  necessarily have to lie within $R_{4}$, but a worldline lying exclusively within the $R_{4}$ is characterized as a spacetime line that describes the temporal  evolution of a $R_{4}$point, and the totality of all spacetime lines in the $R_{4}$ characterizes the temporal cosmic motion of the real $R_{3}$ (physical universe). 

The $C$ system of world coordinates spans, in $1 \leqq p \leqq 6$ dimensions. Thus, there are initially 6 one-dimensional structures $V_{1}$ as coordinates $x_{k}$ and 15 surfaces $V_{2}$,  as well as 20 subspaces $V_{3}$ as well as 15 subspaces $V_{4}$ and 6 hypersurfaces $V_{5}$ of the world $R_{6}$. In all of these $V_{p}$, structures of the world are possible. Of the 20 subspaces $V_{3}$ , 18 are of a complex nature,  while one is spanned by the 3 imaginary coordinates  and another turns out to be the real physical $R_{3}$. In exactly the same way,  spacetime $R_{4}$ is also one of the 15 subspaces $V_{4}$.
