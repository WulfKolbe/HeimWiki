It can be concluded that, through the integration of $a$ into these Hermitian forms, a mapping process also takes place, specifically in the form of gravitons of the static gravitational fields of this $M q$. However, if $b, c$ and $d$ were to describe all possible $M q$ in the $R_{4}$, then this would mean that, in principle, there can be no gravity-free matter and that it remains impossible to shield existing gravitational fields; for no matter how they were designed, gravitational shields would, due to the additive nature of gravity, merely superimpose their ponderable or non-ponderable mass additively  with respect to the field source of the already existing source. 

For the $R_{6}$ signature, the possibilities expressed by $\alpha_{5}=\alpha_{6}=\sqrt{ \pm \tau}$ resulted in $(+++-++)$ and $(+++---)$, from  which—due to the empirical stability of atomic and stellar systems—the negative branch $i \sqrt{\tau}$ was selected, as expressed in (4). Had this decision remained open, structure (19) would also have resulted; however, in that case, solution structures of the form $e^{i x}=\cos x+i \sin x$ would be determined solely by $x_{4}$. Even  in this undecided case, a general solution would have been possible, whose special case $a$ depends only on $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$. In the third domain of validity, an abstract function space would also exist here that requires convergence of $\psi$ in the entire plane $\left(x_{5}, x_{6}\right)$,  i.e., $\iint \psi \psi^{*} d x_{5} d x_{6}<\infty$. However, using the solution to (19) in the third domain of validity with $\varsigma^{2}=x_{5}^{2}+x_{6}^{2}$, this convergence then yields the result $\varsigma^{2}<0$, which is only possible in the case of $(+++---)$, i.e., (4). However, this property (4), which is inherent in relation (19), is the necessary condition for a compact real $R_{3}$ as a unit $s_{(1)}$ in the $R_{6}$; however, atomic and stellar stabilities are possible only in such a subspace $R_{3}$, so that these stabilities may also be contained in (19) as an expression of the empirical principle (b).
