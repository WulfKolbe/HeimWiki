A cardinal number $m$ is defined as any representative $M$ from a class of mutually equivalent sets.  It is denoted below by $m=|M|$. In the case of a finite set, the cardinal number specifies the number of elements in the set. If the sets $M_{k}$ exist, then these sets form a set complex $K\left(M_{k}\right)$, thus defining a cardinal number complex $K\left(m_{k}\right)=\left|K\left(M_{k}\right)\right|$. These definitions  of abstract set theory are to be applied in the following to investigations regarding the temporal historicity of $n=1$ according to  $n>1$. 

At the time $t=0$ of the origin of the world, there definitely exists a $R_{6}$,  which is spanned by the coordinates $x_{k}$ metronized by $1 \leqq k \leqq 6$.  With a length of $x_{0}$ in $[m]$ there is the possibility of a normalization to dimensionless reference quantities $y_{k}=x_{k} / x_{0} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{m}$, for  which (as for the $x_{k}$ ) the structuring $\left\{x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}\right\},\left\{x_{4}\right\},\left\{x_{5}, x_{6}\right\}$ holds in accordance with (3c) due to the hermeticity following from (19). However, this means that there are also hermetic subspaces $R_{m} \subset R_{6}$ with $m=3, m=1$ and $m=2$ for $t=0$.
