In what follows, I will denote the respective indexing as $a \alpha$ or $a \beta$, or $d$, respectively, a quantity with respect to the respective complex hermetic form. In the case of $\varsigma_{(a \alpha)}$ with $\tau \rightarrow 0$ this neutral character becomes particularly evident at sufficiently large intervals $r$. Although in the metronic region $\tau>0$ the real condensation stages are bipolar—that is, ambiguous with respect to  sign—the same applies to $\tau \rightarrow 0$ all eigenvalues in the eigenvalue region $0 \leqq r<\varepsilon$ and also in the decay region $r>\varepsilon$  there is still a potential bipolarity $\psi_{k l}=\left(1-e^{ \pm \lambda_{k l} y}\right)^{-1}$ with  $y^{2}=r^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}$, which, however, disappears for $r \gg \varepsilon$ because, in the fourth validity region $\psi=\left(1-e^{\lambda r}\right)^{-1}$, $\lim _{r \rightarrow \infty} \psi=0$ must be required. $\psi$ thus becomes a unique field profile here, but one that can correspond only to a gravitational field exhibiting no polarity whatsoever. $r \gg \varepsilon$ is undoubtedly possible only because every spatial condensation, due to the architecture of the universe, is assigned a characteristic $\varepsilon$value, by which the structure in question is evaluated. The complex condensations described by case (aα) must therefore be interpreted as neutrocorpus particles in the physical $R_{3}$  , whose spectrum is described by the possible $\varepsilon$ values  in the $x_{5}$ range of the world. Obviously, the singular surface occurring for $\tau \rightarrow 0$ is the $R_{3}$-complement of this  $\varepsilon$ value, so that $r=\varepsilon$ must be interpreted as the spatial complementary radius of the neutrocorpuskles. For $\varsigma_{(a \alpha)}=\varepsilon$, the following holds in the range  $0 \leqq r<\varepsilon$ of the micromar eigenvalues $r=\frac{2 n_{r}+1}{2 n_{\varsigma}+1} \varepsilon$ according to Equation (25); however, this relationship always yields $n_{r}<n_{\varsigma}$, because it applies only to $r<\varepsilon$. The complementary radius that closes the micromare eigenvalue spectrum is therefore, in principle, not $(a \alpha)$ by systems of quantum numbers, so that $\varepsilon$ cannot be expressed as a number-theoretic function of integer indices either. However, this would mean that there cannot be a discrete spectrum of neutrocorpus in $R_{3}$, which contradicts experimental observations. It follows from this that the case $(a \alpha)$ is not valid; that is, for the spectrum of neutron-core nuclei, only the case $(a \beta) \hat{=} c$ can be considered.
