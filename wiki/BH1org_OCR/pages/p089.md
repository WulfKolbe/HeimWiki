Using the auxiliary function $\eta(r)=m / r$ the differential equation can be transformed, and this transformation allows for separation of variables and homogenization when $l n r$ is used as the new argument. The solution $r \underline{q} e^{-\underline{q}}=A$ is ambiguous with $\underline{q}=1 \pm \sqrt{1-4 a m / r}$, but due to the impossibility of infinite self-energy potentials, only the negative branch $r q e^{-q}=A$ with $q=1-\sqrt{1-\varepsilon \varphi}$ can be considered. Here, $r \varphi=\gamma m$ and $8 c^{2} \varepsilon=3$ were used for abbreviation. $A=$ const is the integration constant, which, due to $\varphi \rightarrow \varphi_{n}$ and $\left(\varepsilon \varphi^{\nu}\right) \approx 0$, can be determined for all integer $v>1 \mathrm{zu} 16 c^{2} A=3 \gamma m_{0}$. This constant is therefore a real number, but the left-hand side of the solution  $r q e^{-q}=A$ also allows for complex values, which, however, must not occur due to the real-number property of  $A$. For this reason, the radical must satisfy $1-\varepsilon \varphi \geqq 0$, whereby the equation for $\varphi=\varphi_{e x t}$  already sets the extremum according to $\varepsilon \varphi_{\text {ext }}=1$.

$\underline{\mu}_{g}=m(r)-m_{0}$. Due to the assumed spherical symmetry, $\underline{\mu}_{g}$ represents the field mass within the volume
