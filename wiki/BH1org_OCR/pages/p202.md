Non-interchangeability refers to three additional semantic units, namely $s_{(2)} \equiv x_{4}$ as well as $s_{(3)} \equiv x_{5}$ and $s_{(4)} \equiv x_{6}$. These $1 \leqq j \leqq 4$  semantic units $s_{(j)}$ are, according to (4), in no way considered semantic elements of the $R_{6}$ and the concept of hermeticism $\bar{\lambda}\left(V_{k}\right) \neq \overline{0}$ must therefore refer to these $s_{(j)}$. In this way, the set of $Z_{1}$ possible forms of hermeticism is reduced, through the restriction of a $V_{k}$ to $\binom{4}{k}$ possibilities, an initial reduction is achieved to the number  $Z_{1} \rightarrow Z_{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{4}\binom{4}{k}=15$ of possible forms of hermeticism. 
In what follows, for the sake of brevity, the metronic notation ${ }^{m} \bar{C}$ will be retained for an infinitesimal tensor scheme specified at the variance level. If, in (19b), the transition to the infinitesimal domain is carried out as in (8a) and 
$\bar{\lambda} \hat{\{ }\} \rightarrow \alpha\left(2 \bar{T}-\frac{1}{4}{ }^{2} \bar{g} T\right)$ is finally approximated macroscopically, then 
${ }^{2} \bar{T}={ }^{2} \bar{T} \times$ as the energy density tensor in $R_{6}$. However, the derivation of the heuristic relation (9) showed that, due to its hermiticity and the fact that it is at least semidefinite, $T \geqq 0$, according to  ${ }^{2} \bar{T}=s p\left({ }^{2} \bar{M} \times{ }^{2} \bar{M}\right)$, is the iteration of a uniform field tensor ${ }^{2} \bar{M}=-{ }^{2} \bar{M} \times$ in $R_{6}$. On the other hand, since, by virtue of $\operatorname{sp} C \widehat{\}} \rightarrow{ }^{2} \bar{R}$—that is, ${ }^{2} \bar{R}-\frac{1}{2}{ }^{2} \bar{g} R=\alpha^{2} \bar{T}$—the source-freedom property $\overline{\operatorname{div}}_{6}{ }^{2} \bar{T}=\overline{0}$ satisfies principle (a) as a consequence of the Hermitian nature of $R_{6}$, then, by 
${ }^{2} \bar{T}=s p\left({ }^{2} \bar{M} \times{ }^{2} \bar{M}\right)$, $\overline{\operatorname{div}_{6}}{ }^{2} \bar{M}=\overline{0}$ must also hold. In the non-Hermitian  $R_{4}$, a phenomenologically unified field tensor is likewise defined, whose four-dimensional vector divergence is proportional to a four-current consisting of  inertia and electric charge, if (d) is determined by $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{1}}\right)$ and $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{2}}\right)$ in the form (*) and (*a). For this reason, in $\overline{\operatorname{div}_{6}}{ }^{2} \bar{M}=\overline{0}$, the double derivative of the spacetime tensor segment and its partial derivatives according to $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$ can only describe the concepts of inertial mass and the electric charge field, because the four-dimensional vector divergence of the uniform tensor field must be interpreted as a doubly singular mapping of the $R_{6}$ structure.
