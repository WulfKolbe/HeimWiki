However, energy densities must be invariant under the allowed coordinate transformations; that is, they must form the components of a canonical energy density tensor—that is, a tensor of second rank—whose divergence-free property is expressed by (a). These 36 different components can only be accommodated in a six-row quadratic matrix scheme. Such a required six-row energy density tensor can only be represented in a space whose number of dimensions is identical to the number of rows of the tensor; for the rows and columns of a tensor are always vectors. 

For this reason, it stands to reason to extend the $R_{4}$ by two hidden dimensions $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$ to form a $R_{6}$, such that  spacetime $R_{4}$ is a subspace of this $R_{6}$ . Since the $R_{6}$ implies  spacetime as a subspace, the $R_{6}$ to be constructed below to be referred to as a “world,” because it must, in any case, contain all possible physical structures. The  Cartesian right-handed coordinate system $C$ of the $R_{4}$ must therefore, due to the existence of the $1 \leqq(i, k) \leqq 6$ world dimensions $x_{k}$, it must be extended by two  normal coordinates $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$ relative to the empty $R_{4}$ normal coordinates $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$ to the reference frame $C$  (also Cartesian and right-handed) of the world $R_{6}$. To construct this Cartesian system of world coordinates, it seems essential to first clarify the existence and algebraic  nature of the hidden world dimensions $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$ . 

If, in the general case, it is assumed that the system (3) is described in a general $R_{n}$, then (3) would consist of a number of $z_{1}=n^{3}$ spectra $\lambda_{p}(k, m)$, of which (3a) are empty, as indicated by $2 n^{2}$. Here, too, $n$ would appear twice for $k=m$ , so that the number of empty spectra is $z_{2}=2 n^{2}-n$.  The number of non-empty spectra according to (3a) would then be  $z=z_{1}-z_{2}=n^{3}-2 n^{2}+n=n\left(n^{2}-2 n+1\right)=n(n-1)^{2}$, and these  elements $\lambda_{(p)}(k, m) \varphi_{k m}^{(p)} \sim \varepsilon_{k m}^{(p)}$ would then, according to (a), due to the invariance, fill out the quadratic scheme of a tensor of rank $N$ and the  matrix defect $O$, which can be represented in a $R_{N}$ with $N>n$.
