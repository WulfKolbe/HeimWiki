According to the assumptions, a gravitational-dynamic process is to be described that manifests itself in $\vec{\mu}$. For this reason, it could be assumed that $\vec{\mu}$ propagates in $R_{3}$ either in the sense of so-called “gravitational radiation” or at least in the sense of a field disturbance of a potential field. With $a^{2} \neq 0$ in $0<\left|a^{2}\right|<\infty$ , one can heuristically write divgrad $\vec{\mu}=a^{2} \ddot{\vec{\mu}}$, although this is somewhat speculative. Because of $a^{2} \neq 0$, the negative sign of the divgrad operator can be included in $a^{2}$ , which, together with $a^{2} / \alpha=\beta=$ const $\neq 0$, yields the relationship $\alpha \operatorname{rot} \vec{G} \sim \alpha \beta \ddot{\vec{\mu}}+\vec{w}$. The ambiguity of the sign is now limited to the constant $\beta$. Apart from $\operatorname{rot}(\sigma \vec{v})$, $\vec{w}$ is essentially determined by the gradient of the unknown source distribution $\operatorname{div} \vec{\mu}$ and appears dimensioned in $k g m^{-3} s^{-1}$. For  this reason, it is appropriate to regard $\vec{w}$ to be regarded as a one-dimensional spatial field $\vec{f}(x)=$ that is constant with respect to $t$, which must be multiplied by a time-dependent factor. Undoubtedly, the source distribution of $\vec{\mu}$ is significantly determined by the temporal variation of the differential field density $\sigma_{\mu}$ , so that, heuristically, $\vec{w}=\dot{\sigma}_{\mu} \vec{f}(x)$ could be set, especially since this makes time integration possible. One obtains, if one takes into account that in $M(x, t)=\mu(x, t)+M_{(0)}$—due to $M_{(0)}=$—it is constant with respect to $x$ and $t$, the time dependence appears only in $\mu$, so it must be $\dot{\sigma}_{\mu}=\dot{\sigma}$,  $\alpha \operatorname{rot} \vec{G} \sim \alpha \beta \dot{\vec{\mu}}+\vec{f} \int \dot{\sigma} d t . \operatorname{Im}$ volume $V(x)=$ is constant, the density must be integrated from $\sigma_{(0)}$ up to the total differential density $\sigma$; for this interval encompasses the entire density distribution  of the source field $\sigma_{\mu}$. It follows that $\operatorname{rot} \vec{G} \sim \beta \dot{\vec{\mu}}+\left(\sigma-\sigma_{(0)}\right) \vec{f} / \alpha$. Here,  the divergence relation is given by divrot $=0$
