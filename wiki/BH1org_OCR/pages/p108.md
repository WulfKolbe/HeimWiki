If $p=2$ is set according to (15), then a surface $F$—that is, a  $R_{2}$, may no longer be regarded as a point continuum, but  must consist of a finite number $0<n<\infty$ of integer real  $n \leqq N$ of elementary surface quanta, the metrons $\tau>0$, which are bounded by the geodesic lines of $F$. This fact, however, necessitates a revision of infinitesimal analysis, regardless of $p=2$; for this analysis is founded on two limit relations, namely the integral and the differential quotient, whose existence presupposes an arbitrary divisibility of the surfaces—that is, a point continuum. If $y=f(x)$ is any function that is continuous in a domain $x$ of the $x, y$ - plane, then a region of the surface lying between two zeros,  which is bounded by a curve segment $f(x)$, an abscissa segment $a \leqq x \leqq b$ and the ordinates  $y(a)$ and $y(b)$, is described by the integral 
$F=\int_{a}^{b} y d x=\int_{a}^{b} f d x$, if $\tau=0$—that is, a continuum  $R_{2}$—is assumed. However, according to 
$\int_{a}^{b} y d x=\lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \sum_{v=1}^{n}\left[y_{v-1}\left(x_{v}-x_{v-1}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(y_{v}-y_{v-1}\right)\left(x_{v}-x_{v-1}\right)\right]=$ 
$=\frac{1}{2} \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} \sum_{v=1}^{n}\left(y_{v}+y_{v-1}\right) \Delta x_{v}=\lim _{v \rightarrow \infty} \sum_{v=1}^{n} \Delta F_{v}$, this integral is a limit. Here, $n \rightarrow \infty$ is equivalent to $\Delta F_{v} \rightarrow 0$, which contradicts Equation (15), since according to this equation, at most $\Delta F_{v} \rightarrow \tau>0$  with $n<\infty$ for $F<\infty$ at most, and this yields, for $\Delta F_{v}=\tau$, the  limit $\int_{a}^{b} f d x \equiv \lim _{\Delta F_{v} \rightarrow \tau} \sum_{v=1}^{n} \tilde{y}_{v} \Delta x_{v}=n \tau \quad$, resulting in $\quad \tilde{y}_{v}=\frac{1}{2}\left(y_{v}+y_{v-1}\right) \quad$.
