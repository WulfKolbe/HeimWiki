If the integration constant in $f=\underline{A} e^{2 \beta R}$ is $\underline{A}$, then the first integration yields $\frac{d \chi}{d R}+\frac{i}{4} \underline{\lambda}=\beta \frac{f-1}{f+1}$, which makes a further integration possible. We obtain $\chi+\frac{i}{4} \underline{\lambda} R-$ const $=\beta \int \frac{f-1}{f+1} d R=$  $=\frac{1}{2} \int \frac{f-1}{f+1} d \ln f=\frac{1}{2} \ln (1+f)-\frac{1}{2} \int \frac{d f}{f^{2}+f}=\frac{1}{2} \ln (1+f)+$  $+\int \frac{d(2 f+1)}{1-(2 f+1)^{2}}=\frac{1}{2} \ln (1+f)+\frac{1}{2} \ln \left(-\frac{1}{f}(1+f)^{2}\right)=$  $\frac{1}{2} \ln \left(-\frac{1}{f}(1+f)^{2}\right)$. Substituting into $\chi$ and raising to a power then yields $w \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{f}}(1+f) e^{-i \underline{\lambda} R / 4}$. Because of $\psi=w \vartheta$, $w$ describes the distribution  of the process projected onto $R_{4}$ within $R_{3}$. Since, due to the structure  of $\vartheta$—that is, the time evolution—$\lim _{t \rightarrow \infty} \vartheta \neq 0$ always remains constant, regardless  of which of the possibilities $R_{ \pm 4}$ is realized, $\lim _{R \rightarrow \infty} w<\infty$ must be required if $\lim _{t, R \rightarrow \infty} \psi<\infty$ is to remain. $\psi$ is a state function and must therefore converge in principle, because  an abstract function space must exist for $\psi$; that is,  convergence must be in the form $\lim _{t, R \rightarrow \infty} \psi=\lim _{t \rightarrow \infty} \vartheta \lim _{R \rightarrow \infty} w<\infty$, meaning that $\lim _{R \rightarrow \infty} w<\infty$ and $\lim _{t \rightarrow \infty} \vartheta<\infty$ must be satisfied. $w$ is uniquely determined by the path $w \sim\left(1+\underline{A} e^{2 \beta R}\right) \exp \left(-\frac{1}{4} \underline{\lambda} R-\beta R\right)$, because the real subspace $R_{3}$ is uniquely determined (the ambiguity $R_{ \pm 4}$ refers  to $x_{4}$). However, this unique path of $w$ satisfies the convergence condition only if $\operatorname{Re} \beta=0$ and $\operatorname{Im} \beta \geqq 0$; for only then does $w$ become a complex periodic function.
