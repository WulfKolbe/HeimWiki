This gravitational field causes the phenomenon of gravity,  but this gravity can always be transformed if  the heavy body is in a system of force-free gravitational motion (e.g., free fall), while the gravitation causing this gravitational force remains invariant as a scalar field $\varphi_{n}$. However, with respect to the empty $R_{3}$, this state must be an energetic state, so that, according to the energy-matter equivalence, a field mass must also be assigned to the gravitational field state, which in turn corresponds to an additional gravitational field source. The consequence of this would then be a slight deviation in the spatial distribution of the field from $\varphi_{n}$, which, although far below any measurable limit, could nevertheless appear under suitable conditions. Such a correction can only take effect in the form that $M=M(r)$ is composed of $M_{0}=M\left(r_{0}\right)$ and the gravitational field mass $M(r)-M_{0}$ in the relevant volume. The Newtonian function $\varphi_{n}$ would therefore have to be corrected to $r \varphi=\gamma M(r)$ and $\vec{G}=\operatorname{grad} \varphi \mathrm{zu}$. In the micromare range, the question to be decided is whether the gravitational field is a quantum field or not. In the case of the electric field, the interaction appears as a ponderomotive force, whereby the quantum character of this field finds its expression in the uncertainty relation of canonically conjugate quantities. If one now imagines the ponderomotor force to be caused by a corresponding gravitational field, then, according to Bondi, it is not clear why—merely by changing the state that causes the ponderomotor force—the canonically conjugated quantities of this general uncertainty relation, which are independent of the specific field state, should no longer be subject to it. Be that as it may, in any case there must be, in the micromar range, elementary gravitational fields of the $M q$, which are elementary because the $M q$ represent material  ultimate units and the gravitational field apparently behaves additively with respect to its field source.
