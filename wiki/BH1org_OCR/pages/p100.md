Extension of $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{2}}\right)$ by $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{1}}\right)$ and $(\mathbf{c})$, which phenomenologically describes the gravitationally determined structure of space. Principle (c)  is expressed in $\varsigma<\infty$ due to $h>0$ and atomism  $m_{0}>0$. Neglecting (c) would result in $h=0$ and $m_{0}=0$  and thus result in $\varsigma \rightarrow \infty$, because in (12a) the denominator becomes 0 to the third power,  while the numerator becomes 0 to the second power. In the differential equation leading to (11), in the form $\varepsilon\left(\frac{d \varphi}{d r}\right)^{2}+4 H\left(\frac{d \varphi}{d r}+H \varphi\right)=0$ with $r H=\frac{1+x}{1-x}$ and $x=r / \varsigma$, neglecting (c) thus implies the limits $\lim _{\varsigma \rightarrow \infty} H=1 / r$,  that is, $\varepsilon\left(\frac{d \varphi}{d r}\right)^{2}+\frac{4}{r}\left(\frac{d \varphi}{d r}+\varphi / r\right)=0$. Neglecting $\left(\mathbf{d}_{1}\right)$  would imply not only $M(r)=M_{0}$ for all $r$, but also imply $c \rightarrow \infty$ and  $\omega \rightarrow \infty$ according to (10). This results in a decomposition of $R_{4}$ into $R_{3}$ and $t \sim x_{4}$ as well as the transition of the group $\hat{B}$ into the Galilean group of $R_{3}$ and $\lim _{c \rightarrow \infty} \varepsilon=0$, thus resulting in $\frac{d \varphi}{d r}+\varphi / r=0$. However, for this differential equation, $r \varphi=$ const always holds, which is the characteristic of $\varphi=\varphi_{n}$ according to $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{2}}\right)$ in the form $r \varphi_{n}=\gamma M_{0}$. This form $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{2}}\right)$ thus appears to be historically conditioned, whereas (11)  appears to be an extension of the empirical data $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{2}}\right)$ through the empirical findings $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{1}}\right)$  and (c). 

We can now attempt to investigate the extrema of the  gravitational spatial structure (11). Since $A$ and  $(1-r / \varsigma)^{2}$ are real since they are squares, this also forces the realness $\operatorname{Im}\left(q e^{-q}\right)=0$ of the left-hand side of (11), which requires  $1-\varepsilon \varphi \geqq 0$. If $r=R>\varsigma$ is the location of this realness boundary  of relation (11), then $\varphi(R)=\varphi_{\text {ext }}=1 / \varepsilon$ holds, that is,  $1-\varepsilon \varphi(R)=0 \quad$ or $\quad \varepsilon \gamma M(R)=R(1-R / \varsigma)^{-2} \quad$ according to (11b).  On the other hand, $q(R)=1$ and therefore $R(1-R / \varsigma)^{-2}=e A$ according to  (11), which, upon comparison, yields $\varepsilon \gamma M(R)=e A$. Using the approximation  $2 A=\varepsilon \gamma M_{0}$, this becomes $M(R)=M_{0} \cdot \frac{e}{2}$, which, for the reality bound $x=R / \varsigma$, yields the quadratic determination equation

> Erweiterung von $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{2}}\right)$ durch $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{1}}\right)$ und $(\mathbf{c})$, welche die gravitativ be dingte Raumstruktur phänomenologisch beschreibt. Das Prinzip (c)  findet seinen Ausdruck in $\varsigma<\infty$ wegen $h>0$ und der Atomistik  $m_{0}>0$. Eine Vernachlässigung von (c) hätte $h=0$ und $m_{0}=0$  und damit $\varsigma \rightarrow \infty$ zur Folge, weil in (12a) der Nenner in dritter,  aber der Zähler in zweiter Potenz zu 0 wird. In der zu (11) führenden  Differentialgleichung in der Fassung  $\varepsilon\left(\frac{d \varphi}{d r}\right)^{2}+4 H\left(\frac{d \varphi}{d r}+H \varphi\right)=0$ mit $r H=\frac{1+x}{1-x}$ und $x=r / \varsigma$ be deutet also die Vernachlässigung von (c) den Limes $\lim _{\varsigma \rightarrow \infty} H=1 / r$,  also $\varepsilon\left(\frac{d \varphi}{d r}\right)^{2}+\frac{4}{r}\left(\frac{d \varphi}{d r}+\varphi / r\right)=0$. Eine Vernachlässigung von $\left(\mathbf{d}_{1}\right)$  würde nicht nur $M(r)=M_{0}$ für alle $r$, sondern auch $c \rightarrow \infty$ und  $\omega \rightarrow \infty$ nach (10) bedeuten. Dies hat eine Dekomposition des  $R_{4}$ in den $R_{3}$ und $t \sim x_{4}$ sowie den Übergang der Gruppe  $\hat{B}$ in die Galileigruppe des $R_{3}$ und $\lim _{c \rightarrow \infty} \varepsilon=0$, also $\frac{d \varphi}{d r}+\varphi / r=0$  zur Folge. Für diese Differentialgleichung gilt aber stets $r \varphi=$ const ,  was das Kennzeichen für $\varphi=\varphi_{n}$ nach $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{2}}\right)$ in der Form $r \varphi_{n}=\gamma M_{0}$  ist.
