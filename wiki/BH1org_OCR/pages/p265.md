If $t=T \ll \theta / 2$ is the current  world age $T \approx 1,72 \cdot 10^{115}[s]$, then the cosmogony of matter must have taken place during the interval $0<t<T$. Apparently, prior to the time $T_{1}<T$ of a cosmogony of matter, some global symmetry group applies that cannot be broken as long as $\tau(t)>0$  is sufficiently large. Since $\dot{\tau}<0$ holds for $\dot{D}>0$, $\tau$ decreases with the age of the universe until $\tau_{1}=\tau\left(T_{1}\right)<\tau\left(t<T_{1}\right)$ is reached. It is conceivable that, at $\tau_{1}$, a symmetry group breaking could initiate the cosmogenesis of matter at $t=T_{1}$. 

For the current values, using the momentary natural constants from (15), $\tau_{T}=\tau(T) \approx 6,15 \cdot 10^{-70}\left[m^{2}\right]$,  as well as $D_{T}=D(T) \approx 6,03 \cdot 10^{125}[m]$ and 
$n_{T}=n(T)=\pi D_{T}^{2} / \tau_{T} \approx 1,86 \cdot 10^{321}$. During the  interval $0 \leqq t<T_{1}$, the $R_{3}$ is completely free of matter, whereas in the  interval $T_{1} \leqq t^{\prime} \leqq T$, matter exists in the universe. Although criteria for determining $\tau_{1}$—and thus $T_{1}$—are still lacking for the time being,  it can certainly be assumed that $T-T_{1} \ll T$ holds. Certain empirical indications suggest that this interval spans several $10^{10}$  years—possibly around $10^{18}[s]$. This would still mean $T /\left(T-T_{1}\right) \approx 10^{97}$, that is, $T-T_{1}$ is extremely  small, especially relative to the current geological era $T$. For the interval $T_{1} \leqq t^{\prime} \leqq T$, therefore, according to Vol. II, V, 6, despite the very high but decreasing values of $\dot{D}, \dot{\tau}$ and $\dot{n}$, $T-T_{1}$ can be approximated very well by $\left(D(T)-D\left(T_{1}\right)\right) /\left(T-T_{1}\right)=\delta D / \delta t^{\prime} \approx 0$ as well as 
$\left(\tau(T)-\tau\left(T_{1}\right)\right) /\left(T-T_{1}\right)=\delta \tau / \delta t^{\prime} \approx 0$ and 
$\left(n(T)-n\left(T_{1}\right)\right) /\left(T-T_{1}\right)=\delta n / \delta t^{\prime} \approx 0$, i.e., $\dot{D} \approx 0, \dot{\tau} \approx 0$ and $\dot{n} \approx 0$, are satisfied.
