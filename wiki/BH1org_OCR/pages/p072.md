On the other hand, the $T_{i k}$ are the components of a canonical energy-density tensor, which, due to the general interpretation of (6)  must depend on both the $g_{i k}$ and their first and second partial  coordinate derivatives. By analogy with general relativity, there is also in the $R_{6}$—under the assumption (6)—exactly one divergence-free structure tensor, which is constructed from the $g_{i k}$ and their first and second partial  coordinate derivatives, namely $R_{i k}-1 / 2 g_{i k} R$,  so that, with the proportionality factor $\alpha=$ const, this fact can be summarized in $R_{i k}-1 / 2 g_{i k} R=\alpha T_{i k}$. This Hermitian tensor equation satisfies principles (a) and (b), while (d) is contained in the forms $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{1}}\right)$ and $\left(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{2}}\right)$ as a special case in $R_{4}$ and $R_{ \pm 4}$, respectively. Given the matrix traces $T=T_{k}^{k}$ and $g_{k}^{k}=6$, the matrix trace $-2 R=\alpha T$ follows, that is, $R_{i k}=\alpha\left(T_{i k}-g_{i k} T / 4\right)$. 

Due to the transition of the microstates $\varepsilon_{i k}^{\prime} \rightarrow T_{i k}$ into the macroscopic field continuum of the structure $g_{i k}$ or $\Gamma_{k m}^{i}$, respectively, there must be  in the micromare region a convergent function $\left\{\begin{array}{cc}i \\ k & m\end{array}\right\}$ in the  $R_{6}$ for which the transition to the macromare region consists of $\left\{\begin{array}{cc}i \\ k & m\end{array}\right\} \rightarrow \Gamma_{k m}^{i}$. However, if $\left\{\begin{array}{cc}i \\ k & m\end{array}\right\}$ exists, then, by analogy to the non-Hermitian $R_{4}$ structure, there must also be a functional operator $C$ whose components $C_{p}$ depend on the partial coordinate derivatives and the $\left\{\begin{array}{cc}r \\ p & q\end{array}\right\}$ in such a way that, upon transition to the macroscopic continuum, the operator action becomes $C_{p}\left\{k^{p}{ }_{m}\right\} \rightarrow R_{k m}$. Due to the Hermitian nature of the $\left\{\begin{array}{cc}i \\ k & m\end{array}\right\}$ and their convergence, there must therefore exist an abstract function space such that $\left\{\begin{array}{cc}p & m\}\end{array}\right.$ describes the action of a state operator on a convergent state function.
