All metronic functions $\varphi(n)$, whose metronic argument is one-dimensional, can therefore be interpreted geometrically as state functions that assign to each element—that is, to each metron of a simple tensorium characterized by $n$ , a metronic state value $\varphi$ that varies discontinuously with the metron numbers $n$.  This concept of the metronic function and the simple metronic domain must undergo a dimensional extension in addition to the metric one, for every metronic function—as a number-theoretic function of integer indices—can always be interpreted as a sequence of numbers, yet in addition to simple sequences $L=1$, there are also multiple number sequences $L>1$ and, consequently, metronic functions $\varphi\left(n_{i}\right)_{1}^{L}$ that depend on $L$ metronic arguments $n_{i}$. If the $\xi_{(i) k}$ are related to $1 \leqq k \leqq p$ the geodesic coordinates of the  simple tensor $i$, then $n_{i}\left(\xi_{(i) k}\right)_{1}^{p}$ would hold, i.e., every sequence $n_{i}$ of the  argument denotes a sequence of metrons, that is, a simple metronic tensor $n_{i}\left(\xi_{(i) k}\right)_{k=1}^{p}$, because the dimension number of $\tau$ is $p$. Furthermore, the $\xi_{(i) k}$ are always geodesic coordinates  of the relevant tensor $n_{j}$, because, according to the fundamental theorem of metron theory, all metrons are geodetically bounded and satisfy the principle of continuous connection. According to the foregoing, these geodetic coordinates fundamentally form a geodetic metronic grid $\xi_{(i) k}\left(n_{i}\right)$, which, due to $\tau>0$, is determined solely by the integer metron number $n_{i}$ of the simple tensor.  Accordingly, since every sequence $n_{i}$ of the $L$-dimensional metronic argument represents a simple tensor, to set $\varphi\left(n_{i}\right)_{1}^{L}=\varphi\left(\left(\xi_{(i) k}\right)_{k=1}^{p}\right)_{1}^{L}$, which, after introducing a general coordinate system, geometrically leads to the representation $\varphi\left(n_{i}\right)_{1}^{L}=\varphi\left(x_{k}\right)_{1}^{p L}$. However, these $p L$ coordinates $x_{k}$ cannot possibly all be independent of one another, i.e., $p L$ is generally not valid, despite its formal agreement with Equation (15b).
