Over time, the question of the fundamental structures of matter has led to a number of theories among high-energy physicists. Quantum field theory, combined with the application of symmetry principles (including that of gauge fields), can be regarded as the unifying concept of the currently best-known theories in elementary particle physics. These include quantum electrodynamics, which describes the interaction between light and matter; the Weinberg-Salam model, which unifies the electromagnetic and weak interactions; the quark model, which pertains to the strong interactions, and the supergravity theories, through which  scientists hope to unify all four interaction fields and provide a  representation of all elementary particles discovered experimentally to date, while simultaneously applying the principle of broken  symmetry. Finally, ten-dimensional superstring theory is  being used in an attempt to overcome the difficulties encountered in supergravity theories. 

The quark model can indeed describe the internal structure of hadrons; however, details regarding the internal structure of quarks or leptons—to the extent that they exist—cannot currently be specified.  Another problem is that the electroweak and strong interactions differ significantly from the gravitational interaction. On the one hand, there are phenomenological manifestations embedded in Euclidean space; on the other hand, deviations from a Euclidean spatial structure (Riemannian geometry) point to physical phenomena such as the gravitational field and mass.
