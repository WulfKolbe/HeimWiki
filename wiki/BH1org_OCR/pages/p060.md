The construction of (3c) suggests that the sets of $R_{6}$ coordinates are structured in the form $\left\{x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}\right\},\left\{x_{4}\right\},\left\{x_{5}, x_{6}\right\}$. In the  case of $R_{6} \rightarrow R_{12}$, this structure most likely takes the form $\left\{x_{p}, x_{p+1}\right\}$ if the numbers 7, 9, and 11 are assigned to $p$. If this is assumed and it is taken into account that, according to (3d), for $n=0$ through $n=2$, the value is always $N=2$ for the positive branch and $N=0$  for the negative branch of (3d), then, in the sense of $R_{4} \rightarrow R_{6} \rightarrow R_{12}$, the relation $R_{6} \cup R_{2} \rightarrow R_{6} \cup R_{0}$ would be possible. Given the  specified structure of the set of $R_{12}$-coordinates, the $R_{12}$ is thus transformed back into a $R_{6}^{\prime}$. Accordingly, the transitions $R_{4} \rightarrow R_{6} \rightarrow R_{12} \rightarrow R_{6}^{\prime}$ with $R_{4} \subset R_{6} \subset R_{12}$ and $R_{4} \subset R_{6}^{\prime} \subset R_{12}$ apply.
