The denominator $U=1+w^{2}$ reaches a maximum because the masses are then at their minimum level. For $U \rightarrow \mathrm{U}_{\text {max }}$, therefore, 
$\underline{M} \rightarrow M$ or $M_{d}^{4} / M_{c}^{4}=\frac{1}{U_{\text {max }}}$ holds. 
In $U=1+w^{2}$, the sine falls within the interval $0 \leqq \sin ^{2} \varphi \leqq 1$, so that  for $U_{\text {max }}$, it can only be $\left(\sin ^{2} \varphi\right)_{\text {max }}=1$ or $\sin \varphi_{\text {max }}= \pm 1$. Because of $\varphi \sim 1 / z$, $\varphi_{\max } \pi z_{\min }=N$ implies that $\sin \left(\frac{N}{\pi z_{\min }}\right)= \pm 1$ or $\frac{N}{\pi z_{\text {min }}}= \pm \arcsin \mathrm{l}= \pm \pi(2 p+1) / 2$ with an integer $p \geqq 0$ and $N \geqq 2 p+1$. Because of $z \sim Q_{ \pm}^{2}$, $z_{\min }>0$ implies the existence  of a smallest electric charge $Q_{\text {min }}^{2}$, for which the determining equation $\pm N(2 p+1)^{-1}=\pi^{2} z_{\text {min }} / 2$ holds. Substituting $z \rightarrow z_{\text {min }}$ into $9 \hbar z=2 \pi^{2} Q_{ \pm}^{2} R_{-}$ then yields $\pm 9 N(2 p+1)^{-1}=$  $=\pi^{4} Q_{\text {min }}^{2} R_{-} / \hbar$, where, due to $\operatorname{Im} Q_{ \pm}=0$—and thus also $Q_{\text {min }}^{2}>0$—the positive branch must be chosen. From $N \geqq 2 p+1$, it follows that for the absolutely minimal charge field, $N=2 p+1$ holds. Let us set $Q_{\text {min }}(N=2 p+1)=\varepsilon_{ \pm}$ as this elementary charge field, for which a natural constant $\pi^{2} \varepsilon_{ \pm}= \pm 3 \sqrt{\hbar / R_{-}} \neq 0$ results. Setting this constant yields a numerical value for $\varepsilon_{ \pm}$ that is only +0.125% above the measured value of the electron charge.  Using the integers $q \geqq 0$, the existence of an elementary charge field thus implies the quantization $Q_{ \pm}=q \varepsilon_{ \pm}$ of electric charges. If $\varepsilon_{ \pm}$ is substituted with $N=2 p+1$ is substituted into $\varphi_{\text {max }}$ and the  quantization $Q_{ \pm}$ is used in $w^{2}$, then it follows that 
$U_{\text {max }}-1=\left(z^{2} \sin ^{2} \varphi\right)_{\text {max }}=\left(2 \pi^{2} Q_{ \pm}^{2} \frac{R_{-}}{9 \hbar}\right)^{2}=4 q^{4} / \pi^{4}$ or, using $\eta_{q}^{4}=1 / U_{\text {max }}=\left(1+4 q^{4} / \pi^{4}\right)^{-1}$, the representation of the $d$terms $\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{d}}=\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{c}} \eta_{q}$, where $\eta_{q} \sqrt[4]{4 q^{4}+\pi^{4}}=\pi \quad$, $\quad Q_{ \pm}=q \varepsilon_{ \pm} \quad$, and  $\pi^{2} \varepsilon_{ \pm}= \pm 3 \sqrt{\hbar / R_{-}}$are consequences of principles (b) and (c). 

Since for $q=0$, $\eta_{q}=1$ and $M_{d}=M_{c}$ always hold, but $q$ specifies the electric charge quantum number, the interpretation of the $d$ Hermitian operator is that of a ponderable particle, but one associated with an electric charge field—
