Regardless of such cosmological considerations, the problem arises—when describing elementary material structures—of determining the inertial contributions of $\bar{\lambda}_{0} \perp \vec{Y}$. The logical starting point for the deduction is therefore the lepton triad $v_{L}, e_{ \pm}, v_{B}$ in their minimal masses, whereby it is irrelevant whether $v_{L}$ and $v_{B}$ can actually appear in $R_{4}$ and be interpreted as real neutrino types. This triad apparently consists of unit structures in which $( \pm p)_{x}$ with $x \hat{=}(c, d)$ occurs only in the charge $\underline{N}=1$. According to V., 1., and (96b), the masses of these unit structures, $m_{L B}$ and $m_{e}$, are explicitly known; likewise, according to VI., 4., the protosimplex structure with the prototrope conjuncture is known. Although it seems impossible to determine the $\underline{\bar{\lambda}}$magnitudes of the inertia of individual $( \pm p)_{x}$ to be determined, the synmetronic structures form typical proto-simplex combinations that recur in all terms of the spectral function and thus also characterize its deepest level. Thus, for mere ponderable inertia, the combination is  $( \pm(1,4))_{x},(+7)_{x}$, and for the charge field in the case of $d$-Hermetry,  `( \pm(2,3,5)),(-6)). For $\mu_{S}$, however, the only remaining possibility is $\mu_{S}=m_{B}-m_{e}$.
