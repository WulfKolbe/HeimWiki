# Elementary Structures of Matter, Volume 2 — page 325

Cited from the Begriffsregister as `(2, 325)`. English is a DeepL translation; the German original follows each block and is the authority.

Despite the highly approximate nature of the idealization of terrestrial data (e.g., an isotropic sphere instead of a level asteroid with an anisotropic density profile, etc.), the theoretical data still appear comparable to the actual geomagnetic field, especially since the Tertiary-induced generator effect remains entirely unexplained. For this reason, one could infer that $\beta > 0$, with all the consequences this entails for gravitational dynamics. A decision in favor of $A$ or $A^{\prime}$ , however, appears impossible on this basis, due to the  high uncertainty in the paleomagnetic $B_{\text{max}}$ determination and  the associated uncertainty regarding the present-day  $S$ factor. For further empirical verification of relation (106a), the magnetic fields of stellar objects whose angular momentum density can be determined astrophysically would be suitable. As early as 1928, SWANN and LONGACRE ${ }^{(1)}$ speculatively postulated, on empirical grounds, a proportionality between magnetic flux density and angular momentum density. More recently, it has become possible to determine the magnetic fields of celestial bodies and their angular momentum densities relatively well in several cases. Thus, SIRAG ${ }^{(2)}$ also provided an empirically derived—though not theoretically derived—formula for such stellar magnetic fields,  which is intended to cover the empirical aspects of such magnetic fields just as, for example, the purely empirical formula proposed by BELLERT ${ }^{(3)}$ does with regard to cosmological redshift and its anomalies. It should be noted here that this empirical relationship, adapted to astrophysical conditions, coincides with the derived relationship (106a)—up to a numeric factor of little relevance—under the assumption $\mu=S=1$.  For example, equation (106a) with $\mu=S=1$ reproduces the solar magnetic field relatively well, but it reproduces that of the pulsar Harx - 1.78 vir very well. It may be that the extreme properties of such pulsars satisfy particularly well the approximation conditions under which (106a) was developed.
