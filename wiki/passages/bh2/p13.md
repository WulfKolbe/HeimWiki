# Elementary Structures of Matter, Volume 2 — page 13

Cited from the Begriffsregister as `(2, 13)`. English is a DeepL translation; the German original follows each block and is the authority.

Regarding the notation used in the formula set, it should be noted that, in general, the standard defined by DIN was followed, provided that the scope of application was that of infinitesimal calculus, where $\tau=0$. There were only three instances where this standard was deviated from for reasons of practicality. Thus, $C$ is denoted as a matrix by the symbol `{ }^{\text {^ }}` in the form $\hat{C}$. If the matrix elements $C_{i k}$ are those of the complex algebraic field, then this means $C_{i k} \neq C_{i k}^{*}$; however, if an index transposition is adjoint to this complex conjugation, this is expressed by the symbol × in the form $\left(C_{i k}\right)^{\times}=C_{k i}^{*}$. Thus, if $\hat{C}=\hat{C}^{*}$ is a matrix over the real algebraic number field, then $\hat{C}^{\times}$ simply denotes the transposed form, but in the complex case, $\hat{C}^{\times}$ becomes the complex-conjugate transposition. 

Instead of the commutator symbol $[a, b]=a b-b a$, which is defined by the norm, the old symbol $(a \times b)_{ \pm}=a b \pm b a$ is used  (which, with the upper signs, also applies to the anticommutator); for $[a, b]$ had already been assigned by the author to a different concept in earlier studies. Finally, instead of the very practical nabla operator, the old operator symbols div, grad, and rot are also used in $R_{3}$, because when working in higher-dimensional spaces $R_{n}$, the operator becomes indexable by the dimension $n$  is indexed according to $\operatorname{div}_{n}, \operatorname{grad}_{n}$, and $\operatorname{rot}_{n}$. Here, however, $\operatorname{div}_{n}$ describes only the scalar divergence of a vector field in $R_{n}$. If it is the vector divergence of a tensor field that reduces the tensor degree $m$ by 1, then this is denoted by $\overline{\operatorname{div}}_{n}$ , whereas the tensor divergence of the field in question—which extends the tensor degree $m$ to $m+1$—is denoted by $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}_{n}$. These operators, however, are rarely needed in this text. 

In the domains of validity $\tau>0$, on the other hand, a notation not covered by DIN is used, though it was explained in Volume I.
