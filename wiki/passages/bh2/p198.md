# Elementary Structures of Matter, Volume 2 — page 198

Cited from the Begriffsregister as `(2, 198)`. English is a DeepL translation; the German original follows each block and is the authority.

Only one flukton is described, but this encompasses several fundamental flows that differ by $\underline{\alpha}$ with respect to the signature permutations. This signature isomerism of the elementary flows—that is, the occurrence of multiple condensers within the classes of a coupling group—inevitably stems from the fact that at most three structural units correlate, but there are always four condenser signatures. According to Equations (76) through (76b), there is, in turn, for each system of these signature-isomeric fundamental flows a system of enantiostereoisomeric flow aggregates $F_{v}=\binom{\alpha \beta}{\kappa \lambda} F\left(q_{j}\right)_{1}^{v} \omega$ of cyclic condenser flows with respect to the systems $q_{j}$ of condenser sources and sinks, from which it follows that, in general, the number $N$ of elements in a system of signature-isomeric elementary flows can be very high. If, on the other hand, these condenser flows are identical in their morphology, then $N$ is determined solely by the permutation possibilities of the  condenser signature in question. 

If we are dealing with a general Hermitian form $x \hat{=}(a, b, c, d)$ in $R_{6}$ such that the correlator $\hat{\gamma}_{(x)}=\left({ }^{2} \bar{\gamma}_{(\mu \nu)}\right)_{3}$ has $n \leqq 9$ distinct elements as a result of the application of a sieve operator,  which differ from ${ }^{2} \bar{E}$, then a total of $n^{2}$ condensers can be formed from these elements, of which $Z=n(n-1)$ differ in basis and contrasignature, and thus describe correlations. The remaining $n$ have the form $\left[\begin{array}{c}\widehat{\alpha \beta}+ \\ -\alpha \beta\end{array}\right]_{+}$, for which there is no correlation and thus no condenser flows.  These condensers, which are not capable of forming flux aggregates, nevertheless constitute metronic condensation stages; specifically, for $\alpha=\beta=3$, they constitute the short-range field of spatial condensation that does not occur in metric stages. These condensations without condenser fluxes, however, enclose the flux aggregates and should therefore be referred to as shield fields. First, there are the singular shield fields $\alpha=\beta$, which encompass the Hermetic characteristic, while the shield fields $\alpha \neq \beta$ are referred to as correlative shield fields
