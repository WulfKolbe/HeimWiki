# Elementary Structures of Matter, Volume 2 — page 285

Cited from the Begriffsregister as `(2, 285)`. English is a DeepL translation; the German original follows each block and is the authority.

Inductively, $2 V_{\alpha}=V_{\varepsilon}$ and $V_{a}=V_{\varrho \varrho}$. For the upper bounds, with respect to $H$, $V_{\beta}$ is proportional to that portion of $V_{\omega}$ that corresponds to the $p$-zone $j=3$. However, since there are only three zones $j \leqq 3$  in the bulk $R_{3}$ region of the structure, one could conclude that $3 V_{\beta} \sim V_{\omega \varepsilon}$, where the proportionality factor can only be the coupling constant of the electromagnetic field coupled at $j=3$ (since $q>0$ ), i.e., the fine-structure constant $\alpha$ of light, so that $3 V_{\beta}=\alpha V_{\omega \varepsilon}$ must be set. In the case $G$, on the other hand, it can be speculatively  assumed that $V_{b}$ is the potential $V_{\varrho \ell}$ of the spacetime minimal condensation $k=q=1$, because for all $q>0$ the condenser fluxes determining the charge field must exist at least in this minimal form; otherwise, $q=0$ would hold. Thus, $e V_{b}=V_{\varrho \ell}^{\prime}(q=1)$ holds. As the limit of the Metron integral associated with $B_{4}$ in $G$, we have in any case $W_{a}=V_{\omega \omega}$, where such an integral would initially extend infinitesimally up to $V_{\varepsilon}$ , followed by a further integration from $V_{\varepsilon}$ to $W_{b} \sim V_{\delta \delta}$. In $W_{b}=C_{k} V_{\delta \delta} \mathrm{must be}$, the proportionality factor given by $\left(2 C_{k}\right)^{k+1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+Q_{\sigma}\right)$ takes into account the continuation of the zone $j=4$. Since $Q_{\sigma}=2^{s-1}-1$ with $s=k^{2}+1$, and because $k^{2}-1=(k+1)(k-1)$, this $\sigma$-connection implies that $C_{k}=2^{k-2}$, so $W_{b}=2^{k-2} V_{\delta \delta}$. When performing the metronic integrals, the metronic variation $\partial_{e}$ of the relevant potential must always be added to the lower limit, because, according to the rules of metronic integration, this variation is only compensated for when the metronic integral is evaluated.
