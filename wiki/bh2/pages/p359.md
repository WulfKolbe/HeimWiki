In (112), $M(N)$ depends not only on the integer variable $N \geqq 0$ but also on the operation parameters $\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2}, \alpha_{3}, W, a, b$ and $\phi$, which, in turn, according to the corresponding relations, depend solely on the elements of $\hat{I}$ of the relevant $V_{6}$—that is, according to (102), to $k, P, Q, \kappa, C, q_{x}$ and $\varepsilon$. In this set of quantum numbers, in turn, according to relations (99) through (100a), the numbers $C$ and $q_{x}$, but also $Q$ and $\kappa$ are reduced to $k, P$ and $\varepsilon$ , whereby the possible $P$ values are in turn limited solely by $k$ via $G=k+1$. This means that, ultimately, all possible  $M(N)$ from (112) in all properties, aside from the positive integers of the resonance orders, ultimately trace back solely to the configuration number $k$ and the decision $\varepsilon= \pm 1$. For $N>0$, the configuration zone occupancies (using the alternative notation here) $n_{1}=n, n_{2}=m, n_{3}=p$ and $n_{4}=\sigma$ are not invariants in the sense of (102), but they are in the case of $N=0$. In contrast to the digits from $\hat{I}$, the $n_{j}$ are integer  occupancy parameters that, together with $N$ and $\hat{I}$, describe all properties  of the general $c$ or $d$ hermeticism $M(N)$.
