Thus, the number of elementary fundamental fluxes of a flucon is determined—independently of the hermetic  subspace—solely by the square of the capacitor number or configuration number $k$. However, this implies $Z_{\kappa}=Z_{k}=2^{k^{2}}$, where $k>0$ always remains constant if a condensation state exists at all in $R_{6}$, which is also expressed by (72). On the other hand, $k$ and thus $\kappa$ is fundamentally independent of the coordinate grid of $R_{6}$ and therefore also independent of time, so that $k=\operatorname{const}\left(R_{6}\right)$ holds. Furthermore, since $\kappa=k^{2}$ holds, this condenser index $k>0$ is a characteristic of a temporally constant metric configuration and contour. However, based on this interpretation, it follows that the time-invariant protosimplex sets $Q_{j}$ of the configuration zones of a stratonical term can be determined from $Z_{k}$. 

To determine the time constants $Q_{j}$ using $j \leqq 4$, one must proceed inductively. If there are material elementary structures that, with $\underline{N}=1$ represent unit structures for which $n_{j}=0$ also holds, then there are obviously temporally stable framework structures whose configuration zone occupancies depend solely on the $Q_{j}$ , and whose masses are constant over time. In fact, two elementary structures can be empirically detected that can be interpreted as temporally constant framework structures, namely the electron $e^{-}$ and the proton $p$.
