From the twelve capacitor systems $F$ for the four hermeticity forms, all capacitor maxima follow from $\partial F=0 \mathrm{zu} N^{+}$, because here the correlation reaches its minimum—and thus also becomes extreme. The coupling maximum, on the other hand, is determined by the disappearance of the condensers—that is, the external-field selectors—via $F=0$, from which its respective position can be determined via $N^{-}$. $N^{ \pm}$ is characterized, due to the design of the $F$, by the dependence $N^{ \pm}(\underline{\alpha}, \underline{\lambda})$ on the capacitor signature; but when the condition $\underline{\lambda}=\underline{\alpha} \lambda$ is satisfied, this is reduced to $N^{ \pm}(\underline{\alpha})$. Consequently, the positions of the condensation and coupling extrema in $R_{6}$ (relative to the second domain of validity) are determined solely by the correlation exponents of the respective condenser signature. If the respective condenser system $F$ satisfies not only the existence of $N^{ \pm}$, i.e., cyclicity, also satisfies the condition of field activation of the condenser fluxes, then for the four forms of hermetry, the condenser fluxes $\quad N_{a}^{+}(1) N_{a}^{-}, \quad N_{b}^{+}(1) N_{b}^{-}, \quad N_{b}^{+}(2) N_{b}^{-}, \quad N_{b}^{+}(1,2) N_{b}^{-}$,  $N_{c}^{+}(1) N_{c}^{-}, N_{c}^{+}(1,3) N_{c}^{-}, N_{d}^{+}(1,2) N_{d}^{-}, N_{d}^{+}(1,3) N_{d}^{-}, N_{d}^{+}(2,3) N_{d}^{-}$, and $N_{d}^{+}(1,2,3) N_{d}^{-}$ can be described, because [1] and [2] of type $d$ appear only as $\left[\begin{array}{ll}1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1\end{array}\right]_{+}$ and $\left[\begin{array}{ll}2 & 2 \\ 2 & 2\end{array}\right]_{+}$—that is, without correlation—and thus cannot form a condenser flux; since $N^{-}$ is not defined in this case. Furthermore, since $N^{ \pm}$ depends only on the $\underline{\alpha} \underline{\lambda}$ and these exponents differ only by proportionality factors when the respective indices of a condenser signature are permuted, the following correspondences arise between the condenser fluxes $N_{a}^{+}(1) N_{a}^{-} \equiv N_{b}^{+}(1) N_{b}^{-} \equiv N_{c}^{+}(1) N_{c}^{-} \equiv A, N_{b}^{+}(2) N_{b}^{-} \equiv B$,  $N_{b}^{+}(1,2) N_{b}^{-} \equiv N_{d}^{+}(1,2) N_{d}^{-} \equiv C, \quad N_{c}^{+}(1,3) N_{c}^{-} \equiv N_{d}^{+}(1,3) N_{d}^{-} \equiv D$,  as well as $N_{d}^{+}(2,3) N_{d}^{-} \equiv \boldsymbol{E}$ and $N_{d}^{+}(1,2,3) N_{d}^{-} \equiv F$, so that only these  six types of condenser flows need to be examined in the coupling structures of the  hermetic forms.
