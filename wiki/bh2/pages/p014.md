To investigate which metronic structural functions of $R_{3}$ can be deduced whose finite real limit values for $\tau \rightarrow 0$ yield the elements of the coefficient matrix (110d); for this causes the relations (109) through (111) to lose their rather heuristic character. The  still-open physical questions were specified in (114), provided one restricts oneself to the stationary state of dynamic equilibrium. Finally, it should be noted that in the first edition of Volume I, which is now out of print, both the derivation of a constant factor and that of a unified relationship for the lifetimes of the elementary corpuscles were announced for Volume II. Since Volume I is to be republished in any case in a supplemented and improved edition, it seemed more reasonable for practical reasons to investigate this constant factor within the framework of a phenomenological theory of gravitational dynamics. Although a heuristic relationship exists with regard to the lifetimes, it by no means qualifies as a unified one, because it applies only to the terms $N=0$, but  not to all $N>0$ . Instead, we intend to derive a unified relationship for the full band widths and another for the magnetic spin moments; however, this requires knowledge of $Q(N)$ from (114). For this reason, the derivation of these band widths  and magnetic spin moments, as well as the solution to the problem in  (114), are reserved for a later study, as are a clarification of the  interaction processes and a cosmogony of matter. 

In the present work, frequent reference has been made to numbered relations in Volume I. To facilitate reading, the equations of physical content numbered in Volume I are listed in an accompanying booklet in the form in which they will be derived in the planned new edition of Volume I. 

In accordance with this new edition, in this paper ${ }^{2} Q_{(\mu \nu)}^{(\kappa \lambda)}$ is designated as the correlation tensor, or as the coupling tensor, if a scalar coupling selector exists; whereas the term “composite selector” is used for the variance-level factor that generates this tensor.
