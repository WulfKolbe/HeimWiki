Although the triad (96b) describes the elementary configuration patterns of $c$ and $d$ hermeticism, the spectrum of complex hermetics is divided by (98a) into the two spectral regions $k=1$ and $k=2$, , whereby the invariant configuration number, in turn, according to (90a), limits the interval of possible invariant $P$ values from (89). This means, however, that there are already a total of three multiplets for $k=1$ and as many as four for $k=2$, each of which consists of $I=P+1$ components $y$. In addition to the elementary configuration patterns that constantly repeat in (27)—which are expressed in (96b) as a correction to (32) via phenomenological inertial masses— there must therefore be another system of fundamental structural patterns whose zone occupancies $(n m p \sigma)$ are determined by fundamental invariance properties of the $c$ and $d$ terms. These invariance properties, in turn, must find their expression in sets of  quantum numbers which, on the one hand, like $k P$ and $Q$ according to (98a)  and (89) describe the spin-isomorphic multiplets, and on the other hand,  enable, via $q=0$ or $q \neq 0$ enable the assignment of individual terms to the $c$ or $d$ spectrum. Furthermore, according to (93), the  decision $\varepsilon$ with respect to $R_{4}^{ \pm}$ can be used as such an invariant property. For the multiplets, therefore, the theorem $(k P Q)_{\varepsilon}$ definitely applies, whereas for the individual components $y$, $q_{y}$ of the charge field (29) must still be used if $q_{y}$ the charge quanta are the number $q$ of the component $y$ with a charge sign, i.e., $\left|q_{y}\right|=q$.  While these quantum numbers are in any case subject to invariant conservation principles and symmetries, these  symmetries do not necessarily apply to the parameters $n_{j}$ of the $1 \leqq j \leqq 4$ structural configuration zones or the structural parameter quadruplets $(n m p \sigma)$ do not necessarily have to hold if the protosimplex charge $\underline{N}>1$ holds.
