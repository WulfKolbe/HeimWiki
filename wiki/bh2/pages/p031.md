The operating state is achieved by $\vec{X}=\vec{Z}=\overrightarrow{0}$ in the $R_{4}$. With $\operatorname{div}_{4} \vec{f}=\operatorname{div}_{4} \vec{F}=0$, $\vec{K}=0$ holds, so that $\vec{Z}=\overrightarrow{0}$ also applies to the $R_{4}$ -  charge current, whereas the neutral current always exists due to $\vec{N} \neq \overrightarrow{0}$. For simplicity, let $\vec{f}=$ be constant and $\vec{F}=$ be constant with respect to time $t$ , and let $\dot{\varphi}_{,{ }_{\varepsilon}}=\dot{\psi}_{,{ }_{\eta}}=0$ hold, while $\dot{W}_{,{ }_{\varepsilon}} \neq 0$ and $\dot{W}_{, \eta} \neq 0$ remain unchanged. Under these conditions, in addition to $\vec{K}=\overrightarrow{0}$ and $\vec{Z}=\overrightarrow{0}$, the neutral $R_{4}$-current $\vec{X} \neq \overrightarrow{0}$, because its complement $\vec{N} \neq \overrightarrow{0}$ also remains. As a partial time derivative of this complementary neutral current, we then obtain $\vec{N}=\vec{e}_{5} \dot{W}_{, \eta}-\vec{e}_{6} \dot{W}_{, \varepsilon}$, where it can be observed that whenever $W_{, \varepsilon} \neq 0$ and $W_{, \eta} \neq 0$ exist, $W_{,^{\prime} \eta} \neq 0$ can also exist, provided that $W$ does not depend linearly on $\varepsilon$ and $\eta$. On the other hand, due to the commutativity of partial derivatives, 
$W_{,^{\prime} \eta}=W_{,^{\prime} \gamma^{\prime} \varepsilon}=G \neq 0$, so that applying the chain rule yields $\stackrel{\dot{W}}{\rightarrow}_{, \eta}=G \dot{\varepsilon}$ and $\dot{W}_{, \varepsilon}=G \dot{\eta}$, i.e., 
$\vec{N}=G\left(\vec{e}_{5} \dot{\varepsilon}-\vec{e}_{6} \dot{\eta}\right)=-i G\left(\vec{x}_{5}-\vec{x}_{6}\right)$. For $\vec{N}=\overrightarrow{0}$, the $R_{4}$-complement $\dot{\vec{X}}=\overrightarrow{0}$ follows, that is, the steady state $\vec{X}=$ const $\neq \overrightarrow{0}$. If $\dot{\eta}>\dot{\varepsilon}$, then, due to $\vec{X}=-\vec{N}$, this implies a rise of $\vec{X}>\overrightarrow{0}$  by $\vec{N}<\overrightarrow{0}$, however, $\dot{\varepsilon}>0$ counteracts this increase until, at $\dot{\varepsilon}=\dot{\eta}$,  the steady-state condition $\vec{N}=\overrightarrow{0}$ with $\vec{X}=$ const is reached in $R_{4}$. On the other hand, $\dot{\varepsilon}>\dot{\eta}$ would indeed cause $\vec{N}>\overrightarrow{0}$ and the decrease in the neutral $R_{4}$-current $\vec{X}<\overrightarrow{0}$. 

Since the $R_{4}$ flows describe both time-varying and stationary organizational states due to the internal and external dynamics of all $R_{4}$ structures, $\varepsilon$—and thus $x_{5}=i \varepsilon$—be considered a measure  for evaluating such time-varying organizational states. As early as antiquity (especially in Aristotle), organizational states with a temporal finality were defined as  entelechies. If this concept of entelechy, as described above, is redefined in such a way that it takes quantitative physical conditions into account, then the entelechy of a material system would be defined as its organizational state as a function of time.
