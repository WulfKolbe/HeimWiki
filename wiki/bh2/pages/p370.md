The $\bar{y}_{(\alpha)}^{(\beta)}$ and $\bar{w}_{(\alpha)}^{(\beta)}$ can be combined into two partial pseudocompositions. If $a$ denotes the running summands  of one partial composition and $b$ those of the other, then  for the metronic $R_{6}$ vector fields, $\bar{C}=\sum_{a} \bar{y}_{(\alpha)}^{(\beta)}$ and  $\bar{D}=\sum_{b} \bar{y}_{(\alpha)}^{(\beta)}$, so that only two systems of equations,  $\underline{\partial}_{m} C_{p}-\underline{\partial}_{p} C_{m}=\sum_{a} \underline{\lambda}_{p} \underline{y}_{m}-\sum_{a} \underline{D}_{p} ; \underline{w}_{m}$ and  $\underline{\partial}_{m} D_{p}-\underline{\partial}_{p} D_{m}=\sum_{b} \underline{\lambda}_{p} \underline{y}_{m}-\sum_{b} \underline{D}_{p} ; \underline{w}_{m} \quad$, need to be analyzed.  For further description, a stationary stability state is assumed such that only the components in $R_{4}$ remain relevant, i.e., the following holds for all summands $\underline{y}_{5}=\underline{y_{6}}=0$ and $\underline{w}_{5}=\underline{w}_{6}=0$,  but also $\underline{\lambda}_{5}=\underline{\lambda}_{6}=0$ and $C_{5}=C_{6}=D_{5}=D_{6}=0$, whereas in the case of dynamically stable equilibrium, no neutral or electrically charged currents can flow either, so that all changes in $\bar{C}$ and $\bar{D}$ must vanish in $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$. Under  these conditions, the transition $\tau \rightarrow 0$ to the third validity region can now be carried out. First of all, the  transition to this region means that the subphysical structures  of internal correlations no longer appear. Approximatively, therefore, all coupling tensors ${ }^{2} \bar{Q}_{(\alpha)}^{(\beta)} \rightarrow{ }^{2} \overline{0}$ can be neglected, which—according to (58a) and covariant Hermitianity—immediately results in $\underline{D}_{p} ; \underline{w}_{m}=0$ for all $p$, including $\sum_{a} \underline{D}_{p} ; \underline{w}_{m}=\sum_{b} \underline{D}_{p} ; \underline{w}_{m}=0$. Finally, due to the transition $\tau \rightarrow 0$,  the limit relation $\lim _{\tau \rightarrow 0}{\underset{\mathcal{X}}{\mathfrak{X}}}_{k} F_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}} f_{i}=f_{i, k}$ holds for a function  $F_{i} \rightarrow f_{i}$ at $\tau \rightarrow 0$. If we now assume for the $1 \leqq p \leqq 4$ components in $R_{4}$ that all $\underline{\lambda}_{p}$ become identical to $i \lambda_{p}$, so that $\underline{\lambda}_{p}=i \lambda_{p}$ can be set, then $\lim _{\tau \rightarrow 0} \sum_{a} \underline{\lambda}_{p} \underline{y}_{m}=$ also follows  $=i \lambda_{p} \lim _{\tau \rightarrow 0} C_{m}=i \lambda_{p} g_{m}$ and $\lim _{\tau \rightarrow 0} \sum_{b} \underline{\lambda}_{p} \underline{y}_{m}=i \lambda_{p} \lim _{\tau \rightarrow 0} \mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{m}}=\mathrm{i} \lambda_{p} h_{m}$  in the third domain of validity $g_{p, m}-g_{m, p}=i \lambda_{p} g_{m}$ and $h_{p, m}-h_{m, p}=$  $=i \lambda_{p} h_{m}$.
