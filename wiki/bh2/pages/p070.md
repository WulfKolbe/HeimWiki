It can only be stated that $D_{f}$ and $D_{m}$ structure the proto-universe in some way, which in turn somehow initiates cosmic motion and determines the structure of the world itself. 

However, if the $R_{3}$ unfolds from its initial state in the course of cosmic motion according to $D(t)>D_{0}$, then there must also  be a final stage, that is, an end time $T_{e}$ with a final diameter $D_{e}=D\left(T_{e}\right)$. For $D_{e}$, however, there can only be two fundamental possibilities: namely, $D_{e} \rightarrow \infty$ or $D_{e}<\infty$. In the case of $D_{e} \rightarrow \infty$, $\tau_{e}=0$ would apply, and thus $\gamma_{e}=0$, as well as $h_{e}=0$ and $\mu_{(e) 0}=0$, but not $\varepsilon_{(e) 0} \rightarrow \infty$, whereas $\mu, \kappa$ and $\vartheta$ remain as elementary constants of every condensation. However, $\vartheta>0$ in the final state $D_{e} \rightarrow \infty$ also implies an inherent contradiction, just as does the existence of elementary charges $\kappa>0$ without a lower limit of the mass spectrum (27), which also vanishes with $\tau_{e}=0$, whereas the upper limit $\mu \sqrt[4]{2}$  remains, but does not generate a gravitational field due to $\gamma_{e}=0$. Furthermore, the lower terms of the spectral function must continue to exist as mass quanta, even though $h_{e}=0$ holds. Finally, there would also be $\omega_{e}=c_{e}=0$,  but according to the unitary transformation matrix in the Euclidean $R_{6}$  would result in singular points of infinity for all states, even though the Euclidean properties hold exactly due to the fact that $D_{e} \rightarrow \infty$ vanishes. These logical contradictions, however, disappear if $D_{e}<\infty$ is assumed. Such an assumption would imply that $D(t)$ increases with $A>0$ up to a maximum value of $D_{\max }=\Lambda<\infty$, but after reaching $D \rightarrow \Lambda$, it would reverse to $A<0$ if $D_{e}<\infty$ applies to the eschatological end-time universe. The logical contradictions resulting from $D_{e} \rightarrow \infty$ are thus resolved by $D_{0} \leqq D(t) \leqq A<\infty$, $A \geqq 0$, and $\dot{\tau} \leqq 0$, but $\Lambda>D(t) \geqq D_{e}>0$, as a contractive connection, prevents $A<0$ and $\dot{\tau}>0$.
