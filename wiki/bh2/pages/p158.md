The four forms of condensation described above  $\hat{\gamma}_{(a)}, \hat{\gamma}_{(b)}, \hat{\gamma}_{(c)}, \hat{\gamma}_{(d)} \hat{=}(a, b, c, d)$ differ essentially in the following groups of properties: namely, metric properties $m$, structural $s$, and configurational $k$. The metric  properties refer to the algebraic properties  of the hermetic forms, that is, to $\operatorname{Im} \mu \neq 0$, with $\operatorname{Re} \mu=0$ or  $\operatorname{Re} \mu \neq 0$, whereas $s$ encompasses the basis and contrasignatures of the respective  coupling structures. The configurative class of properties, on the other hand, contains all properties of the condenser source systems. There are clearly defined affinities among the four forms of condensation with respect to the three groups of properties. Affinities of the first class $(m),(s),(k)$ are conceivable, as well as second-class affinities $(m s),(m k),(s k)$ and third-class affinities $(m s k)$, whereby the four configuration forms $\binom{4}{2}=6$ are capable of forming combinations corresponding to the second class. In the third affinity class, there are apparently no affinities at all; for in this class, the affinity would be so strong that it would become the identity. In the second class, on the other hand, according to the preceding analysis, the affinities $a(m s) b$, as well as $b(s k) c$ and $c(m s) d$, exist, whereas the first class comprises $a(s) c$ and $b(s) d$.
