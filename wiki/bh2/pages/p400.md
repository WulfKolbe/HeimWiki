Burkhard Heim, a graduate in physics, was born in Potsdam in 1925. In 1944, an explosion occurred during laboratory work at the Reich Institute for Chemical Technology in Berlin, in which he lost both hands, became almost completely blind, and suffered severe hearing loss. Beginning in 1945, he studied chemistry; in 1949, he began studying theoretical physics in Göttingen, completing his studies in 1954 with a Hauptdiplom. Since 1949, he has been conducting independent research on a general field theory in which all physical fields and their sources are uniformly described as dynamic properties of purely geometric structures. This theory was developed over the past three decades under—as one can easily imagine—extremely difficult external conditions and has been partially published in several stages since 1975. These consist mainly of material presented at the Imago Mundi conferences and published both in the conference proceedings and in monographs by Resch Verlag in Innsbruck. Interest in Heim’s theory is growing steadily and has been particularly stimulated—even among experts—by *Elementary Structures of Matter*, Vol. 2, 1984, as well as by the second edition of *Elementary Structures of Matter*, Vol. 1, 1989, which have received special recognition even among experts.
