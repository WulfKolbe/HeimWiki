In the expression for $\alpha_{3}$, the fine-structure constant $\alpha$ appears in the function $H$. Using $4 \pi^{2} e_{ \pm}= \pm 3 \sqrt{2 \vartheta \hbar / R_{-}}$ and the quantum electrodynamic statement $e_{ \pm}^{2} \sim \alpha^{\prime}$, a theoretical value for $(2 \pi)^{5} \alpha^{\prime}=9 \vartheta$ (29a); however, the numerical value shows that $\alpha^{\prime}$ is well below the tolerance limit of the measured value. Nevertheless, this discrepancy amounts to -0.0015%. However, since the theoretical value $e_{ \pm}$ matches the empirical value without any error deviation, this discrepancy can only be attributed to the fact that the quantum electrodynamic relation $e_{ \pm}^{2} \sim \alpha^{\prime}$ used is incomplete, so that $\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha$ is -0.0015\% lower. 

The system emitting the photons of the $H$ spectral series—that is, the $H$atom, is described in its ground state by the electrically stationary interaction between the proton $p\left(e_{+}\right)$ and the electron $e^{-}$ as the $s$ term on the $K$ shell. $p$ and $e^{-}$, however, have been empirically identified as fundamental material units—that is, as ponderable stratonic $d$hermetries. This means, however, that $p$ and $e^{-}$  must in any case exhibit the fourfold contouring of the $R_{3}$ by the configuration zones $j \leqq 4$. Furthermore, the  $d$character of both $R_{3}$ configurations is characterized by the internal electric charge field components of $e_{ \pm}$, which are expressed by (98).
