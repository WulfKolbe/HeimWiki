However, this relationship can be divided by $\left(E-\delta^{\prime} E-\delta E+\delta_{1} E\right)$. Since $\left|\delta_{1} E\right| \ll\left|E-\delta^{\prime} E-\delta E\right|$ and  $\left|\delta_{1} E-\delta E\right| \ll\left|E-\delta^{\prime} E\right|$ remain constant, a good approximation is  $\left(E-\delta^{\prime} E\right)\left(E-\delta^{\prime} E-\delta E+\delta_{1} E\right)^{-1} \approx 1$ and  $\left(E-\delta^{\prime} E-\delta E\right)\left(E-\delta^{\prime} E-\delta E+\delta_{1} E\right)^{-1} \approx 1$. This means, however, that $c^{2}\left(\delta_{1} E-\delta^{\prime} E-\delta E\right)\left(E-\delta^{\prime} E-\delta E+\delta_{1} E\right)^{-1} \approx \int_{s^{\prime}}^{\varrho^{\prime}} \vec{g}^{\prime} d \vec{r}^{\prime}+\int_{S}^{\varrho} \vec{g} d \vec{r}-$  $-\int_{\varrho}^{s_{0}} \vec{g} d \vec{r}=K$ (let $K$ be a reduction), and here it must be taken into account that for the path of light $\cos \left(\vec{g}^{\prime}, \vec{r}^{\prime}\right)=-1$ between $s_{0}^{\prime}$ and $\varrho^{\prime}$, as well as  $\cos (\vec{g}, \vec{r})=-1$ between $\varrho$ 'and $\varrho$ along $s$ due to $g>0$, but $\cos (\vec{g}, \vec{r})=+1$ between $\varrho$ and $s_{0}$ applies. This means, however, that $K=\int_{\varrho}^{S} g d r-\int_{s_{0}^{\prime}}^{\varrho^{\prime}} g^{\prime} d r^{\prime}-\int_{\varrho}^{s_{0}} g d r=\varphi(s)-\varphi(\varrho)-\varphi^{\prime}\left(\varrho^{\prime}\right)+\varphi^{\prime}\left(s_{0}^{\prime}\right)-$  $-\varphi\left(s_{0}\right)+\varphi(\varrho)=\varphi(s)+\varphi^{\prime}\left(s_{0}^{\prime}\right)-\varphi\left(s_{0}\right)$, because $d \varphi=g d r$ and $\varphi(\varrho)=\varphi^{\prime}\left(\varrho^{\prime}\right)=0$ always hold. The key now is to eliminate $\varphi$.  Equation (11) contains $\varphi(r)$ only implicitly, but an unknown continuous position function $f(r)$ for which $r=r_{0}$ holds at the surface of the gravitational field source $f\left(r_{0}\right)=1$. 

Using this function, $\varphi(r)=\frac{\gamma M_{0}}{r} f(r)\left(1-\frac{r}{\varrho}\right)^{2}$ can then be written, although $f(\varrho)=0$ may remain. From relation (11), it follows that $q=1-\sqrt{1-\frac{3 \varphi}{8 c^{2}}}$ or $\frac{3 \varphi}{8 c^{2}}=1-(1-q)^{2}=q(2-q)$
