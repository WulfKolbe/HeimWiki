The relativistic basic dynamics arising from c- and d-hermeticism obviously stems from a series of symmetries (possibly including those of higher order) that involve at least the already known quantum numbers $(k P Q)_{\varepsilon} q_{y}$ . 

By analogy with the multiplet $I=P+1$ of the space-spin isomorphisms with $I \leqq I_{\text {max }}=G+1=k+2$ according to (90a), a multiplet of the integral space-spin component (89a) of the straton spin (89)  could also be defined in the form $I_{R}=2 J+1=Q+1$ with respect to the spinor or tensor terms. For the $Q$ enumeration, there are obviously two possibilities; either $J=\underline{J}$ changes such that tensor and spinor terms always alternate, so that $\partial \underline{J}=1 / 2$ holds; or the tensor or spinor character is preserved, which implies $\partial J=1$. For the possible variations of $Q$ , $\partial Q=2$ or $\partial \underline{Q}=1$ applies due to $Q=2 J$. If the term character is preserved, then it would be natural to represent $\partial Q$ as the difference between the number $G=k+1$ from (90a) of the quasi-corpuscular structural regions of an internal nature and $Q$, so that $\partial Q=G-Q$ holds for all values of the $P$ interval. However, due to the conservation of the term character, $\partial Q=2$ holds, i.e., $Q=G-2=$ $=k-1$. Furthermore, in the two $P$intervals, there are still points $\underline{P}$ where a change in the term character could occur, such that at $P=\underline{P}$ the multiplet appears doubled, that is, the isomorphism with respect to $Q(P)=k-1$ identifies one multiplet $P=\underline{P}$ and $\underline{Q}(\underline{P})$ the other multiplet. With respect to $\underline{Q} \neq Q$, one could assume that $\underline{Q}$ is the portion of the Metron integral $S I_{R} \partial I_{R}$ corresponding to each of the $G$ quasi-corpuscular internal structural regions, so that $\underline{Q}=\left(\boldsymbol{S} I_{R} \partial I_{R}\right): G$ holds, which would be identical to $G \not \partial \underline{Q}=I_{R} \partial I_{R}$. Since $\partial \underline{Q}=1$ holds here, $\partial I_{R}=\partial \underline{Q}=1$ also holds for $I_{R}=\underline{Q}+1$, so that the integration can be performed as the sum $G \underline{Q}=S I_{R} \partial I_{R}=\frac{1}{2} I_{R}\left(1+\overline{I_{R}}\right)+A$ if $A=$ is the constant of integration.
