In $(\varepsilon k, \varepsilon P, \varepsilon Q, \varepsilon \kappa)$, replace the configuration number with $B=k-1$, so that (101) takes the form $(v)(\varepsilon)(\varepsilon B, \varepsilon P, \varepsilon Q, \varepsilon \kappa) C\left(q_{x}\right)_{0}^{P}$.  Here it becomes clear that in the transition $b \rightarrow d+\bar{d}$ or $b \rightarrow c+\bar{c}$, the complex Hermitian matrices appear in pairs. Only in the meson region is this not necessary for (1), because, due to $(1)(0000) 0(0)$, $2 b \rightarrow \eta \hat{=} c(1)$ is always possible when one photon is characterized by $Q=+2$ and the other by $Q=-2$. A symmetry also arises in the case (5)(0200)0(+1,0,-1); because, due to $\pi^{+} \hat{=} \bar{\pi}^{-}$ and $\pi^{-} \hat{=} \bar{\pi}^{+}$ as well as (0200)0(0) for $\pi^{0}$, the $\pi$ triplet is the anti-triplet of itself, which also holds for the $\eta$ singlet. Because of this  property of the $\pi$ triplet, the abbreviated notation  (5) $\hat{=}\left(\pi^{ \pm}, \pi^{0}\right)$ is therefore possible. In the case of $k=2$ or $B=1$, however, $b \rightarrow d+\bar{d}$ or $b \rightarrow c+\bar{c}$, $(v)$ always appears in pairs with $(\bar{v})$ due to $B=1$.
