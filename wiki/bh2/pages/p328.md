It is important to examine thin sections of paleoconchylogical material from appropriate geological epochs; for the growth zones reflect the respective ambient temperature. However, oceanographic changes in the seafloor profile of the relevant shelf sea could—since only material from such shelf seas would be suitable for examination, because climate changes likely reach the bottom of shallow seas but not that of the deep ocean— cold-water currents could divert these changes, thereby simulating a short-term climate collapse. For this reason, fossil wood from gymnosperms or angiosperms from the same epoch would need to be examined in parallel, because a short-term climate collapse can be detected in the latest growth rings. Since only gymnosperm and angiosperm trees form such annual rings, the investigation must be limited to the most recent glacial periods, because this flora did not emerge until the Tertiary Period. An indication of the sudden, short-term onset of a glacial period could be seen in the fact that a fossilized herd of mammoths was found intact in a present-day permafrost region, possibly dating from the beginning of the last glacial period, and likely had no means of escape due to the suddenness of the climate change. 

If this model of astrophysical magnetic fields and terrestrial glacial periods, based on (106), is accepted, then it could be concluded that not every radially orbiting galactic density concentration necessarily causes a glacial period. Rather, it  depends on whether, as a result of a sufficiently strong scattering of sunlight combined with minimal geothermal heat (relative to the surface of the fracture zone), a sufficiently  large snow cover develops under suitable boundary conditions as a consequence of a global climate collapse, which forms a dynamically deepening cold pole (principle of feedback), because $H_{2} O$—in addition to having a high heat capacity—has the property of reflecting approximately 10\% of the incident sunlight in its liquid state, but in the solid state, approximately $90 \%$ of the incident sunlight.
