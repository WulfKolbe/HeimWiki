Only one flukton is described, but this encompasses several fundamental flows that differ in terms of $\underline{\alpha}$ with respect to signature permutations. This signature isomerism of the fundamental flows—that is, the occurrence of multiple condensers within the classes of a coupling group—inevitably stems from the fact that at most three structural units correlate, but there are always four condenser signatures. According to Equation (76) through Equation (76b), there is, in turn, for each system of these signature-isomeric elementary flows a system of enantiostereoisomeric flow aggregates $F_{v}=\binom{\alpha \beta}{\kappa \lambda} F\left(q_{j}\right)_{1}^{v} \omega$ of cyclic condenser flows with respect to the systems $q_{j}$ of condenser sources and sinks, from which it follows that, in general, the number $N$ of the elements in a system of signature-isomeric elementary flows can be very high. If, on the other hand, these condenser flows are identical in their morphology, then $N$ is determined solely by the permutation possibilities of the  condenser signature in question. 

If we are dealing with a general form of hermeticity $x \hat{=}(a, b, c, d)$  in $R_{6}$ such that the correlator $\hat{\gamma}_{(x)}=\left({ }^{2} \bar{\gamma}_{(\mu \nu)}\right)_{3}$ has, as a result of the application of a sieve operator, $n \leqq 9$ different elements  that differ from ${ }^{2} \bar{E}$, then a total of $n^{2}$ condensers can be formed, of which $Z=n(n-1)$ differ in base and contrast signatures, and thus describe correlations. The remaining $n$ then take the form $\left[\begin{array}{c}\widehat{\alpha \beta}+ \\ -\alpha \beta\end{array}\right]_{+}$, for  which there is no correlation—and thus no condenser flows.  These condensers, which are not capable of forming flux aggregates, nevertheless constitute metronic condensation stages—specifically, for $\alpha=\beta=3$ the near-field of spatial condensation, which does not occur in metric stages. These condensations without condenser fluxes, however, enclose the flux aggregates and should therefore be designated as shield fields. First, there are the singular shield fields $\alpha=\beta$, which encompass the hermetic characteristic, while the shield fields $\alpha \neq \beta$ are referred to as correlative shield fields
