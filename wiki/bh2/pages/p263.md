Regardless of such cosmological considerations, the problem in describing elementary material structures is to determine the inertial contributions of the $\bar{\lambda}_{0} \perp \vec{Y}$. The logical starting point for the deduction is therefore the lepton triad $v_{L}, e_{ \pm}, v_{B}$ at its minimal masses, whereby it is irrelevant whether $v_{L}$ and $v_{B}$ can actually appear in the $R_{4}$ and be interpreted as real neutrino types. This triad apparently consists of unit structures in which $( \pm p)_{x}$ appears with $x \hat{=}(c, d)$ only in the charge $\underline{N}=1$. According to V., 1., and (96b), the masses of these unit structures $m_{L B}$ and $m_{e}$ are explicitly known; the same applies according to VI., 4., the protosimplex structure with the prototropic conjugate structure. Although it seems impossible to determine the $\underline{\bar{\lambda}}$ values of the inertia of individual $( \pm p)_{x}$, yet the synmetronic structures form typical proto-simplex combinations, which recur in all terms of the spectral function and therefore also characterize its deepest level. Thus, for mere ponderable inertia, the combination $( \pm(1,4))_{x},(+7)_{x}$ is characteristic, and for the charge field in the case of $d$hermetry, $( \pm(2,3,5)),(-6)$. If $\underline{N}=1$ now holds, then the elementary partial masses are determined by the protosimplex combinations $\left[( \pm(1,4))_{x},(+7)_{x}\right] \hat{=} \mu_{+}=m_{B}-m_{L}$ and $[( \pm(2,3,5)),(-6)] \hat{=} \mu_{-}=m_{e}-m_{L}$. Furthermore, however, there must also be a mass $\mu_{S}$ of the straton spin, which is determined by the general conjugate field. For $\mu_{S}$, however, there is only the possibility $\mu_{S}=m_{B}-m_{e}$.
