To analyze this number-theoretic function, one must take into account that every form $x$ must have components in the imaginary subspace $V_{3}\left(x_{4}, x_{5}, x_{6}\right)$, which maps to the real subspace $R_{3}$ according to $V_{3} \perp R_{3}$  and complements it to form $R_{6}$. For any radius vector  of this $V_{3} \mathrm{mu} \beta$, there is therefore a unit vector $i \bar{e}_{t}$ with $\bar{e}_{t}^{2}=1$ such that the straton spin $\bar{\sigma}=\bar{\sigma}_{t}+\bar{\sigma}_{r}$ is composed of the components $\bar{\sigma}_{t}\left(V_{3}\right)$ and $\bar{\sigma}_{r}\left(R_{3}\right) \perp \bar{\sigma}_{t}$. Using the unit vector $\bar{e}_{r} \perp \bar{e}_{t}$ of a radius vector in $R_{3}$, we then have $\bar{\sigma}=i \bar{e}_{t} s+\bar{e}_{r} \sigma_{r}$, where $s=\frac{P}{2}$,  where $2 s=P \geqq 0$ is a positive integer, and $\bar{e}_{t} \perp \bar{e}_{r}$ or $e_{t}^{2}=e_{r}^{2}=1$, respectively. Here, $\sigma_{r}$ denotes the spatial spin $J$ of the straton or pseudostraton with $2 J=Q \geqq 0$ (a positive integer), but this is actually the $R_{3}$ component of the general straton spin $\bar{\sigma}$.  For this reason, a function $z(J)$ must appear in $\sigma_{r} \sim J$ as a proportionality factor, so that $\sigma_{r}=z J$ must be set. If there is a relative motion $\vec{v} \neq \overrightarrow{0}$ in the $R_{3}$ for the form $x$, then $z$ is in any case determined by a direction factor, which is to be understood as the cosine of the angle between $\vec{v}$ and the space spin direction $\bar{e}_{r}$.  On the other hand, however, $J$ is the projection of the integral cyclic condenser fluxes onto the $R_{3}$, so that an additional factor in $z$ the exponential term $\exp (i(\pi J+\varphi))$ appears, which depends on the condenser extrema that are fluctonically conjugate to the flux phase $\varphi$. For $z(J)$, therefore, $z=\cos \left(\vec{v}, \bar{e}_{r}\right) \exp (i(\pi J+\varphi))$ must hold. Since the $R_{3}$component of $\bar{\sigma}$ must be interpreted as the cyclic motion of the inertial mass (the term $x$), the $R_{3}$ motion, $\vec{v} \| \bar{e}_{r}$ must occur,  so that the phase number becomes $\alpha_{p}=\cos \left(\vec{v}, \bar{e}_{r}\right)= \pm 1$. However, this phase number is of a relative nature, because in the case of the ponderable forms $c$ and  $d$, this motion can always be reversed, whereas for the  imaginary forms $\alpha_{p}=+1$, it can be fixed. Thus, $\alpha_{p}$ does not prove to be a structural property of the $\vec{v}$ in $R_{3}$, but rather as a peculiarity of the structure from which $x$ was emitted.
