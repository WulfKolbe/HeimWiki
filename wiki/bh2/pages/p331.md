In contrast to the approximate macroscopic relationship between the magnetic field and the mechanical spatial angular momentum density examined above, the systems (98) through (102)  clearly describe, in a unified manner, very fundamental properties of those  material elementary structures that arise as complex condensations  $M(c, d)$ from the partial spectra $c$ and $d$ in (27) as invariant  basic patterns of the protosimplex charge 1. On the one hand, the quantum number sets of the $1 \leqq \nu \leqq 12$ multiplets $(v)$ can be compared indirectly, according to (101) through (101c), with the empirical properties of stable and metastable ponderable $M q$ , which leads to an interpretation of all multiplet components $(v)_{x}$ in terms of $0 \leqq x(v) \leqq P_{v}$. On the other hand, it was found that the time-dependent  $1 \leqq j \leqq 4$ occupation numbers $n_{j}(t)$ of the four configuration zones for  $n_{j}=0$ and $P=1$, while neglecting the unknown spin function $F_{S}$—as given in (98b) through (98d)—replicate, with unexpectedly good accuracy, the mass of the $e^{-}$ for $k=1$ and that of the $p$ for $k=2$,  where the slight deviation must be attributable solely to $F_{S} \neq 0$. The empirically observable temporal stability of the electron  and proton can also be understood through $n_{j}=0$, because due to $k=$ const the $Q_{j}(k)=$ const represent a temporally constant skeletal structure of the configuration zones. 

Given this fact, it seems reasonable to investigate whether there are uniform relationships that, so to speak, serve as selection rules for selecting the permitted quadruplets $n_{j}$ of all multiplet components $(v)_{x}$ from the set of all possible integer quadruples.
