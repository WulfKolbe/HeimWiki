Despite the highly approximate nature of the idealization of terrestrial data (e.g., an isotropic sphere instead of a level asteroid with an anisotropic density profile, etc.), the theoretical data still appear comparable to the actual geomagnetic field, especially since the tertiary-induced generator effect remains completely unexplained. For this reason, one could conclude that $\beta>0$ holds, with all the consequences this entails for gravitational dynamics. However, a decision—specifically regarding $A$ or  $A^{\prime}$—appears impossible on this basis, due to the  high uncertainty of the paleomagnetic $B_{\text {max }}$ measurements and  the associated uncertainty regarding the present  $S$ factor. For further empirical verification of the relationship (106a), the magnetic fields of stellar objects whose angular momentum density can be determined astrophysically could be considered. As early as 1928, SWANN and LONGACRE ${ }^{(1)}$ speculatively postulated, on empirical grounds, a proportionality between magnetic flux density and angular momentum density. More recently, it has become possible to determine the magnetic fields of celestial bodies and their angular momentum densities with relatively good accuracy in several cases. Thus, SIRAG ${ }^{(2)}$ also provided an empirically derived—though not theoretically derived—formula for such stellar magnetic fields,  which is intended to cover the empirical aspects of such magnetic fields just as, for example, the purely empirical formula proposed by BELLERT ${ }^{(3)}$ does with regard to cosmological redshift and its anomalies. It should be noted here that this empirical relationship, which is adapted to astrophysical conditions, agrees with the derived relationship (106a)—subject to the condition $\mu=S=1$—except for a numeric factor of little relevance.  For example, (106a) with $\mu=S=1$ reproduces the solar magnetic field relatively well, but reproduces that of the pulsar Harx - 1.78 vir very well. It could be that the extreme properties of such pulsars particularly well satisfy the approximation conditions under which (106a) was developed.
