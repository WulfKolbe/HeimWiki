With the help of these condenser bridges within the individual terms of the spectrum of spacetime condensations, it is now possible to investigate the systems of condenser sources and condenser sinks; however, this is far more complicated than the corresponding investigation of the condensation forms $b$ and $c$. This complication stems from the fact that all condenser bridges form complex network systems, the simplest of which are constructed from the $K^{--}$. The bridge system of the $K^{++}$ is extremely intricate, whereas, on the other hand, there is a symmetry between $(K, C)^{+}$ and $(K, C)^{-}$, as expressed in Equation (71). Bridge-free elements exist only in $\mathrm{I} a$, but not in $\mathrm{I} h$ or $\mathrm{II}(a h)$, which also represents an internal structural deviation from the forms $b$ and $c$. Despite these interconnection systems of the condenser bridges, in the case of $d$,  only the four types of source-sink systems exist in the condensations, as in the case of $(b c)$; for these distributions stem from the subsystems I $a$, II $a$, I $h$, II $h$ of the coupling groups, which also  describe the internal coupling structure in the case $d$.
