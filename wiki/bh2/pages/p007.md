On the one hand, these manifestations are embedded in Euclidean space; on the other hand, deviations from a Euclidean spatial structure (Riemannian geometry) point to physical phenomena such as gravitational fields and mass. 

A unified description of all known fields and particles, derived from a common basis, is not currently available in an empirically verifiable form. Although Einstein attempted in his later years to unify electromagnetism with gravity, this purely mathematical theory was not successful. 

Heim’s theory, now available in two volumes, breaks entirely new ground in this regard and therefore differs significantly from previous theories. It may therefore often be very difficult for the reader to follow the lines of reasoning presented. These difficulties apparently arise from the use of new terminology and the highly condensed formulation of the conclusions. 

Heim’s theory should be understood here as a framework whose basic idea is the geometrization of physical entities. Its most essential features are: 
1. The existence of a six-dimensional space in which the physically accessible four-dimensional spacetime is embedded. The  transcoordinates $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$ are of an imaginary nature,  and their direction is reversible. 
2. Quantization of the multidimensional space due to a geometric area unit $\tau$ that cannot be undershot, which is of the same order of magnitude as the square of the Planck length. 
3. Novel cosmology and the resulting Hermitian manifold geometry. The Hermitian fundamental tensor lying in $R_{6}$ is compositely composed of the non-Hermitian fundamental tensors describing the manifold geometry.
