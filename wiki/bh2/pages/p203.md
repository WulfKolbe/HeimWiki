If the stereoisomerism of the $N$ is fixed relative to the screen field system of the protosimplex in question,  then the double spin configuration $Z_{1}=2$ should result for $N=1$. For $N=2$, $Z_{2}=2 \cdot 1 \cdot 2=4$ then holds, and for $N=3$, $Z_{3}=2 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3=24$ is completely evident. The complete induction then yields, for any $N>3$, the number-theoretic law $Z_{N}=2^{N-1} \cdot N!$ governing possible spin isomerisms of the flukton. Since all fundamental fluxes of the flucton differ in their signature-isomeric correlation exponents and stereoisomeric flux aggregates, the flucton is constructed from $N$ conceptually distinct elements, for which (again relative to the screen field) $N$ ! possible permutations exist. However, these $N$ ! permutations, however, can only be different relative positions of the basic flows within the domain of definition of the flucton in question. This means, however, that the $N!$ permutations are the possible stereoisomers of the flucton with respect to the screen field system. If $S_{N}$ denotes the number of possible stereoisomers, then $S_{N}=N$ ! and $Z_{N}=2^{N-1} \cdot S_{N}$ for the  number of spin isomeries. However, for each of the $N!$ stereoisomeric basic flux combinations, there are $Z_{N}$ spin isomerisms, so that there must be $E_{N}=Z_{N} \cdot S_{N}$ enantiostereoisomers for the flucton in question. Here, the concept of enantiostereoisomerism of a fluc ton seems particularly relevant, because for every fluc ton isomerism with respect to the total condenser spin (+) of all fundamental flows, there must always be a mirror-symmetric spin system (-) that is identical to (+) except for the spin orientation. Here, $( \pm)$ simply denotes the indexing of the respective isomerism, where (-) is the enantiomorph of (+). If $E_{N}^{( \pm)}$ denotes the number of these enantiostereoisomeric flucctons, which occur in pairs that are mirror-symmetric,  then $2 E_{N}^{( \pm)}=E_{N}$ holds.
