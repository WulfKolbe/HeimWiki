$( \pm p)-(\mu)-( \pm q)$ refers $\mu$ to the direct exchange of fluktonen with regard to ${ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(\mu)}$. Such a correlative conjunction is  fundamentally ambiguous because $-(\mu)$—always relates only two prototypes. If, on the other hand, the so-called contact conjunction is present—which is defined by $( \pm q)-(\mu)-(\mp p)$—then $\mu$ remains ambiguous;  because in this case, a flukton always leads via $-(\mu)-$ into a shield field, which, however, is incapable of forming a conjunction. However, since $(-q)$ belongs to the same structural unit as $(+p)$, the shield field can mediate contact with another flukton via the same conjunction. Finally, there is also the possibility that the shield field mediating this contact is $(+7)$ and $\mu=3$, so that  $( \pm q)-(3)-(+7)$ defines the equally ambiguous stratonconjunctive, which is thus a special case of the general contactconjunctive. It further follows from this that certain valencies with respect to possible conjunctors can be assigned to the protosimplex structures, such that the protosimplex valency is identical to the number $s$, which, as condenser signatures, define the respective protosimplex. Accordingly, $s=2$ is $(-k)$ with $k \leqq 5$, but $s=3$ corresponds to $k=6$, because all three structural units define the world fluctone. In contrast to these fluctones, one cannot speak of a valency between $(+k)$ and $k \neq 6$; since these shield fields can only mediate a correlation via contact conjunctions or the Straton conjunction. 

The definition of the conjunctor based on the concept of the structural unit and the flukton conjunction is evidently not yet complete;  for not all basic flows of a flukton need to pass through  a conjunctor to engage in correlative exchange (flukton conjugation).
