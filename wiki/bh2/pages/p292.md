This determination of $\underline{P}$ appears ambiguous and can therefore only be carried out by partially adapting the derivation heuristically to empirical data. One could proceed by assuming $\underline{I}=\underline{P}+1$, as well as $\underline{I}_{R}=\underline{Q}+1$ and $I_{R}=Q+1$ with $Q=k-1$ or $\underline{Q}=2 k-1$, respectively, and with freely available constants $\alpha, \beta$ and $\gamma$ (possibly just numbers as well as variations $\partial_{\varepsilon}$ in the order of magnitude $\sqrt{\tau}$) to generate three functions $X, U$ and $Z$ according to $\alpha \partial_{\varepsilon} X=\underline{I} \partial_{\varepsilon} X-\partial_{\varepsilon} \underline{I} \quad$ as well as $\beta \partial_{\varepsilon} U=\left(\underline{I}+\underline{I}_{R}\right) \partial_{\varepsilon} U- -\partial_{\varepsilon}\left(\underline{I}+\underline{I}_{R}\right)$ and $\gamma \partial_{\varepsilon} Z=I_{R} \partial_{\varepsilon} Z-\partial_{\varepsilon} I_{R}$ , and determine the freely available constants such that $X$ appears as the arithmetic mean  of $U$ and $Z$, that is, as $2 X=U+Z$.
