What seems essential, however, is that the incident component $h \omega_{R}=E_{R}$ of the resonance energy is  interrelated with the structure $\hat{I}$ from (102) of the term $(v)_{x}$ of the mass $M_{x}$. This invariant basic pattern describes the steady-state  of the dynamic equilibrium of internal correlations of  condenser fluxes of the ( $\pm p$ ), which together form an integral flux aggregate  of a cyclic nature. Since, for the condenser flux in the integral case or in $(-p)$, the flux velocity $\bar{w}$ (as the imaginary part of the global velocity $\bar{Y}$ ) and, during the steady-state equilibrium, cyclic integral condenser fluxes periodically restore the initial state, there must be a defined temporal period $\theta_{x}>0$. This also implies that there is a frequency $\omega_{x}=1 / \theta_{x}$ in $(v)_{x}$ of the integral cyclic condenser flow.  Since, on the other hand, $M_{x}$ in the form $M_{x} c^{2}$ can also be expressed in terms of the energy measure, the resonance condition $\omega_{R}=\omega_{x}$ can be assumed, whereby in the steady-state case $w \approx c$ is always attainable. If $M(N)$ is thereby achieved, then $E_{R}>\left(M(N)-M_{x}\right) c^{2}$ would also have to be satisfied.
