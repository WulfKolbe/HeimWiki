As $N$ progresses, one enters increasingly earlier regions of $R_{4}$, whereby all regions, as $R_{2}$-subspaces of $R_{3}$, must satisfy principle (15a).  However, if $D$ decreases, then according to (41), the absolute value of $\tau$ increases while satisfying (15a). After a number of $N=J<\infty$ steps into the past, $D\left(t_{J}\right)$ would ultimately be such that an element $\tau_{J}$  encloses $R_{3}$. However, since $\tau$ can never be undershot, at $N=J$ a boundary of the past would be set such that $t_{J}=0$ as the temporal origin with $D\left(t_{J}\right)=D_{0}$ of $R_{3}$ may be set at this origin, for which $D_{0}>0$ must be assumed according to (43). The  temporal origin of $R_{3}$ thus lay at $t=0$ on the cosmological time scale, so that the past of $R_{3}$ lies within the time interval $0 \leqq t<T$,  if $T$ denotes the current cosmic age (relative to the present $R_{3}$).
