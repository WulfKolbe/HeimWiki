Since the spectrum (27) contains the imponderable mass terms of all $x \hat{=}(a, b, c, d)$ Hermitian forms, $m$ is exactly the fourfold superposition of partial spectra of each $x$ Hermitian form.  Each of these partial spectra $x$ contains a pseudocontinuous or discrete manifold of condenser terms with a wide variety of $m$values of the same hermeticity—that is, the same synmetronic structure.  This respective term manifold, distinguished in $m$, means that the relevant synmetronic structure of the hermetic form $x$ consisting of protosimplexes $( \pm q)_{x}$ and conjunctive laws can apply only to unit structures of this $x$ hermetic structure. On the other hand, for any Hermetic form $x$, such a unitary structure is evidently a configurative synmetronic basic pattern, which $x$ and constantly repeats itself in the higher terms $m_{x}$ of the partial spectrum $x$ in excitation levels. 

The fluktonic property of every $( \pm p)_{x}$ determines a symmetrical condensation state $\bar{\lambda}_{(\mu \nu)}^{(\kappa \lambda)}=\bar{\lambda}$, which, together with the complex world velocity $\vec{Y}=\vec{v}+i \vec{w}$, establishes the principle of inertia $\underline{\lambda} \perp \vec{Y}$  according to (77) and thus assigns to each $( \pm p)_{x}$ an inertial effect as mass, provided that $( \pm p)_{x}$ is fluktonically integrated via conjunctives into the correlative internal structure of the mass term. If, however, the spectral terms of a hermetic form differ in their inertial masses despite having the same synmetronic structure, then this can only be possible because the individual $( \pm p)_{x}$ differ in their  valuation, that is, in the $\underline{\bar{\lambda}}$ intensity. The respective base and contrast signature of $\underline{\bar{\lambda}}$ are determined solely by $x$. The distinction in intensity among the $\underline{\bar{\lambda}}\left(( \pm p)_{x}\right)$ can only refer to $\underline{\bar{\lambda}} \perp \vec{Y}$, because this is a property that applies equally to all $( \pm p)_{x}$ in equal measure, unlike ponderability and electric charge. For example, the protosimplexes (±(1,2,3)) are imponderable,  while $( \pm 5)$ and $(+7)$ have no gravitational structural component. The inertial intensity of a $( \pm p)_{x}$ is thus determined solely by $\bar{\lambda} \perp \vec{Y}$ and can therefore only appear as an integer multiple of a minimal unit condensation (due to the stepwise nature of the condensation).
