Here, the constants $A_{i}$ and $B_{r}$ can be freely set to $i \leqq 3$ and $r \leqq 4$ . The values $A_{1}=B_{1}=B_{4}=1$, as well as $2 A_{2}=2 k+1$, $2 B_{2}=B_{3}=k$, and $A_{3}=1-4 k$, is optimally adapted to the empirical values of the electron and proton. This yields $H$ and $G$ after raising $H=\frac{\alpha}{3}\left(1+\sqrt{\eta_{q k}}\right)\left(\frac{\sqrt{Y}}{\eta_{q k}^{2}}\right)^{2 k+1} \eta_{q k}^{3}$ and  $G=\frac{\eta_{11}}{e \eta_{q k}}\left(2 \eta_{q k} \sqrt{Y}\right)^{k}\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{\eta_{q k}}}{1+\sqrt{\eta_{q k}}}\right)^{2}$. 

The ratio $Y=\frac{X(z+1)}{X(z-1)}$ remains open; it can be traced back to the sequence  $X_{v}=X_{v-1}+X_{v-2}$, and thus again to the class of metronic functions $\varphi$, which already appeared in the derivation of (96b).  Since this concerns the transition $j=3$ to $j=4$ and the second  domain of validity, the metronic coefficient $z$ of the  $R_{3}$ cells is extremely large, and $X(z)$ also satisfies the selector  $\partial^{2}-3 \partial+()=0$. However, this means that the two Limes relations $\lim _{z \rightarrow \infty} \frac{X(z)}{X(z-1)}=\xi$ and $\lim _{z \rightarrow \infty} \frac{X(z+1)}{X(z)}=\xi$—and thus also $\lim _{z \rightarrow \infty} \frac{X(z+1)}{X(z-1)}=\xi^{2}$—hold. However, due to the second metronic  domain of validity, (relative to the correlation center)  $z$ in the transition $j=3$ to $j=4$ is so high that, in principle, $Y=\xi^{2}$ can be set, whereby $2 \xi=1+\sqrt{5}$ holds according to (96b).  With $Y=\xi^{2}$, the following holds for $H$ and $G$:
