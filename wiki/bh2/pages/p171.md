The summarized cyclicity condition for each flux aggregate is thus also the condition for the corresponding composite term in the complex partial spectrum in (27) to be time-defined and to have a temporal stability interval determined by the number of periods $A=C$. However, every cyclic condenser flux must result in a spin due to its rotation, so that the terms of the condensation spectra can possess spins and spinors—namely, when the spins of the condenser fluxes do not cancel each other out. Otherwise, spin-free condensations must occur. 

Since $\left({ }^{2} \bar{\varrho}-\frac{1}{2}^{2} \bar{\gamma} ;() \varrho\right) ; n \sim^{2} \bar{T}$ and, according to the energy-matter equivalence ${ }^{2} \bar{T} \sim^{2} \bar{\sigma}$, is proportional to the mass-density tensor in $R_{3}$ , the law according to which the space compressor seeks to reach its minimum level implies that the matter field quanta always structure themselves in such a way that their mass density reaches a minimum value, specifically with respect to the given coupling structure. If, on the other hand, radioactive transmutation of a complex condensation occurs, then—due to compressor isostasy—this transmutation can only take place in such a way that condensations at lower compressor levels are formed during the transmutation. This means, however, that the transmuta tion is always a decay process in which $L>1$ new condensations of lower compressor levels are formed.
