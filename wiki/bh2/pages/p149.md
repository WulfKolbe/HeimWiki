In other words, hermetic source units are contrasted with non-hermetic sink units. However, there must still be an internal distribution within $a$ or $h$, because in every case there are gradual differences in hermeticity between primary and secondary condenser units. The two possible classes of this internal distribution system are then $Q_{4}^{+}(\mathrm{I} h \rightarrow \mathrm{II} h)$ and anti-hermetic $Q_{4}^{-}(\mathrm{II} a \rightarrow \mathrm{I} a)$.  From the structure of the six coupling groups and the law of condenser bridges, $Q_{1}=Q_{1}\left(K_{j}^{++} \rightarrow K_{j}^{--}\right)_{1}^{3}$ follows directly, and $Q_{3}=Q_{3}\left(K_{i}^{+} \rightarrow K_{i}^{-}\right)_{1}^{6}$ follows secondarily. While in these two systems all condenser bridges act as sources or sinks, this is not the case for the bridge-free primary sources. One obtains $Q_{1}^{(0)}=$  $=Q_{1}(1,2,3 \rightarrow 6,5,4)$, where 1, 2, 3 refer to $\mathrm{I} h$ and 6, 5, 4 refer to $\mathrm{I} a$. If the indexing $K_{\alpha, \beta} \equiv\left(K_{\alpha}, K_{\beta}\right)$ denotes two condenser bridges of the same type, then the scheme of coupling groups for the hermetic primary and secondary sources yields $Q_{2}^{+}=Q_{2}\left(1,2,3 \rightarrow K_{3,5}^{-}, K_{1,6}^{-}, K_{2,4}^{-}\right)$, that is, here the same coupling groups in $\mathrm{I} h$ are associated with two secondary sink-like condenser bridges each. The antihermetic class of these sources, in turn, is bridge-free. Here, two coupling groups from $\mathrm{II} h$ each act on two corresponding ones in $\mathrm{I} a$, resulting in $Q_{2}^{-}=Q_{2}((1,6)(2,5)(3,4) \rightarrow(4,5)(4,6)(5,6))$.  It remains to consider the two internal source systems.  For the hermetic form, $Q_{4}^{+}=Q_{4}\left(1,2,3 \rightarrow K_{5,3}^{+}, K_{6,1}^{+}, K_{4,2}^{+}\right)$ follows, whereas for the antihermetic class, $Q_{4}^{-}=Q_{4}\left(K_{2,4}^{-}, K_{1,6}^{-}, K_{3,5}^{-} \rightarrow 4,5,6\right)$ results.
