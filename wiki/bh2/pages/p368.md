Since valence has become proportionality and $g_{k m}^{\prime}$ represents the only field of interaction, $g_{k m}^{\prime}$ must, due to the geodesic equation, be interpreted as a tensorial gravitational potential excited by the phenomenological field source $V_{k m}$. In this approximate Hermitian framework, the separation of the gravitational field (structural part) from the field source (phenomenological part) has thus been accomplished. These Hermitian relations 
$R_{k m}^{\prime}-g_{k m}^{\prime} R^{\prime} / 2 \sim V_{k m}$, however, represent the fundamental equations of general relativity, whose empirical range is thus  fully covered by (19). On the one hand, the exact Schwarzschild solution applies to these fundamental equations, which, in the case of sufficiently  low mass densities or sufficiently large $R_{3}$distances  (relative to the Schwarzschild radius) approximates the classical Newtonian law of gravitation. On the other hand, however, the group of permitted isomorphisms—expressed in the fundamental tensor $g_{k m}^{\prime}\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{4}\right) \neq \delta_{k m}$—under the condition of pseudo-Euclideanity $g_{k m}^{\prime} \sim \delta_{k m}$ of a $R_{4}$ of signature $(+++-)$ the Lorentz group of $v=\mathrm{const}$ relatively moving in the $R_{3}$ $R_{3}$, characterized by a regular quadratic orthogonal matrix $\hat{A} \hat{A}_{T}=\hat{E}$. If we assume, approximately, $v \ll c$ and separate, in $R_{4}^{\text {i }}$, the physical $R_{3}$ the phenomenological time, then the Lorentz group decomposes in the familiar manner into the transformations of the Galilean group of classical mechanics, which, in turn, using the molecular concept, encompasses all anthropomorphic manifestations of mechanics—such as the mechanics of the rigid,  elastic, or plastic bodies, just as  thermodynamics—as multipoint mechanics—encompasses statistically disordered masses in motion, aerodynamics and hydrodynamics as the statistical motion of mass points with a time-constant preferred direction, or acoustics as such motion with a time-periodically changing preferred direction, or gas dynamics as a branch of fluid dynamics in which the flow velocity exceeds the speed of sound.
