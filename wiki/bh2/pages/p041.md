The distance $R_{0}$ is the largest possible radius in the physical $R_{3}$ and thus $D=2 R_{0}$ the greatest possible distance in this space, so that  $D$ must be interpreted as the diameter of the real $R_{3}$—that is, the universe. The starting point for determining $D$ is the law of gravitation $r q e^{-q} \sim(1-r / \varrho)^{2}$ with 
$q=1-\sqrt{1-\frac{3 \varphi}{8 c^{2}}}$, because the reality requirement 
$\operatorname{Im} q=0$ or $2 \omega c-\varphi \geqq 0$ with $r \varphi=\gamma m(1-r / \varrho)^{2}$ sets the reality limits (14) through (14c). The upper reality limit $R_{+}=R\left(M_{0}\right)$ with  $M_{0}=L m$ according to (11) is such that $R$ diverges for $m \rightarrow 0$,  that is, $R=R_{0}$ reaches the maximum possible value for the lower  bound $m_{L}$ of the spectrum of complex condensations according to (32)  when $L=1$ is set as the gravitational field source in (11). Substituting $M_{0}=m_{L}=m_{0}$ from (32) into $R_{+}=R$ from (14) then yields, with $D=2 R_{0}$ and $R\left(m_{0}\right)=R_{0}$, a representation of $D$ that still contains an approximate term due to (32), but is well-suited for estimation. To obtain the exact formulation, (30) and $n=n_{N}$, as well as $R=R_{0}$, are substituted into (27), but the substitution must take into account that the projection of the spherical space structure onto the plane results in concentric circles, which in (30) yields the unit circle $\pi E$ with $E=1\left[m^{2}\right]$. Equation (27) then yields $m_{L}=m_{0}=m\left(n_{N}, 0\right)$ as the exact lower bound of the point spectrum of complex Hermitian operators, which must be substituted into the expression for the upper real boundary (14). This then yields $R_{0}=\frac{D}{2}$, such that $D$ is algebraically expressed in terms of the elementary empirical constants $\gamma, h$ and $c$ on the one hand and $E$ on the other.
