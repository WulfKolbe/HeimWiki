If the assumed galactic compression front were to reach the planetary system during the minimum of this geothermal heat (at the surface), then a global climate collapse would at least be conceivable, which—under suitable environmental conditions at higher latitudes—could usher in a glacial period within a few years. If $b$ is the width of the compression front, then the glacial period would have to extend approximately over the time $t_{g}=b / V$ , with $t_{g}$ ranging from approximately $6 \cdot 10^{4}$  to $1,8 \cdot 10^{5}$ years (at least during the Quaternary). Paleontological and glaciological findings would, for example, be reflected in the pattern of these fronts in such a way that, similar to the end of the Permian, a very broad front toward the end of the Upper Cretaceous caused a long-term glacial period that concluded the Mesozoic, and ushered in the Tertiary period with the onset of the Eocene. Later, in the Quaternary, this front was followed by four narrow, lamellar fronts, which defined the chronological sequence of the Minz, Gündel, Riß, and Würm glacial periods. Since the interplanetary gas density increases sharply during the passage of such a front and this gas is ionized, intense inductively caused variations in the geomagnetic field must be expected, so that the shielding effect of this field against extraterrestrial ionized particle radiation—and thus the formation of a radiation belt (magnetic bottle)—is constantly disrupted. In this way, an increased proportion of ionizing radiation on the Earth’s surface within the biosphere could raise the mutation rates of genomes, while the affected organisms could simultaneously be subjected to strong selective pressure due to glacial climatic conditions. 

This briefly outlined picture of glacial periods and their cause  $V \neq 0$, caused by a galactic magnetic field, is only debatable as a hypothesis if it can be demonstrated that the onset of a glacial period does not occur gradually and steadily over the long term, but rather abruptly within a few years.
