The mass spectrum $m(n, q)$ from (27) is a pseudocontinuum in which the Hermitian forms $a$ and $b$ give rise to this pseudocontinuum, which is superimposed on the discrete point spectrum of the terms $c$ and $d$  of complex condensations; for (27) is, on the one hand, the  general solution to the complex compositional Hermitian problem,  and, on the other hand, this spectral function contains at least the minimal $d$hermety, which, as a minimal mass, can still carry an elementary charge field $e_{ \pm}$. Since the $c$ and $d$terms, due to their ponderability, can only be the empirically known elementary corpuscles, their empirically verifiable radioactive decay could be interpreted as a term transition—regulated by compressorisostasy—within this discrete point spectrum superimposed on the pseudocontinuum. Therefore, there must exist a term selector $T$ that performs a selection of the ponderable mass centers $M$ of the discrete spectrum of complex condensations in accordance with $T ; m(n, q)=M(c, d)$. However, this term selector $T$ can only be identified via synmetronics; yet all $M$ that are logically possible at all according to this synmetronics are then captured, are captured; however, in a second selection process, only those terms that have a  sufficient $x_{4}$ extent $\Delta t>0$ are to be selected as relevant; for only event structures with a minimum duration of existence can be regarded as physically existing  elements of the world. The synmetronic analysis of $T$ must therefore be carried out in such a way that the minimum existence time can also be defined for the second selection; for only then can the selected point spectrum be compared with the properties of the empirical elementary particles.
