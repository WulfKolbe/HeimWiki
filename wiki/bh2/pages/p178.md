For all $v \geqq 4$, there can be no further structural degeneration at all; because of the four coupling classes within the coupling structure, the $v \geqq 4$ can only be regular in structure. In contrast, a possibility for degeneration exists for $v \geqq 4$ within the system of the condenser flow itself; for it is precisely because of the regularity of the coupling structure that the cyclic condenser flow for $v \geqq 4$ decompose into several partial flows of degenerate structures $v \leqq 3$, which, however, superimpose cyclically. There is no analogue for this flow superposition in the regions $v \leqq 3$. For each flow aggregate $v=4$, there are $v!=24$ structural isomers. Furthermore, as with all $v \geqq 4$, regular enantiostereoisomerism occurs here for the first time; because within a group of structural isomers, there are always multiple $\omega$ values and therefore multiple flux spins, depending on whether the structural isomer in question allows for degeneracy or remains regular. In the case of $v=4$, there are $\binom{6}{4}=15$ basic types of flux aggregates, namely (1,2,3,4),  $(2,3,4,5),(3,4,5,6),(1,2,5,6),(1,3,5,6),(1,4,5,6),(3,2,5,6),(4,2,5,6)$,  (1,2,5,4), (1,3,5,4), (1,2,6,4), (1,3,6,4), (1,2,3,5), as well as (1,2,3,6) and (2,3,4,6). Nor can such a specification be made for $v=6$; for the one possible flow aggregate (1,2,3,4,5,6) has 720 possible structural isomerisms.
