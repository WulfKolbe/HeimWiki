Since  in (106a) the secular variation of $S=+1$ decreases according to $S \rightarrow 0$, one could speculatively assume that the temporal coincidence of such a radial galactic compression front with $S \approx 0$ over a period that is very short in terms of Earth’s history forces the field to reform in the opposite direction; for in (106a), both signs are possible. This speculation gains credibility because NEGI and TIWARI applied a variant of Fourier analysis (using square-wave instead of sine-wave oscillations) to the field reversal events of the geomagnetic field during the last $5,7 \cdot 10^{8}$ years, and in doing so found, over $10^{6}$ years, regularities with periods  of 285, 114, 64, 47, and 34. This may suggest  external causes in the form of density fluctuations along  the solar system’s galactic orbit for the polarity reversal events,  though these would always have to occur $S=0$ synchronously. 

The remarks in (106a) should not necessarily be interpreted as a foray into other fields of expertise, but rather as heuristic suggestions based on (106). 

Due to the far-reaching consequences, (106a) should, if possible, also be investigated experimentally in the laboratory. For such experiments, a sufficiently large isotropic sphere (or, more generally, a body of revolution) made of a material with a high specific gravity and sufficient mechanical strength should be used. This material should also be an electrical insulator so that the centrifugal force arising during rotation with $w$ does not act as a charge-separating force on the metal electrons, thereby preventing a rotation-induced equatorial circular current. A magnetometric measurement would be advisable—due to the factor of 2 in (106a)—specifically at $B_{V}$ at one of the rotational poles; because, in contrast to $B_{H}$ at the equator $\varphi=0$, at the rotational pole at $\varphi=\pi / 2$, that is, $\sin \varphi=1$, the value $B_{V}=2 A D \mu S$ would result as the maximum value.
