In addition, there is also  a symmetry of the secondary systems; for within each coupling group, the configuration of II $h$ always follows from that of II $a$—and vice versa—when the basis and counter-signatures are swapped. Based on these symmetries, the condenser bridges of all six coupling groups can now be assembled. A direct consequence of this symmetry must be an analogous symmetry of the primary and secondary condenser bridges. In Ia, there is initially a bridge 1, 4, 5 in $\left[\begin{array}{cc}1 \\ 1 & 1\end{array}\right]$ and $\left[\begin{array}{cc}1 & 1 \\ 1\end{array}\right]$, as well as a bridge 2, 4, 6 in $\left[\begin{array}{c}\alpha \\ \alpha\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}{cc}\alpha & \alpha \\ \alpha\end{array}\right]$ and a bridge 3, 4, 6 in $\left[\begin{array}{ll}1 & \alpha \\ \alpha & 1\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}{ll}\alpha & 1 \\ 1 & \alpha\end{array}\right]$. From this, it can be concluded that 4 is the intersection of two condenser bridges, while 3 connects the ends 5 and 6 of the bridges that intersect at 4.  A completely analogous bridge construction is found in $\mathrm{I} h$; for, with respect to $\left[\begin{array}{c}1 \\ 1\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}{cc}1 & 1 \\ 1\end{array}\right]$, the bridge 2, 3, 6, and 1, 3, 5 with respect to $\left[\begin{array}{c}\alpha \\ \alpha\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}{cc}\alpha & \alpha \\ \alpha\end{array}\right]$, and 1, 2, 4 with respect to $\left[\begin{array}{ll}1 & \alpha \\ \alpha & 1\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}{cc}\alpha & 1 \\ 1 & \alpha\end{array}\right]$. Here, the two bridges 2, 3, 6 and 1, 3, 5 intersect at 3, whereas their starting points 1, 2 are bridged by 4. There is thus complete mirror symmetry between these two primary bridge constructions in I $a$ and $\mathrm{I} h$. K_{3}^{--}=5,3,6$.  The condensers from $4 \mathrm{I} a$ to $6 \mathrm{I} a$ and $1 \mathrm{I} h$ to $3 \mathrm{I} h$, in which the base and counter signatures differ, are not capable of forming any bridges.
