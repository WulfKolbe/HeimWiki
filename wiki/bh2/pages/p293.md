The multiplets ( $k P Q$ ) of the fundamental patterns ( $\varepsilon$ from (93) have been omitted for brevity) must all be distinct from one another in the sequence of their invariants, whereby the commas in ( $k P Q$ ) are omitted provided that this does not result in ambiguities. In fact, this is also the case for the two $P$ intervals and for $k=2$ with regard to the multiplet doublings. For $k=2$, $G=3$, $Q=k-1=1$, and $\underline{Q}=2 k-1=3$ hold, which occurs for the singlet $P_{-}=2-k=0$ and the quartet $P_{+}=2 k-1=3$. Thus, (201) $\neq(203)$ and  (231) $\neq(233)$ are satisfied for $k=2$. In contrast, (99) yields a contradiction for $k=1$; for although $G=k+1=2$, $Q=k-1=0$, and $\underline{Q}=2 k-1=1$ hold, $P_{+}=P_{-}=1$ is true, so that a total of three  doublets $(110)_{A}$ and the two spinor doublets $(111)_{B}$ and  $(111)_{C}$ appear. Although $A$ is a scalar doublet distinct from the spinor doublets, but due to the identity of the quantum numbers, the sets $B=C$ are identical, which contradicts the assumption $B \neq C$.
