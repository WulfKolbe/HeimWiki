The internal correlation of any condensation system therefore depends on the temporal evolution of the structure of all coupling extrema, so that, following an analysis of the entire coupling structure, it appears possible to determine the stability time. After such a stability time, a restructuring of the coupling structure must then occur, leading to a spontaneous change in the composition field of the condensation in the sense of a change in the internal correlation. This might make it possible to find a term selector for the ponderable partial spectrum from (27). 

According to relation (68a), each coupling extremum $Q_{(\mu \nu) \text { ext }}^{(\kappa \lambda)} \equiv\binom{\kappa \lambda}{\mu \nu}$ a coupling group that contains all coupling extrema arising from permutations of the signature digits, whereby the digits of a condenser signature must always remain together, because only these digits of a signature are permuted. A coupling group can therefore comprise at most eight coupling selectors as extrema. With respect to such a coupling group, according to (68a), all condensers of the corresponding signature must behave pseudo-anthermetically—primarily those that bear the two signature specifications of the coupling group as the base and counter signatures, and secondarily those that contain any pair of digits of the coupling group as the basis signature. This classification is a consequence of the fact that, according to (68a), the coupling enforces a pseudo-antihermetic property at the coupling extremum, and the base signature of a condenser is determined by partial metric differentials. These primary and secondary pseudoantihermetic capacitors of a coupling group are accompanied, in the extremal region of this group, by a system of primary and secondary hermetic capacitors whose basis signatures contain no signature indices of the coupling groups whatsoever.  Since each coupling group comprises three such capacitor systems, all coupling groups must be interrelated. Thus, hermetic and antihermetic capacitor bridges can always exist between two coupling groups;
