The concept of the parton finds a natural interpretation through the central zone  $j=1$. Finally, there is an analogy to the quark model through the possible tripartite division of the elementary charge field (29)  according to (104) in connection with the speculative subconstituents  (103), which in turn would be identical to the quarks conceived in the quark model. In the case of the empirically observed jets in deeply elastic collisions, the subconstituents are not released,  but the jets directly point to the existence of these $G=k+1$  subconstituents. It should also be noted here that recent experiments in high-energy physics point to the existence of so-called preons as structural building blocks of the $G$ subconstituents. Should these preons prove to actually exist, then their interpretation as protosimplexes—which in any case must be regarded as the true ultimate units of material elementary structures—is virtually inevitable. 

However, a concrete statement regarding such revisions of currently somewhat speculative models can only be made once  the structural theory discussed above can be made self-consistent.  This is not yet the case; for (112) refers only to the case of a stationary state, which is determined by an internal dynamic equilibrium of correlating flux aggregates, a fact reflected in the constancy of the real part of the initial relation. Due to this limitation, it has not yet been possible to find a unified relationship for the bandwidths that is consistent with (112). Furthermore, the dependencies on the resonance order shown in (114) remain open for the time being. Only when these functions and the bandwidths are known can an attempt be made to develop a unified description of the magnetic spin moments for $Q(N) \neq 0$ and the interactions (cross sections) in the sense of a correspondence theory. 

However, if one considers the numerical mass values that can be determined from (112), it becomes apparent that, on the one hand, the empirically  measured particle masses are fully reproduced, but, on the other hand, a significantly larger number of theoretical  terms appear that have not yet been empirically verified.
