This volume is a continuation of the previously published first volume, *Elementary Structures of Matter*, which is, however, already out of print and is scheduled to appear shortly in a second, significantly improved and expanded edition. While the first volume merely explored the logical domains and established a specific methodology (analysis based on the premise of geometric fundamental units $\tau>0$ ), this second volume attempts, using the foundations laid in the first volume, to develop a unified description of the material world, which will prove to be a unified theory of elementary corpuscles—that is, of the elementary structures of matter. 

However, the question raised at the end of the first volume regarding the separation of the spectrum of ponderable elementary corpuscles from the pseudo-continuous background of all possible imponderable field energies could only be answered through a  preliminary cosmogonic examination of the structure of spacetime itself. Although admittedly somewhat complicated, these lines of reasoning should be followed despite the difficulties involved, because these complications ultimately resolve themselves into facts of monumental simplicity. The uniform spectral relationships of the  particle properties can be numerically calculated without difficulty. A selection of such numerically determined data  can be found in the appendix of tables. As in the preliminary remarks to the first volume, the uncertainty factors $Y_{k}$ have been included here in those mathematical relationships that have not yet been fully clarified. In determining the theoretical numerical data in the  appendix of tables, $Y_{k}=1$ was assumed for all cases, and, as  in the first volume, the international system of units was used for all numerical calculations, so that the constants of influence $\varepsilon_{0}$ and induction $\mu_{0}$  as well as Newton’s gravitational constant $\gamma$ and the action quantum $h=2 \pi \hbar$ express the measurement system used.
