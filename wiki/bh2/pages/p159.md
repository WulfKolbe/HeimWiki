Finally, the affinity $c(m s) d$ means that $d$ is no longer accompanied by $a(1)$, but rather that the  gravitonic $a$ terms now appear only in signature (11).Furthermore, $d$ contains both photonic properties, represented by the  signatures (12) and (22), and complex ponderable properties (13) and  (33). In addition, however, another signature property appears,  namely (23), which does not occur in $a, b$ and $c$. This means that (23) can only describe the electric charge field that causes the condensations $d$, because these forms can be interpreted in terms of charged particles. In this static electric field, due to (23), photonic properties (2) must be combined with those of ponderability or statics (3), to which the confinement of the electrostatic field and its simultaneous propagation at the speed of light (geodesic null line in $R_{4}$) must be attributable. If we now also take into account the first affinity class $a(s) c$ and $b(s) d$ taken into account, it becomes apparent that, based on these affinities alone, there must be transmutational transitions between the fundamental forms of condensation. These presumed transitions would be progressive $a \rightarrow b \rightarrow c$ or $a \rightarrow b \rightarrow d$, or conversely, reversible $(c d) \rightarrow b \rightarrow a$.  Here, $a \rightarrow b$ is possible when time is partially condensed, and $b \rightarrow c$ when this time condensation transitions into a complex spatial condensation. In the case of $b \rightarrow d$, however, this transition of the condensation must occur only partially, whereby the accompanying term $a$ disappears as an independent condensation. However, this disappearance is by no means tied to the transition $b \rightarrow d$; rather, due to $b(s k) c$, the transition $c \rightarrow d$ can also occur, since here only a partial shift to an imaginary time condensation needs to take place upon the disappearance of the independent term $a$. In both  cases $(b c) \rightarrow d$, however, due to the partial spatial and temporal condensation, an electric charge field term characterized by signature (23) must arise.
