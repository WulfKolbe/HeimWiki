Reference frame $R_{6(0)}$ for the $1 \leqq \mu \leqq 3$ lattice cores ${ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(\mu)}$, an analysis of the partial compositions can be developed in the sense of a Hermitian theory of the internal—that is, synmetronic—structure of a condensation of the composition field. Furthermore, due to this possibility of coordinating the $C_{k}$, these lattice nuclei prove to be genuine structural units of the world, such that for $\hat{\gamma}_{x}$ the  indexing $x \hat{=}(a, b, c, d$, 
a selection $\operatorname{der}\left[\begin{array}{c}\widehat{\mu v} \\ -\quad+ \\ \kappa \lambda\end{array}\right] \neq \widehat{0}$ (with respect to the basis signatures) can be carried out for a form of hermetry. 

If individual lattice kernels defined in this way as structural units are hermetic (for example, the structural units $\mu$ and $v$),  then this also holds for the element ${ }^{2} \bar{\gamma}_{(\mu v)}$ from $\hat{\gamma}$. According to the hermetic definition, however, ${ }^{2} \bar{\gamma}_{(\mu \nu)}=$ is constant with respect to the antihermetic coordinates but appears as a function of the hermetic coordinates. These hermetic semantic architectural units, however, refer—according to the interpretation of the lattice cores as structural units—to the respective structural units coordinated with them. If one takes (53) and (53a) into account, and if one of these architectural units is antihermetic—that is, if, for example, with respect to the structural units, $v$ , then ${ }^{2} \bar{\gamma}_{(\nu v)}=$ const. holds, which, due to ${ }^{2} \bar{\gamma}_{(\nu v)}=s p\left({ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(\nu)} \times{ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(\nu)}\right)$, immediately implies that ${ }^{2} \bar{\kappa}_{(\nu)}=$ const. also holds. According to this analysis, it is now possible to classify the specific hermeticity conditions discussed in IV. with respect to $\hat{\gamma}$. There are four possible forms $x$ of hermeticity, which are defined by the influence of sieve operators $S(\mu)$ on $\hat{\gamma}$  in such a way that $S(\mu)$ causes the structural unit $\mu$ to become antihermetic.
