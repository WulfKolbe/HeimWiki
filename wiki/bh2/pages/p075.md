For the time being, absolutely nothing can be said about the nature of such a cosmogony of matter, unless a possible internal structure of the four Hermetic forms from IV were known, which might possibly provide a partial answer to the question of cosmogony. Speculatively, one might assume that the cosmogony of matter—which, like the one in IV, is of an unknown nature—might somehow be related, after the unknown period $T_{M}<T$ of the present world age, to the state of expansion $D\left(T_{M}\right) \geqslant D_{p}$ and the pico-physical $R_{3}$ structure $\tau$; however, this question cannot yet be investigated either. On the other hand, it seems reasonable to first consider the  cosmic motion described by (49) and (49a) in terms of  its boundaries and to analyze its microstructure, as determined by (35a) and (48), with regard to the hermeticity problem.
