There must be $\binom{3}{2}=3$ aggregates $F_{2}$ that, together with $\omega=3$, restore the initial state. The possible aggregates are distributed among the degenerate forms as follows, namely 
$(a \alpha \beta):(1,2) 3,(1,6) 3,(2,6) 3$, furthermore 
$(b \alpha \beta):(3,4) 3,(3,6) 3,(4,6) 3$, as well as 
$(a b \alpha):(1,3) 3,(1,5) 3,(3,5) 3$ and 
$(a b \beta):(2,4) 3,(5,4) 3,(2,5) 3$. For these cyclic flux aggregates of degenerate coupling structures in the cases $v=1$ and $v=2$, there is  no structural isomerism yet, but in both cases there is the possibility of a spin of the relevant condenser flow. This condenser spin, relative to a structuring of the condensation, apparently corresponds to $\omega$ of the cyclic flow and must therefore be ambiguous. There is, therefore, in fact a spin isomerism of the condenser spin, which determines the isomerism possibilities for $v<3$ alone. Only for $v \geqq 3$ do structural isomerisms arise due to $v!>2$. In addition to these structural isomerisms $v \geqq 3$, however, spin isomerisms are always superimposed due to cyclicity $\omega \geqq 1$. However, structural isomers are always stereoisomers in the $R_{6}$, so that for every real flow aggregate $v \geqq 3$ there must be at least one enantiostereoisomeric (spatially mirror-symmetric) aggregate. $v=3$ can also require a simple degeneracy of the coupling structure. In the degenerate case, there are only four basic types with $\omega=2$, namely $(a \alpha \beta) \equiv(1,2,6) 2$, furthermore, $(b \alpha \beta) \equiv(3,4,6) 2, \quad$ as well as $(a b \alpha) \equiv(1,3,5) 2$ and $(a b \beta) \equiv(2,4,5) 2$;  each of which has $3!=6$ structural isomers with the corresponding spin isomers. For the remaining flow aggregates $v>3$, there can be no further degeneracy in the coupling structures, that is, if the coupling structure is degenerate in any way, then $v \leqq 3$ always occurs, but in the case of the regular structure  without the possibility of degeneration $v>3$. For $v=3$, in addition to the four  types of degeneration with $\omega=2$ there are also $\binom{4}{3}=4$ regular types  with $\omega=4$, namely (1,2,5)4, (6,3,1)4, (4,2,6)4, and (1,3,4)4; thus, $v=3$ exhibits ambivalent behavior with respect to simple degeneracy—
