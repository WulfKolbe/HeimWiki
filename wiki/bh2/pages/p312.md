The length $s_{H}=2 \pi r_{H}$ of a $K$ shell meridian appears to be $s=2 \pi y<s_{H}$ , which, however, can only be attributed to invariance with respect to $\hat{A}_{-}$, i.e., it finds its expression in $s=s_{H} \sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}$ or $y=r_{H} \sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}$  with $v_{H}=c \alpha>0$. Accordingly, this invariance, together with the momentum $m v_{H}$ and the invariant transverse mass $m\left(v_{H}\right)$,  of the $e^{-}$ the relation $E_{k}=m v_{H} c=\alpha m c^{2}$, which, when substituted, yields $e_{-}^{2}(1-C)=4 \pi \varepsilon_{0} r_{H} \alpha \sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}} m c^{2}$. Here, $m c^{2}$ is the  energy quantum $h v_{H}=c h / \lambda_{H}$ of the circular electron wave of length  $\lambda_{H}$ of $e^{-}$, which is also regarded as a wave. Substituting $m c^{2}=c h / \lambda_{H}$ and taking into account $\varepsilon_{0} \mathrm{c} \mathrm{R}_{-}=1$ with the electromagnetic  impedance of free space $R_{-}=\sqrt{\mu_{0} / \varepsilon_{0}}$ then follows,  if the quantum duality of $e^{-}$ in the form  $\lambda_{H}=2 \pi r_{H}$ is also used $e_{-}^{2}(1-C)=4 \pi \alpha \sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}} \frac{\hbar}{R_{-}}$. 
In this relation, in turn, the previously verified statement (29) can be substituted, yielding $(2 \pi)^{5} \alpha \sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}=9 v(1-C)$. Because of $C \sim A_{1} A_{2}$ and the fact that the integration constant $A$ can be expressed in terms of $4 A_{1} A_{2}=A$ there must be a proportionality $A \sim C$, because $C$ is a correction term of (29a) and the product $A_{1} A_{2}$ describes the structural deviation of the charge fields of $e^{-}$ and $p$ in the case of correspondence  to the $H$ atom is described by the internal charge field components. However, $C$ provides only the correction term of a structural element—that is, an occupied configuration zone of the electron bound to the  $p$ field by the electromagnetic correspondence. Since $e^{-}$ and $p$ are unit structures with $\underline{N}=1$ but $n_{j}=0$, in which all $j \leqq 4$ configuration zones are occupied with the intact  framework structure $Q_{j}>0$, the value 4—that is, $A=4 C$— or $C=A_{1} A_{2}$, be assumed; however, an uncertainty arises here that can be accounted for by the factor $Y_{3}$ (as described in the preliminary remark).
