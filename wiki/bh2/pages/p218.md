Since $G$ are merely subconstituents of $(+7)$ with respect to the symmetry properties caused by the correlative coupling structures $k$, it seems conceivable that these metaphorically quasi-corpuscular subconstituents cannot freely appear as actual corpuscles. It follows directly from this interpretation of $P_{\text {max }}=G(k)$ that all components of a spin-isomorphic multiplet must be characterized by the same $k$ value, even though these components (as composite $\bar{\lambda}$ terms) may well belong to different forms of hermeticism. 

While $\sigma_{t}$ is always imaginary according to (89a) due to $P \geqq 0$ (one could  exclude $P=0$), this does not hold for $\sigma_{r}$. The algebraic character of this spatial spin depends on $P_{a}$, that is, on whether $2 J=Q$ is even or odd. First, let us consider $Q=2 n$ with the positive integers $n \geqq 0$. Because of $J=n$, $P_{a}= \pm 1$, and thus $\sigma_{r}= \pm i n$ is imaginary in the same way as $\sigma_{t}$.  However, according to quantized canonical field mechanics, in the case of $Q=2 n$, the wave function of this state is always described by a tensor field of $\operatorname{Grad} J_{T}=n$, so that these $\bar{\lambda}$ terms should be referred to as tensor terms. Since the carrier space of the abstract  function space of such a tensorial function is $R_{6}$, the following holds:
