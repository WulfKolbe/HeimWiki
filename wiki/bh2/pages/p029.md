If the minor components are expanded into the invariant secular polynomial, an algebraic equation of degree equal to the matrix rank results; however, in the case of a $R_{6}$second-order tensor of matrix type 6 is identical to that of the quadratic matrix. However, an algebraic equation of the 6th degree cannot be solved in general. Nevertheless, it can be established that, in general, the characteristic equation and its solutions exist, because in any case $\overrightarrow{\vec{T}}=\overrightarrow{T^{×}}$  and, due to the tensor nature with respect to the permitted coordinate transformations, the regularity $\operatorname{def} \vec{T}=0$—that is, $r g \vec{T}=6$—must also hold. Furthermore, if—for physical reasons—the at least semidefinite nature $s p \overrightarrow{\vec{T}} \geqq 0$ is assumed, then, regardless of the specific structure of $T_{i k}$, solely from these general properties of $\overrightarrow{\vec{T}}$ the existence of $\vec{M}$ is immediately evident, regardless of whether $\stackrel{\overrightarrow{3}}{T}$ can be explicitly represented by  phenomenological physical field quantities  or not. 

The empirical principle (a) from I., 1. requires $\overline{\operatorname{div}_{6}} \vec{T}=\overrightarrow{0}$, which  necessarily implies the field tensor $\overline{\operatorname{div}_{6}} \stackrel{\vec{M}}{\vec{M}}=\overrightarrow{0}$ as well.  Now, $\stackrel{\vec{M}}{M}$ is to be understood as the double rate of change of the $R_{4}$ segment $\vec{M}_{(4)}$ , whose source, according to $\overline{\operatorname{div}}_{4} \vec{M}_{(4)}=A \vec{I}$, is proportional to a complex $R_{4}$ current $\vec{I}$. If the decomposition $A \vec{I}=\vec{X}+i \vec{Z}$ applies to this current, then $\vec{Z}$ describes an electric charge current and $\vec{X}$ that of the inertial mass. Thus, in the case of a neutral current, $\vec{Z}=\overrightarrow{0}$ may indeed hold, but $\vec{X} \cdot \overrightarrow{0}$ always remains true, because every type of dynamics is associated with at least one energy flux, for which the energy-matter equivalence fundamentally applies. 

In $\overrightarrow{\vec{M}}=-\overrightarrow{\vec{M}}^{\times}$, let $M_{k 5}=i f_{k}\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{4}\right) \varphi\left(x_{5}, x_{6}\right)$ and  $M_{k 6}=i F_{k}\left(x_{1} \ldots x_{4}\right) \psi\left(x_{5}, x_{6}\right)$, but $M_{56}=i W\left(x_{5}, x_{6}\right)$, where the $1 \leqq k \leqq 4$ functions $f_{k}$ or $F_{k}$ are the components of the $R_{4}$vectors $\vec{f}$ and $\vec{F}$.
