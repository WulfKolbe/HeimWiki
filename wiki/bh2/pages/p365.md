Although, due to (118a) and (112), neutrino states behave like  ponderable $M q$, on the other hand, due to ( $0 \mid$, the characteristic feature of ponderability—namely, the protosimplex occupancies of complex hermeticity—is missing. At most, neutrino states with $\phi<0$ could be regarded as terms of imaginary Hermitian symmetry, but in addition to the  criteria of ponderability in (118b), so that the neutrino states, which are possible as $v_{x}$ radiation, may be regarded as neither ponderable nor imponderable.  Rather, as a consequence of applicable symmetry principles, these neutrinos appear to be non-metric states of the empty but metronized $R_{3}$, which, as $v_{x}$radiation, transfer certain invariance properties across the $R_{3}$ and, so to speak, act as “field catalysts” reactions of ponderable $M q$—reactions that would not take place without this influence. The theoretical $v_{\beta}$ value (or $v_{R}$ or $v_{P}$ ) appears to be quite consistent with the Heidelberg $\nu_{\beta}$ measurements ${ }^{(5)}$, which were carried out during the double $\beta_{-}$-  process $T e^{130} \rightarrow X e^{130}$ and $T e^{128} \rightarrow X e^{128}$.
