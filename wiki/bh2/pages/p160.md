A pseudoaffinity $c(m k) d$ refers to the complex property common to $c$ and $d$. The solutions to the synmetric fundamental problem are determined by $\exp ( \pm \lambda \mu)$ with $\lambda=\lambda^{*}$ and $\mu \neq \mu^{*}$  (complex), i.e., $\lambda \mu=\alpha+i \beta$ or $\exp ( \pm \lambda \mu)=e^{ \pm \alpha}(\cos \beta \pm i \sin \beta)$, from which it follows that only $\operatorname{Im} \mu$ condensation stages, whereas $R e \mu=\alpha$ forms a $R_{3}$ structural field of a metric nature that is free of condensation stages, which encloses the condensation stages and, with $e^{-\alpha}$, exhibits a strongly decaying short-range interaction character. Despite the absence of condensation stages, this field appears quantum-like in the third domain of validity because it is determined by the signatures (33) and (3) just as much as by (13) and (23) or (31) and (32), as well as by the overall configuration of the $c$ or $d$ coupling structure. To classify such structural fields and their internal configurations, it seems reasonable to define a  characteristic configuration number $k$ (an integer) as a kind of  principal quantum number. $k=0$ would mean that the term in question does not exist, so $k \geqq 1$ must hold. On the other hand, $k$ must remain below a finite bound $k_{\text {max }}<\infty$, because, for physical reasons, all structural configurations must remain finite—that is, they must not diverge.
