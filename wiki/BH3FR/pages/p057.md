From these coupling structures, it can be seen that there are, in principle, six different forms (76) of condenser flows that dynamically build up the internal correlation structure in the hermetic forms. This integral dynamic correlation thus also entails an integral synmetronic flow period, after which the initial state of the coupling structure—that is, the distribution of the coupling and condenser maxima—is restored. If, after at least one such period, the initial state is no longer regenerated, this results in the radioactive decay of a complex hermetic form or, in the case of an imaginary condensation, an energetic transformation—in which, however, the principle of compressor isostasis is always preserved,  which is phenomenologically expressed in the principle of conservation of energy. The term in question obviously does not exist if the initial state of the dynamic flow system is not reached even during the first integral flow period. In the case of complex hermetries, the temporal extent of a compositional term is apparently characterized as a temporal stability interval by an integer (at least 1)  cyclic integral flow periods, such that such a  temporal stability interval is characterized by the steady state of a dynamic equilibrium of the synmetronic condenser flows in  the integral cyclic flow.
