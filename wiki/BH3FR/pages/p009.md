The glossary provides brief explanations, primarily of those terms that were newly introduced by Heim. 

The formula index includes formulas (*), (*a), (*b), (1) through (118c) from Volumes 1 and 2 of *Elementary Structures of Matter*. These are followed by the formulas for the methodological relations M1 through M31c from Volume 1, Chapter III, which replace relations A through Z(5)b from the first edition of Volume 1. 

As for the notation of these formulas, the standard defined by DIN was generally followed, provided that the  scope of application pertains to the calculus of infinitesimals $\tau=0$. There were only three instances where, for practical reasons, we deviated from this standard. Thus, $C$ is denoted by the symbol `{ }^{\text {^ }}` in the form $\hat{C}$ as a matrix. If the matrix elements $C_{i k}$ belong to the complex algebraic field, then this means $C_{i k} \neq C_{i k}^{*}$; however, if an index transposition is adjoint to this complex conjugation, this is expressed by the symbol $x$ in the form $\left(C_{i k}\right)^{\times}=C_{k i}^{*}$. 

Instead of the commutator symbol $[a, b]=a b-b a$ defined by the norm, the old symbol $(a \times b)_{ \pm}=a b \pm b a$ is used,  since $[a, b]$ had already been assigned by Heim in earlier studies to a different concept. Finally, instead of the very practical nabla operator, the old operator symbols div, grad, and rot are also used in $R_{3}$, because when working in higher-dimensional spaces $R_{n}$ the operator with the dimension number $n$ becomes indexable according to $\operatorname{div}_{n}$,  $\operatorname{grad}_{n}$ and $\operatorname{rot}_{n}$. Here, however, $\operatorname{div}_{n}$ describes only  the scalar divergence of a vector field in $R_{\mathrm{n}}$. If it is the tensor gradient $m$ by 1, this is denoted by $\overline{\operatorname{div}}_{n}$, whereas the tensor divergence of the relevant field—which extends the tensor gradient $m$ to $m+1$ is expressed by $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}_{n}$. However, these operators are rarely needed in this text. 

In the domains of validity $\tau>0$, on the other hand, a notation not covered by DIN is used, but it is explained in Volume 1.
