A finite number of defined boson-fermion transformations, which are determined by the number $N$. Assuming $N=8$, there are 70 particles with spin $s=0,56$, particles with $s=1 / 2,28$  particles with $s=1,8$, particles with $s=3 / 2$, and 1 particle with $s=2$.  These extended models exhibit an additional, very significant property. 

In quantum field theory, diagrams in which virtual  particles form closed loops lead to infinite energy values  as soon as one sums over all possible energies. In quantum electrodynamics, these infinite contributions were eliminated using the renormalization method; in quantum gravity, however, even this method failed. In the extended supergravity theories, the infinite contributions cancel each other out, and diagrams with one or two closed loops yield a finite value. This result is promising, but it is still marred by the following properties of the extended models. 

Although $N=8$ describes a large number of particles, this number is still too small compared to the multitude of particles already discovered. For $N>8$, at least two gravitons appear, which, however, should be detectable macroscopically. An extension of the supergravity theory beyond $N=8$ is therefore not  accepted. A possible revision of this theory could, under certain circumstances, resolve the problems identified.
