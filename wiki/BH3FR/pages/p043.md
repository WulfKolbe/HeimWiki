Given the metronic nature of the world-torium, one can infer—using the methodological relations (M1) through (M31)—a projection theorem (30) for metronic spatial structures onto a plane. Applying this theorem to (27) and (27a) then allows for the explicit derivation of lower bounds for complex hermiticity as complex minimal condensations. Thus, via (31), an exact expression is obtained for the mass of the lower bound of the $c$spectrum as $m_{L}$ and the approximate mass $m_{e}>m_{L}$ as the lower  bound of the $d$spectrum, which is characterized by the fact that, as a  minimum mass, it is still capable of supporting a charge field (29). According to  (27) and (27a), a neutral complement $m_{e}$ a neutral complement $m_{B}$ would still be conceivable, which, however, unlike $m_{L}$ and $m_{e}$, does not need to exist in reality as an elementary structure. The components of this triad (32) of minimal masses depend solely on the empirical natural constants of the action quantum, the speed of light, and the gravitational constant (Newton), whereby the speed of light, as a derived constant, is determined—just like the wave impedance of empty space in (29)—by the influence and induction constants.  For this reason, the induction, influence, and gravitational constants, as well as the action quantum, are designated as fundamental empirical  constant of nature. Substituting the numerical measured values  of these constants shows that $m_{e}$ is identical to the empirically measured electron mass with an error of approximately  0.53%,  while the two electrically neutral components of the triad differ from $m_{e}$ by approximately $1 \%$. It should be noted here that these considerations initially reveal a criterion (33) which specifies the conditions under which a material elementary structure with rest mass can transmute via a $\beta$ decay.
