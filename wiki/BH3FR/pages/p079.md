All atomic structures of matter—and thus also its macroscopic properties—are attributable solely to the elementary structures of matter (112). If one disregards the arbitrariness of the units of measurement—which is reflected in the calibration factor of (112)—it can be established that the properties of every term of complex hermetics can be traced back, on the one hand, to the natural limit values $\pi, e$ and $\zeta$ according to (110d) and, on the other hand, to twelve integers of the scheme (115). The four  occupation parameters can thereby be reduced, according to (110), to the sequence of positive  integers corresponding to the resonance orders, whose upper  bound—just like the protosimplex generator—traces back solely to the sets of quantum numbers of the schema (102). However, according to (99) through (100a), these quantum numbers can be reduced solely to the configuration number $k$ and the decision $\pm 1$ of temporal helicity. For the nuclear structures of the atoms of material elements and their electron shells, only the invariant basic patterns of resonance order 0 are certainly relevant, whereas $k$ can be substituted by the baryon number $B=k-1$, for which, due to (98a), only the values 0 or 1 are possible. Thus, the entire range of material manifestations in the physical world appears to trace back to the number 0 and the unit with its two possible signs (time helicity). Despite the extremely complicated path toward a unified structural quantum field theory of material elementary structures, this fact appears to be of truly monumental simplicity.
