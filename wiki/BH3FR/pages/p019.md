# 2. Unification of the field theories associated with the various interactions

 

Similar to Riemannian geometry, the application of which shed light on the nature of gravitation, the concept of gauge fields has recently been developed, in which the notion of symmetry plays a central role. It is assumed that all four fundamental interaction fields can be explained as gauge fields. 
Laws of nature can retain their form under coordinate transformations, such as rotation and translation. If all points in space are subjected to the same transformation, then the resulting symmetry is called global. In contrast, a symmetry is local in nature if each point in space is transformed independently of its neighboring points. If laws of nature are invariant under a global symmetry, then invariance under a local symmetry can only be achieved by introducing new fields. These are called gauge fields (Yang–Mills fields). Although they play an essential role in nature, they are not observable. However, it turned out that the gauge symmetry can be spontaneously broken
