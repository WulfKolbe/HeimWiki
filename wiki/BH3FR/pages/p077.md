The scope of the unified structural theory presented here is relatively broad, ranging from the macroscopic structure of spacetime to certain, not yet fully understood, astrophysical phenomena—such as the absence of gravitational systems of a higher order than galaxy clusters, cosmological redshift anomalies, and the magnetic fields of rotating celestial bodies (which may shed light on Earth’s glacial periods)— to the  elementary structures of matter in the microcosmic realm. Furthermore,  the connection to the current state of knowledge in theoretical physics is evident. Through a chain of approximations of the world selector relation (19) into the fourth domain of validity of the macroscopic field continuum, the fundamental relations of general relativity emerge, thereby encompassing the entire empirical domain of classical mechanics. If, on the other hand, the synmetronic world selector relation (58)—derived from (19) via (53)—is applied to $b$  hermetry—that is, the photon (timelike pseudohexametry in the  correlator)— then a chain of approximations (selected according to other criteria) can be extended into the third domain of validity, which explicitly yields the time-dependent Dirac operator. This, in turn, covers the entire empirical range of exactly valid  quantum electrodynamics, whereby the approximation of the Dirac relation in the fourth validity region leads to the electromagnetic law of induction and thus to classical electrodynamics and  optics.
