According to Heim, fundamental physical phenomena such as gravity, electromagnetism, mass, and electrically charged mass are merely expressions of a non-Euclidean geometry (in the sense of such polymetries). Geometric considerations serve as the basis for the computational determination of the quantum numbers of elementary particles. Instead of Einstein’s field equations, fully geometrized, polymetric, and metronized eigenvalue equations appear in a six-dimensional space, whereby the phenomenological energy-momentum-density tensor is replaced by a geometric expression containing the fundamental tensor. The geodesic equation in HEIMS theory contains fundamental condensers, which can be interpreted as general interaction fields of tensor potentials. Only in the presence of a gravitational field can the fundamental condenser associated with gravity be eliminated. The special status of gravity becomes apparent here.

In addition to Planck, HEIM arrives at another, purely geometric form of quantization, which arises from the inferred existence  of an irreducible unit of area $\tau$, which he calls the “metron.” Its size corresponds approximately to the square of the Planck length. A six-dimensional space $R_{6}$, in which the four-dimensional spacetime $R_{4}$ accessible to us is embedded, is quantized.  A field theory in continuous spacetime becomes an algebraic theory consisting of multiples of metrons. The analogy between the action quantum—which occurs only in multiples of an integer—and the unit of area $\tau$—which also occurs only in multiples of an integer—is striking.
