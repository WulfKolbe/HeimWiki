The defining feature of structural theory is evidently the relativization of the concept of “elementary” as suggested by synmetronics. With regard to the property of being matter, elementary corpuscles—as well as photons and gravitons—are indeed elementary and can be addressed as elementary structures of matter. However, with regard to the Synmetronics of internal coupling structures, this concept of the elementary applies just as little as it does to the quasi-corpuscular subconstituents of partial flux aggregates. The actual structural unit of last resort here is the protosimplex, which, however, can by no means be a primordial phenomenon of the world. A protosimplex is composed of the basic flux aggregate of a flucton and static shielding fields, whereby these two prototropes both trace back to the concept of the elementary synmetronic condensation stage. Such a stage, however, presupposes a protofield, and this in turn presupposes a constant field activator. These field activators, in turn, preform the protofield by inducing a metronic spin anisotropy in the completely isotropic empty world (16b). The actual primordial phenomenon of the world is therefore a single metronic spin anisotropy that possesses no properties whatsoever except for its existence or nonexistence. The state space of such a primordial phenomenon is therefore a two-dimensional complex vector space that is isomorphic to the real (compact with respect to the rotation group) three-dimensional space, whose coordinates are interchangeable. Thus, the universe appears to be characterized by its prototrope structure. 

In general, the unified synmetronic structure theory  seems to imply the so-called quark model of elementary particles, provided that certain corrections are made to this model; for the  ordering scheme of the quark model is also provided by the structure theory, while, moreover (and in contrast to the  quark model), the particle masses are by no means problematic. Here, a numerical determination of the terms in (112) and their comparison with the experimental data speak for themselves. Furthermore, the empirical introduction of the concept of the preon or rishon through the theoretically derived concept of the protosimplex also appears to be justified.
