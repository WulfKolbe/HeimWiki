According to HEIM’s theory, the hidden parameters mentioned in the section “Quantum Theory” would be possible; however, these are organizational states in HEIM’s framework and do not imply either a purely deterministic (“The world is a clockwork”) or a purely indeterministic physics (“'The world is just  coincidence'”), but rather represent a middle ground between these  two approaches. According to him, world events unfold within a $R_{6} \mathrm{ab}$. The timelike transcoordinates $x_{5}$ and $x_{6}$, which are also referred to as organizational states, steer events toward an actualization in the $R_{4}$. Of the 6 world coordinates $x_{1}$ through $x_{6}$, , $x_{5}$ is designated as the entelechial dimension and $x_{6}$ as the aonic dimension, whereas $x_{4}, x_{5}, x_{6}$ are of an imaginary nature. $x_{5}=i \varepsilon$  should be regarded as a measure for evaluating temporally changing organizational states and is the inverse of the concept of entropy (a measure of disorganization), whereas $x_{6}=i \eta$ is the coordinate through which the $x_{5}$ structure is guided during its temporal transition to the steady-state, dynamically stable condition. From a manifold of possible events of a particle characterized by $x_{6}$, a $x_{5}$ , and consequently, separated by a time interval mediated by $x_{4}$, a first world point is transformed into a second. Only when there is a large number of similar events does a corresponding $x_{5}$ structure give rise to macroscopic causality. The probabilistic nature of an event, as derived from known quantum theory, would thus only arise through a mapping of $R_{6}$ onto $R_{4}$.  The formalism of standard quantum field theory—which has essentially only been experimentally confirmed for quantum electrodynamics—would therefore not be transferable to Heim’s $R_{6}$ since quantum theory presupposes a $R_{4}$. Furthermore, a necessary condition of quantum field theory is the existence of locally valid differential equations. This assumption does not apply to Heim’s metron theory and would only be satisfied after a limit transition $\tau \rightarrow 0$. Consequently, HEIM uses mean values instead of probability amplitudes in his calculations.
