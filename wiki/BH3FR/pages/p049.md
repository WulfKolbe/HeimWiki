Redshift is attributable to direction-dependent density anomalies. It can also be seen from (45) that there is a critical distance (46) in space relative to the observer, beyond which the redshift diverges to infinity, because over this distance the entire photonic energy is consumed via the gravitational scattering process. Numerical calculations show that this critical distance is completely identical to the Hubble radius. Consequently, the universe of general relativity is merely a relative optical universe (46a), within which photonic signals can be received, whereas the real universe, according to (37b), is substantially larger (46b) and can contain a great many such optical space segments in the sense of optical universes. Thus, the cosmological paradox could be resolved with (11), although the interpretation of the redshift as a Doppler effect had to be abandoned.  According to this picture, the entire universe appears dynamic, but can be approximated as quasi-static. It also appears finite  and unbounded, yet quasi-infinite due to (37b) and (15). While this does indeed eliminate the simple but speculative image of a cosmogonic Big Bang, the question of a cosmogony of spacetime becomes all the more interesting.
