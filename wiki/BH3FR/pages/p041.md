However, since this spectrum is superimposed from the four partial spectra and the spectra of the complex condensations overlap as point spectra (discrete) with a larger thermal spacing, the quasi-continuum can only be attributed to the imaginary condensations $a$ and $b$. Although one can already derive, in advance, from the description of d-hermeticism an empirically verifiable relationship for the elementary electric charge (29) and a relatively good approximation for the Sommerfeld fine-structure constant of light (29a),  the spectra of ponderable elementary structures of matter ( $c$ and $d$ ), no conclusions can be drawn that would be empirically verifiable using the technologies of  high-energy physics. Consequently, the central problem for the second volume of this exposition is the question of separating the partial spectra. This involves finding a term selector that separates the discrete point spectra of the complex Hermitian forms $c$  and $d$ from the background of the pseudocontinuous imaginary Hermitian forms $a$ and $b$.
