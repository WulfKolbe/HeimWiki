According to the quark model, each hadron contains two or three quarks; however, according to HEIM’s theory, an elementary particle has four configuration zones. The fourth zone characterizes the outer region of a particle. It has a relatively low energy density. The three remaining high-density zones could correspond to particles that have been experimentally detected. They could be identical to quarks, in which case two configuration zones could be very close together and could be interpreted as a single quark.  These internal structures cannot be isolated as individual particles from the structural framework, which would explain the confinement of the quarks. Color charges, which are necessary in quark theory to hold three quarks with the same spin together as a consequence of the Pauli principle, would not need to be postulated. Interaction fields emanate from the outermost zone, and their intensity remains locally finite. The question of renormalization would be rendered moot here.
