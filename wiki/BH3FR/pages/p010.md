In order to better understand physics in all its diverse manifestations and to be able to make the broadest possible range of theoretical predictions, scientists seek laws of nature that describe more than just a narrowly defined set of physical phenomena. Part of these efforts to develop a unified view of nature is the realization—gained only in our century—that natural phenomena are ultimately governed by four fundamental forces acting between elementary particles. These forces are mediated by interaction fields: the electromagnetic, gravitational, strong, and weak fields. The latter two were discovered only in the first half of our century due to their short range. 

The goal of modern physics is now to discover laws of nature that describe all known particles and interaction fields collectively. This unification is approached using principles that have proven useful and successful in the past. These include the concept of gauge fields, the search for suitable symmetry groups of minimal scope, and the application of supersymmetry transformations to various fields. 

Burkhard HEIM’s theory, now available in two volumes, has likewise set itself the goal of describing as many known elementary particles as possible in a unified manner.
