Regardless of the diverse (and in some cases seemingly contradictory) properties of these elementary corpuscles and the many possible interpretations of them, it can be stated that these material elementary structures are certainly centers that obey the principle of inertia, as well as centers of interactions of whatever nature, whose existence can be experienced by the observing human being using the technological methods of high-energy physics. Since experience always presupposes the processing of that experience, and this processing in turn presupposes experience, but only what happens can be experienced—and occurrences are temporal consequences of events—events can be regarded as elements of the  experiencable world, which in turn can be identified as points in a four-dimensional spacetime through three spatial and one temporal coordinate. Consequently, elementary particles must in any case be understood as event structures of a spacetime in the sense of non-Euclidean deformations, whose structure is determined by the respective fields of interaction that characterize the particle in question. 

Since all these corpuscles behave inertially and, on the other hand, the  principle of equivalence of inertia and gravity holds, gravity appears not only to be an interaction field that applies equally to all elementary corpuscles; rather, it proves to be, in addition, the general, macroscopic background phenomenon against which microscopic events unfold. For this reason, an initial attempt was made to develop a phenomenological theory of gravitational dynamics in the macroscopic realm ((*) to (*b)), whereby it becomes apparent that the field and its source must always be considered as a single unit.
