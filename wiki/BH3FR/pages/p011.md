The history of physics in our century is inextricably linked to the name Albert Einstein. In addition to his contributions to quantum theory, he was the discoverer (inventor) of the special and general theories of relativity. Even at a young age, he was fascinated by the problem of what would happen if he were traveling at the same speed as a beam of light. He would then be able to observe the light beam as a static electromagnetic field, which, however, contradicts Maxwell’s equations. 

Albert A. Michelson and Edward W. Morley had already attempted, in the second half of the 19th century, to detect differences in the speed of light by using a light beam emitted both in the direction of the Earth’s motion and perpendicular to it; however, the result was negative. 

Einstein’s solution to these problems was the special theory of relativity (STR), which he published in 1905 and which dealt with the relativity of space and time between two inertial frames moving relative to one another at constant speeds. Einstein realized that light propagates in every inertial frame in all directions at the same speed. An observer in the first frame will detect, in the second frame, a change in the time and length scales that depends on the observer’s relative velocity with respect to the second frame. 

Thus, the ether theory—which postulated an absolute space and an absolute time—had been disproved.
