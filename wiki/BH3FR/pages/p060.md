Since the spatial structure field can be visualized as superimposed on the flow structure—as if “laid over” it, so to speak—this structural field is referred to as a straton. The proposed notation (82) for fluctones and shield fields as prototropes is exceptionally practical, because (82) allows the complex correlative coupling structures (69) through (71a) to be written very simply. A flukton is always enveloped by a shield field (±), thereby creating a most elementary structure, namely a so-called protosimplex (82a). These protosimplexes obviously possess no material properties. Furthermore, due to the principle of compressor isostasy, they can never exist on their own. It is only through the correlation of these protosimples via their fluktonic prototropes that the typical characteristics of the material properties of the elementary structures of the second order become apparent. The synmetronic coupling structures of the hermetic forms from (69) to (71a) are thus self-contained, correlative flow systems of protosimplexes (82a). 

Due to the various steric arrangements of the protosimplexes possible in the world tensorium and the possibility of fluctonic ortho- and paraspins, as well as the diverse signature isomerisms of synmetronic condenser signatures, there must be higher-order isomerisms of the dynamic correlative coupling structures (83) through (83b),  for which there is a good metaphor in organic chemistry—optically active antipodes—so that these isomeries have been termed enantiostereoisomeries; for every coupling structure, there must be—with respect to its dynamic character—an enantiomorph that behaves in a mirror-symmetric manner in all components dependent on the flow system and must appear phenomenologically as an antistructure. 

The correlation between two fluctones can only occur through an exchange of the condenser maxima around a correlation maximum (correlation center) and is formally described by the operation of the so-called conjunctives (84) and (84a).
