The approximations (79a) through (79c) show that every complex condensation in physical space is characterized by an exponentially steeply decaying near-field, a fact that has long been known empirically. 

By analogy with the derivation of (79), it becomes possible to determine the six existing forms of elementary synmetronic condenser flows (80), although additional information (80a) regarding the respective correlator of the relevant hermetic form had to be derived. Typical of the flows described by (80) in the case of a correlation exponent of 1 is the pseudohomonous identity of the basis and counter-signature of the fundamental condensers that occur (81). 

The elementary synmetronic vector eigenvalues (58) are evidently, with respect to the time dimension, dynamic—or, in special cases, static—structural archetypes of the world, which are briefly referred to as prototopes. If the fundamental condensers in question are heteronomously signed (i.e., the basis and contra-signatures are different) and if the existence condition of the condenser flow via field activators is satisfied, then these are dynamic prototopes, which are referred to as fluctones and can be represented by (80). If, on the other hand, the systems consist of homonomous or pseudo-homonomous fundamental condensers (81), then the condition for the condenser flow is not satisfied, so that under condition (81) the prototropes appear statically as screen fields of a singular or correlative nature. Accordingly, in accordance with (80), there are six classes of fluctones, of which the sixth fluctone is also referred to as the world fluctone, because its basic flow pattern, as a consequence of eneametry, encompasses all structural units of the world. For the remaining five fluktons, there are five classes of shield fields, which—in contrast to the flukton (–)—are indexed with (+).
