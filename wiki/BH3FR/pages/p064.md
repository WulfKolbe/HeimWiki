It is applied to individual cases because, at first, more needs to be learned about the physical nature of the coupling structures and the terms of complex Hermeticism. 

It is in the nature of the concept of the protosimplex that, under specific conditions, multiple protosimplexes superimpose in such a way that their fluktonic prototropes form a higher-order fundamental flow system. Thus, the coupling structures of the hermetic forms—in which only simple protosimplexes correlate via their condenser flows—appear as elementary basic patterns whose simple protosimplex configuration is determined by invariant parameters, that is, by quantum numbers (such as $k, P, Q$ and $q$ as electric charge numbers). Since the property of inertia (77) stems solely from protosimplex correlation, these protosimplexes are essentially relevant with respect to the energy-mass of the  term of a hermetic form, so that, due to the superposability of the protosimplexes, there must be a spectrum of excited states for every invariant fundamental pattern. It therefore seemed reasonable to define the concept of protosimplex charge, whose structural analysis leads to the defining relations (96) through (96b). According to this, an elementary ground pattern would be characterized by the fact that all protosimplexes correlating in the relevant coupling structure have charge 1, whereas the terms of the associated excitation spectrum are characterized by higher protosimplex charges. The mass contributions of individual protosimplexes can obviously be described by taking the differences of minimal complex condensations (32), but this presupposes that the lower bound of the $d$ spectrum is also specified. A synmetronic analysis of the coupling structure of this lower $d$ bound  (which must certainly be regarded as an elementary fundamental pattern) yields  a correction (96b), in which only the component $\beta$ of the strongest internal coupling is unknown; however, for this component, $\beta \approx 1$ can be set as a good approximation for the time being. The mass contributions of the possible proto-simplex structures as well as of the integral straton spin are given by (97), which follows from the triad (32) refined by (96b).
