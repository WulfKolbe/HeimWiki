The coupling constant of the electromagnetic interaction $e^{2} / \hbar c$, on the other hand, is a numerical value independent of $D$ in Heim’s theory. At present, no conclusions can yet be drawn in Heim’s theory regarding the coupling constants of the strong and weak interactions. 

Even though the strong and weak interactions do not yet explicitly follow from Heim’s theory (because Heim has not yet addressed them), it can nevertheless be regarded as a unified field theory encompassing all four interactions. This is evident, among other things, in the derived formulas for the theoretical determination of the rest mass and quantum numbers of elementary particles, whose values agree very closely with those determined experimentally. Only $\gamma, h, \varepsilon_{o}$ and $\mu_{o}$ are used as empirical natural constants. Another feature of a unified theory is the fact that simplifying the tensorial metronized eigenvalue equations by first forming traces and then applying a series of approximations yields, on the one hand, the Einstein field equations, while the application of a different trace formation, combined with the simultaneous implementation of approximation steps, yields the Dirac equations of quantum electrodynamics. A connection between general relativity and electrodynamics is thus established. 

However, in its present form, Heim’s field theory provides only  a basic framework for a theory that still leaves a great many questions  and answers unresolved. Nevertheless, the wealth of theoretical data consistent with experimental results points to such a successful concept that this theory cannot be ignored and requires intensive study.
