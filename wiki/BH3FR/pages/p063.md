The concept of correlation always refers to the internal coupling of synmetronic condenser flows, which is characterized by the coupling maximum, in which the fundamental condensers disappear as condenser minima, such that a correlation center is always a Euclidean or pseudo-Euclidean region on the  order of magnitude of a metronic cell; that is, these coupling centers must always appear point-like in terms of current experimental physics technologies, which under certain circumstances can simulate a lack of structure. 

The condenser maxima, on the other hand, are characterized by coupling minima; yet, due to the validity of the geodesic equation, they must be understood—in both the compositive and synmetronic cases—as  interaction potencies that act outward from the coupling structure (as the source structure) and are referred to as correspondence fields. With these correspondence fields (94) through  (95a), the entire spectrum of possible interactions can be described, the center of which—as the source region—is the concrete term  of a hermetic form.
