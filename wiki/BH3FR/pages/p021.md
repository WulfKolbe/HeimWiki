Gravitational interaction is still ruled out here.  However, the $S U(5)$ theory cannot explain the observed cosmological baryon-to-photon ratio. Furthermore, according to this theory, there should be just as many monopoles as there are baryons. The predicted proton decay also does not appear to occur (Irvine-Michigan-Brookhaven experiment). 

Another possibility for unifying the strong and electroweak interactions would be the assumption of subconstituents in quarks and leptons. The 6 different types of quarks with distinct quantum numbers, whereby each  quark can have one of 3 color charges, the 3 leptons already discovered $e, \mu$ and $\tau$, and the corresponding neutrinos $v_{\varepsilon}, v_{\mu}, v_{\tau}$  —together with their antiparticles—already form such a large group that a composition of even more elementary particles can be speculatively assumed. This leads to the preon and rishon models, according to which a quark or lepton consists of 3 subconstituents. However, the theories mentioned are neither internally consistent nor experimentally verified.
