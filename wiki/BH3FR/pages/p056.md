To classify the internal correlations of these complex partial spectra from (27) and (27a), it becomes necessary to use a number $k$ as a configuration number, which does not exist for  imaginary condensations but, for complex condensations, lies within the closed integer interval (72) between the  value 1 and an unknown but finite maximum value. 

From the distribution of the extrema of synmetronic fundamental condensers and the scalar coupling selectors (68), conclusions can be drawn  about the temporal behavior of the correlative coupling structures (69)  through (71a). First, it follows that the anti-Hermitian constant selector of a synmetronic fundamental selector can exist only in the opposite signature of a fundamental condenser, which implies the covariant Hermiticity of these condensers. On the other hand, these anti-Hermitian components of the correlator elements act as  field activators in the subspaces of the respective structural units (73), such that they latently preform a field state through constant metronic spin orientations in  the respective subspace.  The fundamental condensers, on the other hand, appear—like the condenser of the composition field—as general synmetronic interactions, because even in the synmetronic case a geodesic equation  (74) can be metronized, whereby for each of these condensers there exists a synmetronic space compressor that strives for the lowest possible compression level. It can also be shown that, due to the Hermitian symmetry in the world tensor, divergence-free conditions (74a) must hold with respect to the compositional space compressor, which is formed by the tensorial interaction of the space condenser with the compositional fundamental condenser. According to this principle of compressor isostasis, the tensorial selector derived from the compositive space compressor in (74a) remains divergence-free regardless of the synmetronic space compressors, i.e., the composite compression state that characterizes the relevant hermetic forms remains preserved regardless of the compensatory tendency (74) of the synmetronic compression states.
