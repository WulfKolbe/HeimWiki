# 6. Quasiparticle Subconstituents of the Terms of Complex Hermeticism

 

Since the double component of the straton spin in the imaginary subspace of the world according to (90) specifies the number of spin-isomorphic terms of a  multiplet, the upper bound (90a) describes the number of partial flux aggregates in the entire coupling structure of a $c$ or $d$ term that form their own correlation centers in real space;  since the entire correlative coupling structure can never refer to a single common spatial correlation center due to the presence of 3 synmetronic structural units (53). This upper bound is the configuration number increased additively by 1, for which, however, there are only two possibilities (98a). In the mesonic region, there are thus, due to $k=1$, only two, but in the baryonic region, with $k=2$, there are a total of 3 partial flux aggregates with their own centers of correlation in physical space. These internal partial fluxes have  no corpuscular properties and cannot be isolated,  yet it is possible to conceive of them as quasi-corpuscular subconstituents. Here, an analogy to the currently discussed  quark theory seems to emerge. Here, too, two quarks are required for mesons and three for baryons, which are supposed to have point-like properties. Under this assumption, the empirical concept of isospin can be replaced by the concept of the up or down quark. In fact, both isospin and the quark concept were introduced for empirical reasons, to bring a principle of order to the very great diversity of empirical particle properties, whereby a plausible interpretation of the quark concept becomes just as problematic as one of isospin. If, on the other hand, one assumes the $G=k+1$ partial flux aggregates (90a) to be quasi-corpuscular subconstituents, then this provides a principle of order that unproblematically reflects the empirical facts.
