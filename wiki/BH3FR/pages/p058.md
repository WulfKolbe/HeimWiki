Every elementary cyclic condenser flux has a spin vector perpendicular to the flux plane. It can be shown (77) that the vector eigenvalues (58) of the synmetronic world selector are parallel to these spin vectors, while the flux velocity corresponds to the magnitude of the imaginary part of the world velocity (5c). This orthogonality with respect to the world velocity also holds for the compositional eigenvectors (19), which, however, refer to the integral correlative flux aggregate of individual synmetronic elementary condenser fluxes of all forms of hermetry. Thus, if such a form of hermetry is accelerated in physical space, this corresponds to a temporal change in imaginary rotations (5c). Since (77) must be satisfied without exception, a resistance opposes the disturbance of orthogonality caused by the acceleration, because the orthogonality reestablishes itself in every element of time. This resistance counteracts the accelerating cause and appears phenomenologically as a general principle of inertia, which every form of hermetry satisfies. Another property common to all forms of hermetry is gravity; for the entelechal-aeonic structural unit—that is, the form of hermetry $a$—appears as a “transcomponent”  (with respect to spacetime), while the $a$ terms appear in physical space as graviton systems  (24) of a gravitational field. It is possible that the fundamental reason for the equivalence principle of inertia and gravitation lies in  this fact. The property of imponderability $(a, b)$ or  that of ponderability $(c, d)$ is determined solely by the algebraic behavior  of the hermetic form, whereas the property of the electric  charge field appears only in the case of the eneametry of complex condensations. The properties of the elementary synmetronic cyclic condenser fluxes with respect to flux velocity and diameter are summarized in (78).
