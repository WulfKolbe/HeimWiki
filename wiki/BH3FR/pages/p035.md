Under these conditions, the equivalence principle (2) can then be extended to a general system of structural nonlinear state equations with spacetime structural levels (3), whereby the covariant  indexing of the mixed-variable triple indexing of the spacetime state functions is non-Hermitian, and therefore the covariant can be split into a Hermitian and an anti-Hermitian component. Since three indexings independently run through the digits 1 through 4 of the spacetime coordinates, there are $4^{3}=64$ discrete point spectra of structural eigenvalues in the sense of spacetime structural levels. However, it can be shown that, as a consequence of the non-Hermitian spacetime structure, 28 of these 64 spectra remain empty in principle (3a), and thus need not be taken into account, so that $64-28=36$ spectra are to be expected.  According to the principle of structural equivalence, these structural levels  are always energy-equivalent; however, in the case of a closed system (which is a matter of boundary conditions) must satisfy a symmetry of the global Poincaré group and thus must be treated as components of an energy-density tensor of this group. However, the square matrix of such a tensor must have six rows, because 36 energy spectra must be accommodated. Since the rows of a tensor are vectors, the tensor can only be represented in a space whose dimensionality is identical to the type of the tensor matrix. For this reason, two hidden world coordinates were introduced, through which spacetime is extended to a six-dimensional world, such that spacetime appears as a subspace of this world. An analysis of the nature of these hidden coordinates shows that both coordinates are imaginary, like the light-time of Minkowski space, so that the signature of the world is uniquely given by  (+ + + ---). In contrast to time, the two hidden dimensions are reversible but cannot be interchanged with one another or with the other dimensions of the world; whereas the real coordinates of the physical subspace—which is compact with respect to the rotation group—are interchangeable, as described by (4) through (5c).
