Although these coupling structures are “dynamic in themselves,” one might doubt  whether such a dynamic structure can be mapped onto an algebraic scheme at all—which is certainly true for the decay and  interaction processes (correspondences); however, during the intervals of temporal stability, this dynamic structure is in a steady state of dynamic equilibrium (principle of compressoriosis), which, under the condition (97a) can certainly be mapped onto an algebraic scheme if the case of complex hermetics is chosen in (58). First, a synmetronic analysis of the state of the electric charge field inherent to enameetry yields a system of internal charge field components (98) analogous to the external relation (28), which is appropriate with regard to the implied $d$ spectrum. This immediately yields a boundary relation (98a) for the configuration number $k$ and the electric charge quantum number $q$. Accordingly, for the configuration number $k$, the only possibilities are $k=1$ or $k=2$, while the electric charge quantum number can lie between 0 and 3. 

Under the stability condition (97a), (58) can be solved using the energy-matter equivalence and the protosimplex concept. This yields a system of relations described by (98b) through (98e), with which the energy masses of the ponderable elementary fundamental patterns can be described. Here, the following picture emerges for the ponderable $c$ and $d$ terms (regardless of the property of invariant basic patterns): Such an elementary structure of matter is a micromare region of space characterized by a fourfold contouring of correlating protosimplex zones (configuration zones). In the very dense (nearly impenetrable) central zone, the protosimplex occupancy increases—as the sum of the cubic numbers—from 1 to the value of the corresponding occupancy parameter. This central region is surrounded by the inner zone, in which the protosimplex occupancy increases dramatically in a similar manner (up to the occupancy parameter of the inner zone). This internal zone, in turn, is surrounded by the mesozone, whose density increases linearly up to the mesozone’s occupancy parameter, which is thus—
