Rather than having to use a large and complex symmetry group, the radical unification approach seeks a “primordial field”—a common origin for the invariance groups of the various subtheories. In 1973, HEISENBERG argued that the fundamental symmetry group and the number of fundamental fields should be minimal, and that higher-order symmetries and phenomenological fields should be generated solely by a dynamical process. 

According to radical unification, most of the broken symmetries observed in elementary particle physics are structural symmetries that arise from a many-body structure of the particles. The gauge group $S U(3)$ of the strong interaction is not added as a new group to the conceived base group, but is derived from it. The three colors are assigned not to the group $S U(3)$ with eight gluons, but to the group $S O(3)$ with three gluons. The electroweak symmetry group $S U(2) \times U(1)$ and $S O(3)$ thus trace back to a single basis group. The radical unification approach includes the primal field hypothesis of Heisenberg’s world formula. 
c) Geometric Approach 
1) Geometrodynamics 

John A. WHEELER’s geometrodynamics takes the Einstein–Maxwell equations as its starting point and relates the energy density to the curvature of empty spacetime.  Topological interpretations are provided for the distinct nature of the vacuum, leptons, and quarks. “Knots” in the fine structure of the topology of spacetime are regarded as elementary particles.
