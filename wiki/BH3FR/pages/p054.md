The work is based on the four validity regions (Volume 1), whereby the first validity region contains exact solutions for low metron numbers,  while the second validity region contains a first approximation in the range of high metron numbers. The third validity region refers to the infinitesimal limit $\tau \rightarrow 0$, while the fourth validity region describes the transition to the macroscopic field continuum (correspondence principle). The synmetronic fundamental problem is presented as a partial solution (62) through (64) in the second validity range. In (64), it becomes particularly clear that the compositional condenser aggregate, raised to the power of the synmetronic correlation exponent, becomes identical to the analogous synmetronic condenser aggregate, where the correlation exponent represents the ratio of compositive to synmetronic condensation stages. This characteristic value of the correlation exponent is specific to the partial solution in the second domain of validity and is clearly illustrated by the numerical ratio (65a) described by the eigenvalue selectors (65). 

The complete solution to the synmetronic fundamental problem results from a metronic integration of its partial solution. Here, it first becomes apparent that the regions of extrema of the linear aggregate of relevant fundamental condensers are identical to the inflection regions of the corresponding fundamental selectors (66).  The solution to the fundamental problem is then the statement (67) with respect to the fundamental selectors; however, this is only one component of this complete solution. The other statement relates to the correlation tensor and can be derived via (66a). There must be a scalar coupling selector (68) that describes the interrelationship between the correlator elements, such that the general correlation tensor, in the case of symmetronics, exhibits the nature of a coupling tensor of internal correlation processes. An analysis of the extrema shows that the maxima of this scalar coupling selector—that is, the maxima of internal structural correlations—always coincide with the minima of the corresponding fundamental condensers,
