The world can be replaced by a calculus of differences, in which constant area differences $\tau$ are assumed. In Chapter III of the first volume, covering (M1) through (M 31b), such a calculus is developed under very  general assumptions, specifically in $N$-dimensional space with $p$-dimensional metrons. Since here the content of every $p$-dimensional region is an integer multiple  (15a) of the metronic elementary quantity, all functions become  selectors that select sequences of numbers, while structural state functions of the metric space state become so-called condensors. Here, the concept of a condenser appears as a metaphor; for if a metronic space (metronic tensor) deformed in any non-Euclidean way is projected onto a Euclidean  space, then, due to $\tau=$ const, the metrons in the reference space would appear shortened and condensed, and this condensed state is described by the selector of the fundamental condenser. Such fundamental condensers are therefore, as state selectors of the metaphorical condensation state, those selectors that describe the metric state of a space with respect to the chosen reference space. Finally, this selector theory of fundamental condensers shows that polymetric structures are possible. Furthermore, the theorems derived in this chapter provide a scheme of metronization rules that allow a  fact formulated in infinitesimal calculus (point continuum) to be transposed into the structures of a metronic tensorium, provided that the metron in question and the nature of the space in question are known.
