A whole spectrum of higher energy levels arises from the fundamental pattern.  However, these terms cannot be generated according to this pattern (107); rather, due to  the nonlinearity of the zone occupancies, they arise from a single process  of energy transfer as resonance, so that the higher-energy  terms can be described as the resonance spectrum of a ground pattern. For the time-dependent occupation parameters, there is a lower bound determined by the framework structure and a finite upper bound (107a) for each configuration zone.  It follows directly from the construction principle (107) and this interval of time-dependent occupation numbers that these occupation numbers for the central zone always remain positive, but for the remaining zones they may also have a negative sign, thereby erasing parts of the framework structure of the respective zone. Since the mesozone increases linearly—that is, its variation behaves algebraically in the same way as the occupation of the external zone—a protosimplex transfer (107b) between the external and meso zones if, for example, the occupation of the external zone were to fall below its lower bound, which can occur in baryon resonances. In this case, the last linear rise would be removed from the mesozone during the protosimplex transfer, and its occupation number would be reduced by 1, thereby compensating for the forbidden negative occupation of the extern zone. However, this transfer is  possible only between the external and mesozones; that is, if the  mesozone were completely unoccupied, the negative occupation  of the external zone could not be compensated for, and the resonance term would  not exist. In this way, forbidden terms arise in the theoretical resonance spectrum that must be excluded. 

An examination of the ground-state terms and their resonances in an abstract multidimensional lattice allows for a description of the energetic term shift and thus the explicit derivation of the formation law for the configuration zones—occupations of all ground-state terms and their resonances—by a protosimplex generator (108), which is based on a base rise, a structural power, and an excitation function derived from the sequence of positive integers—
