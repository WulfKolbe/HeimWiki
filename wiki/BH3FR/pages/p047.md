The ratio of electrostatic to gravitational attraction was investigated, which revealed, on the one hand, that the physical concept of field lines must be revised due to $\tau>0$ and that the number of field lines always remains finite (40), while on the other hand, using (39a) in comparison with empirical astrophysical data, it could be shown that, of the three-valued problem (38), only the positive branch of an expanding universe (41) can be considered. However, this has the consequence that the described discrepancy between $D / 2$ and the Hubble radius leads to a cosmological paradox (41a) if the cosmological redshift is interpreted as a Doppler effect. To resolve this cosmological paradox, one would either have to assume completely unrealistic large values for the temporal change in $D$, or this redshift must be interpreted differently. The first possibility is ruled out because, otherwise, (5c) would imply a Lorentz matrix in spacetime that would be incompatible with the empirical reality of special relativity, since for the imaginary part of the world velocity, one would have to use not approximately the speed of light in a vacuum, but precisely that unrealistically large value for the rate of change of $D$. The solution to the cosmological paradox (41a) can therefore only consist of finding, in place of the interpretation of the redshift as a Doppler effect, another process that allows for a consistent interpretation of the redshift and its anomalies.
