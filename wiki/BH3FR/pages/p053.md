For the sake of brevity, the term “synmetronics” is used as a technical term to describe the special case of polymetry with a three-row correlator matrix in the six-dimensional world tensor. Any attempt to carry out a synmetronics of the world must ultimately aim to discover the law of composition of synmetronic partial structures, as demonstrated in (54) through (56a). This led to the conclusion that the non-Hermitian elements of the world’s correlator, that is, the polymetric fundamental selectors of the partial structures, have constant selectors as anti-Hermitian components, whereas only the Hermitian component is relevant as a correlation power and depends functionally on the respective lattice selectors. Consequently, however, all fundamental condensers of the Hermitian form characterized by the respective sieve operator become Hermitian (56a) with respect to the covariant basis signature. The actual composition law (57) of the partial structures follows from an analysis of the parallel shifts and consists of an additive superposition of the—in general—  synmetronic fundamental condensers—relative to the respective Hermitian form—into the Hermitian fundamental condenser of the composition field. Using this composition law, (19) can be split, which leads to the representation of a set of synmetronic world-selector relations (58) dependent on the hermetic form and (58a),  while the correlation tensors in (59) help determine the synmetronic fundamental condensers. 

An analysis in the abstract function space shows that the  synmetronic fundamental condensers can be interpreted as convergent state selectors of the synmetronic space state of the relevant  subspace of the world tensor (60), such that the synmetronic condensation levels (59) also lie in discrete point spectra, especially since all functional selectors—such as synmetronic space condensers, etc.—prove to be Hermitian.  Since the two relevant matrix traces (60a) of the synmetronic world selector (58a) also exist, the synmetronic fundamental problem can be formulated using the completed form (61) and (61a).
