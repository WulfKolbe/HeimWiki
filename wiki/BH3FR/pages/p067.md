The possible invariant ground states (101) are thus completely described by these six quantum numbers and the choice of time helicity. According to relations (99) through (100a), all  quantum numbers of such a pattern can be reduced to the configuration number $k$ , so that for the two possible values (98a) of this number, all sets of quantum numbers (101) of the invariant basic patterns can be numerically determined (101a), resulting in 5 multiplets $k=1$ and  7 multiplets $k=2$. If one considers these 12 possible multiplets of invariant fundamental patterns in comparison with the measurement data of stable and metastable elementary particles, it becomes apparent that the configuration number (72), when increased by 1 and combined with the baryon number—which was introduced for empirical reasons— but $P / 2$ is identical to the isospin number, which was also introduced for empirical reasons. Accordingly, $Q / 2$ is identical to the empirical spin quantum number, and the  structure distributor $C$ is identical to the empirical strangeness number.  With this interpretation of structural quantum numbers, an interpretation (101b) of the coupling structures described by the invariant fundamental patterns as empirically discovered elementary particles also becomes possible. However, a neutral counterpart to the electron has not yet been empirically discovered; nevertheless, this neutral electron is identical to the lower bound of the $c$ spectrum (32). The baryonic $\Delta$ quartet appears empirically as the beginning of the $\Delta$ resonances, while the $o$quartet, with the exception of $o^{--}$, can indeed empirically simulate corresponding $\Xi$ resonances. Interpreting the configuration number as the baryon number plus 1 allows, together with time helicity, a rewriting (101c) of the  patterns (101); however, the scheme (102) proves advantageous for practical use because the fundamental symmetry applies only to $k$ and $P$ , and thus has a very small scope.
