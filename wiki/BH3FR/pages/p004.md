With the publication of Volumes 1 and 2 of *Burkhard Heim: Elementary Structures of Matter*, Heim’s theory of elementary structures is now available in a form that poses a challenge even for experts. A new terminology, numerous  references to individual formulas, and the highly condensed formulation of the conclusions sometimes present the reader with great difficulty in following the ideas presented. Furthermore, even the expert is likely to find it somewhat difficult to compare Heim’s theory with the best-known theories of modern physics. 

This introduction therefore addresses these challenges and seeks to facilitate the reading of the two volumes through an introduction, a comparison of Heim’s theory with modern physics, a summary of the fundamental ideas from Volumes 1 and 2, and a glossary of terms and formulas. 

In Section I, Dipl.-Ing. Walter Dröscher examines the development of modern physics since the beginning of our century and contrasts Heim’s theory with the best-known theories of modern physics. This comparison highlights not only the originality of Heim’s theory but also a wealth of points of contact with the other theories, which should make it easier to grasp this entirely new perspective on elementary particle physics. 

In Sections II and III, Burkhard Heim provides a very concise summary of the discussions in *Elementary Structures of Matter*, Volumes 1 and 2, which, given Heim’s style of expression, requires the utmost concentration. Even reading this summary—and especially the aforementioned volumes—makes the glossary of terms provided in Section IV and the set of formulas listed in Section V indispensable aids.
